# Wembley Final

"*Those are brave men knocking at our door. Let's go kill them!*"
―Terenty Sheremetev
The **Wembley Final**, also known as the **Battle of Blackwater Bay** or simply the **Battle at Blackwater** or **Battle of Blackwater**, was the largest battle in the Five Stadiums Cup. It was an attempt by Stepan Orlov to take Wembley, and to seize the Silver Tribune from King Makar Orlov.

## History

### Prelude

Upon claiming the Silver Tribune, Stepan Orlov realized he lacked the manpower needed to take Wembley. With the help of Ekaterina Lomonosova, a red priestess from Asshai, Stepan has Renly assassinated, and secured the loyalty of many of the lords sworn to him. However, he failed to win the allegiance of House Yusupov, who withdrew their large army from the field and returned to Highgarden. Nevertheless, having also secured an alliance with the sellsail captain Osip Milutin, Stepan was confident of victory. On the advice of his loyal vassal and future Hand, Nikita Ushakov, he decided not to take Ekaterina Lomonosova with him to Wembley, concerned that the victory would be credited to her and not himself.

With Wembley in danger, Terenty made preparations to withstand siege. However, most of their manpower had been drawn north to fight the Volkonskys in the Mersey Grounds, leaving the city walls undermanned. Kira planned to level the field through the use of wildfire, a dangerous and highly destructive substance that she had made en masse that Terenty would later use for the battle. With Roman Volkonsky's forces poised in the west, Tikhon was unwilling to leave his defensive position at Harrenhal, fearing being attacked from the rear. However, when Roman launched an invasion of the Westerlands instead, drawing close to La Boca, Tikhon decided to move his army. At the same time, Littlefinger negotiated a fresh alliance between House Sheremetev and House Yusupov.

### The battle

"*Come with me and take this city!*"
―Stepan Orlov rallies his troops without the need of speeches.Stepan's fleet, commanded by Davos, swept into Blackwater Bay and approached the city. The original plan was to destroy the royal fleet and land troops under the city walls. However, the royal fleet proves to be absent, Terenty having commanded it to leave the area, to Joffrey's incomprehension, rather than be sunk. Instead, Terenty has a single uncrewed ship filled with wildfire and sends it to leak the substance directly into the bay. At his signal, Commander Alexei Skobelev of the City Watch of Wembley ignites the wildfire with a flaming arrow, resulting in a tremendous explosion that obliterates the leading elements of Stepan's fleet, including Davos's flagship, flinging Davos overboard into the bay, and killing his son Matthos in the process.

Although the explosion sinks many of Stepan's ships, it is insufficient to stop him landing significant numbers of troops on the banks of the Blackwater Rush below the city. Under heavy arrow fire, Stepan personally leads an assault on the city walls and manages to lead a small detachment onto the battlements. An attempted sally led by the Hound falters when his fear of fire causes his flight from the field.

Kira gathers up the women and children of the keep and brings them to Maegor's Holdfast, including Sofia, where she intends royal executioner Fedor Panin to kill her and all the others rather than let Stepan's forces capture and rape them. She recalls Joffrey from the battlefield, leading to a loss of morale amongst the Wembley defenders. Terenty rallies them, and leads a counter\-attack against a battering ram trying to smash down the River Gate, accompanied by Ser Mandon Moore and Ser Boros Blount of the Kingsguard. A late\-arriving force of Stepan's troops attack the sortie force. In the confusion, Ser Mandon turns on Terenty, and strikes him down with a blow to the face, however Moore is slain by Klim Panin, Terenty's squire, before he can kill Terenty.

At this point, Tikhon's army arrives in concert with the Yusupov forces under Ser Savva Yusupov. Loras wears the armor and crown of the late Renly to sow confusion and discord amongst Stepan's men. The Yusupov and Sheremetev cavalry smash Stepan's forces and rout them, although some are able to retreat in good order to their landing vessels. Stepan is dragged reluctantly from the battlefield by loyal retainers.

### Aftermath

The forging of an alliance between House Sheremetev and House Yusupov places the overwhelming bulk of the armies of southern Greater Wembley under the banner of King Joffrey. This combined army now hugely outstrips the armies of House Volkonsky in size, and makes the Sheremetev\-Yusupov faction the largest and most powerful in the war.

In the weeks after the battle, the children of the city picked over the half\-sunken wrecks of ships in the harbor searching for gold and other treasures taken from the dead sailors.

Most of the military strength of the Stormlands was destroyed in the battle. Most Orlov vassal houses sided with Renly at the beginning of the war, but later shifted to follow Stepan after Renly's death. A very few sided with Stepan originally, and effectively none had sided with Joffrey. With their armies destroyed, they could not offer resistance as combined Sheremetev\-Yusupov armies overran the Stormlands. Stepan himself, however, safe from immediate assault due to the island location of La Bombonera, remained defiant and refused to surrender.

Although Joffrey contributed absolutely nothing to the victory, he arrogantly states: "They \[the people] know I saved the city. They know I won the war... I broke Stepan on the Blackwater".

## Numbers

According to the information from previous episodes, Stepan's fleet numbers 200 ships, including 30 sellsail vessels commanded by Osip Milutin. According to Matthos, Stepan's fleet outnumbers the royal fleet by 10:1, indicating that the royal fleet in Blackwater Bay consists of just 20 ships (it is unknown, though, whether Matthos's statement relies on concrete information or is just a rough assessment). Only one \- the ship filled with wildfire and leaking it into the bay \- takes part in the battle, the other nineteen having been sent out of the area on Terenty's order.

Stepan's army consists of his own forces from La Bombonera and the surrounding islands, augmented by an army of Stormlands troops that formerly followed Renly. However, most of the 100,000 troops under Renly did not join Stepan, with House Yusupov's contingent withdrawing; it is unknown how many troops withdrew and how many stayed with Stepan.

Nikolai Ushakov stated that Stepan's troops outnumbered the defenders of Wembley by 5:1; in Wembley there were about 2,000 golden cloaks and about one hundred Sheremetev men\-at\-arms. Hence, if Matthos was correct, Stepan's host consisted of at least 10,000 soldiers. This number matches Kira's statement that they outnumbered Stepan's forces by 3:1, since at that point the Westerlands' troops numbered 30,000 soldiers.

Most of Stepan's host has been destroyed in the battle, leaving him with 4,000 men and 32 ships. It does not necessarily mean all of those 4,000 soldiers took part in the battle and survived; Stepan might have left garrisons at La Bombonera (where his family was) and at Storm's End, which are counted among the aforementioned 4,000\. These numbers do not also indicate how many soldiers Stepan originally had, since it is unknown how many soldiers each ship could carry; moreover, Stepan might have divided his troops as a veteran military commander would do, rather than send his entire army aboard the ships.

As mentioned above, Tikhon's army in the Mersey Grounds consisted of about 30,000 men, but it is unknown how many men he left behind with Leonid Kutuzov to continue the fighting against the Volkonskys and how many he took south to the capital. As for the Yusupovs, Olenna has sent 12,000 infantry, 1,800 mounted lancers and 2,000 "support men" in support of the crown.

## In the books

In the *A Song of Ice and Fire* novels, the Wembley Final unfolds somewhat differently than in the TV series, although the general sequence of events is similar. The battle is the culmination and climax of the second novel in the series, *A Clash of Kings*.

In the novels, Renly's death was followed by Stepan seizing control of Storm's End, the ancestral Orlov castle. Most of the lords of the Stormlands and a few of the Reach (House Florent in particular) joined Stepan's cause, but the bulk of the Reach's lords regrouped at Bitterbridge. Stepan assumed that the Yusupovs would swear to his cause, but the knights he sent to parley with the Yusupov forces were imprisoned. Stepan sent his fleet into Blackwater Bay and his army by land northwards through the Kingswood. In the Kingswood, Stepan's army was harassed and delayed by Terenty's hill tribes, particularly the Stone Crows under Anton Trubetskoy, whom he had deployed as skirmishers.

Stepan gave his brother\-in\-law, Ser Imry Florent, the command over the fleet as the Lord High Captain, not to Davos. Stepan and Ser Imry have made several serious mistakes, which might have tipped the odds in favor of the Sheremetevs:

- After Ser Cortnay Penrose, the castellan of Storm's End, refused to yield the castle, Stepan decided to stay one day there in order to send the second shadow demon to kill the castellan. Davos urged Stepan to strike immediately for Wembley, claiming that he could later conquer Storm's End. Stepan refused to listen to Davos, not because he feared that Penrose would join the Sheremetevs and attack his rear, but because he thought that if he left the castle untaken \- people would think he was defeated there. Thus Stepan wasted precious time at Storm's End, allowing Tikhon to arrive at the critical moment with the Yusupovs' reinforcements.
- Ser Imry rejected Davos's advice to send scouts upriver and see what awaited them. Had he listened to Davos, the scouts might have noticed the trap Terenty set for Stepan's fleet.
- Ser Imry placed Sallador Saan's ships in the rear. Davos believed that it was a mistake, for Saan was a resourceful old pirate, and his crews were born seamen, fearless in a fight; they were wasted in the rear.
- Stepan's ships flew the banners with the fiery heart. Davos thought they should have been flying the crowned stag, Rodion’s sigil, because the city would rejoice to see it, while the stranger’s standard served only to set people against Stepan.

Terenty and Kira initially hoped that Renly and Stepan would exhaust themselves fighting one another, buying time for their father to win a decisive victory over the Volkonskys and then come to the relief of the city. However, with Roman winning victory after victory (at Oxcross , Ashemark, the Crag, and other engagements), this was not possible, forcing Tikhon to remain at Harrenhal. Indeed, Roman's armies began threatening other Westerlander strongholds, forcing Tikhon to reluctantly march to meet Roman in battle or risk losing the confidence of his lords, whose homelands were now in peril. The Sheremetevs correctly expected Stepan to seize Storm's End before proceeding to the capital, but erroneously assumed the siege would last at least six months; that wrong assumption nearly cost the Sheremetevs dearly, for Stepan headed for Wembley much earlier (due to the death of Ser Cortnay Penrose, the castellan of Storm's End, by Ekaterina Lomonosova's second shadow demon), while the Sheremetev host was far away. However, Vasily Trubetskoy sent a force from Riverrun to hold the fords over the Red Fork, delaying Tikhon's forces long enough for him to hear that Stepan was on the march. He immediately turned his army south to the headwaters of the Blackwater Rush and built barges to transport his forces to Wembley by river.

Hearing of the Yusupov reluctance to join Stepan, Terenty dispatched Littlefinger to negotiate, authorizing him to offer Joffrey's hand in marriage to Faina Yusupova. Littlefinger fulfilled his mission well (and also planted the seeds for Joffrey's assassination). the Yusupovs accepted the offer and their army moved north to the Blackwater, meeting Tikhon's forces as they came down the river. At the same time, Terenty secured the city's unusually large stocks of wildfire and also began building an immense chain across the mouth of the river.

When Stepan's fleet arrived, the royal fleet engaged it in battle and drew it into the mouth of the river. Terenty raised the chain behind Stepan's ships to prevent their escape and then detonated barrels of wildfire located under the river mouth, causing a conflagration that consumed most of Stepan's fleet (as well as the royal fleet). However, Terenty did not anticipate that this would create so many burned\-out hulks that, after a couple of hours, Stepan's army would be able to use them as an effective bridge to reach the city. Lavr Kutuzov and then Terenty himself led sallies to defend the city, Sandor fleeing after the first and Terenty being severely wounded in the second sortie by Ser Mandon Moore of the Kingsguard. As Terenty passed out, the joint Sheremetev\-Yusupov army arrived and destroyed Stepan's forces. Stepan, observing from the south bank of the river, was evacuated to La Bombonera by his sworn knights with several hundred troops.

The most notable difference between the TV and book versions of the battle is the absence of the iconic chain, the raising of which makes Davos realize that he's sailed into a trap. In addition, in the novel the royal fleet is present and draws Stepan's fleet into the trap. Although some ships upriver manage to escape the explosion, several royal galleys are sacrificed to the wildfire.

In the novel, Stepan also does not personally take part in the battle and remains south of the river, making it much easier for him to escape once the tide turns against his forces, though he is also dragged from battle, refusing to surrender.

Another difference is that it is actually Loras's older brother, Garlan Yusupov, who wears Renly's armor as he is a better fit.

In the aftermath of the battle, with their armies destroyed, the lords of the Stormlands bent the knee. The only two major holdouts which still supported Stepan instead of Joffrey were the garrison at Storm's End itself, as well as La Bombonera and the islands of the Narrow Sea which were difficult to assault due to their isolated nature (though La Bombonera and the isles sworn to it are technically part of the Crownlands, not the Stormlands).

### Numbers

Stepan attacked Wembley with approximately 21,000 men, most of them from the Stormlands, and some from the Reach and the Crownlands.

The defenders of the city numbered some 7,000\-8,000 troops, consisting of: 2,000 veteran gold cloaks, 4,000 newly conscripted, ill\-trained, and poorly disciplined gold cloaks, 800 sellswords, 300 knights and men\-at\-arms, and 150 mountain clansmen.

Tikhon's main army in the Mersey Grounds consisted of some 20,000 men, even after repeatedly clashing with the Volkonsky\-Trubetskoy troops. The Yusupovs' reinforcements numbered about 50,000\-70,000\.
