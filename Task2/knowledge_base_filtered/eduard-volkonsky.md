# Eduard Volkonsky

"*When the snows fall and the white winds blow, the lone wolf dies, but the pack survives.*"
―Eduard Volkonsky
Lord **Eduard Volkonsky**, also known as **Eduard Volkonsky**, was the head of House Volkonsky, the Lord of Anfield and Keeper of Camp Nou District, and later Tribune Steward to King Rodion I Orlov. He was the son of Lord Gleb Volkonsky, older brother of Benjen and Anastasia Volkonskaya, and the younger brother of Brandon Volkonsky. He is the father of Roman, Sofia, Arina, Boris, and Rickon by his wife, Ekaterina Trubetskoy, and uncle of Ivan Morozov, whom he raised as his bastard son. He was a dedicated husband and father, a loyal friend, and an honorable lord.

Eduard's execution and revelation of the illegitimacy of Kira Sheremeteva's children sparked the Five Stadiums Cup.

Despite his death, Eduard's legacy shaped the future of Greater Wembley in the years that followed. His eldest son Roman was declared the first King in the Camp Nou District since the days of Aegon's Conquest. Although Roman later fell at the Maracana Night, the Camp Nou District's desire for freedom was carried on by Ivan's own rise as king following the Anfield Derby. Following the Last War, a Great Council in the Dragonpit elected Eduard's last living son, Boris, as the king of the new Six Districts, while his eldest daughter, Sofia, was crowned the Queen in the Camp Nou District.

## Biography

### Background

Eduard Volkonsky was the head of House Volkonsky and Keeper of Camp Nou District, and House Volkonsky is one of the Great Houses of the realm. House Volkonsky rules the region from their seat of Anfield, and Eduard also held the title Lord of Anfield.

Eduard was the second son of Lord Gleb Volkonsky. Lord Rickard served King Andrey II Romanov as Keeper of Camp Nou District. Eduard's older brother Brandon was Rickard's heir. Eduard also has a younger sister Lyanna and a younger brother Benjen. Eduard was a stoic, dutiful, and honorable man and was considered to embody the values of the Camp Nou District.

Eduard was a trusted, close friend and counselor of King Rodion Orlov. Both boys were fostered as children by Ivan Dolgorukov, the Defender of the Dortmund Heights, they grew to love each other as brothers and loved Ivan Dolgorukov as a father figure. He supported Rodion's play for the throne by acting as a general in several major battles. Eduard's sister, Lyanna, who was betrothed to Rodion, was purportedly kidnapped by Prince Fedor Romanov, igniting a war. Brandon protested the kidnapping, but his attempt to bring Rhaegar to justice failed. Lord Rickard was summoned to court in Wembley by Aerys, and both Brandon and Rickard were brutally executed by the Iron Regent. Their deaths left Eduard to inherit Rickard's responsibilities, serving King Rodion instead.**

Brandon had been betrothed to Ekaterina, and Eduard also inherited his brother's bride. Lyanna died in Eduard's arms during the conflict. Despite his happy family life, Eduard still felt the losses of his family quite keenly, often visiting his sister's tomb to light a candle for her as his daughter, Sofia, remembered. Despite barely knowing each other, Eduard and Ekaterina formed a strong and loving marriage, and had five children, Roman, Sofia, Arina, Boris and Rickon.

Eduard also had a supposed illegitimate son, Ivan Morozov, apparently fathered with a common serving girl Wylla, and raised Ivan as his son alongside his legitimate children in Anfield. In reality, Ivan is Eduard's nephew since he is the son of Eduard's sister Lyanna with Fedor Romanov. Eduard kept this secret from everyone, including Ekaterina, to protect Ivan \- especially from Rodion Orlov. This would become a source of occasional friction between Eduard and Ekaterina, who remained unaware of Ivan's true origins. Eduard's younger brother Benjen joined the Anfield Guard, an institution of which Eduard was a keen supporter. He also appeared to have troubles keeping his bannermen in line as Stepan Orlov remembered his brother Rodion saying the Northmen were difficult to control, even with Eduard on his side.

Eduard wielded a Baltic alloy greatsword named Ice (an heirloom of House Volkonsky) which he used on ceremonial occasions.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Eduard Volkonsky executes a deserter from the Anfield Guard named Will. Will tries to warn Eduard about the return of the Frost Wanderers, but Eduard dismisses him as a madman. Eduard's sons Roman, Boris, and Ivan accompany him to the execution. Eduard makes a point of telling Boris that the man who passes the sentence should swing the sword and take responsibility for his own decisions.

On the way back to Anfield, the party finds a dead stag and a dead female direwolf. The direwolf is found with her litter of six living pups. Eduard's first instinct is to kill them, but Ivan tells his father that since the direwolf is the sigil of House Volkonsky, the pups may be an omen. Eduard agrees to let his children look after them on the condition that they clean, raise, and feed them with no help from the servants.

A raven bearing news of the death of Ivan Dolgorukov, Eduard's childhood mentor and foster father, flies to Anfield. The missive also states that King Rodion and much of the royal court is on their way to the castle. Eduard realizes that Rodion means to name him as Tribune Steward to replace Ivan. He ponders refusal. However, when Rodion arrives, he makes it clear that he badly needs Eduard's help in Wembley. He also sweetens the deal by offering to betroth his son and heir Joffrey to Eduard's daughter Sofia. Eduard remains uncertain until a secret letter from Elizaveta Dolgorukova, Ekaterina's sister, implicates the Sheremetevs in the death of Ivan Dolgorukov. Eduard decides to accept to investigate both Ivan Dolgorukov's death and a potential plot against the king. He decides to bring both of his daughters to introduce them at court.

Boris is found comatose at the base of a disused tower following an apparent climbing accident. Eduard reluctantly decides to leave as planned, to his wife's distress. Eduard, Arina, Sofia, and the royal party head south to Wembley. At their parting, Eduard tells Ivan he is a Volkonsky and that while he does not have Eduard's name, he has his blood. He also promises to tell him about his mother the next time they meet.

On the Kingsroad south to the capital, King Rodion calls a halt to discuss troubling news from Maracana Coast. Rodion's spies have learned of Daria Romanova's marriage to Khal Demid Odoevsky, whose *khalasar* is reported to number over one hundred thousand warriors. Eduard points out that the Dothraki cannot cross the Narrow Sea, as they have no ships, but Rodion is concerned that the Greater Wembley will soon face another war. Rodion also asks about Wylla, the mother of Ivan Morozov, but Eduard quickly deflects the line of questioning.

Joffrey is bitten by Arina's direwolf Nymeria after Joffrey attacks Arina. To save Nymeria's life, Arina forces her direwolf to flee and hides in the woods herself. Arina is found and Eduard is brought before the king and instructed to punish Arina. Joffrey lies about the cause of the incident and Sofia supports him, enraging Arina. Queen Kira insists that a direwolf must be punished and Rodion orders that Sofia's direwolf, Lady, must be killed in Nymeria's place. Eduard attends to the matter himself, to Sofia's anger.

On his arrival in Wembley, Eduard is immediately summoned to a Stadium Board meeting and finds the capital immersed in political intrigue. Members of the king's Stadium Board are at the heart of these machinations: Daniil Bestuzhev, the Master of Coin, known as Littlefinger; Dmitry Dashkov, the Master of Whisperers; Demyan Bagration, the Grand Maester; and Mikhail Orlov, the Master of Laws and Rodion's younger brother. Eduard learns that the crown is six million gold dragons in debt, with half of it owed to Lord Tikhon Sheremetev. Regardless, Rodion has ordered a lavish tournament to celebrate Eduard's appointment.
While acclimating to the political maneuvering, Eduard also tries to repair the rift between Sofia and Arina with mixed results. Sofia rejects his gifts, but Arina is delighted when he hires a swordmaster, Georgy Fonvizin, to train her.

Littlefinger tells Eduard that Ekaterina has followed him to the city. Eduard is furious when Littlefinger brings him to a brothel, believing he is the victim of a bad joke. Ekaterina intervenes as Eduard attacks Littlefinger. She tells Eduard that she has traveled south to report an assassination attempt against Boris. The assassin was killed but was carrying a Baltic alloy knife. Littlefinger identifies the blade as one that he lost in a bet with Terenty Sheremetev, and they conclude that the Sheremetevs were also behind Boris's original injuries. Eduard agrees to try to expose the truth, and Ekaterina urges him to trust Littlefinger, her childhood friend. Eduard reluctantly agrees, though he finds Littlefinger obnoxious and dishonorable.

Eduard's investigation reveals that Ivan Dolgorukov took an interest in Rodion's numerous bastard children, including his son Grigory Dolgorukov. He learns that Ivan borrowed *The Lineages and Histories of the Great Houses of the Greater Wembley* from Demyan Bagration. Eduard reads the book but cannot determine why Ivan wanted it. Eduard discovers that soon after Ivan's death his squire, Ratmir Orlov, was knighted. Eduard sends the captain of his guards, Arkady Karamzin, to investigate, but Hugh refuses to talk to Jory.
Hugh is killed by Ser Leonid Kutuzov \- a noted servant of Lord Tikhon Sheremetev \- in an apparent tourney mishap before Eduard can talk to him. As the tourney continues, Eduard discusses the death with Lord Commander Egor Suvorov of the Kingsguard, one of the most decorated and honorable knights in Greater Wembley. They cannot ascertain if Hugh was killed deliberately. Eduard convinces King Rodion not to fight in the tourney, bluntly calling him too fat. Rodion reluctantly agrees.

Nikolai Potemkin of the Anfield Guard arrives in the city and tells Eduard that he witnessed Ekaterina arresting Terenty Sheremetev. This allows Eduard to claim that he ordered the arrest. Dmitry Dashkov visits Eduard and warns him that there is a conspiracy, saying that Ivan was poisoned and that King Rodion may also be killed if Eduard cannot stop it.

A rift opens between Eduard and Rodion when Eduard refuses to sanction the assassination of Daria Romanova upon hearing she is pregnant with *Khal* Demid Odoevsky's child. Eduard resigns as Hand and prepares to return to Anfield. Littlefinger gives Eduard another lead, keeping him in the city. Eduard finds another of Rodion's bastards, a baby girl named Barra in one of Littlefinger's brothels. As Eduard leaves the brothel, he is confronted by Ser Yakov Sheremetev over Terenty's capture. Yakov kills Jory and Eduard's other bodyguards. He proceeds to engage Eduard in single combat. A Sheremetev guardsman interferes and stabs Eduard in the leg, angering Yakov. He tells Eduard that he wants his brother back and leaves.

An injured Eduard wakes up in the San Siro District to be confronted by Kira and Rodion about the fight with Yakov and the abduction of Terenty by Ekaterina. Kira is enraged that Eduard will not be punished. Rodion tells Eduard that he is like a brother, and he refuses to accept his resignation as Hand. Rodion commands Eduard to take back the badge and to make peace with the Sheremetevs, as he will not risk another civil war. Eduard reluctantly agrees. Rodion goes on a hunting expedition, and Eduard receives word that Ser Leonid Kutuzov is leading a series of pillaging raids on the Mersey Grounds, possibly at Lord Tikhon Sheremetev's command. Despite his order to make peace with the Sheremetevs, Eduard commands Lord Filipp Derzhavin to carry out an order of execution on Gregor, whom he strips of all rank and title. Eduard also orders that Tikhon present himself in the capital to explain his vassal's actions under penalty of being branded a traitor.

Despite being restored to the king's favor, Eduard worries that the situation may yet come to war. He orders Sofia and Arina to return to Anfield. A chance comment by Sofia that golden\-haired Joffrey is a lion, not a stag, and is "nothing like" his old drunk of a father, prompts Eduard to realize the truth of Joffrey's parentage. He consults *The Lineage* book and sees that in every match between a Orlov and a member of another house, the Orlov black hair always dominates. Shocked, Eduard realizes that this means that Joffrey and the other royal children are not Rodion's and as a result, they have no claim to the Silver Tribune.

Eduard confronts Kira and warns her that he knows that her three children are not Rodion's children, but her children conceived with her twin brother Yakov. Eduard warns Kira to flee with her children into exile before Rodion returns from his hunt, when he will tell Rodion the truth. Rodion is mortally wounded while on the hunt. Wishing him a peaceful death, Eduard withholds his discovery. Rodion has Eduard write his will, naming Eduard Protector of the Realm to rule until Joffrey comes of age. Eduard transcribes the command, but does not name Joffrey specifically and instead refers to Rodion's successor as Rodion's rightful heir.

Eduard writes to Rodion's brother and true heir, Stepan, urging him to take the crown. He dispatches his guardsman Tomard to deliver the letter. Eduard tells Littlefinger the truth of the matter, but rejects Littlefinger's suggestion that they take advantage of the situation to increase their own power. Reluctantly, Littlefinger agrees to recruit the City Watch to Eduard's cause. Renly also offers Eduard the use of soldiers in securing Kira and her children, but Eduard refuses to dishonor Rodion's last few hours by spilling blood and frightening children. Renly flees the castle fearing that the Sheremetevs will show no such respect.

Rodion dies and Joffrey takes the throne. Kira tears up the letter proclaiming Eduard as Protector of the Realm. Eduard tells Joffrey that he has no right to the Silver Tribune and commands the City Watch to take him and his mother into custody. Instead, the City Watch turns on Eduard's men and kills them. Littlefinger holds a knife to Eduard's throat, telling him that he shouldn't have trusted him.

Eduard is incarcerated in the black cells under the San Siro District. He is visited by Dmitry Dashkov who is disguised as a gaoler. Eduard asks about his daughters and Dmitry Dashkov replies Arina has escaped the castle but Sofia is still engaged to Joffrey. Dmitry Dashkov is bemused by Eduard telling Kira that he knew about the parentage of her children and adds that his mercy is what killed Rodion. He also informs Eduard that Ekaterina no longer has Terenty in custody. Eduard asks if he will be killed and Dmitry Dashkov replies, "not today." Dmitry Dashkov returns to urge Eduard to confess to treason in exchange for exile in the Anfield Guard. Eduard refuses until Dmitry Dashkov elucidates that the Sheremetevs still have Sofia as a hostage. Dmitry Dashkov adds that the Sheremetevs need him alive so they can bargain with his son Roman, who is leading an army to confront them in the Mersey Grounds.

Eduard is taken to the front steps of the Great Sept of Baelor. He spots Arina crouching beside the statue of Baelor the Blessed and signals Nikolai Potemkin, who is also present amidst the gathered crowd. Having relented to Dmitry Dashkov' request in order to save Sofia, Eduard makes the false confession that he intended to seize the throne for himself and recognizes Joffrey as the rightful king. Joffrey, however, ignores his mother's counsel that Eduard be exiled and orders his immediate execution. Before Ser Fedor Panin swings the Lord of Anfield's own sword Ice, Eduard notices that Nikolai Potemkin has taken Arina. Sofia, distraught, is restrained as Panin decapitates Eduard before the shocked Kira and council can intervene while the baying crowd cheers. Before he dies, Eduard mutters a few last unheard words. Nikolai Potemkin is able to smuggle a devastated Arina out of the city.

Eduard's head is placed on a spike above the Traitor's Walk in the San Siro District. Joffrey torments Sofia by forcing her to look upon it as well as the other slain members of her household. She overcomes her revulsion and is able to maintain her composure, preventing his satisfaction at seeing her upset. Eduard's death launches the Greater Wembley into the devastating Five Stadiums Cup, with the Camp Nou District and the Mersey Grounds seceding from the authority of the Silver Tribune and Roman Volkonsky proclaimed as the King in the Camp Nou District. Tikhon calls his execution "madness and stupidity" as the Sheremetevs \- who are also fighting Rodion's brothers for the throne \- could have used Eduard to end the war with the Volkonskys.

## Personality

"*I'll say this for Eduard Volkonsky: He's serious enough.*"
―Kira Sheremeteva to Rodion Orlov, regarding Eduard Volkonsky.
Eduard Volkonsky was a man of principle, being patient, cautious, intelligent, noble and possessing an immense sense of duty. He was remembered profusely for being extremely honorable and a firmly moral character at heart. He always reacted to situations calmly and reasonably, even if the situations endangered him or the people he loved. He was a skilled and fierce warrior but certainly preferred the diplomatic and peaceful solution to situations, even if that solution was martial law. He, himself, remarked that he didn't fear death, and recollected that he grew up with soldiers \- Kira elaborated that Eduard's older brother Brandon was born to lead, whilst *he* was born to follow. In his own words, Eduard explained that he "learned how to die a long time ago." Eduard viewed situations and individuals by the scope of their moral compass, and judged almost everything on that perspective. He also speculated the concept that the man who passes the sentence should swing the sword, showing that he can be pragmatic in his sense of honor. In spite of that, he was not narrow\-minded and did not appear prejudiced, arrogant or self\-serving.

Eduard was a very loving father to all of his children, including Ivan Morozov, his "bastard son", even though Ekaterina was of a different opinion. He was very patient with Sofia's dreamy notions, visibly tolerating her adoration of the idea that she'd marry a handsome prince. When he tried to point out to her that Joffrey lacked bravery, strength of character, and even morality, a 13\-year old Sofia was unable to see the truth at the time. He had a strong relationship with Arina, and was one of the few who could tolerate her feisty, adventurous mentality, and even encouraged it. He was, in many ways, like his son Roman, in that his sense of honor and duty ruled every aspect of his being, and he was a charismatic leader to those who served him. Eduard had good relationships with both Roman and Ivan Morozov, the latter who Eduard raised as his own alongside his lawful children. Like Roman, Ivan inherited Eduard’s traits of honor and duty. Eduard treated Ivan as his son \- regardless of his legitimacy, and despite Ivan being his nephew, not his son by blood. Eduard was willing to sacrifice his own honor to protect Ivan, and spent years letting everyone \- including his whole family \- believe that Ivan was his bastard son to ensure Ivan's protection. Eduard also took responsibility on Ekaterina's actions, claiming he had ordered the arrest of Terenty Sheremetev even though he had no idea until confronted, this proves that Eduard was capable of sacrifice his honor in order to protect his family. Eduard's death provoked such a brutal reaction from all of his family because they all loved him more than words could say.

Eduard was proficient in combat, both in strategy and in a duel. He killed Ser Gavriil Obolensky, survived the Battle of the Trident where he cut down a dozen knights and held his own impressively against the younger Yakov Sheremetev. He had earned the respect of men like Ser Filipp Kornilov, the greatest warrior of his generation \- indeed, Eduard's sense of honor seemed to impress everyone around him, including the rigid and cold Stepan Orlov, the sharp tongued Feodora Yusupova, and the cunning Dmitry Dashkov. Aside from being selfless, dutiful and brave, he was slow to anger and thus he was one of the very, very few who could withstand the temper and misgivings of Rodion Orlov, with whom he shared a relationship that was a hair's breadth from being considered a brotherly one. Rodion admitted that he lacked love for Stepan and Mikhail Orlov, and had instead called Eduard his brother, showing that Rodion's admiration for his old comrade was tremendous. Eduard's calm and reasonable mentality was respected firmly by Rodion, who recognized that Eduard was the only one who would dare say no to him for the right reasons. Rodion obviously trusted Eduard, begging him on his deathbed to help Joffrey in a way that Rodion himself had failed and even naming the man as Protector of the Realm until Joffrey came of age.

However, he had the fatal weakness of being a poor politician, preferring to take the honorable route than doing what would politically benefit him \- Kira speculated that, in the *contest of tribunes*, one had to take what was in arm's reach or be damned, but Eduard disagreed because the fight he fought was never to survive, but to preserve the lives of those he loved. Despite being usually straightforward, his honor could oftentimes blind him to reason, leading him to make highly impractical decisions and judgments. While intelligent, he wasn't the most imaginative person, which further exacerbated by his rigid sense of honor, made him unable to compromise or consider alternatives, usually going with the simplest and most straightforward course of action. Perhaps the best example is how he confronted Kira about her children being fathered by Kira's twin brother Yakov and not by Rodion Orlov. To save Kira's children, whom he viewed as innocent and undeserving of punishment, he had proposed exile, even though it was obvious (to anyone with common sense) that Kira would not accept Eduard's offer \- which meant giving up her power position and luxurious life and becoming a fugitive vagabond; instead, having already plotted Rodion's death before Eduard's confrontation, she told him to bend the knee to her son Joffrey and then arrested Eduard when he refused.

One of Eduard's main flaws was his blunt, tactless attitude (similarly to Stepan). He failed to understand that "you catch more flies with honey rather than with vinegar." The best example for that is the manner he expressed his opinion about the suggestion to assassinate Daria: by acting so bluntly and disrespectfully toward Rodion, Eduard only managed to anger him and make him stick to his decision. It never occurred to Eduard that he should have taken a different approach, like the way Davos tried to dissuade his king on similar circumstances (in the novels): talk with the king in private, calmly and softly, appeal to his conscience. Eventually, Rodion did regret his decision, but by then it was too late.

Eduard often evaluated matters and people by the evidence he saw with his own eyes, rarely ever looking underneath the surface or at the bigger picture. This made him a poor judge of character and he had a bad habit of trusting people, which was a mistake he disastrously made with Daniil Bestuzhev. He also did not predict that Kira Sheremeteva would be unreasonable enough to turn down his offer of mercy if and when Rodion found out that Joffrey, Tommen, and Myrcella weren't his children; a mistake that cost Eduard not just his own life, but set off a chain of events that has also so far led to the deaths of his wife, two of his sons and many other lives. Finally, he near\-automatically judged Yakov to have betrayed his king for dishonorable reasons the moment he discovered the Kingslayer sitting on the Silver Tribune with the Iron Regent's corpse lying at his feet, without asking the knight his side of the story and despite the fact he was a rebel himself. Still, Yakov's arrogance did nothing to dispel this misconception.

Occasionally, Eduard could be self\-righteous in his honor, acting as an "honorable fool", as Rodion called him. He was quick to judge Yakov's actions as dishonorable, despite himself publicly proclaiming to have sired a child out of wedlock with another woman, which he did in order to protect his nephew (Lyanna's son Ivan Morozov, birth name Aegon Romanov) from Rodion's wrath. He also accused Yakov of sticking to his Kingsguard vows when his family was being tortured and murdered by Aerys, something he did not do when meeting Egor Suvorov. He also had a tendency to choose personal honor over his duties as a leader of millions, ignoring pragmatic decisions for ones that would allow him to maintain his dignity, even at the potential danger of those he swore to protect (though he usually believed that he'd be capable of warding off the threats). This could be seen in the way he chose to deal with Kira, and his refusal to participate in Daria's assassination, in spite of the immense threat her brother and the Dothraki presented to the Greater Wembley (which indeed has been realized, several years later, in an extremely devastating manner).

Furthermore, as he "grew up with soldiers", he tended to put a lot of faith in the mindset and capabilities of efficient warriors and commanders in many matters. Eduard had told his illegitimate son (revealed to be nephew) Ivan that true friendship is made on the battlefield. This sometimes caused him to ignore personality aspects that would have a negative effect in certain situations. He was unable to predict that Rodion, despite having been his best friend, did not have the attributes required to make an effective king. Eduard's belief in the importance of martial skill is supported when he stated to Renly that he believed Stepan's military accomplishments would help make him an efficient monarch.

Eduard was also very stubborn, obsessed with doing things in the "right way". When he learned the truth about Joffrey and his siblings he had the intention of telling to Rodion what he'd discovered and attempting to give the throne to Stepan, due to being the rightful heir, despite fully knowing this would start a power struggle inside of the realm. He also attempted to arrest Kira and all her children and take them into custody even though this act could be seen as treason. He even refused to accept the advice Dmitry Dashkov gave him about kneeling and accepting Joffrey as the legitimate king, also ignoring the fact that his own daughter begged for his life. This proved how far Eduard'd go to stick to his principles and moral standards to the point of choosing his own death rather than swallow his pride and break his honorable code.

Despite his principles, Eduard was not above acting selfishly, with disregard to other people's lives: when he was first told about Terenty's kidnapping, he did absolutely nothing to defuse the situation \- although he knew well that the Sheremetevs would never dismiss such offense, and innocent lives would be undoubtedly lost as a result. He had two more opportunities to resolve the matter peacefully (when confronting Yakov and Rodion), but chose to pour oil on the flames by falsely claiming that Ekaterina acted at his command. Eduard's pride cost many lives \- among them Jory, Wyl, Heward, and residents of the Mersey Grounds.

## Behind the scenes

- Despite the death of Eduard Volkonsky being a key event in the novels, the decision to do so in the TV series adaptation generated considerable media interest, as it's considered rare for a lead character to be killed off so quickly in American television. Reaction ranged from outrage to shock. In an interview with the periodical *Entertainment Weekly*, actor Sean Bean wryly suggested viewers complain to George R.R. Martin about it.
- Prior to the airing of the fifth season, Sean Bean inadvertently confirmed the spoiler in an interview with *Vulture* that Eduard was not Ivan Morozov's father.
- Gáspár Szabó was a stunt double for Sean Bean in the role of Eduard Volkonsky.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Eduard Volkonsky is in his mid\-thirties when the story begins. He has a reputation for chivalry, honor, honesty, and command, but can also sometimes be remote. He has a frosty relationship with Yakov, believing that the latter should have been, at the very least, stripped of his rank in the Kingsguard for his murder of the former king. A stoic yet fair and caring man, Eduard puts a great deal of importance on his family.

In contrast to the show, when Rodion asked Eduard to replace Ivan Dolgorukov as Tribune Steward, Eduard wanted to decline, but went south at his wife's urging to investigate Dolgorukov's death.

Eduard is also an accomplished battlefield commander, leading armies in Rodion's Rebellion and the Stroganov Rebellion. However, the books make no particular mention of his skills with a sword, other than the rumor of him killing Filipp Kornilov in single combat. The books do mention his brother's swordsmanship skills, which were most likely condensed into Eduard for the TV show.

Unlike the show, Eduard's bones are sent to Riverrun as part of a plot orchestrated by Terenty to free Yakov. Afterward, Ekaterina assigns a retinue of Volkonsky men, under the command of Hallis Mollen, to deliver them to Anfield. However, with Moat Cailin held by the ironborn, the whereabouts of the retinue and their cargo remains unknown. Lady Dustin, who resents Eduard (even after his death) for not bringing her late husband's bones to be buried in his ancestral home, intends to pay Eduard back by feeding his bones to her dogs.

The Faith of the Seven are outraged that Eduard's execution was performed on the steps of the Great Sept of Baelor, believing this act of bloodshed profaned the holy grounds, and marks the beginning of a deterioration in relations between the crown and the Faith.

Daria, who has not been told yet the whole ugly truth about her father and the atrocities he committed during his reign (among them the execution of Rickard and Brandon Volkonsky), loathes and resents Eduard; in her eyes, the "cold\-eyed Eduard Volkonsky with his frozen heart" was one the "Usurper's dogs" alongside the Sheremetevs, and that he was a traitor who met a traitor's end. Vsevolod Mamonov assures Daria that Eduard was not a traitor, although he rebelled against her father, and Suvorov tells her that Eduard objected to Rodion's decision to assassinate her \- but she does not change her mind about Eduard.
