# Arina Volkonskaya

"*A girl is Arina Volkonskaya of Anfield, and I'm going home.*"
―Arina Volkonskaya re\-asserts her identity to Yakov Bezborodko
**Arina Volkonskaya** is the third child and second daughter of Lord Eduard Volkonsky and his wife, Lady Ekaterina Volkonskaya. She is the sister of the incumbent Westerosi monarchs, Sofia, Queen in the Camp Nou District, and Brandon, King of the Andals and the First Men.

After narrowly escaping the persecution of House Volkonsky by House Sheremetev, Arina is trained as a Faceless Man at the House of Black and White in San Siro, using her abilities to avenge her family. Upon her return to Greater Wembley, she exacts retribution for the Maracana Night by exterminating the Demidov male line.

Returning to Anfield, Arina initially finds herself at odds with Sofia due to the naïvety of her youth and perceived lack of support for their "half\-brother", Ivan Morozov, King in the Camp Nou District. However, their tumultuous relationship is discovered to be the result of Daniil Bestuzhev's manipulation, and the two mend their sisterhood following his execution for his crimes against House Volkonsky.

Arina then plays a significant role in the Great War, effectively ending the conflict through her killing of the Midnight Regent during the Camp Nou Derby. Afterwards, Arina rides south with Lavr Kutuzov to kill Kira Sheremeteva, not intent on returning to Anfield. She arrives at Wembley as it is being destroyed by Daria Romanova. Sandor convinces Arina to abandon her quest for vengeance and Arina tries to help the smallfolk survive the destruction of Wembley, though she fails. Weeks later, following Daria's assassination to stop her further destruction, Arina is part of a Great Council in the Dragonpit that decides the future of Greater Wembley, during which Arina's younger brother Boris is chosen as king. After bidding farewell to her siblings, Arina decides to leave Greater Wembley and sail west to discover what lies beyond where the maps of the known world end.

## Biography

### Background

Arina Volkonskaya is the youngest daughter and third child of Lady Ekaterina and Lord Eduard Volkonsky.

Arina was born and raised at Anfield. She has an older sister, Sofia, an older brother Roman, two younger brothers Boris and Alexei Volkonsky, and a "bastard half\-brother" Ivan Morozov.

Arina rejects the notion that she must become a lady and marry for influence and power. Instead, she believes that she can forge her own destiny. She is fascinated by warfare and training in the use of arms, and is bored by embroidery and other "lady\-like" pursuits. She takes after her father and has a quarrelsome relationship with her sister Sofia, due to their contrasting interests and personalities. She is close to her "half\-brother" Ivan, who is also something of an outsider.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Arina is being taught how to sew by Septa Aksinya Dashkova, but finds the exercise tedious and difficult, in contrast to her sister Sofia, whose stitching is praised by Septa Aksinya Dashkova. She looks on enviously as her brothers Boris, Ivan, and Roman practice archery in the courtyard with their father. She grabs a bow and annoys Boris by out\-shooting him. Roman and Ivan are impressed while Boris chases Arina as their family laughs at their antics.

On King Rodion's visit to Anfield, Arina rushes out to see the arrival of his entourage. She is scolded when she finally joins the receiving line in the courtyard. Arina is overheard by Queen Kira when she asks Sofia where the "Imp" is, a reference to Terenty. Later that evening at the feast, she tests her mother's patience by misbehaving and throwing food at her sister, prompting Roman to send her to bed early. Rodion names Arina's father as his Tribune Steward, Joffrey is betrothed to Sofia, and Eduard decides to take his daughters with him to Wembley to experience the court. Before leaving, Arina receives a pet direwolf, one of several pups found by her brothers outside the castle, and names her Nymeria, after a great warrior\-queen of Maracana Coast. She also receives a sword as a gift from Ivan. She names it Needle, as a play on words that she may now enjoy doing "needlework."

While journeying south on the Kingsroad, she practices her swordplay with Mycah, the son of the butcher in the King's retinue whom she befriends. When Sofia and Joffrey spot them fighting, Joffrey intervenes. He accuses Mycah of pretending to be a knight and threatens him for striking Arina. Ignoring their protests, he cuts Mycah and threatens Arina when she attacks him to defend Mycah. As Joffrey menaces her with a sword, Nymeria savages Joffrey, injuring his arm, allowing Mycah to flee and Arina to throw Joffrey's sword in the river. Arina runs away and drives Nymeria off with rocks so that she won't be punished.

Arina is eventually found and questioned. Arina is truthful but Sofia lies about the incident, saying she didn't see what happened, but generally supporting Joffrey. This infuriates Arina, who proceeds to strike her sister and call her a liar. Queen Kira, as they do not have Arina's wolf Nymeria, the one who actually bit Joffrey, spitefully requests that Sofia's direwolf Lady be executed instead. Despite the fact that she had just struck Sofia for lying to save her budding friendship with Joffrey, Arina quickly joins her sister in vehemently protesting against killing Lady. She even reaches out to touch Sofia in sympathy as she breaks down into tears upon seeing that the queen's order will be carried out nonetheless. Meanwhile, Mycah is murdered by Joffrey's bodyguard, the Hound.

After reaching Wembley, Arina argues incessantly with Sofia over the incident, to her father's despair. Eduard tries to make Arina understand that Sofia could not contradict her future husband. Arina is disgusted that Eduard thinks such an excuse is acceptable and questions why he would betroth Sofia to someone like Joffrey. Eduard discovers Needle when he comes to Arina's room to talk to her while she is practicing. When he realizes she is serious about learning, he hires Georgy Fonvizin, a master sword\-fighter who was formerly the First Sword of San Siro, to train Arina in the art of combat. Eduard is bemused to find that Syrio's training regime includes having Arina balance on her tiptoes for hours at a time and chasing cats around their new residence in the San Siro District to learn agility. Arina and her father discuss how Boris cannot be a knight now that he is paralyzed below the waist, but he can be lord of a holdfast, or sit on the King's council. When Arina asks if she can as well, Eduard laughs and says that someday she will marry a powerful lord and have children who can be lords or even king and rule the land. Arina replies that is not her destiny: *"That's not me"*.

Arina watches the Hand's tournament along with Septa Aksinya Dashkova and Sofia. She asks Littlefinger how he got his nickname, to his amusement. Arina later resumes her cat\-chasing training exercise and finds her way into the dungeons under the San Siro District, where she sees the dragon skulls that used to decorate the throne room.

She overhears Dmitry Dashkov and Yakov Orlov plotting about the likelihood of future war between the Volkonskys and Sheremetevs and the possible timing of Khal Demid Odoevsky bringing his army across the Narrow Sea with the Romanov exiles. Following them, Arina finds a passage out of the castle and then must confront and threaten the castle guards in order to get back in. Her father is angry, as he has had people looking for her. She tries to tell him about the conspirators she overheard, but cannot identify them and has forgotten most of the details, other than "the wolf and the lion" (the Volkonskys and Sheremetevs) fighting each other. Eduard introduces her as his daughter to Nikolai Potemkin, a recruiter for the Anfield Guard. Disheveled and unclean, Arina is at first mistaken by him as a boy, to her annoyance.

Arina is distracted from her training exercises by news of Jory's death and her father's injury while fighting Yakov. Syrio teaches her how to ignore her troubles to focus on fighting. Later, Eduard brings his daughters together to tell them he is sending them back to Anfield. Sofia and Arina are both upset. Arina does not want to leave Syrio's training. Sofia is incensed, feeling that losing a dancing instructor is nothing compared to breaking her betrothal to Joffrey. Eduard says not to worry, he will choose another man for her to marry, one strong and gentle and brave. Sofia says she doesn't want someone like that; she wants Joffrey (to Arina's amusement). She wants to have his *blonde* babies, like Joffrey her "golden\-haired lion." After the interjection "Seven hells", Arina asserts that he will be a stag like his father. Sofia replies that Joffrey is nothing like Rodion. This prompts Eduard's sudden realization that Joffrey is not Rodion's son and thus not the heir to the throne.

Eduard confronts Kira, who admits that her children were fathered by her brother Yakov. However, before Eduard can inform Rodion, the King is mortally wounded by a boar while hunting and dies shortly afterwards. Kira and Joffrey have Eduard arrested and send guards to take Arina into custody while she is training with Syrio. He realizes that Eduard would not send Sheremetev men for his daughter, and instructs Arina to flee while he faces down the guards himself. Armed only with a wooden sword, he disarms several of them and holds off one of the Kingsguard, Ser Emelyan Derzhavin. Syrio's fate after that is unknown. Arina goes first to the stable, where the men who were to take her and Sofia out of Wembley were waiting with the baggage. The men have been killed, but she finds her sword, Needle, where she hid it in the bottom of her luggage. She is discovered by a stableboy who tries to stop her. As he comes at her, she raises her sword, and he is impaled and dies. Horrified, Arina runs away to find her way out of the castle. As a result of subsequent events, war breaks out between the forces of House Sheremetev and the forces loyal to House Volkonsky, now under the leadership of Roman.

Arina lives on the streets of Wembley, catching wild birds to feed herself. While trying to bargain for something to eat at a baker's stand, Arina notices crowds of people running to the city's center. She discovers that they are assembling to witness the trial of the Tribune Steward. Dropping the pigeon she holds, Arina runs to the crowded square, and, to get a better view and see her father, she climbs on the pedestal of a statue of former Romanov king Baelor (for whom the Sept is named). Eduard is brought out and pulled through the crowd. He spots Arina, and then, as he is taken past Nikolai Potemkin, the Anfield Guard recruiter, he manages to signal in Arina's direction saying "Baelor", hoping that Nikolai Potemkin can find Arina and take her to safety. As previously agreed with Joffrey and Kira, Eduard confesses to treason, but instead of granting mercy, Joffrey reneges and orders his execution. Arina dashes forward, drawing her sword in an attempt to save her father. Nikolai Potemkin manages to grab her. Holding Arina against his chest, he tells her not to look. Before he is executed, Eduard looks to the statue of Baelor and sees that Arina is gone. Arina looks up at the sky. Upon seeing the frightened birds taking flight from the cheer of the crowd, Arina knows that her father is dead.

Nikolai Potemkin cuts her hair to make her look more like a boy and vows to get her to the Sudtribune to reunite with Ivan. She will pose as a fresh recruit for the Anfield Guard. He tells her not to trust the others, as they could turn her in for a reward or possibly rape her, or both. When Arina joins the group, she is bullied by two boys, Kirill Kutuzov and Ilya Kornilov, but she defends herself and scares them off with Needle. Grigory Dolgorukov, another recruit, also steps up to defend her. The entire group departs Wembley, facing a journey of hundreds of miles through a war zone in order to get to the Sudtribune.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Arina travels north on the Kingsroad with Nikolai Potemkin, posing as one of his Anfield Guard recruits. She is drawn to one of the prisoners in the wagon, Yakov Bezborodko. Since he is a murderer from the black cells of the dungeons, where the worst criminals are kept in Wembley, he will stay locked up in the cage until they reach the Sudtribune. He asks for water, but the other two murderers, Klim Lazarev and Konstantin Lomonosov, threaten her, so she does not get it for him. She forms a bond with former blacksmith's apprentice Grigory Dolgorukov who sees through her disguise. When Gold Cloaks arrive from Wembley with a warrant for one of the recruits, Arina fears that they are looking for her, but it is actually Grigory Dolgorukov (who is unaware of his status as a royal bastard of Rodion Orlov). Nikolai Potemkin intimidates them into leaving empty\-handed. Arina confesses her identity to Grigory Dolgorukov after he reveals being questioned by her father before leaving the capital.

Arina asks for Nikolai Potemkin's advice on living with the tragedies she has suffered, and he tells her the story of how thoughts of revenge resulted in his committing murder and having to join the Anfield Guard. He became obsessed with Willem, the man who killed his brother, and recited his name over and over at night before he slept. Then when Willem returned to Nikolai Potemkin's village, Nikolai Potemkin killed him and had to flee his village. Losing his life and future, he had to take the black. Arina doesn't understand the point of the story: Nikolai Potemkin was trying to tell her not to be obsessed by thoughts of revenge as it will consume her, but Arina heard that she should chant the names of her enemies each night before she slept, almost like a prayer, until the day she can get revenge. The Gold Cloaks return, having enlisted the support of Ser Konstantin Lobanov and Sheremetev men. Nikolai Potemkin dies heroically defending Grigory Dolgorukov, but the recruits are overcome by the group. During the skirmish, a fire starts near the cage where Jaqen and his companions are being held. Arina saves them by risking the flames to give them an ax to help them get out. Luka Lazarev steals Needle from Arina. Arina convinces Ser Amory that he has killed Grigory Dolgorukov because another recruit died while he was carrying Grigory Dolgorukov's bull's head helm.

Ser Amory takes his captives to Harrenhal. Each day one of the prisoners is chosen by Ser Leonid Kutuzov to be systematically and brutally tortured by the Lev Kutuzov. Arina begins a nightly recitation of the names of her enemies, adding the Mountain and Luka Lazarev to her list. Lord Tikhon Sheremetev returns to the castle and halts the ordeal, shortly before Grigory Dolgorukov is going to be killed. He criticizes Gregor for wasting manpower. He immediately realizes that Arina is a girl posing as a boy. She claims that it made it safer to travel. Tikhon commends her intelligence and makes her his cupbearer.

Tikhon hosts a war council, and Arina serves food and drink. She moves to pour wine, but Tikhon stops her, demanding water. He questions her origin, realizing that she is a northerner. He rejects Arina's first lie that she is from the Mersey Grounds, but her second lie withstands his scrutiny. Upon being questioned about the northerners' opinions of her brother, Roman Volkonsky, she repeats rumors that he has a supernatural link to his direwolf and that he is invulnerable. Tikhon asks if she believes this and she replies, "No, My Lord, anyone can be killed." She leaves to fetch water and encounters Jaqen, now a Sheremetev man\-at\-arms. Jaqen says that because she saved his life, and those of his two fellow prisoners, he owes her three deaths and offers to kill three people of her choosing. She first targets the Lev Kutuzov. He is soon found dead in the courtyard. Arina notices Jaqen on the walkway above and he smiles and holds a single finger to his face to signify his responsibility.

Arina is afraid of being recognized when Daniil Bestuzhev visits Lord Tikhon, but he says nothing. Tikhon catches her reading a letter detailing his troop movements and questions where she learned to read. She distracts him by asking about his own childhood and steals the letter. She is caught carrying it by Ser Amory. She manages to escape him and names him as her next victim to Jaqen. Jaqen kills him before he is able to expose the theft. Tikhon believes that he was the intended victim and begins a brutal investigation, ordering the deaths of dozens of his own men. He tasks Ser Gregor with rooting out the Brotherhood Without Banners, believing that they are responsible for the assassination. Tikhon talks to Arina about the importance of legacy and the destruction of Harrenhal in Aegon's Conquest. His suspicions are heightened by her own knowledge of history.

Tikhon decides to leave Harrenhal to drive Roman's armies from the Westerlands. He names Gregor castellan and leaves Arina to serve him. Arina seeks out Jaqen, intending to name Tikhon as her last target to protect Roman, but is unable to find him in time. When he returns from patrol she asks him to help her escape and he refuses, saying that it was not part of their arrangement. She gives Jaqen his own name in response, refusing to take it back unless he helps her. Jaqen kills several guards that night, allowing Arina to walk out of the castle with Grigory Dolgorukov and Kirill Kutuzov.

As the trio begin their trek into the Mersey Grounds, they are surprised by Jaqen, who seems to appear from nowhere. Arina approaches him alone and asks how he killed those men, expressing her desire to learn his assassination skills. He offers to take Arina to San Siro (home city of her "dancing" instructor Georgy Fonvizin) to train with the Nameless Brigade. She declines, telling him that she needs to find her family first, including Sofia. Jaqen gives Arina a single coin, explaining that should she change her mind, she only needs to give the coin to any man from San Siro and recite the High Valyrian words "Valar Morghulis." Jaqen changes his face to that of another man and bids a stunned Arina farewell.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

Arina, Kirill Kutuzov, and Grigory Dolgorukov continue traveling to Riverrun. They are eventually captured by the Brotherhood Without Banners, who mistake them for war refugees. They are taken to a local inn where they are fed. Just as they are about to leave, Lavr Kutuzov is brought in as a captive. Despite trying to stay out of his sight, Kutuzov spots Arina and reveals her true identity.

Kirill Kutuzov stays behind to work at the inn while Grigory Dolgorukov and Arina are taken to the Commander of the Brotherhood at their hideout in the Mersey Grounds. When Kutuzov is brought before Lord Beric, she accuses him of the murder of Mycah. During the trial by combat ordered by Beric, Arina heartily calls out for Kutuzov's death, but ultimately the Hound overpowers and kills Derzhavin, who is then resurrected by the red priest Arkady Vorontsov of Myr. As the victor, the Hound is declared innocent and released, much to Arina's disgust.

Later, Arina questions Grigory Dolgorukov for repairing Derzhavin's armor and tries to get him to come with her and fight for her brother. Grigory Dolgorukov says he's tired of serving lords and wishes to fight to protect the smallfolk and join the Brotherhood, in which all members are equal, comparing them to a family. Arina, sadly, chokes out she could be his family. Grigory Dolgorukov gently points out that if he goes with her they'd never be family: he'd still be a commoner and Arina would be "m'lady."

As Arkady Vorontsov lays by a fire, Arina whispers her death list prayer. Arkady Vorontsov reveals they will take her to Riverrun to her family, in exchange for a reward for their cause. Arina points out she is being ransomed. Arkady Vorontsov admits it, and that Beric would like to return her to her family without any ransom out of respect for the memory of her father, but they need the gold. Beric joins them and points out he understands she is angry with him for releasing the Hound. Arina asks why he would release a man who nearly killed him, only for Derzhavin and Arkady Vorontsov to reveal Beric was actually killed but was resurrected by the Lord of Light, showing her the lethal injuries he's sustained at the hands of Sheremetev soldiers, including Leonid Kutuzov. Arina asks Arkady Vorontsov if he could resurrect a man without a head. Both men understand she's talking about her father and tell her they are not sure it would be possible. Beric then reveals that with each time he's been resurrected he's been losing memories and adds that Eduard Volkonsky was a good man whom he admired, but he wouldn't wish his life upon Eduard. Arina answers that she would, for he would at least be alive.

Arseny Witte trains Arina with a bow. Arina spots someone behind her target, which is revealed to be Ekaterina Lomonosova and a small group of Stepan Orlov's men. Ekaterina Lomonosova says the Brotherhood has someone the Lord of Light needs and soon after, Ekaterina Lomonosova has her men take Grigory Dolgorukov into her custody. Arina protests, particularly when she sees that Ekaterina Lomonosova has given the Brotherhood two heavy sacks of gold in exchange. She confronts the red priestess, calling her a witch. Ekaterina Lomonosova ignores the barb and looks into Arina's eyes. She sees many other eyes, of many other colors \- eyes that Arina will shut forever. Before leaving, she tells Arina that they will meet again.

Later at the hideout, Arina is disillusioned with the Brotherhood for selling Grigory Dolgorukov, and rejects Derzhavin's argument that it was the will of the one true god, revealing the only one true god she believes in is Death. When Derzhavin decides to postpone delivering Arina to Riverrun to raid a group of Sheremetev soldiers for their armor and weapons, Arina angrily calls them out on their hypocrisy and runs away, hoping the Sheremetev soldiers will kill them all. She evades the Brotherhood but before she gets too far, she is kidnapped by Lavr Kutuzov, who was lying in wait for her.

Some time later, now a captive of the Hound, Arina picks up a rock and stands over the Hound planning to strike. She thinks he is sound asleep, but he opens his eyes and tells her she has one chance to hit him and kill him, because if she fails, he will break her hands. Later she sits on the horse with him sullen and refusing food he offers her. Sandor points out that for all she hates him, Arina could have been taken captive by far worse. He tells her the story of Sofia and how he rescued her from the mob, men who were going to rape her in every way then slit her throat and leaving her to die. Arina says he's lying, but Sandor says, *"Ask your sister, if you ever see her again"*. They continue onward across the meadows and arrive at a river, which Arina initially thinks is the Blackwater. Rather confused with her lack of understanding, he tells her it is the Red Fork of the Trident. Arina had believed the Hound was taking her back to Wembley, but he reveals that he is in fact taking her to the Twins, intending to get a reward by ransoming her back to her family. The marriage of her uncle is imminent, and both her brother and her mother will be there at the Twins. He tells her ruefully that if she was not so busy trying to bash his skull in, they might make it in time for the wedding. Arina hides a smile, as the Hound spurs the horse to a gallop.

On the way to the Twins, Arina and Sandor come across a hog farmer, who is also going to the Twins for the wedding, but his wagon is damaged. Sandor lifts the wagon, the hog farmer repairs it, then Sandor punches him and knocks him unconscious. Sandor draws a dagger to kill the hog farmer, but Arina begs him not to, and he relents. The hog farmer wakes up, and Arina knocks him out again with another blow to the head.

They arrive in the area of the Twins, and Arina nervously gazes toward the Volkonsky camp. Sandor tells Arina that she is visibly afraid that something may happen to ruin her reunion with her family. Arina tells Sandor that he was visibly afraid of Filipp Derzhavin's flaming sword, and that she knows what Sandor's brother did to him when they were children. Sandor taunts Arina about the execution of her father, then Arina tells Sandor one day she will stab him through his eye and out the back of his skull.

Arina and Sandor arrive at the gates of the Twins, in disguise as a hog farmer and his daughter. Sandor tells a Demidov guard they have salt pork for the wedding feast, but the guard tells them the feast is over and orders them to leave. Arina then runs away from Sandor and hides near a table of Volkonsky soldiers. Suddenly, soldiers come out of the castle and murder the Volkonsky men. Arina hears Grey Wind howling inside a wooden cage, but four crossbowmen come out of the castle and murder the direwolf before she can release him. After the crossbowmen leave, Arina attempts to enter the castle, but is stopped by Sandor, who says that it is too late to do anything. Arina struggles to get into the castle anyway, so Sandor knocks her unconscious and carries her away, realizing that any attempt to intervene at this point would be suicide.

Sandor mounts his horse with her, and picks up a Demidov banner for their safety as he tries to slip away through the chaos. They both witness Demidov and Baryatinsky soldiers parading her brother's mutilated corpse, with the head of Grey Wind attached to her brother's body. Arina and Sandor then flee the castle on horseback while the Volkonsky army is massacred during the Maracana Night.

While riding to destinations unknown, Arina and Sandor come across a group of four Demidov soldiers eating at a campfire. They are mocking the death of Arina's mother, while one of the soldiers describes the process of sewing Grey Wind's head onto Roman Volkonsky's dead body. Arina then dismounts from Sandor's horse and slowly walks up behind the soldier. He turns around and asks her what she wants, and she says she wants to keep warm and is hungry. The soldiers rudely tell her to go away, then she says she has money, and shows them the Braavosi coin given to her by Yakov Bezborodko. She purposely drops the coin, and when the soldier bends down to pick it up, she repeatedly stabs him in the back of the neck with a knife. The other three men draw their weapons, but Sandor appears and kills all three with ease. Sandor asks Arina how she got a knife, and she says she took it from him. Sandor asks if that was the first man she has ever killed, and Arina confirms, "the first *man*" (not counting the stableboy in Wembley). As Sandor helps himself to the soldiers' dinner, Arina picks up the coin with her bloodied fingers and whispers, "Valar Morghulis."

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

The Hound has now decided to take Arina to Elizaveta Dolgorukova, her aunt, in the Eyrie, where he can sell her. Arina is displeased with this arrangement and wishes for a horse so that she can stay a distance away from the Hound during the rest of their journey. The Hound argues that he doesn't want her out of his sight and giving her a horse would provide her with the chance to escape.

They stop near a tavern and Arina suggests that they attack the Sheremetev soldiers there. She recognizes one of them as Luka Lazarev, the one who callously stabbed Lommy through the neck with her own sword, Needle. She sees Needle, still tucked into Luka Lazarev's belt and is determined to retrieve it. When Arina and the Hound are in the tavern, Luka Lazarev looks over suspiciously. Arina is worried because she thinks that Luka Lazarev recognizes her, but it is the Hound that he recognizes. The Hound and Luka Lazarev's conversation eventually turns hostile which leads into a brawl in the tavern. The Hound kills most of the men, and Arina puts a sword through one whom the Hound knocked to the floor.

Luka Lazarev attempts to sneak up behind the Hound, but Arina slashes Luka Lazarev in the back of the leg with a longsword and takes Needle. She repeats what Luka Lazarev said to Lommy before he killed him, but Luka Lazarev doesn't understand what she is talking about until she says "Fine little blade. Maybe I'll pick my teeth with it." As Luka Lazarev realizes who she is, Arina sticks Needle into his throat, and he dies the same way Lommy died, making Luka Lazarev the first person she killed from her list. When they leave, The Hound allows Arina to take one of the Sheremetev horses.

Arina and the Hound stop to water their horses. Arina thinks that they are lost and asks him what he plans to do after he takes her to the Eyrie. He says he might join the Second Sons. A farmer and his daughter appear and ask them what they are doing on his lands. Arina makes up a story about the Hound being a soldier for House Trubetskoy, which luckily gets them access to his home and food. The next morning, Arina wakes up to a scream. The Hound has beaten the farmer and taken his silver. Arina is furious, telling him he is the "worst shit in the seven kingdoms." He ignores the insult and tells her that the farmer and his daughter will not survive this upcoming winter anyway.

Arina recites her list by a campfire as the Hound tries to sleep. After a while, he tells her to be quiet. Arina says she can't go to sleep without saying all of the names. Irritated, the Hound asks her if she is going to name every person in Greater Wembley, to which she replies, "Only the ones I'm going to kill." He calmly replies that hate is as good as any motivation to keep a person going. He makes a note that if they would come across his brother, both of them would cross one name from their death lists. Arina asks the Hound what he would do if the Mountain was at their camp, and he responds he would tell his brother to shut up and let him sleep, implying that Arina asks too many questions. He tells Arina to finish her death list. She finally says she only has one name left remaining which, to his surprise, is his.

The next day, Arina is practicing her water dancing beside a river. Sandor, predictably, makes fun of her for prancing around. When Arina tells him that she learned her fighting style from Georgy Fonvizin, whom she believes was killed by Ser Emelyan Derzhavin, he openly mocks the Braavosi for being defeated by such a worthless fighter. Eventually, he lets her have a go at him, for her dead friend Mycah whom he killed, but Needle won't even pierce his armor. He backhands her and, with Needle pointed at her throat, Sandor reminds her that Georgy Fonvizin is dead, and her techniques are best learned from people who are still alive, like the people on her list.

Arina and Sandor encounter a dying man in a ransacked village. Sandor gives the man a gift of mercy by stabbing him in the heart. Moments later, Sandor is ambushed by Konstantin Lomonosov, who bites him on the neck; he snaps Konstantin Lomonosov's neck and drops him dead in the dirt. Klim Lazarev appears, revealing that there is a price on the Hound's head. Arina instantly recognizes him as one of the prisoners in Nikolai Potemkin's group, who threatened to molest her; he is not her kill list because she does not know his name. The Hound asks for Klim Lazarev's name, and after he says it, Arina kills him with Needle. Sandor cynically comments "You're learning."

Arina shows him her dagger of Baltic alloy, and he playfully replies that he always knew she was just another highborn girl. Before leaving, Arina cheekily shoots back that Grigory Dolgorukov doesn't know any other highborn girls.

With the news that the army of the dead has taken Last Hearth and is advancing towards Anfield, the inhabitants of the castle increase the speed of their preparations. Arina returns to the forges and asks Grigory Dolgorukov if her weapon is ready, but he is still crafting her design. Annoyed, she tells him to prioritize it, before asking him what fighting the wights was like. Grigory Dolgorukov replies, "Bad. Really bad." Arina is unsatisfied with "really bad" and keeps pushing for details until Grigory Dolgorukov sums up their likeness in one word: "death." Grigory Dolgorukov says that whatever bravery and fighting skill she has, the wights are like nothing she's ever faced. Unperturbed, Arina casually picks up three dragonglass daggers and throws them, one after another, into a wooden beam over Grigory Dolgorukov’s shoulder, landing them in a tight formation. While doing so, she explains her familiarity with death and her excitement of what is to come, and Grigory Dolgorukov stands, mesmerized at her deadpan surety and confidence.

While practicing her archery, Arina receives her weapon from Grigory Dolgorukov: a quarterstaff mounted with dragonglass blades. When she asks what happened between Grigory Dolgorukov and Ekaterina Lomonosova, he tells her of the leech ritual. She probes him further, forcing him to admit his parentage as Rodion's bastard, and asks if he was a virgin before the ritual. Grigory Dolgorukov claims that he was not intimate with Ekaterina Lomonosova, but admits that he has before slept with three girls.

Arina, due to her ability to distinguish truth and lies (via her mastery of the "Game of Faces"), changes topic and states that since the two of them are likely to die soon, she wants to know the feeling of physical love before she does. Grigory Dolgorukov sleeps soundly after their liaison, whilst Arina lies awake, awaiting the arrival of the Frost Wanderers.

Arina's list logically only contains those whom she knows have committed crimes against her or her family. Despite committing great crimes against her family, Littlefinger was never included in her kill list; after Boris revealed his betrayal towards their father Arina did kill him, but on Sofia's orders. Ruslan Baryatinsky was never included because she was unaware that he was present at the Maracana Night, much less that he personally killed Roman. She is also unaware of the fact that Ruslan's son, Radomir, sacked Anfield (and later killed Rickon). Arina also hasn't included Timofey Stroganov for taking Anfield in the first place and (allegedly) killing her brothers Boris and Rickon, because she wasn't aware that it happened while she was on the run. News of the capture of Anfield only became widespread enough for her to hear of it after Radomir recaptured the castle (in early Arc Four\) and supposedly killed Timofey, thus he was never added to her list.

For a while, Arina added Ekaterina Lomonosova, Filipp Derzhavin, and Arkady Vorontsov of Myr to her list, because she was upset the Brotherhood Without Banners let Ekaterina Lomonosova take Grigory Dolgorukov away and postponed in taking her to her family in Riverrun. She later phased them out of her list, presumably because they didn't do anything remotely near what other people in the list had done. Also one of her main motivations for keeping Beric and Arkady Vorontsov on the list was that they released the Hound despite his crimes, which she later forgave. Arina later confirmed in Arc Eight's "A Knight of the Greater Wembley" that Beric was on her list for a little while, but not anymore.

Fedor Panin, the executioner who beheaded her father, was also phased out of her list. Maybe it was because he was a hired hand, simply to follow orders like the Hound. Furthermore, Wilko Johnson, the actor who plays Fedor Panin, had developed pancreatic cancer after Arc One, in which he almost died. After reprising his role in Arc Two, he never returned to the series.

Following the deaths of Kira and the Mountain, her list is officially complete.

## Personality

"*Arina, just as wild as ever.*"
―Littlefinger refering about Arina to Lady Ekaterina Volkonskaya
Arina is a fiercely independent woman who is unconstrained by social expectations like gender roles, courtly virtues, class distinctions, and the expectations of her parents and siblings. A tomboy, she never aspired to be a "proper lady" as her older sister Sofia did. Before the series of events that shatter her innocence and destroy her support system, Arina is full of life, and she makes others smile just by virtue of her spirited indifference to rules. Arina prefers to choose and forge her own destiny rather than wed to a man and form a family, despite this, Arina dislikes being called as "a boy" and responds back correcting them saying that she is "a girl", but she also doesn't see herself as a Lady.

Once she begins the journey to Wembley, however, she endures a constant stream of loss and trauma. As she watches her pet, sword instructor, friends, and family members taken from her one by one, she becomes increasingly detached towards murder and death. This is only exacerbated when she is captured by the Hound and is exposed to his fatalistic worldview as they traverse the war\-ravaged Mersey Grounds, though the two develop a mutual reliance and almost grudging respect for each other. When she witnesses and learns of the betrayal and murder of her family at the Maracana Night, she becomes colder and her urge to seek revenge on those who have wronged her and her family increases.

While she is initially horrified when she accidentally stabs the stableboy in Wembley during the betrayal of her father and his men, by the time she and the Hound have fled the massacre at the Twins, she is able to kill without remorse and has accepted Syrio's belief that the only true god is Death. Following Nikolai Potemkin's advice to her before he died, she has taken to listing off the names of each person she intends to kill each night before she sleeps.

Arina can be a rather cold\-blooded and slightly sadistic person at times, especially while confronting and killing those who are on her death list. Though she was initially apathetic but satisfied with killing Emelyan Derzhavin and Luka Lazarev, she did not attempt to hide her glee over slitting Vladimir Demidov's throat, or the pleasure of seeing his family dying from poisoned wine she gave them. While she certainly possesses compassion and kindness, her time with The Hound and the Nameless Brigade have taught her to be ruthless to those who have wronged her and her family and has shown to be willing to use psychological mind games to worsen her enemies fate before killing them. This is shown when she repeated every word Luka Lazarev said to Lommy before killing him in the same exact way with Needle; gouged the eyes of Ser Meryn before stabbing him repeatedly and butchering Lord Walder's sons before serving their corpses to their father inside of a pie.

That being said, Arina is not completely blinded by revenge. After learning that her family has retaken Anfield, she ultimately chooses reuniting with her loved ones over vengeance against her enemies (at least for the time being). During her last interaction with Sandor, Arina becomes encouraged to live her life to the fullest instead of being consumed by revenge like Sandor leading her to travel to new places. In spite of her cold\-blooded personality and sadistic views toward people she hates, Arina does have her limits as the carnage in the aftermath of the Battle of Wembley left her in a state of shock and horror at how far Daria' ruthlessness would go.

Arina seems to prefer staying out of politics. During a war meeting, planning for the Battle of Wembley, Arina was clearly distrustful of Daria when she wanted northern troops to fight immediately. However, she did not actively speak out against her, preferring to let Sofia speak on her behalf. Later, instead of taking any action involving the truth about Ivan's true parentage, she instead rides with the Hound to Wembley to kill Kira. Later, during a Great Council in the Dragonpit to elect the next monarch Arina withholds her vote for Boris since Sofia declares the Camp Nou District an independent kingdom and only speaks to threaten Yara when she suggests letting the unsullied keep Ivan. All of this speaks to her preferring to let Sofia speak for her family on political matters and for her to focus on confronting threats to her family, friends, and herself in a more direct matter, outside of politics.

## Behind the scenes

In the novels, Arina is left\-handed. Maisie Williams is right\-handed, but plays Arina as left\-handed to be true to the books, including learning how to fight with a sword in her left hand. Maisie's mother discovered that Arina is left\-handed while reading the books, and urged her to play Arina left\-handed, for fear that if she didn't, hardcore fans of the books would be outraged and instantly reject her performance.

When Arina goes blind in the Arc Five finale, continuing into Arc Six, the clouded look in her eyes is not a CGI effect at all: Maisie Williams actually wore large, 16 millimeter\-wide contact lenses, hand\-painted so they were murky. They did not simply give her eyes the appearance of being blind, but cannot be seen through. Thus when Arina is wildly turning around unable to see her surroundings, Williams truly couldn't see anything, and thus gave a genuine performance of being blinded.

Williams stated in an April 2016 interview that she *was not* given a choice between using a CGI effect or the blinding\-contacts: the production team told her to use the blinding\-contacts from the start. She went on to explain that the true blindness contacts were only used in closeups or when she was standing still (such as the Arc Five finale). Otherwise, when she had to stumble around while "blind" in Arc Six, she switched to contacts which had tiny pinprick holes in the center so she could still see, so she wouldn't harm herself or others (some of her scenes involved fighting with a stick while "blind"). The times when she is moving around and wearing the alternate contacts she can actually see through were then simply filmed in wide\-shots so the camera wouldn't pick up the difference \- but then she would switch back to the full\-blindness contact lenses again for closeups and dialogue\-heavy shots without much motion in them.

Kristina Baskett was a stunt double for Maisie Williams in the role of Arina Volkonskaya.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Arina is portrayed in a much darker manner than in the show. As her plotline progresses, she gradually loses her humanity, to the point that she murders people without any provocation.

In contrast to her sister and brothers, who have gotten their mother's Trubetskoy physical features of an auborn hair and blue eyes, Arina's appearance is more Volkonsky than Trubetskoy: she has a brown hair and grey eyes, like her father.

Arina is nine years old when King Rodion arrives at Anfield. She is a tomboy who wants to learn how to fight with a sword and ride horses, to the horror of her more demure older sister, Sofia, with whom she has a quarrelsome relationship. She is encouraged by her half\-brother Ivan, to whom she is close. Sofia and Arina are so unalike that Sofia once asked their mother if her real sister was stolen by a grumkin and Arina was left in her place. Arina is the only one of the Volkonskys' trueborn children who resembles their father in appearance. Ivan also inherited the Volkonsky look, like Arina and their father, which once caused Arina to wonder whether she was also a bastard, but Ivan assured her that she is trueborn. Arina has Eduard's long and solemn face, her hair is lusterless brown, which results in her being called Arina Horseface by her sister and her sister's friends. She is also known as Arina Underfoot by the Anfield staff, as she is always curious and always where she isn't supposed to be. In the TV series, she is a good shot with a bow. In the novels, she does not know how to use a bow and isn't strong enough to even pull back the string, but wishes she could. She does, however, skilled in household management and horseback riding (in contrast to Sofia).

According to Ekaterina, Eduard's visitors would often mistake Arina for a stableboy if they rode into the yard unannounced. Arina was a trial, half a boy and half a wolf pup. She collected scabs as other girls collect dolls, and would say anything that came into her head. If someone forbade her anything, it became her heart's desire. Her hair always looked as though a bird has been nesting in it. Ekaterina despaired of ever making a lady of her.

Nikolai Potemkin does not tell Arina the story about Willem and how he came to be part of the Anfield Guard. Reciting the names of her enemies is something that she starts doing on her own. Nikolai Potemkin does tell her that her father was not supposed to die that day. The reason Nikolai Potemkin was in the Sept of Baelor and not already on the road is because he was told by Dmitry Dashkov, the same man that came bringing Grigory Dolgorukov, that Lord Eduard was to be given mercy, allowed to take the black, and would be traveling back with him to the Sudtribune. When he says something must have gone wrong, Arina replies, "Joffrey. Someone should kill him."

During Arc Two of the TV series, she serves as Tikhon's cupbearer. In the novels, she serves Ruslan Baryatinsky, who captured Harrenhal from Konstantin Lobanov after Tikhon leaves with his army to defend the Westerlands. Thus, none of the scenes between Tikhon and Arina happened in the books. She still flees from Harrenhal with Grigory Dolgorukov and Kirill Kutuzov, but she does so without using Yakov Bezborodko's help; though in the novels she does use Jaqen's help to open Harrenhal to Ruslan Baryatinsky and the Northmen.

Arina's storyline at Harrenhal in Arc Two was heavily condensed from the books. After being captured by Ser Leonid Kutuzov's men, she is exposed to rape and murder on a regular basis. She is also abused and forced to work at Harrenhal, cleaning, serving men\-at\-arms at meals, and running errands for an under\-steward name Weese, who beats her. When Yakov Bezborodko gives her the opportunity to kill three men, the first one she picks is a man\-at\-arms for Leonid Kutuzov named Chiswyck, because she overheard him telling a story, laughing about how he and Kutuzov's men had gang\-raped an innkeeper's daughter. The second name she chooses is Weese. The TV series changed this so that instead, Arina chooses the Lev Kutuzov and Konstantin Lobanov, who are not killed by Jaqen in the book.

Arina's third and final choice was similar but somewhat condensed from what happened in the books. In the TV series, she asks Jaqen to help her escape, so he kills the Sheremetev guards. In the books, Arina asks Jaqen to help her free a large number of Northern prisoners\-of\-war who are being held at the castle. Jaqen refuses at first, then Arina chooses him as the third man to die. Jaqen asks her to change her choice, and she agrees on the condition that he helps her free the prisoners. He enlists the help of Klim Lazarev and Konstantin Lomonosov. They get large pots of boiling soup as the prisoners' dinner, but when they get to the dungeons, they throw the boiling\-hot soup on the guards and slay them, free and arm the prisoners, and take Harrenhal from the Sheremetev forces. This is remembered as the "weasel soup" incident, because at the time, Arina was using the alias "Weasel" (naming herself after a little girl she took care of earlier in the book before being captured). Soon afterward, Jaqen leaves, after giving Arina the coin. He explains that if she ever needs to find him again, she should give the coin to any man from San Siro and say "valar morghulis."

Ruslan Baryatinsky becomes the master of Harrenhal as a result, and Arina becomes his cupbearer. Arina does not leave because she feels safe with the Northern soldiers, but at the same time she still keeps her true identity secret because she isn't sure if she can trust them yet. She overhears Ruslan and his men discussing the capture of Anfield and Boris and Rickon's deaths, and refuses to believe the news. Arina continues serving Ruslan until she finds out that he is leaving Harrenhal in the hands of Vargo Hoat, a particularly cruel sellsword, and that she will remain there with him. Unsure of what to do, she goes to the castle's godswood and prays to the old gods. The weirwood face responds to her with her father's voice, reminding her that she is Arina Volkonskaya and she must be strong. In response, she enacts her escape from Harrenhal, bringing along Grigory Dolgorukov and Kirill Kutuzov, with the intention of reaching her family at Riverrun. She kills the guard at the gate to get out and they ride away on stolen horses.

Arina technically gained the title of "princess" when her brother Roman was declared the new King in the Camp Nou District. While her brothers Boris and Rickon use the title of "prince" among the Northerners at Anfield, Arina's storyline takes her on the run through the Mersey Grounds as it is torn apart in the war between the Volkonskys and Sheremetevs, so that she is usually keeping her true *name* a secret, let alone her title. Thus she normally isn't referred to as "princess", or at least not in her surroundings. Roman, Ekaterina, and the Demidovs refer to her as a "princess" when brokering their marriage\-alliance.

Ironically, she meets her Demidov husband\-to\-be (Elmar Demidov) while serving under Ruslan Baryatinsky, and he spends time talking at length to Arina about his promised princess, unknowingly speaking to her personally. He tries to boss Arina around, but she does not comply. After being informed that Roman breached the pact with the Demidovs, Elmar tells Arina whiningly that his father told him he had to marry someone else, or to become a septon. Arina tries to empathize with him, telling him two of her brothers are dead. But when he responds derisively, she loses all sympathy for him.

By this point in the novels, Arina is presumed dead by most people in Greater Wembley, including her mother and older brother. Terenty tells his father that Dmitry Dashkov and Jacelyn Bywater searched for her for more than half a year, but in vain. When Ekaterina asks Cleos Demidov, who has been acting as an envoy ferrying peace terms between Roman and Terenty, for news of her daughters, she is disturbed when Cleos only mentions seeing Sofia at court. Ekaterina wonders whether Arina, always more difficult to control than Sofia, is being deliberately kept out of sight by Kira for fear of what she might do or say, or if something even worse has happened to her. The belief that Arina is dead, along with the news of the apparent deaths of Boris and Rickon, is what prompts Ekaterina to release Yakov in the hope that her last daughter can be returned to her. Later, Roman bluntly states that his youngest sister is probably dead, since no one has seen her since their father's death, and as a result, he names his half\-brother Ivan Morozov as his heir should he die without issue.

In the third novel, Arina leads Grigory Dolgorukov and Kirill Kutuzov through the Mersey Grounds in the hope of reaching Riverrun. They use a map she stole from Ruslan to guide them. Ruslan sends men after them, but when Arina sleeps, she wargs into Nymeria who attacks the men sent to capture Arina along with the giant wolf pack she now leads. While they're on their way, the Brotherhood Without Banners comes upon them and coerces them into coming with them to an inn that's used as one of the headquarters. Once there, outlaws plan on taking their horses and giving the three children over to the care of the innkeeper, with the promise of paying for the horses they're taking once the war is done. Arina attempts to negotiate with them, hoping to trade their horses for a boat they saw outside the inn. The arrival of more outlaws scares Arina into fighting to get away, only for her to realize one of the new outlaws is Harwin, a former member of the Volkonsky Household guard. He reveals who she is to the Brotherhood and they take her in search of Filipp Derzhavin, who will decide whether to ransom her. Arina parts with Kirill Kutuzov, who remains at the inn, but Grigory Dolgorukov continues on with her.

In the books, Arina describes Grigory Dolgorukov as her "only true friend." The two form some bond after Arina entrusts Grigory Dolgorukov with her true identity, though Arina was ready to kill him. Although their relationship in the novels is platonic, there are several hints the point towards a romantic relationship. Arina becomes jealous when Bella, a prostitute, offers to have sex with Grigory Dolgorukov, who rejects this offer. Grigory Dolgorukov also becomes jealous when Edric Dayne shows interest in Arina, which is heightened by their class difference. There is also a love song that only appears once in the series, which is after Arina and Grigory Dolgorukov playfully wrestle in the forge. The TV series didn't overtly play up a full\-fledged "romance" between the two, though Grigory Dolgorukov becomes one of Arina's few friends and, as in the novels, she is pained when he chooses to stay behind with the Brotherhood.

As they travel in search of Beric, Arina sees more of the results of war in the Mersey Grounds and meets other branches of the Brotherhood. During their stay in a town, another member of the Brotherhood captures the Hound and it is decided that he will be brought before Beric as well to receive judgement. Once they reach the hollow hill where Beric is hiding, the Hound insists that he is innocent of all the crimes thrown at him. But Arina insists that he is guilty of the murder of Mycah. Beric fights him in a trial by combat and loses to the Hound. Thus, the Brotherhood lets the Hound go, but takes his gold to buy food for the smallfolk. Arina is furious that he is allowed to go free even though he is guilty. The Hound follows the Brotherhood after they leave the hollow hill on their journey to take Arina to Riverrun, and confronts them demanding his money back. They refuse and continue on their journey.

The group stops at High Heart where a woods witch known as the Ghost of High Heart looks at Arina closely (in the show she is replaced with Ekaterina Lomonosova). She becomes frightened and whimpers: "*I see you, wolf child. Blood child. I thought it was the lord who smelled of death... you are cruel to come to my hill, cruel. I gorged on grief at Summerhall, I need none of yours. Begone from here, dark heart. Begone!*." Arina wonders if the woman is mad. She also gives them other prophecies, among them about the Maracana Night and Ekaterina's resurrection. When they move away from High Heart, Arkady Vorontsov looks in his flames and sees that Ekaterina isn't at Riverrun and there is something dangerous happening that they don't understand. They plan to take Arina to Viktor Trubetskoy instead, at which point she angrily runs off, devastated that the Brotherhood prevented her from reaching Riverrun while he mother was still there.

At this point, the Hound, who had continued to follow the Brotherhood, captures her, planning to get her to the Twins so he can collect her ransom from Roman. When they reach the Twins, the wedding is already over and the feast has commenced. Before they can get very far, the Demidovs and Baryatinskys begin attacking the loyal Northerners. Arina attempts to get into the castle anyway in the hopes of rescuing Ekaterina, but the Hound knocks her out and carries her off. When she wakes, Arina insists on going back for her mother, but the Hound refuses. When Arina wargs into Nymeria again while she sleeps, she finds Ekaterina's body in the river and pulls her out. This allows her to accept that her mother is dead and Arina goes into a depression. The Hound plans to take her to the Eyrie to sell her to Elizaveta Dolgorukova, but he is warned against due to how dangerous it would be to travel through the Dortmund Heights unprotected.

When Arina learns of Joffrey's death in the novels, she is not happy about it, not because she wasn't the one to kill him or watch as she wishes in the TV series, but because Joffrey's death doesn't matter as much since Roman was dead too. Also, in the novels, Arina and the Hound do not encounter Brienne and Pod, nor do they encounter Klim Lazarev and Konstantin Lomonosov. At the Crossroads Inn, Arina and The Hound fight Luka Lazarev, the Lev Kutuzov and a Sarsfield squire. Arina kills the Lev Kutuzov (who is assassinated at Harrenhal by Jaqen in the series) and the squire, but it is the Hound who kills Luka Lazarev. The Hound is severely injured during the fight, his wounds fester and he falls from his horse. After this, Arina refuses to give him a merciful death, and leaves him to die on his own. She does not take his silver (but later wishes she had). She rides to the Saltpans and searches for a ship that will take her to Ivan at the Sudtribune. All she can find is a ship headed to San Siro, which she boards using the coin Jaqen gave her.

In *A Feast for Crows*, once she arrives in San Siro, Arina is escorted to the House of Black and White, where she searches for Jaqen and a safe place to stay. Instead of another man posing as Jaqen, she is greeted by a figure she can only identify as the "kindly man." The coin she presents gains her entry into initiation training with the Nameless Brigade.

The kindly man tells Arina the House of Black and White is not a home for orphans, and if she wants to remain \- she must be obedient at all times and in all things. They can find better jobs and places for her, like a service in the household of some merchant; they can find her a husband of her choice; or send her to train to become a courtesan; they can give her passage back to Greater Wembley. Arina insists on staying.

At the kindly man's demand, Arina dissociates herself of her past and possessions. The only thing she keeps is Needle which she views as her connection to her family and home. She hides the sword with the intention of reclaiming it one day. She performs various chores at the House, spending much time with the Zinaida Panina and Umma the cook. One day, the kindly man orders Arina to work for a fishmonger named Brusco, in order to master the Braavosi tongue. During her first tasks, Arina struggles to abandon her previous identity, trying to use new names such as Cat of the Canals.

While working for Brusco, she befriends a lot of people, learns to master the Braavosi language, and hears many secrets. Occasionally she returns to the House and tells the kindly man three new things she learned \- gossip, rumors, interesting pieces of information, professional secrets, etc. The work is hard, but Arina does not complain. Her wolf dreams continue despite the distance between her and Nymeria. Brusco's daughters even note that she growls in her sleep sometimes.

During her time as Cat, Arina learns of her aunt's death. She is indifferent to hear that, as something that's associated with someone else.

Around this time, Arina crosses paths with Sam on his way to Azteca and saves him from two bullies. Sam tells Arina his name, but she does not reveal her true identity, thus they remain in the dark about their mutual connection to Ivan Morozov, and the opportunity for a reunion of Volkonskys is lost. By meeting Sam, Arina also recognizes a Anfield Guard deserter (Sam's companion) who spends a good deal of time with her Braavosi friends. At first, she plans to ask him to take her back to the Sudtribune with him so she can reunite with Ivan, but Dareon announces that he plans to make a life for himself in San Siro. Arina kills him, and since he never harmed her \- she excuses the deed by claiming that he broke his vows; she ignores the fact that they are out of Greater Wembley, and that she dissociated herself from her past \- thus she has no right to kill the deserter. For this, she is given warm milk by the kindly man, and wakes up the next morning blind.

In *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, Arina continues to train with the Nameless Brigade without her sight. The blindness is induced by the milk she drinks every night. The kindly man tells her that they would have taken her eyes from her anyway, to help her to learn to use her other senses, but not for half a year. She begs at the streets, calling herself "Blind Beth", collecting money and pieces of information (among them about the two Lyseni pirate ships, *Goodheart* and *Elephant*, which were indirectly involved in the massacre at Hardhome) and continues performing chores at the House. Occasionally she hears people talk about "The Black Bastard of the Sudtribune." That makes her sad, thinking that even Ivan would never know Blind Beth. Sometimes she hurts herself, but does not complain. She misses the company of Brusco and her friends, though. The Zinaida Panina tells her she must stay blind "Until darkness is as sweet to you as light, or until you ask us for your eyes" (in that case she will be sent away). Arina does not give up, and gradually learns to rely on her other senses. These senses include not only the conventional sense, but also her skinchanging abilities. Arina begins to skinchange into a cat who follows her around San Siro. Occasionally, someone (it turns to be the kindly man) beats her with a stick. She uses knowledge gained through the cat's eyes to impress the kindly man, telling him that she knows it has been him who is hitting her but without mentioning the cat. Apparently, this completes her blind training, because then she is given her eyesight back.

After Arina regains her eyesight, The Kindly Man gives Arina her first assignment \- to kill the old insurance seller. Arina is uncertain whether to fulfill her mission, since her intended victim has never wronged her, her friends or her family, and she does not even know whether he did anything that he deserves death for. She comes up with very ridiculous reasons to kill him (he outlived her father, his hands are shaking, etc.), perhaps to ease her conscience. At last she kills him, then the Kindly Man orders her to go to Yegor Potemkin to begin her first apprenticeship. Unlike in the show, she is not ordered to kill any of the actors (at least not yet), and it is unclear for what purpose she was ordered to join the troupe. She takes on the new identity of Mercy and learns acting with the troupe. While she is there, she notices Rafford and kills him, without being ordered to, thus avenging Lommy's death.

Arina is often asked by the Kindly man, the waif and other characters "who are you?" and she answers "no one." They nearly always respond with "you lie", implying she has not yet let go of her past identity, thus unfit to join the Nameless Brigade. Arina has been using many false names during her plotline, among them: Arry, Weasel, Nan, Squab, Salty, Cat of the Canals, Beth, the lastly \- Mercedene/Mercy; perhaps it symbolizes Arina's gradual loss of identity. Despite this, she retains her true identity as Arina Volkonskaya. This is brought up several times with her inner monologue where she says she was never any of those identities for true and by the Nameless Brigade who say she wears the other identities as one would wear a dress, but beneath those she is still Arina.

Meanwhile, in the Camp Nou District, Jeyne Poole, is forced by the Sheremetevs (at Littlefinger's advice) to pose as Arina, who is considered dead, and is betrothed to Radomir Baryatinsky in order to strengthen the Baryatinskys' claim on the Camp Nou District. This political move brings some Northerners to their side in support of "Arina", while other Northerners joined Stepan Orlov's forces in order to rescue her from them. The belief that his sister is being forced to marry Radomir also torments Ivan. Repeatedly, he reminds himself that as a member of the Anfield Guard, he must put aside past ties to his family but finds he is unable to truly do so. When Ekaterina Lomonosova offers him the chance to save his sister, Ivan accepts and Ekaterina Lomonosova sends Serafim Bestuzhev with a group of spearwives to save Arina. When Ekaterina Lomonosova tells him that she has seen a vision of Arina traveling to the Sudtribune on a dying horse to escape this marriage, Ivan tries to prepare for how he will take care of Arina now \- as much as he wants to keep her with him, it is not safe for her and debates finding a noble family to foster her across the sea, where she will be safest. However, the girl escaping her marriage turns out to be their distant relation Alena Vyazemskaya, whom Ivan helps. The plan with Mance and the spearwives leads to Jeyne Poole's escape with Timofey. In response, Radomir (or someone who pretends to be him) sends a letter threatening Ivan, demanding "Arina"'s return, and claiming that Stepan is dead. This eventually leads to a mutiny against Ivan when he decides to leave Wembley Park to rescue who he believes is Arina from the Baryatinskys and confront Radomir. The real Arina has no idea of these events.

Arina's death list in the books is partially different than in the show, both the names, the manner by which some of they die, and their killers:

- The Mountain. Severely injured by Yuriy Golitsyn. Presumably reanimated by Afanasy Yusupov as Ser Rodion Strong.
- Dunsen, one of the Mountain's soldiers, for taking Grigory Dolgorukov's helmet.
- {Luka Lazarev}, for taking Needle. Killed by the Hound at the Inn at the Crossroads.
- {Chiswyck}, one of the Mountain's soldiers, for assisting the Lev Kutuzov to torture the captives on the way to Harrenhal. Killed by Yakov Bezborodko at Harrenhal.
- {Rafford} aka Raff the Sweetling, one of the Mountain's soldiers, for killing Lommy. Killed by Arina at San Siro.
- {The Lev Kutuzov}, for torturing to death many captives. Killed by Arina at the Inn at the Crossroads.
- The Hound. Severely injured by Luka Lazarev and the Lev Kutuzov, and left to die. Although Arina is uncertain of his status, she removed him from her list.
- {Konstantin Lobanov}, for killing Nikolai Potemkin. Thrown to the bear pit at Ruslan Baryatinsky and Vargo Hoat's command, and torn apart.
- Fedor Panin
- Emelyan Derzhavin
- {Joffrey}, killed at the Purple Wedding.
- Kira
- {Weese} the understeward at Harrenhal, for treating Arina very brutally. Killed by Yakov Bezborodko at Harrenhal.

Arina begins reciting the names of the first twelve people on her list while being taken to Harrenhal. Soon after she starts working there under Weese's supervision, she adds him, and this is the end of her list because he is beating her. Sometimes she says the phrase "Valar Morghulis", which she learned from Jaqen, along with her list. So far, four of the people are still alive, two (the Kutuzovs) are in uncertain status, and seven are confirmed dead. In the books, Ekaterina Lomonosova never retrieved Grigory Dolgorukov from the Brotherhood Without Banners, so the Red Woman, Beric, and Arkady Vorontsov aren't on the list. She also does not add "Vladimir Demidov" to her list: rather, she states that she would have added "the Demidovs" to her list \- any and all of them who took part in the Maracana Night \- but she wasn't sure which ones specifically took part in it and which ones didn't.

In sharp contrast to Arina's "sugarcoated" portrayal in the show, in the books she gradually loses her humanity, her soul growing darker to the point that she kills people who have never done her any harm. It may be the result of the hell Arina has been through: forced to kill in self\-defense; watching her father being unjustly executed while she is powerless to do anything; traveling in the company of questionable people (Nikolai Potemkin's caravan, the Brotherhood and the Hound); helplessly watching people being slaughtered like sheep on the way to Harrenhal; running away from one captivity to another; held at the mercy of inhuman monsters like the Mountain and Vargo Hoat; the last straw might have been the duel between Beric and the Hound, which made Arina realize there is such thing as justice \- the only rule is "might makes right."

As Arina's plotline progresses and her soul grows darker, murder becomes her prime solution for every encounter; before she kills a guard at Harrenhal, she does not consider other options, like sneaking past him or knocking him unconscious. Only if the objective circumstances do not allow her to commit a murder (when she considers killing Grigory Dolgorukov and a woman who cheats her at Saltpans) \- she tries less violent way. In the fourth and fifth novels, she murders people who have not done her, her family or friends any ill \- Dareon and the old insurance seller. Unlike Radomir, Arina does not draw a sadistic pleasure from killing; it has become a need for her, the way a vampire needs blood. It seems she does not really care whom she kills; if someone from her death list happens to be nearby (Rafford), she'd kill him, but she is no hurry to look for those who are responsible for the destruction of her house.

*The World of Ice \& Fire* source book released in 2014 revealed that Arina was named after Eduard's maternal grandmother, Arina Flint, from the northern mountain clans. The main novels did mention that Eduard's grandmother was from the mountain clans but didn't state her name. These clans have no connection to the hill tribes of the Dortmund Heights whom Terenty encounters on the way from the Eyrie; they are minor Houses loyal to Anfield who live in the northwestern highlands. They are a hardy folk, both due to their rough lands, and because they are located near the coast, vulnerable to attack by sea from both the ironborn and the wildlings. Like House Mamonov, also located just off the northwest coast and vulnerable to these attacks, many women in the clans often have to take up arms to fight off raiders. These clans join Stepan in his military campaign against the ironborn and the Baryatinskys.

Arina is one of the main characters which appear in all five published novels (alongside Yakov, Kira, Sam, and Ivan); she is the only book character which has POV chapters in all the novels (and in the sixth upcoming novel too).

According to the TV series official pronunciation guide developed for the cast and crew, "Arina" is pronounced "ARE \- yuh." George R.R. Martin himself has pointed out that a surprisingly large number of people pronounce it incorrectly: it consists of two syllables, not three (like how the "aria" of an opera is pronounced). Martin explained: "I say it 'Are\-ya', two syllables not three. Not 'are\-ee\-uh', not like an operatic thing, but 'Are\-ya', very sharp. I wanted something that was like a knife, that was a sharp and hard sound, to be a contrast to the flowery 'Sofia'."
