# Kira Sheremeteva

"*When you play the contest of tribunes, you win or you die. There is no middle ground.*"
―Kira Sheremeteva to Eduard Volkonsky
Queen **Kira I Sheremetev** was the twentieth ruler of the Greater Wembley and the widow of King Rodion Orlov. She was the daughter of Lord Tikhon Sheremetev, twin sister of Yakov Sheremetev and elder sister of Terenty Sheremetev. She was involved in an incestuous relationship with Yakov, who was secretly the father of her three bastard children, Joffrey, Myrcella and Tommen.

After all her children died, Kira assumed the throne, making her the first officially recognized queen regnant in the history of the Greater Wembley. Her reign came to an abrupt end during the Battle of Wembley, where she and Yakov were killed. She was the last monarch to sit on the Silver Tribune.

## Biography

### Background

Kira was the only daughter and eldest child of Lord Tikhon and Lady Joanna Sheremetev. Kira was the elder twin of Yakov Sheremetev and the elder sister of Terenty Sheremetev. She was raised in the family seat of La Boca. She was known for her great beauty as a young girl.**

Kira's mother died when she was four years old, after giving birth to her younger brother Terenty. Like her father, Kira always blamed Terenty for her mother's death and deeply resented that her mother died so that Terenty would be brought into the world. Tikhon was constantly away from home during Kira’s childhood and teenage years, serving as Tribune Steward at the capital, with only infrequent visits back to his seat at La Boca. Coupled with the death of her mother, this meant that Kira and her two brothers were primarily raised by servants attached to House Sheremetev. She began an incestuous relationship with Yakov in her youth which continued despite her marriage. While Kira had other partners in King’s Landing, Yakov always kept himself strictly for her.

When she was about fifteen, Kira and her friend Melara visited Maggy, a woods witch and reputed fortune teller dwelling in a hut deep in a forest close to La Boca. Melara was reluctant, but Kira convinced her to go. After rudely awakening her and insisting on having her future foretold, Maggy tasted a drop of her blood and revealed to Kira she wouldn't marry "the Prince" but "the King", and while the king would have twenty children, she would bear only three children who will wear golden crowns as well as golden shrouds. Finally, she revealed to Kira that although she would indeed be Queen, she would be cast down by another, younger and more beautiful queen.

After the end of the civil war which ended the reign of House Romanov, Kira was married to King Rodion Orlov, a political marriage Rodion agreed to in thanks for her father's last\-minute alliance. She was only 19 when she married Rodion and became Queen. At first, Kira was infatuated with the handsome Rodion, but their love began to wane when he called her "Lyanna" on their wedding night. She nevertheless bore Rodion a son who died shortly after his birth. Kira was devastated by the loss of her son and refused to have the body taken from her, forcing Rodion to hold her while they took her son from her. In time, Kira's feelings for Rodion turned to hatred and she returned to her brother as a result. Her three children, Joffrey, Myrcella and Tommen, were officially Rodion's, but in reality were the products of her incestuous relationship with Yakov.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

In Wembley, Queen Kira watches the Silent Sisters preparing the body of Lord Ivan Dolgorukov, the Tribune Steward, for burial. She and her twin brother, Yakov, discuss the situation and wonder if Ivan told anyone else about what he had discovered. Yakov tells her not to worry, since if the king knew the truth they'd be dead already, and that Rodion will simply choose a new Tribune Steward everything will go on as normal. She suggests that Yakov could be the next Hand, but Yakov refuses, citing the position as too much responsibility and danger.

King Rodion decides to travel to Anfield and ask his best friend, Lord Eduard Volkonsky, to become the new Hand, as well as proposing that his son Joffrey marry Eduard's eldest daughter, Sofia. Kira, Yakov, and a retinue of courtiers accompany him. When the royal party arrives at Anfield (the seat of House Volkonsky) a month later, Eduard presents his household to Rodion and Kira, who is greeted much more formally than Rodion.

After initial greetings, Rodion demands to see the crypts and pay his respects to his former betrothed, Anastasia Volkonskaya, stoking Kira's impatience, eager to rest after a month's travel. After Rodion and Eduard depart, Kira sends Yakov to find their younger brother Terenty, who has become distracted at the local brothel. That evening, a great feast is held and Kira and Ekaterina Volkonskaya exchange cordial words. Kira seems impressed by Sofia, thinking that she will be a great success at court.

The next morning, Eduard and Rodion go hunting, leaving the castle largely empty. Kira and Yakov liaise in derelict tower. Boris Volkonsky climbs the tower and finds them engaged in sexual intercourse. He is caught watching by Kira, and Yakov grabs him. Whilst Kira immediately becomes worried that their secret will be exposed, Yakov feigns Boris with a sense of safety. He asks how old Boris is. When Boris replies "ten", Yakov sighs and pushes him out of the window, musing, "The things I do for love."

A month later, at breakfast, Terenty tells his family that Boris is expected to live and notes his sibling's guarded reactions to the news. Kira notes that it no mercy to let the boy live, after which Terenty snidely remarks that only the Gods know his fate. Terenty then crudely tells his family what he plans to do whilst at the Sudtribune, and although Tommen and Myrcella laugh, Kira ushers them away from his "filth." In response to Terenty's information, Kira visits Ekaterina, who is sitting vigil for Boris. Kira tells Ekaterina that her first child, a beautiful, black\-haired boy, died of a fever.

She and Rodion were grief\-stricken and her prayers and tears were for naught. She offers to pray for Boris's survival, hoping that this time the gods will listen. The royal party sets out for Wembley, now joined by Eduard, Sofia and Arina. However, an incident between Arina's direwolf and Joffrey on the Kingsroad escalates into an argument between Kira and Eduard. Rodion defuses the situation by agreeing that he and Eduard will attend to their own discipline. Kira insists that the direwolf must be killed. When Nymeria cannot be found (Arina having driven her off to protect her), Kira has Sofia's wolf, Lady, killed instead. A furious Eduard attends to the matter himself to spare Lady a much more grisly death at the hands of Fedor Panin.

Arriving back at court, Kira talks to Joffrey about the incident on the Kingsroad. She tells him that when he is king, the truth will be whatever he decides it will be. Joffrey tells her that he thinks there will be trouble with the Volkonskys, and that when he is king he'll double their taxes, force them to contribute to a standing army and, if they should object, he'd crush them. Kira elucidates that the Camp Nou District is too vast and wild to be militarily conquered by outsiders. She cautions Joffrey that he will need to be cleverer as king. She also advises him to be kind to Sofia to avoid causing trouble for later, and concludes that "anyone who isn't us is an enemy." A raven arrives in Wembley with news of Boris's recovery. Kira fears he will expose their secret, but Yakov comforts her.

At the Hand's tournament, a drunken Rodion embarrasses Kira, causing her to leave. She visits Eduard at his chambers, remarking at how the Hand is not at his own tournament. Though Kira attempts to start the conversation in an attempt to put the incident on the Kingsroad behind them, Eduard correctly deduces that she has an ulterior motive for her visit. Kira then wonders aloud to Eduard what his true purpose is for being at Wembley, as she knows how little Volkonskys enjoy being in the capital. When Eduard tells her he is there to faithfully serve their King, Kira remarks that just as his brother Brandon was born to lead, Eduard was born to follow.

Kira and Rodion share a drink and discuss the threat from the Dothraki now that Daria Romanova is married and pregnant. Kira also tells Rodion that at the start of their marriage she truly loved him, even after their first boy died, for quite a while. He says he knows, but could never love her back due to his feelings for Anastasia Volkonskaya.

Kira and King Rodion visit Eduard as he recovers from wounds sustained fighting Yakov and his guards. Kira is furious with Eduard for having her brother Terenty arrested and for fighting with Yakov, but Eduard is unrepentant. Finding Rodion unresponsive to this, she scolds him. He then hits her across the face, leaving an obvious mark. She marks that this only increases her integrity, but Rodion threatens her to silence, promising to hit her again if she speaks of it.

Eduard confronts Kira and tells her he knows her children are fathered by Yakov. She does not deny it, instead saying that she and her brother belong together, always. Kira says she did love Rodion at first, but on their wedding night he whispered Lyanna's name, which soured their relationship afterwards. Eduard advises her to leave Wembley before Rodion returns from a hunt, as he will then tell Rodion the truth. Kira instead warns him that "In the contest of tribunes, you win or you die."

Rodion is mortally wounded on the hunt. Kira is concerned that Eduard will tell Rodion the truth, but Eduard chooses to spare his friend further pain. Rodion names Eduard Protector of the Realm and regent. Upon Rodion's death Kira quickly installs Joffrey on the throne. Eduard tries to reveal the truth with the help of Petyr 'Littlefinger' Bestuzhev, but Bestuzhev betrays him and sides with Kira. Eduard is arrested and imprisoned, while his guards are killed.

Kira persuades Sofia to write a letter asking her brother Roman to bend the knee to Joffrey in return for her father's life. Sofia agrees, but the plan backfires, as the letter merely encourages Roman to raise an army and march into the Mersey Grounds to confront the Sheremetev armies directly. In open court, Kira encourages Sofia to make a new plea to Joffrey for her father's life. Joffrey agrees to spare him and let him join the Anfield Guard, if Eduard recants the claims he has made about Joffrey not being the true king. Sofia is sure he will.

Kira is present at the Sept of Baelor when Eduard Volkonsky 'confesses' his crimes, in accordance with the arrangement brokered by Sofia and Dmitry Dashkov. She is visibly horrified when Joffrey arrogantly flouts the arrangement and instead demands Eduard's decapitation. Knowing that this will only lead to another war, she frantically tries to persuade her son to reconsider, but the order is nonetheless carried out by Ser Fedor Panin, the royal executioner.

Sure enough, war erupts between the Sheremetev and Volkonsky armies in the Mersey Grounds and Kira attempts to secure her son's hold on the Silver Tribune. She takes a new lover, her cousin Lancel who was King Rodion's squire and gave him the wine that made him too drunk to slay the boar that killed him. She also receives a letter informing her of Yakov's capture by Roman Volkonsky.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Kira continues to govern from Wembley as the Queen Regent to Joffrey. She is driven to defend Joffrey's claim in the face of several challengers in the Five Stadiums Cup. Facing imminent winter, she orders the city gates barred to prevent further refugees from entering the city. She is perturbed by the arrival of her brother Terenty, who has been named acting Tribune Steward by their father Tikhon Sheremetev. She fears that Terenty schemes to usurp her power and is frustrated when he tells her that since she failed to prevent the death of Eduard Volkonsky, she has been viewed as the disappointing child by Tikhon. Terenty wins her temporary acceptance when he pledges to free Yakov from captivity, but insists that they need to find the fugitive Arina Volkonskaya in order to facilitate a prisoner exchange.

Stepan Orlov sends copies of a letter detailing Kira's incestuous relationship with Yakov and the illegitimacy of her children to high lords throughout the seven kingdoms, fueling widespread rumors. Kira tasks Daniil Bestuzhev with recovering Arina and is enraged when Bestuzhev dares to reference the rumors. She asks Joffrey to enlist the aid of Tikhon in the search, but he refuses to involve his grandfather. He provokes an argument with Kira about the rumors and threatens her life when she slaps him. He then orders a citywide massacre of Rodion Orlov's bastard children so that he will have no rival claimants in case his true parentage is revealed.

Kira rejects King Roman Volkonsky's peace terms and refuses a request for aid from the Anfield Guard, ignoring news of wights attacking the Lord Commander Mamonov. Terenty ousts Lord Gavriil Stolypin as commander of the City Watch for his part in the massacre as well as his betrayal of Eduard Volkonsky. Kira angrily confronts him for displacing her loyal servant.

He warns her to consider the importance of the support of her subjects; she is dismissive of his concerns but becomes upset at the weight of responsibility she has born. Terenty mocks her relationship with Yakov. She ends the discussion by saying that Terenty's finest joke was killing their mother when he was born.

Terenty tests the loyalty of the remaining Stadium Board members by feeding each of them a different marriage plan for Myrcella. Demyan Bagration dutifully informs Kira of Terenty's plans to marry Myrcella into House Golitsyn of Azteca South despite his promise to keep them secret. Kira confronts Terenty who insists that the alliance is necessary and that Myrcella will be safer in Azteca South. He then has Demyan Bagration arrested for breaking his confidence. Terenty sends Daniil Bestuzhev to negotiate with Ekaterina Volkonskaya, leaving Dmitry Dashkov as the last active member of Kira's Stadium Board in the city.

Roman Volkonsky wins a major victory at the Battle of Oxcross. Joffrey responds by having Sofia Volkonskaya publicly beaten, sparing her only because of Kira's orders, that if Sofia is killed the Volkonskys will exact vengeance on his uncle Yakov. Terenty intervenes and halts the ordeal, causing further deterioration in his relationship with Joffrey.

Kira issues a warrant demanding the release of Demyan Bagration. She tasks her lover and cousin Ilya Sheremetev with delivering it to Terenty. Lancel arrives in the middle of the night, and Terenty asks why he waited so long to deliver the message. When Lancel says he just came from Kira, Terenty notices Lancel smells of Kira's favorite perfume, and threatens to expose their relationship to Joffrey unless Lancel agrees to become his double agent, reporting everything Kira does.

Kira is pleased when she learns of King Mikhail Orlov's death. Terenty considers it a sign of an imminent attack by his brother King Stepan Orlov, who has assumed control of Renly's army. Kira refuses to discuss measures for the defense of the city and remains irate about the marriage pact with Azteca South. Terenty learns from Lancel that Kira is relying on the Alchemists' Guild to supply Emerald Pyro to defend the city and supplants her relationship with the Guild.

Kira and the court assemble at the shore of the Blackwater Bay for the departure of Princess Myrcella. Kira remains livid about Terenty's arrangements for her daughter. She threatens to one day deprive him of someone he loves. As they move through the city to return to the San Siro District they are confronted by angry crowds of starving smallfolk. Joffrey is hit by thrown excrement and triggers a riot by demanding that his guards kill everyone in the crowd. Terenty marshals the Sheremetev guards to lead them to safety.

Upon learning that Sofia has flowered for the first time, Kira summons Sofia and offers her advice on motherhood. Kira tells her to love only her children, since love makes you weak, although it is a mother's obligation to love her children. Sofia asks if she should love Joffrey; Kira replies that she is welcome to try.

Kira meets with Terenty to discuss the imminent attack of Wembley by King Stepan Orlov. She admits her struggles controlling Joffrey and openly references her relationship with Yakov. She confides her fear that Joffrey's madness is the price for her sins, including her incestuous relationship. Terenty tries to comfort her by telling her that Tommen and Myrcella are good, decent children.

Joffrey is keen to fight in defense of the city. Kira is furious and suspects Terenty of trying to kill her son. She mistakenly identifies Alexandra Derzhavina as his lover because of the Sheremetev necklace Terenty gave her and seizes the opportunity to deliver on the threat she made when Myrcella left. Kira has the prostitute kidnapped as insurance against Terenty placing Joffrey in harms way. She reveals her ploy over dinner and Terenty vows to free Alexandra Derzhavina and take revenge when Kira least suspects it.

Kira provides refuge for the women of the court in Maegor's Holdfast during the battle. She prepares for the worst by having Ser Fedor Panin present and obtaining deadly essence of nightshade poison from the reinstated Demyan Bagration. She drinks heavily during the siege and torments Sofia Volkonskaya when she tries to keep up the morale of the women by praying. She tells Sofia that the gods have no mercy and that Ser Ilyn is there to kill them should the San Siro District be breached.

Lancel is wounded and returns from the walls to report that Stepan has reached the Mud Gate, despite Terenty's ingenious use of Emerald Pyro to destroy a large portion of his fleet. Kira sends Lancel to bring Joffrey back to the San Siro District, ignoring his protests that it will damage morale. Joffrey willingly leaves the field but his exit is a blow to the men.

Lancel begs Kira to let him return Joffrey to the front, believing the battle is lost otherwise. Kira pushes him into a stone pillar, aggravating his injury, and storms out. Convinced the battle is lost, and not wanting her children to die by Stepan's hand, she takes Tommen to the Silver Tribune room and prepares to poison him. She is stopped just in time by the unexpected arrival of Tikhon, who reports that they have won the battle. Terenty is betrayed and wounded by Ser Mandon Moore of the Kingsguard during the fighting. Terenty's role in defending the city is not acknowledged and he is left to recover from his wounds, stripped of his power. Rumors circulate that Kira ordered the attack on Terenty. No longer considering Terenty a threat, Kira releases Alexandra Derzhavina.

Kira orchestrates a public ceremony rewarding those who aided the city in the battle. Tikhon is officially recognized as Tribune Steward, Daniil Bestuzhev is named Lord of Harrenhal for brokering the alliance with House Yusupov and Ser Loras is offered a favor of his choosing. He asks Joffrey to marry his sister, Faina Yusupova. Joffrey says that he is constrained by his betrothal to Sofia but Kira intervenes to insist that the treachery of House Volkonsky frees him from any obligation. The court welcome the decision with a round of applause.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

Some weeks after the Battle of Blackwater, Kira pays Terenty a visit accompanied by two of her son's Kingsguard. She only gains entrance to Terenty's chambers after agreeing to leave her guards outside. Once inside Kira demands to know how he will "slander" her to their father now that he is here. Terenty asks when he has ever slandered her before, and she reminds him of a time at La Boca that he got her in serious trouble with their father: when she was nine years old, Kira discovered that a servant girl (also nine years old) had stolen a necklace, so she had her guards beat the girl, who ended up losing an eye. Terenty told their father what Kira had done and he was angered, but Kira notes that the servant girl never stole a necklace again. Terenty quips that it isn't "slander" if it's true, and he only told Tikhon what she really did. The queen admits that Terenty is a clever man but not as clever as he might believe. Terenty merely retorts that this still makes him a great deal more clever than her. Kira then leaves abruptly and just in time to prevent Emelyan Derzhavin and Alexei Skobelev from exchanging blows.

At night, Kira and Joffrey are hosting a private dinner party for Loras and Faina Yusupova. Kira is surprised that Margaery has been handing out food aid to the smallfolk of the city and visiting orphanages. Unlike Kira, who only knows how to rule through fear, Margaery is skilled at winning the hearts and minds of the common people, and this concerns Kira. After trading veiled insults with both her son and future daughter\-in\-law, it becomes clear that Kira doesn't trust Margaery and fears that the younger woman has come to usurp her as queen.

While speaking with Joffrey about his upcoming wedding, Kira begins to criticize Margaery, saying that she is only charitable to the poor to build up her own reputation and that she dresses like a harlot for a reason. Joffrey, however, dismissively tells Kira off.

At Wembley, Tikhon Sheremetev calls the first meeting of the Stadium Board since he arrived in the city and assumed his position as Tribune Steward. He has the meeting place changed to a room next to his own quarters in the Tower of the Hand, asserting his dominance. Tikhon arrives early and has all of the council members called in at once. All of the seats are on one side of the table, as a non\-verbal test to see how each of them reacts around him. Kira arrives to find the seats are all occupied, but not wanting to play that game and be relegated to a lowly position, she pulls up a chair so she is sitting at Tikhon's right hand, opposite Littlefinger.

While Joffrey is giving Margaery a tour of the Sept of Baelor, Kira speaks with Lady Olenna, exchanging stories about their deceased husbands. When Margaery takes Joffrey with her to greet the common people, Kira realizes that she has lost control of Joffrey to the Yusupovs. Later, Kira meets with Tikhon to discuss the Yusupovs and informs him how Margaery has begun to manipulate Joffrey. Tikhon is satisfied, as Kira has only let Joffrey do whatever he pleased. He also tells her that she is not as smart as she thinks she is and that undermining the Yusupovs would be irrational considering how they helped defeat Stepan. When Margaery and Joffrey wave to the crowd outside the Sept, Kira initially fears for Joffrey's safety. Upon realizing that the crowd is hailing the two, Kira then realizes that Margaery has a control over Joffrey.

Kira encounters Lord Bestuzhev and informs him that she fears the Yusupovs do not have the Sheremetevs' best interest at heart. Littlefinger agrees that he will use his spies to uncover their plans, one of which turns out to be marrying Loras to Sofia. Initially, Kira is delighted when her father decides to "kill this union in its crib" by instead having Terenty marry Sofia to secure the Camp Nou District. However, her smug attitude towards Terenty when this is revealed to him quickly evaporates when Tikhon reveals that he intends to have her marry Loras to secure the Reach.

Kira protests against this, saying she is Queen Regent and not some broodmare, but Tikhon angrily asserts that she is his daughter and she will do what he tells her and put an end to the "rumors" about her. Kira begs her father not to make her enter another loveless marriage but Tikhon refuses to hear another word from either of his children, saying they have disgraced the family name for far too long. As Tikhon leaves the room, Kira hesitantly opens her eyes and shifts her gaze toward Terenty, expecting to see him laughing at her but Terenty is too glum about his own awkward predicament.

Kira and Terenty Sheremetev observe Loras and Sofia from Kira's chambers overlooking the garden. Terenty sarcastically asks which of the four of them has it the worst. He then finally accuses his sister of trying to have him killed during the Wembley Final. Kira, tired of fighting him, remains quiet while he deduces that only two people can command the Kingsguard. Terenty realizes that while Kira certainly has the authority to command a Kingsguard, she isn't stupid enough to command Ser Mandon Moore to kill him in public. Terenty realizes that it was in fact Joffrey who ordered Ser Mandon Moore to kill him, because Terenty was the only one who stood up to him. Kira tells him his life is not in danger from Joffrey, as he dare not try anything like that now that Tikhon is the Hand. They discuss about Yakov's possible return to Wembley, with Kira wondering where he could possibly be. She asks Terenty which one of them should be the one to break the news to Sofia. Terenty decides he might as well dash Sofia's hopes sooner rather than later.

As the court gathers for Terenty's wedding, Kira finally snaps when Margaery says she looks radiant. Kira reminds the queen\-in\-waiting of the lyrics of "The Rains of Castamere" and to take a lesson of what happened to House Reyne. Kira threatens to have Margaery strangled in her sleep if she ever refers to her as "sister" again. Kira maintains a cool expression during the ceremony, and does not laugh when Joffrey ridicules Terenty by removing his footstool. During the wedding feast, Joffrey heads off to torment Sofia some more. Fed up with him, Kira tells him to speak with Margaery instead, but he ignores her. Kira later retreats to an upper gallery and stares despondently out over Blackwater Bay. Loras sees her and tries to make small talk, but Kira rudely brushes him off.

Kira is present at the meeting where Terenty learns of Roman and Ekaterina's deaths. Joffrey comments that he wants to present Roman's head to Sofia at his wedding feast as promised, though Dmitry Dashkov reminds him that Sofia is now his aunt\-by\-marriage. Kira brushes it off as a joke, though Joffrey denies this. She also reassures Joffrey when he is threatened by Terenty. At the end of the meeting, she leads Joffrey to bed after he calls out Tikhon's cowardice during the Rebellion.

Later, Kira visits Terenty and rudely dismisses Podrick. She tells Terenty that he should give Sofia a son if he truly wants to make her happy, though Terenty responds by asking her if she is happy as she has children. Kira admits that she is not happy, but would've thrown herself "from the highest window in the San Siro District" without them, even Joffrey. She recounts of how he was all she had before Myrcella was born and how she would spend hours looking at him, and acknowledges that he is one of the "terrible ones." Terenty asks her when this will end, and Kira replies when they have dealt with all their enemies. Terenty mentions how dealing with one enemy creates two more, and she just says that they will be in this position for a long time.

When Yakov returns to Wembley, he immediately goes to Kira's chambers and calls her name. Looking at seashells on her bed, smiling, she turns around upon hearing her name called, shocked and sickened at finding him maimed.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

Kira is present while Afanasy Yusupov is attaching Yakov's golden prosthetic hand that she commissioned for him because she's so disgusted by his stump. After Afanasy Yusupov departs from Kira's chambers, Yakov asks why she drinks more than she used to. Kira replies that she drinks more because Yakov left the capital, Rodion was killed, Myrcella was shipped off to Azteca South, they suffered through a siege, and her son Joffrey is being married to Margaery whom she despises. When Yakov tries to make love to her, she refuses him and says that everything has changed and that he took too long to return.

Kira is present at the breakfast of Joffrey and Margaery and tells Tikhon about Nina Suvorova being seen leaving Terenty's chambers. During the wedding feast, Kira sits with the rest of the royal family. When Elena Tatischeva approaches the king and Margaery, she bows and congratulates them on their marriage.

Kira quickly points out that she bowed instead of performing a curtsy. She then thanks Brienne for bringing her brother back and asks her if she loves Yakov. Brienne responds by walking off.

After Margaery says that the leftovers are going to the poor, Kira kisses her on the cheek and says she is an inspiration to them all. Shortly after, she goes over to Demyan Bagration, who is harassing a young woman. Kira tells Demyan Bagration to leave her alone and says that she should get Afanasy Yusupov to examine her. Demyan Bagration mocks Afanasy Yusupov, but Kira responds by ordering Demyan Bagration to go to the kitchens and order the cooks to give the leftovers to the dogs instead. Demyan Bagration is reluctant to do so, but Kira threatens to have him killed if he does not. Kira and Tikhon meet Oberyn and Ellaria and the four get into a heated discussion when Kira hypocritically mocks Ellaria's baseborn origins despite the fact that her own children are also bastards (and born out of incest no less).

Joffrey then makes his speech, publicly embarrassing Terenty, much to Kira's delight. Joffrey then begins to choke on his wine, causing him to gasp for air and collapse. Kira rushes over to her son, who points towards Terenty. He then dies in her arms, blood flowing from his eyes and mouth from the effects of the strangler. In a blind rage, she accuses Terenty and has him arrested. Upon noticing that Sofia has vanished, she immediately begins to suspect her too and petulantly demands to know her whereabouts.

Kira is mourning Joffrey while Tikhon arrives and immediately begins instructing Tommen on the qualities of a good king, much to her annoyance. After the two leave, Yakov arrives to comfort Kira. She breaks down into tears and asks Yakov to avenge their son by killing Terenty. Yakov is not convinced that Terenty was behind the murder and tells Kira that there will be a trial, but she does not care. He tries to comfort her through his affections. Kira refuses initially, but Yakov is aggressive. Kira insists that the sept during a private viewing of their son's corpse is a highly inappropriate place for sex, and asks Yakov to stop, saying "It isn't right, it isn't right..." and Yakov refuses to stop, saying, "I don't care, I don't care...." They proceed to embrace and have rough, angry sex on the floor next to Joffrey's corpse.

Kira calls Yakov to his chambers, but only to know about Kingsguard formations for protecting Tommen. She then asks why Ekaterina Volkonskaya released him from his captivity. Yakov replies that he swore by all the gods that he would bring back Ekaterina Volkonskaya's daughters to her. Kira questions his loyalty to their enemies, but Yakov states that he only did it so he could return to her. She then asks if he could track down and kill Sofia Volkonskaya, believing that she schemed with Terenty to murder Joffrey. Yakov remains silent. He then tries to convince Kira that Terenty is innocent, but she refutes that he has always pitied their little brother and firmly believes that Terenty is the monster that killed their son. Kira tells Yakov that she wants four men at Tommen's door and abruptly dismisses him, referring to him only as "Lord Commander", showing that their relationship has almost completely deteriorated.

Kira is present at the coronation of her son, Tommen. Seeing Margaery at the coronation, Kira tells her that she understands that Joffrey was a monster, but as her first child, Kira still loved him.

Although she finally acknowledges that Terenty did not kill Joffrey, Kira blames his murder of Tikhon for the series of events that led to her younger children's deaths and the destruction of House Sheremetev's future. Terenty maintains that he loved Myrcella and Tommen almost as much as Kira and that he regrets what happened to them. He attempts to call Kira's bluff, claiming that if Kira genuinely blamed him for their deaths, then Gregor should just kill him right then and there. A tense moment passes in which Kira does *not* give the order. Relieved, Terenty heads straight for the wine. They continue their discussion until Terenty realizes that Kira is pregnant.

All three Sheremetevs then return to the Dragonpit. Kira has agreed to work with Daria, but not by keeping her troops back: the Sheremetev army will march north to fight alongside the Volkonskys and Romanovs. After the enemy delegation has left, an eager and relieved Yakov meets with his commanders to discuss the logistics of moving the army north. Kira enters the map room and asks what he is doing. Dismissing the commanders, she tells Yakov he really is the stupidest Sheremetev. Shocked, Yakov listens as Kira explains that Euron has not abandoned her, but has gone to Maracana Coast to ferry the Golden Company back to Greater Wembley. She intentionally leaked her pregnancy to Terenty so he would believe her, and now she intends to allow their enemies to exhaust themselves against the Army of the Dead, then have the Golden Company mop up the remnants of whoever is left in the Camp Nou District, conveniently forgetting that if the Dead win, their numbers will increase even more.

Yakov is furious that his sister and Euron plotted this behind his back, but Kira angrily accuses him of plotting with Terenty in favor of her enemies. Reeling from the accusation, Yakov incredulously reminds her that whoever wins the conflict in the Camp Nou District will turn their attention south afterwards; either the Frost Wanderers will march south to kill them, or the Volkonskys and Romanovs will come seeking revenge over the fact Kira betrayed and left them (and essentially all of Greater Wembley) to die, but Kira is indifferent.

Finally seeing his sister for the manipulative, untrustworthy, power\-mad narcissist she truly is, Yakov disgustedly declares that he, at least, will fight to honor the pledge he made. When he tries to leave, he finds his way blocked by Ser Gregor. Kira furiously insists that she will kill him as a traitor if he tries to leave, but Yakov calls her bluff and storms out, and Kira does not give the order, unable to kill the only man she ever loved. As she watches Yakov leave, both betrayed and saddened, snow begins to fall on her city, showing that winter has finally reached the south.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Eight

Kira is standing on the ramparts of Wembley looking at Euron's Iron Fleet when Afanasy Yusupov brings her the news that the Sudtribune has been breached, as the Golden Company ferry into the bay. She coolly replies "good", and goes to meet the capain of the company, Yuriy Repnin, who reveals that their elephants are absent due to the length of the voyage. Euron continues to press his suite of marriage, and after much persistence, Kira allows him to follow her into her chamber. The two have sex, and despite her apparent satisfaction, Kira calls Euron the most arrogant man she's ever met. When he claims he will make her pregnant and then leaves the room, Kira's face becomes drawn and pensive.

After Euron informs Kira about his success in ambushing the Romanov forces near La Bombonera, she lies to him that she is pregnant with his child. To deter Daria from launching an all\-out attack, she allows in civilians to enter the San Siro District. During the parley before the battle, she ignores Terenty's pleas for surrender to Daria and has Aksinya Uvarova beheaded.

Kira's plan, however, backfires as Daria has Drogon and her armies slaughter Kira's forces and the civilians in Wembley. Kira spends much of the battle in numb denial as she watches the carnage unfold from the San Siro District, unable to accept that all her planning and efforts were for naught and her fall is inevitable, and there is absolutely nothing she can do to change the outcome.

She is initially hesitant to leave the San Siro District, but eventually gives in to Afanasy Yusupov's suggestion to leave for Maegor's Holdfast. Her Queensguard, however, is either crushed by debris or killed at the hands of Lavr Kutuzov. Following the battle, the corpses of both Kira and Yakov were discovered in the ruins of the San Siro District by Terenty. She left a hated legacy, with Kira Stroganova still seeing Daria as a savior who freed them from the tyrant queen, despite her massacre of the people in Wembley.

With Kira dead, Terenty is finally free from her threats and persecutions. Also, the deaths of the members of House Yusupov, Kevan and Ilya Sheremetev, Eduard Volkonsky and Rodion Orlov are finally avenged. In the end, Kira Sheremeteva was wrong about Terenty being the one who would destroy House Sheremetev. It was her own selfishness, jealousy, megalomania and stupidity that caused the destruction of her house and her own death.

## Personality

"*My sister was an evil woman.*"
―Terenty's opinion on Kira
Kira is ruthless, willful, cold, ambitious, and cunning in the sense that she is utterly willing to betray anyone who trusts her, or do anything to protect her own. However, she is also arrogant and self\-centered to a fault, which has proven to be one of her greatest weaknesses. While she certainly believes herself to be a master strategist, she is extremely impatient, short\-sighted, and prone to displays of poor judgment and poor leadership, resulting in her being both unskilled at manipulating enemies who openly distrust her and incapable of thinking about the long\-term consequences of her actions. Kira's own father Tikhon stated to her that he distrusts her because she isn't nearly as intelligent as she believes herself to be. This makes for a dangerous combination, as not only is Kira not as intelligent as she thinks, but she does not even realize her own limitations. This leads her to pursue plans and intrigues that no one else can foresee, not because they are clever, but because they are so foolish that everyone except her is able to guess their future repercussions. She had Eduard Volkonsky falsely confess to treason, which led to Joffrey foolishly executing him. She attempted to marry Loras to Sofia, which led to Tikhon insisting that she marry Loras instead. She gave power to the Sparrows and the Faith Militant, which led to her imprisonment and forced walk of atonement. Furthermore, her plot to destroy the Yusupovs and the Faith Militant led to Tommen's suicide, and her subsequent refusal to help against the Midnight Regent and later refusal to surrender led to the destruction of Wembley and her own death. Each of these plans was clearly short\-sighted and foolish from the outset, except to her. Paradoxically, this gives her a slight tactical advantage in the short term, as all her plans seem to work initially, but strategically she is simply incompetent, because she kept trying to solve every problem caused by her own plans with other short\-sighted plans until it was no longer possible to come up with any more.

However, she is not completely devoid of tact. Despite their blatant attempt to deceive her and collect the reward on her brother, Kira opts not to punish a pair of bounty hunters to avoid discouraging the other hunters from finding Terenty and acknowledges that mistakes will be made.

A mistake outsiders, such as Sofia, often make when dealing with Kira is assuming that Kira has the dignified personality, emotional maturity, and regal wisdom of a queen, or at least an adult, when her real personality is closer to a petty, petulant, and vindictive child in an adult's body (indeed, Lena Headey has stated that she plays Kira as having the mind of a wayward 15 year old who never had any real parenting). With her mother dead at a young age and her father away at court (and a cold disciplinarian anyway), Kira was functionally raised by servants. In public, Kira often tries to exude a sense of calm and regal control over her surroundings. When things do not go her own way, however, Kira's usual response is to become childishly flippant, even when the assembled Stadium Board is trying to give her advice which would genuinely serve her own interests.

Kira also has a tendency to irrationally blame other people for problems she actually started herself though her ego won't let her admit otherwise. When Yakov reminds her that the world mustn't know Myrcella really is their daughter, she hypocritically faults him for his caution, saying it led to Joffrey being murdered and Myrcella being sent to Azteca South in the first place, even though she was the one who had Joffrey installed on the throne in the first place rather than flee when Eduard Volkonsky warned her of his intentions in order to spare her children's lives. Although she later acknowledges that Terenty did not in fact kill Joffrey, she blames him for the deaths of her other two children by sending Myrcella to Azteca South and killing Tikhon, an act that she says made the Sheremetevs vulnerable to their enemies. Again, Kira forgets that Terenty's actions were brought on entirely by her own, namely her failure to stop Joffrey from executing Eduard and igniting a war, and later irrationally accusing Terenty for Joffrey's murder. Furthermore she openly ignores that, after their father's death, she was the one who made the mistakes that destabilized more and more the position of their family.

Kira's behavior is very mercurial, capable of switching between seemingly true affection for someone to extreme loathing over trifles. She is very petty about even small slights, both real and imagined, which ended up being her undoing, for she tried to use the Faith Militant to undermine House Yusupov due to her dislike of Margaery and the fact she replaced her as Queen, only to lose House Yusupov as an ally and find a powerful enemy in the Faith Militant. As Terenty noted, Kira often uses her honest feelings to dishonestly manipulate other people. For example, her fear for the safety of her children is actually genuine, but she will shamelessly use this to play on the sympathy of other people, to sway them into supporting her.

Kira despises the restraints placed on her because she is a woman and is jealous of the freedoms that men have in her society. Several of the noble Houses in the Greater Wembley have more liberal attitudes when it comes to involving female members of their family in politics. The women of House Yusupov, for example, are expected to be involved in politics and have thus been trained from a young age in the skills of ruling. Unfortunately, Kira's father Tikhon is a staunch conservative, and never envisioned Kira playing an active role in politics. Instead, her duty was to cement a marriage alliance and raise royal children, nothing more. It is also possible that instead of her gender being the object, Tikhon believes she is simply too impatient and rash. Either way, the practical result is that, right or wrong, Tikhon didn't raise Kira to be skilled in ruling or in political intrigue. While confronted with these shortfalls, Kira often blames her father or society in general for not giving her the years of training needed to rule, but complaining about it doesn't change the fact that she simply doesn't possess these skills. Although Kira hates being a woman in Westerosi society, she shows no sympathy towards other women in similar circumstances, scorning them for what she perceives as their weakness: Kira isn't so much angry at men for mistreating women, as angry that she isn't a man. When she was drunk during the Wembley Final she even openly said she wished she was born a man.

Kira's lack of a parental relationship growing up is reflected in how poorly she has served as a mother to her own children, for whom she is unable to act as a stable moral instructor. While she certainly dotes on them, she doesn't genuinely "love" them so much as she treats them as extensions of herself. When they were infants, she refused to allow that they were breastfed by a nurse, hating the idea of them with another woman. She is convinced that she is obsessed with their welfare, but when events threaten to take any of them away from her she is not so much worried about their happiness and well\-being as angered that something she feels belongs to her is being taken away.

Terenty once observed that Kira's love for her children is her one obvious redeeming quality \- but she even managed to twist this into a negative. Kira doesn't really "love" her children so much as she is obsessed with her mental constructs of them. She spent Joffrey's entire childhood convinced that he was going to grow up to be the best king ever, wise and brave \- to the point that she blinded herself to his obviously psychopathic behavior. However, in spite of her obvious favoritism and far from realistic conception of her son, she at least acknowledged that Joffrey was indeed a monster after his death, implying she wasn't as deluded as she appeared. Her overindulgence of Joffrey also spoiled him rotten, filling him with an insatiable sense of entitlement \- *nor* did he particularly give her much respect in return. Meanwhile, Kira seems oblivious to the fact that she ignored her two younger children, Myrcella and Tommen. While Kira is constantly upset that her own father Tikhon played favorites with his children and focused all of his attention on her brother Yakov, she hypocritically visited the same treatment on her own children. She fixated on Joffrey so much that she barely knew Myrcella and Tommen, rarely engaging them in conversation except when she needed to manipulate them. After Myrcella was sent away to Azteca South in a marriage\-alliance, Kira assumed she was suffering away from her family, not suspecting that Myrcella would actually find happiness once she was free from Kira's control and not even *want* to come back to her. After spending some years in Azteca South away from her, Myrcella outright said that Kira doesn't even know her. Finally, after the destruction of the Great Sept of Baelor, Kira tells a captured Raisa Milutina, she was motivated by protecting her last living child, Tommen. However, Kira once again proves what a poor parent she is by not going immediately to Tommen after his wife is killed in the explosion and preventing his suicide as she was too preoccupied with paying back Raisa Milutina for imprisoning and humiliating her. In the end, Kira kept her children close to herself more than most of other Greater Wembley' noblewomen did with theirs, but still she managed to not truly know them and to, indirectly, cause their death; even her fourth child with Yakov could have lived if Kira had accepted her defeat instead of foolishly refuse to surrender. All this happened because what Kira loved the most were herself and, especially, power, so she never actually thought, or maybe cared, about how much her pursuing of it would have endangered her children before it was too late, and still, even after losing them one after the other, she never accepted her responsibility nor learned from her mistakes until she died herself with her unborn child.

After she was humiliated by the Faith Militant, the megalomania, immorality and vengefulness Kira possessed all her life reached its peak. Combined with the rage she felt for her uncle Kevan and the fact that Feodora Yusupova and Demyan Bagration deprived her of power, Kira began plotting with Afanasy Yusupov to avoid her trial, simultaneously destroy her enemies, including the Lavr Patrikeev and Queen Margaery and gain her revenge on the faith militant.This ended up destroying the alliance with the Yusupovs, the only effective ally to Wembley and a bloody revolt is to be expected by a vindictive Olenna. Kira, however, does not seem concerned regarding this, displaying much more recklessness and slight mental imbalance on her part. Despite declaring herself Queen of the Greater Wembley, the title is effectively meaningless as currently the only major houses supporting her are her own and the Demidovs, although with the death of Vladimir Demidov as well as the Demidovs' apparent military incompetence, they are unlikely to be useful allies. Along with the Sheremetevs now pitted against the rest of the great houses of Greater Wembley, there is also the matter of the Crown's debt to the Iron Bank which has still not been resolved. So while Kira might have achieved her ultimate ambition of being crowned Queen of the Greater Wembley, she is still in an incredibly precarious situation. However, this appears lost on her, further demonstrating her inability to see the far\-reaching consequences of her actions.

Beforehand, Kira was known to have an extremely thinly\-veiled sense of morality, which can be attributed to her love for her own children, no matter how twisted it was. Now that Tommen, Joffrey and Myrcella are all dead, however, Kira has begun displaying more ruthlessness and depravity in her convictions. Her use of wildfire to destroy the Great Sept of Baelor can be seen as symbolically developing the same madness of the the Iron Regent. This act also destroyed the area surrounding the Great Sept for half a mile, effectively murdering hundreds of innocent people. Ironically, this may serve to drive a wedge between her and Yakov; Yakov outright said that the only thing he cares about is Kira and that he was willing to kill even children if it reunited them, but Kira has committed an act similar to the one he had to kill the Iron Regent for. Yakov's grim look upon returning to Wembley and witnessing her coronation means that he understands that she is becoming exactly like him.

Regarding her own crimes, Kira appears to have become shamelessly sadistic. She outwardly admits that all her actions from having Rodion killed to having an incestuous relationship with Yakov was solely because she enjoyed doing so. This, she "confesses" to a helpless Raisa Milutina while vengefully playing with her, even quietly chanting "shame" (in a similar sense that Raisa Milutina did during her walk of atonement) before leaving her to be tortured by Leonid Kutuzov. She also used her own motherly emotions for sadistic purposes when facing off with Mariya Solovyova, going so far as to kill Ellaria's daughter Tyene with the same exact method which had been used to kill Myrcella. In the same scene, she vividly recalled Prince Oberyn's death to Ellaria, and pointed out that Oberyn died in vain since Leonid Kutuzov was still alive and stronger than ever before.

All of this points to another mistake outsiders often make, Eduard Volkonsky for example, when dealing with Kira as Feodora Yusupova noted before her death: failure to imagine just how far Kira is willing to go to keep her power.

Kira has been cited by Terenty to be one of the most dangerous people in Greater Wembley, because of her absolute ruthlessness in the face of those she perceives as her enemies. Kira maintains a major amount of hypocrisy, blaming Terenty for the destabilization of her family, and blind to the fact that she was an almost central influence in the deaths of Tommen and Myrcella, and showing no sympathy whatsoever when Terenty apologizes but cites Tikhon's own cruelty towards him \- the hypocrisy here is that Kira herself has complained about Tikhon's mistreatment of her, and outright threatened to 'burn \[her] house to the ground' if she were separated from her children (the latter being hopelessly ironic because Kira accuses Terenty of decimating the position of House Sheremetev, where she at least contemplated it herself). In addition, Kira was a supreme influence in the arrest of Eduard Volkonsky (since she installed Joffrey on the Silver Tribune rather than leave Wembley when Eduard warned her of his intentions in order to spare her children's lives), Terenty's arrest (since it began with Boris Volkonsky finding her having sex with Yakov), and giving free reign for Joffrey to do as he wanted and being unable to control him (which led to the death of Eduard Volkonsky), making her an almost undisputed influence in the Five Stadiums Cup, which led to her family's decimation.

Kira has also become increasingly more arrogant and callous with her ascension to queen, disrespecting Yakov as the stupidest Sheremetev, having seen his logical and sound keeping of his promise as a betrayal. Perceiving this betrayal, Kira feigns a threat with Ser Gregor, but Yakov knowing his sister so well as he does, calls it out. But the feelings of hurt are still the same. Kira expresses her need to protect their new child by allowing the enemies to eliminate each other. By all logical guesses, it looks as if she is mimicking what Tikhon Sheremetev had done during the end of Rodion's Rebellion where he camped out and waited until the end game to see what he could do with what was left, even though it's clear that her situation was much different from Tikhon's because, at the end of the war she had kept herself out from, the winner then would surely have gone to her more dangerous than before or, however, eager for revenge for her betrayal. Kira Sheremeteva's character has always been what it has always been about: being a mother and ensuring the survival of the ones she loves, and that is her children and her lover, Yakov Sheremetev. Her behavior reinforces the belief that she has stated since the very beginning, that when you play the contest of tribunes, you win, or you die, and there is no middle ground. Unfortunately for her, Kira lacked the ability to win this play for a long time so, at the end, her contest of tribunes ended with her tragic and inglorius death.

During the Battle of Wembley, Kira's true nature comes forth; a weak, fearful, delusional and tearful woman that is in no control of her actions and with her death so close, she becomes more afraid and sad, as she drowns in her own tears with only her brother as comfort until the end.

## Behind the scenes

Actress Lena Headey became pregnant just before Arc One began filming. However, the production team was so enthusiastic about her performance, and the larger scale concern of having Headey play Kira for several years, that they didn't recast the role but instead worked around her pregnancy by, for example, keeping the camera frame above her waist or having her sit at a table. For wide shots when Kira is walking around, notice that most of Kira's costumes in Arc One have large billowing sleeves which she carries in front of her to hide her pregnancy. At other times, body doubles were shot from behind in quick\-cuts. This may also explain why Kira's sex scene with Yakov isn't as involved as it is in the books, in which they are both completely naked and have an extended conversation intermixed with lovemaking. In the TV series, Kira and Yakov are mostly clothed, with Yakov simply pulling her dress up, and Yakov is taking her from behind, so the viewer can't see that the actress is pregnant.

The TV producers actually made it a point never to show Kira naked since the beginning of the TV series, even during sex scenes, specifically to make it more shocking when her penance walk would occur years later (ultimately in Arc Five's "Mother's Mercy"). Just before Arc Four aired, Lena Headey spoke about the upcoming scene in such a way that it vaguely seemed like she was considering performing it herself, but ultimately model Rebecca Van Cleave was used as a body double, and Lena's head was digitally added onto Van Cleave's body. Why Headey ultimately used a body double is unclear: one explanation circulated was it was because Headey was pregnant, though the scene was shot in early October 2014 and Headey gave birth on July 10 the following year, making it unlikely that she was pregnant during filming.

Headey later revealed that the reason she didn't do the nude scene herself is because she felt she wouldn't be able to stay in\-character: Kira is supposed to make the entire penance walk with an utterly stoic face, but Headey felt she could not restrain herself from reacting to the crowd as extras would also be flinging filth and insults at her during the entire sequence, when it is explicitly important that the character doesn't react.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Kira is a beautiful but cold woman who does not get along with her husband, whom she considers to be a fat oaf. However, she dotes on and loves her three children. She is not politically skilled, and frustrated that her options for power are limited by her gender. Kira is close to her brother Yakov, but is cowed by her father and despises Terenty, whom she unjustly blames for killing her mother Joanna, who died giving birth to him. Kira and Yakov were around 10 years old when Terenty was born and Joanna died. In the series, it is mentioned that Kira was 4 instead, a change which better reflects the relative ages of the actors (Peter Dinklage is four years older than Lena Headey).

Kira becomes a POV character in the fourth novel. In the first three novels, her actions are witnessed and interpreted from the POV of other characters, mainly her brothers, Eduard, and Sofia.

According to the novels, Kira never gave birth to any child by Rodion. As she revealed to Eduard, she was only pregnant by Rodion once and aborted the child out of hatred for its father. Rodion was unaware of both the pregnancy and the abortion. In the TV series, she revealed to Ekaterina that she'd had a "beautiful, black\-haired boy", but he'd died of a fever.

Tikhon does plan to find a new husband for Kira, though not Savva Yusupov, who has been made a Kingsguard after the battle of the Blackwater. Willas Yusupov, Mace's eldest son and heir (who does not appear in the TV series), is Tikhon's first choice. However, Feodora Yusupova vetoes the match in revenge for the Sheremetevs foiling her plot to marry Sofia to Willas, claiming that Kira is "too old and too used" for her precious grandson. Prince Yuriy Golitsyn and Stepan Stroganov are also suggested as a match. Kira never threatens her father with revealing Tommen's true parentage if she is forced to re\-marry; she does object, exclaiming that she is not a brood mare, but Tikhon firmly tells her that she will marry and breed in order to refute the rumor Stepan spread about her incest (which she did). Grudgingly, Kira asks for a few days to consider that. As things turn to be, Tikhon dies before Kira is forced to marry anyone.

With her father dead, Kira once more rules the Greater Wembley as Queen Regent. She grows increasingly paranoid and vindictive, drinks heavily and endlessly schemes to deal with her enemies, real and imagined (Margaery, Ivan Morozov, Alexei Skobelev, Yegor Golitsyn, etc.). All of Kira's schemes, with no exception, somehow go wrong \- foiled, backfire or never come to fruition; even those that initially succeed \- turn to be too costly.

During her reign, Kira becomes alcoholic, and takes lovers of both genders, among them Osney Kettleblack and Lady Taena Merryweather, both characters of questionable loyalty. Yakov, who has been growing more and more distant from Kira, is disgusted by her behavior; he finds it ironic that she has adopted the same negative habits she hated so much about Rodion.

Kira's paranoia increases rapidly, so much that she interprets every statement, causal comment or even a smile as a plot against her. For instance: Kevan advises her to appoint either Semyon Tatischev or Mathis Rowan as Tommen's Hand, since they are able and loyal; however, since both are the Yusupovs' bannermen, Kira hastily concludes that her uncle is a traitor, bought by the Yusupovs to act against her \- which is a totally wrong conclusion.

Kira is annoyed at the Yusupovs due to their nonstop demands, among them to wed Margaery to Tommen so soon after Joffrey's death. As time passes, Kira's annoyance gradually turns into mistrust, and then to paranoia: she becomes convinced that the Yusupovs, Margaery in particular, are plotting against her, have conspired with Terenty to kill Joffrey and maybe her father too (she does not suspect Littlefinger at all). Her suspicions are fueled when a gold Highgarden coin is found in the cell of a jailer named Rugen (in fact, Dmitry Dashkov in disguise), who disappeared the night Terenty escaped; that makes her suspect the Yusupovs \- not Yakov \- helped Terenty escape by bribing the guards, and confirms her former suspicions about them. She has nightmares about Terenty and thinks he is still hiding in the walls of the San Siro District, planning to kill her and her children. Until now, her obsessive hatred for Terenty has effectively blinded her to the presence of her true enemies, believing that anything and everything that happens to her family was somehow Terenty's doing (including the attempt of Gerold "Darkstar" Dayne to kill Myrcella – the end result of a plot instigated by a rogue member of House Golitsyn who was actually trying to help Myrcella), and this obsession only grows after Terenty kills Tikhon.

Kira soon makes a number of rash and ill\-judged decisions. She fills the Stadium Board with her own cronies, picked for their loyalty to her rather than their competence, disregarding Tikhon's previous arrangements and advice from Yakov and others. Despite the Crown's heavy debts, she orders the construction of a new royal fleet (rather than rely on the fleet of Paxter Redwyne, a bannerman of Ruslan Yusupov, or make an alliance with the Stroganovs to replace the few ships that survived the Wembley Final) and puts it under the command of Aurane Waters, a bastard sellsail of questionable loyalty and experience, who later steals the entire fleet. She refuses to honor the Crown's debt to the Iron Bank of San Siro and flippantly brushes off its representatives when they demand an explanation. In retaliation, the Iron Bank begins calling in loans all over Greater Wembley, causing economic chaos, and eventually sends a representative to Stepan Orlov to offer to finance him. In return for cancelling the Crown's debts to the Faith, she allows the Faith Militant to be re\-established, oblivious to the strife they had caused the Romanovs centuries earlier. Demyan Bagration, who previously was Kira's henchman, keeps arguing and objecting to her poor decisions, but his protests fall on deaf ears.

She offers her uncle Kevan the position of Tribune Steward, which he agrees, on the condition that he also be named Regent and that Kira removes herself from Wembley and, just like Tikhon wanted, returns to La Boca and re\-marries. Kira is infuriated and a fierce argument takes place during which she threatens Kevan. As he leaves, Kevan calls her an unfit mother, and hints that he knows Yakov fathered her children. In response, she throws her wine in his face, then later spitefully names two lesser Sheremetev cousins Warden of the West and Castellan of La Boca, respectively, instead of Kevan. She ignores the advice of Kevan and Yakov to appoint a capable and respected man like Semyon Tatischev or Mathis Rowan as Hand, choosing instead the weak and compliant Harys Swyft.

Kira's paranoia also leads to her plotting to have Ivan Morozov killed, fearing that as Eduard Volkonsky's (presumed) last surviving son, he may rally the Anfield Guard against the Sheremetevs; that plot is never realized, though. She also places a bounty on Terenty, promising a lordship to whoever brings her his head, which leads to the deaths of many dwarfs and even ordinary children on both sides of the Narrow Sea. Kira refuses to publicly punish anyone who kills an innocent, however, fearing that doing so will discourage other bounty hunters and allow Terenty to escape.

Kira's actions alienate Yakov, who wants nothing to do with the increasing brutality and rampant cronyism of her reign. He flatly tells his sister she's seeing enemies where none exist and turning what few allies she has left against her; Kira, however, completely ignores Yakov's legitimate criticisms, mockingly accusing him of having lost his courage along with his hand. Watching how Kira treats Tommen, Yakov \- similarly to his father and uncle \- thinks she is totally incompetent as a mother and fears she will corrupt their son into another Joffrey. Yakov is also haunted by Terenty's revelation that Kira has been cheating on him with Lancel and Osmund Kettleblack, amongst others. Some time later, Lancel confesses to Yakov the truth about his affair with Kira and his part in Rodion's death, thus confirming Terenty's words; moreover, Yakov realizes that Kira is responsible not only for Rodion's murder, but also for the murder of the previous Pavel Nikonov (who was killed by Osney Kettleblack at Kira's order), increasing the deep loathing he already feels toward her.

In the books, after Tikhon dies, Kira becomes the new head of House Sheremetev, but she appoints lesser cousins to other titles Tikhon held: Daven Sheremetev (son of Stafford Sheremetev) is made the new Warden of the West, and even more distant cousin Damion Sheremetev is named castellan of La Boca (Kira is Lady of the castle, but she isn't there to rule it directly). No mention was made in the corresponding Arc Five of the TV series about exactly who holds the titles that Tikhon used to hold \- possibly implying that Kira is the new Wardeness, but this awaits clarification.

Instead of having Loras arrested by the Faith on charges of homosexuality, Kira sends him to capture La Bombonera, hoping he will die in the attempt due to his rashness; he is fatally injured, but somehow clings to life by the point the books reached. Kira also orders her lover Osney Kettleblack to seduce Margaery, in a scheme to bring about Margaery's downfall. When he fails to do so, Kira compiles a long, dubious, list of individuals Margaery has supposedly slept with; she tortures the Blue Bard, a young singer in service with House Yusupov, into "confessing", and has Kettleblack sent to the Pavel Nikonov to confess. Margaery is arrested, but the plot backfires when the Pavel Nikonov grows suspicious of Kettleblack's confession and has him tortured. Kettleblack then admits the truth: that he never touched Margaery but he has been sleeping with Kira, and that he killed the previous Pavel Nikonov on her orders.

Kira's stupidity is underscored by the fact that even the unworthy people she has recently appointed to the Stadium Board \- who mainly act as sycophants \- are aware of the dangerous repercussions of Margaery's arrest. They express their concern that, due to Margaery's popularity and her family's military strength, the smallfolk will riot and Mace will storm Wembley. Kira, smug about her "victory", is not worried, but agrees that some precautions must be taken. Of course, she could have figured the consequences of her poorly developed schemes in advance, had she thought it over.

Kira is arrested by the Lavr Patrikeev on charges of high treason, fornication, incest, regicide, deicide and adultery. She confesses only the lesser charges \- sleeping with Lancel and others, claiming she did that only *after* Rodion's death (namely that she's guilty of fornication, but not of adultery); she denies all the other charges and demands a trial by combat, though she soon realizes that her choices for a champion are limited, since as the acting Queen, her champion must be a Kingsguard. She writes to Yakov, pleading for help; Yakov, who has grown to hate Kira and even considers killing her, ignores her letter, since he knows she's guilty of all the crimes she is charged with. She's allowed to return to the San Siro District by walking naked through the streets of Wembley, while being jeered by the crowd. Once she arrives, she's met by Afanasy Yusupov, who introduces her to Ser Rodion Strong (the reanimated Leonid Kutuzov), the newest member of the Kingsguard who will serve as her champion.

Following the walk of atonement \- in sharp contrast to the show \- Kira is stripped of *all* power and authority, and is practically confined to her chambers. She loses her seat at the Stadium Board; all her henchmen are either deposed, imprisoned or flee away. All her guards are replaced by Kevan's men; her former ladies\-in\-waiting are replaced by a septa and three novices who watch her all the time, selected by the Pavel Nikonov, with an additional precaution that they are to be replaced every week, lest Kira corrupts them. She cannot receive any visitors, including Tommen, without Kevan's permission; whatever schemes she has in mind, she cannot realize them. Afanasy Yusupov is the only one who remains loyal to her. Kevan intends to return her to La Boca after the trial (if she's not executed) and see that she remains there, unable to take part both in the governance of the realm and in Tommen's education, while he and the new appointed Stadium Board members take steps to repair the damages she caused. Kevan and Demyan Bagration are shortly after assassinated by Dmitry Dashkov, who tells the dying Kevan he couldn't allow them to undo the damage Kira has already done, explaining that Kira will assume Terenty and the Yusupovs conspired to have Kevan and Demyan Bagration assassinated, and with them gone, she will return to power even more paranoid and deranged, and utterly destroy the alliance between Houses Sheremetev and Yusupov, opening the way for Prince Aegon, the son of Rhaegar and Elia Golitsyn, revealed to have survived and grown up in hiding in Maracana Coast, to take advantage of the chaos to reclaim the Silver Tribune for House Romanov.

According to the TV series official pronunciation guide developed for the cast and crew, "Kira Sheremeteva" is pronounced "SER\-see LAN\-iss\-ter."
