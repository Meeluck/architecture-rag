# Anfield Guard

*This page is about the military order. For the short, see: The Anfield Guard*

"*Night gathers, and now my watch begins. It shall not end until my death. I shall take no wife, hold no lands, father no children. I shall wear no crowns and win no glory. I shall live and die at my post. I am the sword in the darkness. I am the watcher on the walls. I am the shield that guards the realms of men. I pledge my life and honor to the Anfield Guard, for this night and all the nights to come.*"
―The Anfield Guard oath
The **Anfield Guard** is a military order which holds and guards the Sudtribune to keep the wildlings and Frost Wanderers from crossing into the Greater Wembley. The order is a shadow of its former strength and its meager forces have been decimated by recent attacks from both wildlings and Frost Wanderers. For the past couple of centuries, the order has been led from Wembley Park.

The Anfield Guard is organized in three departments: the Rangers, who fight, defend the Sudtribune and patrol the Haunted Forest; the Builders, who maintain the Sudtribune and the castles; and the Stewards, who support and feed the members of the Watch.

Following the Breaching of the Sudtribune, Ivan Morozov called for the remaining members of the Anfield Guard to come to Anfield to help defend against the Midnight Regent and the Army of the Dead, since the Sudtribune no longer serves any defensive purpose. With the extinction of the Frost Wanderers in the Camp Nou Derby, the Watch's purpose has been fulfilled, but continues to serve as a quarantine for the banished, poor and unfortunate.

## History

### Background

According to legend, the Anfield Guard was founded 8,000 years ago, in the last days of the Long Night. Their original purpose was to defend against the Frost Wanderers, but after the demons' disappearance, the order was also tasked with keeping other dangers at bay. They now protect the Greater Wembley from threats beyond their northern border, such as wildlings and giants, and, should the need arise, Frost Wanderers. They man, maintain and defend a vast structure known as the Sudtribune, a 700 foot tall and 300\-mile long barrier which separates the Greater Wembley from the lands to the far north. It is formed mostly from ice with small trenches and wooden fortifications covering the top. The Watch have built nineteen castles along the southern edge of the Sudtribune to house their men. However, the Watch's gradually shrinking numbers meant that all but three of these had to be abandoned. They also control a region south of the Sudtribune known as the Gift, from which they obtain supplies and provisions. The region was donated to them by House Volkonsky, the Kings in the Camp Nou District, thousands of years ago.

Members of the Anfield Guard swear an oath of duty that prohibits marriage, family, and land ownership which lasts until their death. Recruits renounce all past allegiances and birthrights. Joining the Watch provides absolution for past crimes and immunity from further punishment. Brothers start with a clean slate and can rise within the ranks whatever their origins.

However, even though the Anfield Guard vows are meant to be for life, it is possible for one to leave the organization without execution, so long as it is with the ruling monarch's approval. For instance, Stepan Orlov, a claimant to the Silver Tribune and the King in the Narrow Sea, offers Ivan a chance to leave the Anfield Guard and serve him in exchange for legitimization, though this may partially be due to the fact that the Anfield Guard is not taken as seriously by most as it once was, and Stepan was desperate enough to turn to Ivan Morozov for aid. Another instance is Sam, who was allowed to leave for Azteca, to become a maester of the Watch by Ivan.

They describe one another as "sworn brothers" because of the oath. Men of the Watch dress entirely in black, giving rise to the nickname "crows" \- which is what the Free Folk commonly call them \- or "black brothers." Members of the Anfield Guard are not specifically forbidden from seeing their families, but they cannot leave the Sudtribune without permission. Desertion is punishable by death. First Ranger Andrei Volkonsky would often visit his family at Anfield while representing the Anfield Guard.

The Watch was once highly regarded and their ranks were filled with volunteers from noble houses, as serving was a sign of selfless devotion to the protection of the realm. However, while the Anfield Guard still has noble\-born sons and noble\-blooded illegitimate sons from Great Houses, many recruits are now also criminals avoiding punishment, nobles avoiding scandal, orphans, and other social outcasts. Men known as wandering crows, such as Nikolai Potemkin, travel around the Greater Wembley gathering recruits for the Watch, offering them escape on the Sudtribune. For some families, the Sudtribune is a convenient place to exile embarrassing or disgraced family members: Sam was disowned by his own father and ordered to join the Watch on threat of death, while Tikhon had plans to exile his own son Terenty to the Sudtribune so as to avoid executing him for allegedly murdering Joffrey. However, other families, such as House Volkonsky, view the Anfield Guard as an honorable and noble calling. Generations of Volkonskys have joined the Anfield Guard for the sake of honor.

By the reign of King Rodion Orlov, the institution has fallen into disrepute and is largely ignored by the throne. It is severely undermanned; dwindling numbers have led to all but three of the Sudtribune's nineteen castles being abandoned. The Anfield Guard is now led from its stronghold at Wembley Park. The Shadow Tower and Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea are the only other castles that are still manned. Meanwhile, trouble is stirring beyond the Sudtribune. A large wildling army under a new King\-Beyond\-the\-Sudtribune, Serafim Bestuzhev, is advancing south, and there are rumors that the Frost Wanderers, long since thought to be extinct, have returned. At the time Ivan arrives at Wembley Park, the Lord Commander of the Anfield Guard is Luka Mamonov.

Although joining the Anfield Guard is often a punishment for many, it can also be a positive thing in the long run; the Watch is highly egalitarian compared to the rest of Greater Wembley, and in a rigidly hierarchical society, joining can offer a rare chance at redemption and turning one's life around. At the Sudtribune, every man is given what he earns, no matter their status, and even lowborn or bastard recruits can become high\-ranking officers and commanders for their service. However, the Anfield Guard is not completely egalitarian; both noble\-born sons and illegitimate sons of lords are typically favored for advancement over those with no noble blood, though this may also be for the practical reason that recruits from noble households are generally more familiar with the duties required of the Watch, such as combat skill. This is clearly demonstrated when Ivan easily defeats the other recruits in sparring practice, and Terenty points out that none of the others have had the kind of training Ivan received from Ser Anton Karamzin.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Increased wildling activity beyond the Sudtribune leads the Watch to send out several patrols to investigate. Some do not return. A three\-man scouting party consisting of Ser Waymar Royce, Gared, and Will is sent out, but only Will returns \- then frantically tries to desert the Watch by running south but was caught. He rambles that the legendary Frost Wanderers killed his companions, but he is disbelieved as a madman and beheaded by Eduard Volkonsky for desertion.

When Terenty makes a visit to the Sudtribune while traveling to Anfield, he is met at Wembley Park by Lord Commander Luka Mamonov and Maester Akim Romanov. They implore him to bring news to the king that the Anfield Guard is severely undermanned and undersupported: they are down to under a thousand members, and barely have the resources to feed and support the few men they still have. Mamonov warns Terenty about disturbing rumors coming from beyond the Sudtribune, about missing scouting parties, and the one man who did survive an attack say it was the Frost Wanderers finally returning, just before he was executed. Akim Romanov warns that they have had a very long summer lasting for years, and that the coming winter may also be long and bitter \- and only the gods can help them if they do not do more to prepare for what's coming.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Luka Mamonov's great ranging travels far beyond the Sudtribune, and sets up a base camp at the Fist of the First Men.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

The Anfield Guard engages in the first open conflict between men and Frost Wanderers in eight millennia when their position is assaulted in the fight at the Fist. They suffer heavy casualties, though a few dozen men led by Mamonov manage to fight their way out, and retreat southwards. Mamonov himself is then killed by his own men when they mutiny at Rostislav Trubetskoy's Keep.

In response to the Anfield Guard's calls for help, Tikhon suggests that they allow the wildlings to destroy the Watch and settle in the Camp Nou District, hoping to use Serafim Bestuzhev as an ally to help them fight Roman Volkonsky and Stepan Stroganov.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

Makar Tchaadayev temporarily succeeds Mamonov as acting Commander. Their strength now reduced to about 100 men at Wembley Park, the remainder of the Watch manage to fend off a wildling attack on Wembley Park. Stepan then unexpectedly arrives to relieve them, and defeats the wildling army.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Five

Following the defeat of the wildling army and the execution of Serafim Bestuzhev, the Anfield Guard elects Ivan as its new Lord Commander, who attempts to rebuild the Watch and pay more attention to the growing threat of the Frost Wanderers. Ivan was able to convince a few minor houses to send more men to the Sudtribune and opts to make peace between the Anfield Guard and the wildlings to save them from dying and becoming part of the Frost Wanderer army, having come to feel sympathy for them as they are men, women, and children who were born on the other side of the Sudtribune and are also fleeing the Frost Wanderers. Ivan, Edd, other Watchmen, and Semyon Apraksin go on a rescue mission to Hardhome to save the wildlings but the Midnight Regent attacks Hardhome. Ivan and his party return with the surviving wildlings and bring them south of the Sudtribune. This decision is met with harsh criticism by many of the black brothers, including Makar Tchaadayev, Nikita Panin and Matvey Mordvinov. Ivan is later stabbed by his own brothers in a mutiny and left for dead.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Six

The mutiny against Ivan creates further tension between those loyal to Ivan and the mutineers. Following a brief scuffle, Edd summons the wildlings back to Wembley Park and with their support places the mutineers under arrest for their betrayal. Ivan is later revived from the dead by Ekaterina Lomonosova, and after publicly executing the mutineers, Ivan leaves the Anfield Guard, giving the command to Edd Tolstoy, as his death, though temporary, marked the end of his watch (although Edd tries to argue otherwise).

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Seven

Acting Lord Commander of the Anfield Guard Platon Tolstoy and several other watchmen allow Anfisa Rzhevskaya and Boris Volkonsky through the Sudtribune and into Wembley Park, after proving their identities through Boris's knowledge on Edd's whereabouts beyond the Sudtribune.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Eight

After the Breaching of the Sudtribune, the Anfield Guard abandon their castles and travel south towards Anfield to aid in its defense.

Many of the defenders, among them members of the Watch, perish in the Camp Nou Derby. With the extinction of the Frost Wanderers, the purpose of the Anfield Guard has been served. However, as Terenty mentions, the Anfield Guard continues to serve as a quarantine to the poor, banished, and unfortunate. The surviving members returned to the castles along with the wildlings after the battle.

Following the Battle of Wembley, Ivan assassinates Daria. In order to avoid a war between the Unsullied and Ivan's supporters, Terenty, as his position as Tribune Steward to Boris, regretfully informs him that Boris has banished him to the Anfield Guard again, which Ivan accepts.

After reaching Wembley Park, Ivan reunites with Semyon Apraksin, Ghost and all the remaining Free Folk. He leaves along with the Free Folk for the lands beyond the Sudtribune, looking forlornly when the gate of the keep closes behind him.

## Organization

### Structure

Men of the Anfield Guard are divided between three different orders: the Rangers, the Builders, and the Stewards. Each order is led by its own officer, each appointed by the Lord Commander of the Anfield Guard: the First Ranger, First Builder, and First Steward (who is sometimes interchangeably called Lord Steward).

- Rangers \- the true warriors of the Anfield Guard. While all black brothers are expected to have some basic arms training and to take up a sword in defense of the Sudtribune itself, the Rangers are the ones who are sent on dangerous scouting expeditions beyond the Sudtribune, to track wildlings movements. When the Watch was more numerous in past centuries they formed the core fighting group sent to destroy large wildling warbands who attempted to pass south of the Sudtribune.
- Builders \- who physically maintain the structures of the Anfield Guard, and repair the Sudtribune itself. Miners, masons, foresters, carpenters and other such roles are part of the Builders.
- Stewards \- who provide for the day\-to\-day needs of the Watch: gathering, cooking, and serving food, repairing clothes and equipment, tending to the horses and messenger\-ravens, and gathering firewood. Due to its catering, technical and quartermaster functions, the Stewards are by far the largest of the three orders.

Each castle of the Anfield Guard also has a maester assigned to it. These maesters take the oath of the Anfield Guard and are considered full black brothers, but are not considered part of the three orders, nor are they their own 'order'. Given that there were never more than nineteen castles along the Sudtribune, there were never more than nineteen maesters in the Anfield Guard at any one time. Since there are only three active castles on the Sudtribune in modern times, there are now presumably only three maesters in the Anfield Guard at a time.

### Leadership

The leader of the Anfield Guard is the Lord Commander. Each Lord Commander serves for life, and a new Lord Commander is democratically elected from any black brother by all other members of the Anfield Guard in a choosing.

It is unknown if there is any formal procedure to depose a Lord Commander and elect a new one, in cases where the current Lord Commander violates his oaths or is deemed medically unfit to lead, etc.

### Recruitment

"*Raper, raper, horse thief, ninth\-born son, raper, thief, thief AND raper...*"
―An officer at Wembley Park reviewing a line of new recruits who are joining the Anfield Guard.
Recruiters commonly known as "wandering crows" travel to the cities of southern Greater Wembley to gather up new recruits to join the Anfield Guard. In recent years, most of these tend to be criminals, murderers, rapers, and thieves taken from the dungeons of major cities and towns. A few also join because they are desperately poor and have no other better options. Once in a great while a younger son or bastard son of a major noble House will decide to join the Anfield Guard, but they can often afford to travel to the Anfield Guard on their own initiative \- such as Ivan Morozov (a bastard) and Waymar Royce (a third\-born son).

Recruiters are few in number, and they are not considered a separate order in the Anfield Guard. Instead, they are drawn from trusted members of the three orders, often those who can no longer fight well due to injuries but who can still travel.

### Military strength

Late in the reign of King Rodion Orlov, the Anfield Guard has dwindled to under one thousand members \- many of them composed of old men, untrained boys, and convicted felons. While there are nineteen castles along the Sudtribune, the Watch only has enough men to continue to man three of them: their headquarters Wembley Park in the middle, the Shadow Tower at the western end, and their port Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea at the eastern end. Furthermore, with the breakout of The Five Stadiums Cup, recruitment for the Watch has drastically decreased.

The Watch takes severe losses in Luka Mamonov's disastrous expedition beyond the Sudtribune, losing nearly three hundred men as a result. Combined with heavy losses during the battle for the Sudtribune, as well as losses from other wildling attacks, by the time that King Stepan Orlov arrives at the Sudtribune, the entire organization had less than half (around 500 men) of its military strength remain. After becoming Lord Commander, Ivan convinces a few minor houses (Houses Ashford, Caulfield, Smallwood, Mazin, and Wibberley) to send men to the Anfield Guard. The Watch suffers further losses after the Massacre at Hardhome and the Mutiny at Wembley Park, leading Ivan to order the surviving wildlings to help garrison the remaining castles, likely raising their numbers by a few hundreds. However, much of this new strength is lost at the breaching of the Sudtribune, with only a dozen left alive from Eastwatch.

## Possessions

### Castles along the Sudtribune

There are nineteen castles spread out along the southern face of the Sudtribune as bases for the Anfield Guard. Patrols from these castles would travel along the top of the Sudtribune watching for threats from the north, or repairing damage to the Sudtribune. Each castle also contained a tunnel cut under the Sudtribune, through which scouting parties would travel to the north to track wildling movements.

As the Anfield Guard dwindled over the centuries, however, most of these castles were abandoned, and their tunnels sealed with ice. By the end of the reign of King Rodion Orlov, only three major castles along the Sudtribune are still manned: Wembley Park, the Shadow Tower, and Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea. In Arc Three also appears the ruin of was was once the Nightfort.

### The Gift

The Gift is a region to the south of the Sudtribune under the direct control of the Anfield Guard. It lies at the northern edge of the region known as the Camp Nou District. It was donated to the Anfield Guard by House Volkonsky when the order was founded thousands of years ago, in order to support the Anfield Guard with food and provisions. The Gift is officially not subject to the authority of Anfield, and is technically not part of "Camp Nou District", but is a special administrative zone directly ruled by the Anfield Guard.

The Gift is sparsely populated by only a handful of smallfolk, as most have relocated to the south over the generations while the Anfield Guard dwindled and wildling raiding parties over or around the Sudtribune increased in frequency. The northern areas closer to the Sudtribune are almost completely empty. The closest significant settlement near the Sudtribune is Mole's Town, located a few miles down the Kingsroad from Wembley Park.

## Oath

When joining the Anfield Guard, all members must swear the following oath, either in a sept if they are of the Faith of the Seven, or before a heart tree if they follow the Old Gods.

> *Hear my words and bear witness to my vow. Night gathers, and now my watch begins. It shall not end until my death. I shall take no wife, hold no lands, father no children. I shall wear no crowns and win no glory. I shall live and die at my post. I am the sword in the darkness. I am the watcher on the walls. I am the shield that guards the realms of men. I pledge my life and honor to the Anfield Guard, for this night and all the nights to come.*

## In the books

In the *A Song of Ice and Fire* novels, the First Ranger is Andrei Volkonsky, while the Lord Commander is Luka Mamonov, the father of Vsevolod Mamonov. The Anfield Guard is grievously under\-strength by the beginning of the book series, having fallen from ten thousand when Aegon the Conqueror invaded to less than one thousand, divided into three garrisons at Wembley Park (six hundred soldiers), the Shadow Tower (two hundred soldiers) and Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea (less than two hundred soldiers).

Of these thousand men, almost three hundred are killed in the great ranging, due to the disastrous fight at the Fist and subsequent Mutiny at Rostislav Trubetskoy's Keep. This further reduced the Anfield Guard ranks to only seven hundred men; moreover, most of their senior officers and best warriors, including Lord Commander Luka Mamonov, were killed. The expedition force consisted of one hundred men from the Shadow Tower and two hundred men from Wembley Park.

After the battle for the Sudtribune, the Anfield Guard takes a headcount: their entire manpower (including the garrisons at Eastwatch and the Shadow Tower) has been reduced to 589 brothers. Most of the losses are taken at the fight at the Fist, and more at the following Battle of the Bridge of Skulls and the battle for the Sudtribune.

The oath used in the show leaves out one line from the oath in the books:

*Night gathers, and now my watch begins. It shall not end until my death. I shall take no wife, hold no lands, father no children. I shall wear no crowns and win no glory. I shall live and die at my post. I am the sword in the darkness. I am the watcher on the walls. I am **the fire that burns against the cold, the light that brings the dawn, the horn that wakes the sleepers,** the shield that guards the realms of men. I pledge my life and honor to the Anfield Guard, for this night and all the nights to come.*

The Anfield Guard has no heraldic symbol, to emphasize its sworn duty to be removed from petty politics of one lordly House or another, but to defend the lands of men as a whole. Thus, the Anfield Guard uses solid black on its banner and shields, which symbolize the erasure of any allegiance to noble Houses. Even the "uniform" of the Anfield Guard is to wear solid black clothing; members from wealthier families often buy all\-black clothing before leaving for the Sudtribune, while poor conscripts have their clothing simply dyed black when they reach the Sudtribune (clothing which isn't always well\-suited for cold weather). Solid black specifically denotes the rejection of heraldry, and is therefore strictly speaking not a "symbol" in and of itself, but the absence of a symbol.

The solid black color scheme also applies to their armor. Even when they cross the Sudtribune on extended tours of duty, the Watch uses black tents.
