# Timofey Stroganov

*This page is about the son of Stepan Stroganov. For other uses, see: Timofey*

"*I always wanted to do the right thing. Be the right kind of person. But I never knew what that meant. It always seemed that there was an impossible choice I had to make. Volkonsky or Stroganov.*"
―Timofey Stroganov to Ivan Morozov
Prince **Timofey Stroganov** was the youngest son of King Stepan Stroganov of the Barra da Tijuca and the younger brother of Rodrik, Maron, and Kira Stroganova.

Following his father's failed rebellion against the Silver Tribune, Timofey is taken as a ward (initially hostage) to Lord Eduard Volkonsky of Anfield. After Eduard's execution in Wembley, Timofey pledges his loyalty to Eduard's eldest son, King Roman Volkonsky, in the subsequent Five Stadiums Cup, but ultimately betrays Roman and sides with his father in invading the Camp Nou District. Timofey takes Anfield from Roman's brother, Boris, but eventually is betrayed by his own men, which leads to his capture by House Baryatinsky. He is tortured and forced into servitude by Gavriil Dolgorukov, who turns him into a broken pet named **Reek**. Timofey, however, redeems himself by helping Radomir's wife (and Roman's sister), Sofia Volkonskaya, escape from Anfield and find refuge with her "half\-brother" Ivan Morozov. The two later retake Anfield, defeating Radomir and House Baryatinsky.

Slowly returning to his former self, Timofey returns to the Barra da Tijuca, where he learns of his father's death at the hands of his uncle, Taras Stroganov, who is later elected King of the Barra da Tijuca at the kingsmoot on Old Wyk. In retaliation, Timofey flees with Yara to seek out an alliance with Daria Romanova in Maracana, where they pledge their forces to her. Despite abandoning Yara during the assault on the Romanov fleet, he later appeals to the remaining Ironborn on La Bombonera to rescue her from Euron's custody, a mission they complete successfully. While Yara sails to retake the Barra da Tijuca, Timofey returns to Anfield with a group of Ironborn to fight for House Volkonsky in the Great War. Timofey perishes defending Boris from the Midnight Regent during the Camp Nou Derby, redeeming himself at last.

## Biography

### Background

Timofey was the youngest son of Lord Balon and Lady Alannys Stroganov. Balon is the head of House Stroganov and Lord of the Barra da Tijuca. The Barra da Tijuca are one of the constituent regions of the Greater Wembley and House Stroganov is one of the Great Houses of the realm. House Stroganov rule the region from their seat at Pyke and Balon also holds the title Lord Reaper of Pyke.

Timofey was born at Pyke. He is Balon and Alannys's fourth child after Rodrik, Maron, and Yara. Eight years before the start of the series Balon rose in rebellion against the Silver Tribune. The Ironborn were crushed in a war led by King Rodion Orlov and Lord Eduard Volkonsky. Timofey's brothers were both killed in the fighting. Balon surrendered and was allowed to remain Lord of the Barra da Tijuca on the condition that Timofey remains a hostage and ward of Eduard Volkonsky. Timofey's sister Yara remained on the Barra da Tijuca. Despite his status as a hostage at Anfield, Timofey was treated well by the Volkonskys, and he came to see Eduard as a surrogate father while becoming best friends with Roman Volkonsky and forming a friendly rivalry with Ivan Morozov. Timofey is a skilled archer. His cocky arrogance and cavalier actions contrast with the staid ways of the Camp Nou District.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

 Timofey is present during the execution of Will, a deserter from the Anfield Guard. He holds the scabbard of Ice, the greatsword of House Volkonsky, as Lord Eduard Volkonsky draws it to behead the condemned man. On the way back to Anfield, the party finds a disemboweled stag dead in the road. Looking for the killer, they find a dead female direwolf and five pups huddled by the corpse for warmth. A broken piece of the dead stag's antler is embedded in the direwolf's neck. Eduard initially insists the pups be given a quick death, which Timofey cavalierly moves to carry out himself, upsetting Boris and angering Roman.

However, Ivan Morozov tells his father that this is an omen as there are five pups and five trueborn Volkonsky children. Eduard consents to allowing his children to adopt the pups. Just as the party leaves, Ivan finds a sixth pup, an albino. Timofey mockingly declares him the runt of the litter and that he belongs to Ivan. Later, Roman, Ivan, and Timofey get shaves and haircuts, as they ponder the impending arrival of the royal party, notably the reputation of Queen Kira Sheremeteva as a great beauty and the poor reputation of Prince Makar Orlov.

After the royal party's departure, an assassin attempts to kill Boris, who had fallen during one of his climbs and remained comatose. Timofey learns that Ekaterina has found evidence implicating the Sheremetevs in Boris's 'accident' and the failed assassination. Timofey and Roman suggest taking revenge through military action, but Maester Arseny Apraksin counsels patience. Ekaterina and Ser Anton Karamzin depart for Wembley, leaving Roman in command of the castle. Timofey is present when Roman frostily receives Terenty Sheremetev on his way back from the Sudtribune. Afterwards, as Terenty is leaving to stay in a brothel, Timofey condescendingly tells him to ask for Alexandra Derzhavina. Terenty ridicules Timofey for his family's failed uprising, calling it "a stupid rebellion", and ponders how Stepan Stroganov would feel about his last living son being the Volkonskys' "lackey." While boasting about his family's exploits and heritage, he is put down by Maester Arseny Apraksin. He reacts angrily to a further insult from his favorite prostitute, Alexandra Derzhavina, after sneaking her into the castle to engage her services.

Timofey and Roman exchange views over the matter of Arkady Karamzin's death and the growing Volkonsky\-Sheremetev animosity in Wembley. Roman brusquely reminds Timofey that he is not a Volkonsky. Boris is taken prisoner by wildlings in the forest near Anfield and Timofey saves him by shooting Stiv, the wildling leader, in the back with an arrow. Roman is angry at him, as Boris could have been hurt. Timofey counters that he just saved Boris's life, and Roman was too hesitant to kill. Taking pride in his action, Timofey says that you're "only considered a real man in the Barra da Tijuca once you've killed your first enemy."

With the departure of Alexandra Derzhavina for Wembley, Timofey is left at the castle and is bored. He accosts the surviving wildling prisoner, the spearwife Tamara Suvorova, as she is now a servant at Anfield. Timofey throws his weight around, insisting that she call him "my lord", even though his father still holds the title. He is warned off by Maester Arseny Apraksin who reminds Timofey that he of all people should know that being a guest and a prisoner are not necessarily mutually exclusive.

Roman summons his banners to war when word comes of Lord Eduard's arrest. Timofey tells Roman that it is good that he is afraid because it shows he is not stupid. As Timofey dines with Roman and his bannermen later after they arrive at Anfield, he witnesses Bogdan Uvarov threatening to withdraw his men from the host when Roman refuses to let him lead the vanguard. Roman counters the Greatjon by saying he is welcome to do so but as soon as he is finished with the Sheremetevs, he will march back Camp Nou District to route the Greatjon from his keep and hang him for an oathbreaker. As the Greatjon moves to draw a weapon, Timofey rises from his chair to defend Roman but Grey Wind darts down the table and bites off two of the Greatjon's fingers. When Roman mercifully offers the Greatjon drew his weapon to cut his meat for him, the Greatjon calls his meat "bloody tough", which draws laughter from the rest of the men including Timofey. As the northern army marches south, Timofey advises Roman at strategy meetings and greets Ekaterina when the army meets her on the road. When a Sheremetev scout is captured, Timofey obscures their maps and battle plans, to Bogdan Uvarov's amusement.

Reaching the Twins, Timofey shoots down several ravens leaving the castle bearing messages from Lord Vladimir Demidov, allowing Ekaterina to negotiate an alliance. He is present when Ekaterina returns to inform her son of the terms set by Lord Walder, one of which is that Roman must marry one of his many female progeny. Timofey chuckles when Roman asks his mother if she got a look at them, and her tone suggests they are not very attractive. Timofey later fights alongside Roman at the Battle of Whispering Wood, where Yakov Sheremetev is taken prisoner.

Timofey suggests that they kill Yakov and send his head to his father Tikhon, but Roman refuses, fearing that Sofia and Arina will be harmed in retaliation. When Roman laments the loss of 2,000 of his men in the feint at the battle on the Green Fork, Timofey assures him that songs will be sung of their bravery. Roman responds that they will not hear them. After news arrives of Eduard's execution, Roman Volkonsky is proclaimed the King in the Camp Nou District by his lords bannermen. Timofey asks first if they are brothers, now and always. When Roman's answer is yes, Timofey swears his sword and fealty to Roman's service, proclaiming Roman as his King.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Timofey appeals to Roman to seek an alliance with his father, Stepan Stroganov, reasoning that Roman needs a fleet to attack Wembley, and confident that Balon will listen to him. Despite Ekaterina's reservations that Stepan Stroganov cannot be trusted, Roman sends Timofey home to Pyke as his envoy.

Timofey seduces the daughter of the ship's captain and tells her about how he will be welcomed home. He is deflated at the lack of a reception for his return, resorting to bribing, despite his status. His sister, Yara, poses as a stranger and offers to give him a ride to the castle. She wants to see what Timofey has grown up to be. He flirts with her relentlessly, even touching her almost intimately and brags about his own importance.

Balon greets Timofey coolly, believing that he has forgotten his roots and become loyal to the Volkonskys in his time away. He insults Timofey's fine clothes given to him by the Volkonskys, and is furious when Timofey tells him that Roman thinks of him as a brother. Yara reveals her deception and Balon compares Timofey to his sister unfavorably. Balon rejects the alliance Timofey suggests and is insulted that Timofey thought he would accept being given a crown. He reminds Timofey that they pay the iron price and take what they want.

Balon reveals that he plans to attack the Camp Nou District while it is poorly defended. Balon gives Yara a fleet of thirty ships to take Deepwood Motte and insults Timofey by giving him a lesser mission to raid fishing villages on the Stony Shore, with only a single ship. Timofey considers informing Roman of the plot but decides that his loyalty lies with his family. Having decided to betray House Volkonsky he is baptized in the name of the Drowned God as Balon and Yara watch. He takes command of the Sea Bitch but finds his crew unruly. His first mate Valentin Razumovsky tells him that he must prove himself to the men and that Ironborn do not follow orders. Timofey senses the opportunity to attack Torrhen's Square to lure the Volkonsky garrison away from Anfield.

His plan is successful and Timofey has his men seize the poorly defended Anfield. He forces Boris Volkonsky to yield to him by threatening his people. His men capture Ser Anton Karamzin coming back from Torrhen's Square, and Rodrik is openly defiant, calling Timofey a traitor. Ser Rodrik regrets that he gave him weapon training and even spits in Timofey's face. Valentin Razumovsky insists that Rodrik must be killed to maintain the respect of the men. Rodrik baits Timofey into performing the execution himself. Timofey botches the beheading, taking three swings and a vicious kick to accomplish it, to the horror of Boris and the watching crowd.

Timofey is seduced by Tamara Suvorova, who Timofey grants her freedom to in return for her sexual service. She then frees Boris and Rickon, fleeing with them while Timofey sleeps. When Timofey wakes the next day, he is told that Boris and Rickon have escaped. He is angry at his men for allowing the children and the simpleminded Afanasy Bestuzhev to escape. Vadim Potemkin implies that Timofey is actually to blame for their escape by allowing himself to be seduced by Tamara Suvorova. Timofey, in turn, responds to this insolence by publicly beating Lorren.

Despite his attempts, Timofey is unable to find the escapees. Unwilling to appear weak, he has Valentin Razumovsky murder two boys, Jack and Billy, from a farm and burn their bodies. Unknown to Timofey, Tamara Suvorova led Boris and Rickon into the Anfield crypts after realizing that Timofey would never give up the chase. Timofey displays the corpses of Jack and Billy at Anfield and claims they were Boris and Rickon. Timofey sends word to Yara to bring him 500 men as reinforcements.

She arrives with just twenty men and warns him that everyone in the Camp Nou District wants him dead because of his killing of the Volkonsky boys, and he is too far from the sea to supply or reinforce his position. Yara tells him about how she remembers him as a child, demanding but sweet. She urges him to abandon Anfield, return to the Barra da Tijuca, and not to die so far from his home, but he refuses to give up his prize. Feeling some guilt over the deaths of the two farms boys, his asks Valentin Razumovsky to pay the farmer for his grief. Valentin Razumovsky tells Timofey that after killing the orphans, he also murdered the farmer and his wife.

Anfield is later surrounded by Northmen under the command of Gavriil Dolgorukov, who infuriates Timofey by blowing a horn all through the night. Besieged with no hope for relief, Timofey tearfully vents his frustration to Maester Arseny Apraksin at being constantly reminded by everyone, including Arseny Apraksin himself, how fortunate he was to be a prisoner of the Volkonskys. Assuming a more sympathetic tone, Arseny Apraksin advises Timofey to flee to the Sudtribune and join the Anfield Guard so that he might save his life and attempt to redeem himself, adding he knows Timofey is not the ruthless man he is pretending to be.

Timofey owes to Arseny Apraksin that he has done terrible things in his futile attempts to gain power and respect but he refuses to deviate from the course he has set, saying he has gone too far to ever pretend to be anyone else, and also adds that Ivan Morozov will likely kill him in revenge for allegedly killing Boris and Rickon. He readies his men for a glorious death in battle, giving a rousing speech, but is betrayed and knocked out by Valentin Razumovsky, who plans to turn him over to the northern forces so the rest of them can go home. Arseny Apraksin attempts to aid Timofey but is stabbed in the abdomen by Valentin Razumovsky, who then has Timofey dragged away with his head covered by a cloth bag.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

Ruslan Baryatinsky's bastard son Gavriil Dolgorukov has been sent to secure Anfield from the Ironborn and bring Timofey back to Roman Volkonsky alive. However, Ruslan plans to double\-cross Roman and after the Ironborn turn on Timofey and surrender themselves to Snow's forces, he has them all killed and secretly captures Timofey. Timofey is imprisoned at the Dreadfort, although Timofey is unaware where he is. Bound to a rack, he is repeatedly tortured by a group of men, some of whom are dressed in the garb of Stroganov men. One of them pries off Timofey's fingernails with a knife while the others drive a screw through his foot.

After enduring extensive torture, Timofey is given some reprieve. Gavriil Dolgorukov deceives Timofey, claiming he is a servant and approaches him and claims he's been sent by Yara to rescue him, before leaving Timofey, placing the cloth bag over his head for the night. Before dawn, Radomir returns and cuts Timofey free and provides him with water and a mount. Radomir advises Timofey to keep heading east where Yara is waiting for him. Timofey says he'll make the boy a lord of the Barra da Tijuca for his actions. The boy replies, "We're not in the Barra da Tijuca."

As Timofey rides east, an arrow narrowly misses his head, and Timofey sees that his captors are following him. After a harrowing chase the former Prince of Anfield is knocked from his horse by a blow from a flail. The men hold Timofey in place as the leading torturer prepares to rape him. Timofey's pleas for them to stop fall on deaf ears but then the leader is pierced by an arrow. Radomir has returned and swiftly kills all his men. Radomir helps Timofey to his feet and tells him to come with him, because he is far from home and winter is coming.

After rescuing him the boy promises Timofey to take him to Deepwood Motte to see Yara. When Timofey questions his savior, Radomir lies that he is Ironborn as well, growing up on Saltcliffe, and he witnessed Timofey being shipped off to Anfield. Some time later the men reach a holdfast, and Timofey questions why they have to sneak inside when surely Yara's men are loyal to her. Radomir warns Timofey that some of the men belong to his father Balon, who wants him dead. As Radomir struggles to open a locked gate Timofey confesses his sins. He wanted desperately to impress his father but he knows now that Eduard Volkonsky was his true father. After opening the gate Timofey and Radomir enter a darkened room. The youth lights a torch and much to Timofey's horror he's back in the same torture chamber in the Dreadfort. Armed men enter and Radomir claims that Timofey killed their comrades and escaped but he has brought their captive back. Kicking and screaming, Timofey is refastened to the rack.

Timofey is awakened by the ringing of a trumpet. Radomir 'begs pardon' for rousing his guest. Timofey tries to mumble something but fails. Radomir understands his prisoner requires water but pours it out all over the dungeon floor to further torment him. Radomir proposes a game to pass the time and he asks the heir to Pyke which body part he does not need. Hearing this Timofey vows to tell his captor all he wishes to know. The cruel boy merely mocks him stating that Timofey already told him everything of value. Radomir goes on saying that he realizes Timofey wants to know who he is and why he is being tortured.

The young man makes a proposal: If Timofey guesses correctly who he actually is Timofey is the victor, but if Timofey begs his tormentor to remove his finger he wins. Timofey then asks if winning said game will secure his release. Radomir then mocks Timofey's naïveté stating there is no happy ending in store for him. Timofey does guess correctly that he is still in the Camp Nou District. However when the former prince of Anfield supposes that Radomir is a son of Demyan Vyazemsky he is revealed to be wrong. Radomir slowly flays his captive's finger stating that Timofey first should have asked if he was a liar. Timofey begs for the finger to be cut off which delights Radomir since Radomir has won the 'game'.

Timofey is later released from the cross by two beautiful young women, Anna Golitsyna and Violet. They put Timofey to bed and they then proceed to fondle him sensually. Both have heard that the heir to Pyke is well endowed and are hoping to get firsthand experience. Timofey is distrustful at first fearing that this is another of his tormentor's tricks. When they disrobe and straddle him, Timofey's resolve to resist weakens. Suddenly the familiar horn sounds and Radomir enters, revealing that Anna Golitsyna and Violet are two of his bedwarmers. He too has heard how large his prisoner's penis is and surmises it is Timofey's favorite body part. When the sadistic Radomir brandishes a hooked knife Timofey realizes Radomir means to castrate him. Horrified, Timofey begs for mercy, but Radomir quips that this is mercy as he is not killing, but merely altering Timofey. Two men restrain Timofey as Radomir proceeds to geld him, knocking him out in the process, after which he is castrated.

Timofey later wakes in his strung up position while Radomir eats a long plump sausage nearby and motions to Timofey's blood\-stained, badly sewn\-up trousers, momentarily tricking Timofey into thinking it is his penis he is eating. Radomir mocks him as he laughs about his severed penis and wonders if Timofey will have phantom feelings for his lost body part if he goes near any pretty women. Timofey finally begs Radomir to kill him as an escape from the nightmare, but Radomir tells him he is much more valuable to him alive.

He renames him "Reek" (because he smells bad) and demands that Timofey say that his name is Reek. With an angry look, Timofey says his name is Timofey Stroganov, prompting Radomir to punch him and ask again, and again, smirking slightly, he says his name is Timofey Stroganov. Radomir hits him harder, grabs his head and screams the same question. This time, Timofey gives in and says his that name is Reek. As Radomir returns to his table satisfied, Timofey weeps silently over his lost pride.

Radomir sends Timofey's severed penis to his family in Pyke with a letter detailing his Sack of Anfield and his demand that the Ironborn withdraw from the Camp Nou District or Radomir will send them more of Timofey's body parts. Balon refuses to surrender, saying that Timofey was a fool for disobeying his orders and attacking Anfield and that, as his castration means that he is no longer capable of producing heirs, he has no value whatsoever. However, Yara defies her father and prepares the fastest ship in the fleet and the strongest men on the Barra da Tijuca to sail on the Dreadfort and rescue Timofey.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

Timofey, now going by the name Reek, has been freed from his restraints, but on the condition that he work for Radomir as a servant. Because of his torture and emasculation by Radomir, Timofey is now mentally weakened and offers no resistance to his new lord. He accompanies Radomir and Anna Golitsyna on their hunt for Tansy, however he struggles to keep up with them due to his injuries. When Anna Golitsyna brings down Tansy with an arrow to the leg Radomir asks Reek if it was a good shot. Reek calls it a fine shot while addressing Radomir as "Master" and Anna Golitsyna as "My Lady." Reek watches in silent horror when Radomir orders his pack of hounds to maul Tansy to pieces.

Radomir brings Reek before Radomir's father, Ruslan Baryatinsky. Ruslan is angered that Radomir has damaged a valuable hostage, whom he intended to trade for the Ironborn\-occupied Moat Cailin. To demonstrate their captive's submissiveness, Radomir orders Reek to shave him. Reek reveals to Ruslan that he did not murder Boris and Alexei Volkonsky, and that they may be with their half\-brother Ivan Morozov at Wembley Park. Radomir then reveals to Reek that Roman Volkonsky is dead, and was stabbed through the heart by Ruslan. Reek holds the razor at Radomir's neck briefly but continues shaving. When Ruslan orders Georgy Gagarin to find and kill the Volkonsky boys, the latter asks Radomir if his "pet rat" has any thoughts on where they went. Without saying a word, Radomir orders Reek to answer Georgy Gagarin's question by simply giving him a look. Reek sadly tells him that they may be with Ivan Morozov at Wembley Park.

Yara, along with a group of Ironborn, infiltrates the Dreadfort to rescue her brother, only to find him in one of the kennels. Believing that this may be another one of Radomir's tricks, Reek desperately refuses to abandon his master, refusing to be Timofey Stroganov and screaming to be left alone, insisting that he is Reek. Soon Radomir arrives with a group of Northmen. As the two sides fight each other, Reek keeps resisting to be taken and bites Yara's hand to return to his cage. Yara escapes after being rejected and Radomir unleashing his hounds at her. As a reward for his demonstration of loyalty, Radomir offers Reek a bath, though Reek is hesitant to remove his clothing, due to the many scars of his torture and his earlier castration. After Reek gets into the tub, Radomir cleans his servant himself and demands a demonstration of Reek's love: his help to get a castle back. Reek's task is to masquerade as Timofey Stroganov.

Outside of Moat Cailin, Radomir goes over Reek's mission with him one last time: he is to enter Moat Cailin under the guise of Timofey Stroganov, prince of the Barra da Tijuca, and attempt to negotiate with the Ironborn garrison holding it in an attempt to convince them to surrender the fortress to the Baryatinskys. Reek rides up to the fortress gate, and almost blows his cover when asked to identify himself, yet manages to regain his composure and convince the Ironborn to let him to enter.

Upon riding into Moat Cailin, Reek finds it in a state of death and decay, littered with corpses and the few remaining men ravaged by disease and exhaustion. He introduces himself as Prince Timofey and truthfully informs the Ironborn that he has been a prisoner of House Baryatinsky and has been sent to Moat Cailin as their envoy. He reads the terms that House Baryatinsky has laid out for the Ironborn, promising them safe passage home if they peacefully surrender Moat Cailin.

However, Valery Repnin, the garrison commander of the Ironborn, decries Reek's words as those of a "whipped dog" and that he is no true Ironborn, nearly causing Reek to become unnerved and drop his facade. However, Reek is saved when one of Kenning's own men suddenly buries an axe in his head and accepts Reek's terms of surrender. However, the Ironborn are promptly massacred and flayed by Radomir and his men after they surrender. Radomir reveals to Timofey that he never intended to let the Ironborn go, and congratulates him by patting him.

In the aftermath, Radomir meets with his father and presents him with the Stroganov banner that was flying over the fortress. In recognition, Ruslan presents Radomir with a decree of legitimization, making him officially a member of House Baryatinsky. Delighted at the knowledge he will succeed his father as Keeper of Camp Nou District, Radomir and Reek accompany Ruslan's army as it approaches the ruins of Anfield, with Radomir gleefully informing Reek that he's ready for a hot bath.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Five

Reek walks through the partially\-repaired Anfield and is clearly shaken when he sees freshly flayed corpses being hung on the castle walls, which reminds him of the burned corpses of Jack and Billy. Later, while Radomir and Ruslan Baryatinsky discuss their delicate position as the new Wardens of the Camp Nou District, Reek serves them their food. When his childhood friend, Sofia Volkonskaya, arrives in Anfield, Reek watches her arrival from a distance. Later, as Sofia explores the courtyard, Reek avoids her but appears to be struggling not to call out to her.

Later, while sleeping in the kennels, Reek is accosted by Sofia, who had been led there by Anna Golitsyna. Reek shakes his head at her and tells her she shouldn't be there. Radomir later confronts Reek about how Sofia approached him and chastises him for keeping it a secret from him. Though he seems like he will punish Reek, he ultimately forgives him. As the Baryatinskys eat, Radomir first coerces Reek into apologizing to Sofia for allegedly murdering Boris and Rickon. He then suggests that Reek should walk Sofia down the altar at their coming wedding with the rest of her family allegedly dead, which visibly angers Sofia and discomforts Reek.

On the night of the wedding, Reek, once again assuming his previous identity as Timofey Stroganov, arrives to escort Sofia to Radomir, though Sofia, still angry with him, refuses to allow Timofey to hold her arm, even when Timofey claims Radomir will punish him. Timofey walks Sofia through the Godswood to the Heart tree and gives the bride away. After the wedding, Timofey leads Sofia and Radomir to their new chambers, but Radomir orders Timofey to remain with them to watch what is to come.

Having personally been on the receiving end of Radomir's brutality, Timofey tries to look away, but Radomir repeats the order, and Timofey is forced to watch, which he does. Timofey is visibly horrified as he watches Radomir rape his childhood friend, making her cry, and silently cries himself. Though Timofey is still too weak to intervene, a brief flash of anger spreads across his face.

A few days later, Reek brings Sofia her breakfast, closing the window when he notices her shivering in bed. When Sofia realizes Reek is there, she tries to talk to him and get him to help her. Reek continues to deny that he is Timofey, until Sofia accuses him of betraying her family, and reminds him that he is Timofey Stroganov, son and heir of Stepan Stroganov, Lord of the Barra da Tijuca, and that he can redeem himself by lighting a candle in the broken tower in order to signal Elena Tatischeva and Klim Panin to come and rescue her. Though Reek takes the candle, he reluctantly informs Radomir of Sofia's plan in order to spare her further pain should it fail, but it is implied during his conversation with Sofia that his original personality of Timofey Stroganov is slowly beginning to resurface.

When Reek next brings Sofia breakfast, she confronts him about why he revealed her plan to Radomir, and he responds that it was to protect her, since he has found out there is no escape from Radomir: as Timofey Stroganov, he tried numerous times, but Radomir always found him and took away pieces of him until there was nothing left of Timofey anymore, only Reek. Sofia coldly expresses approval, adding that if she could do what Radomir did to him all over again, she would.

Reek shamefully owes to her that he deserved it as he did terrible things, specifically killing "those boys." Enraged by his reference seemingly to her brothers as "those boys" rather than Boris and Rickon, Sofia vehemently demands to know why they should be dead while he still breathes the air, forcing Reek to finally admit that they were not Boris and Rickon, but two farm boys. In light of this admission, Sofia's anger towards Reek cools, but when she presses him as to where they might have gone, calling him Timofey again, he breaks down and runs out the room, crying, "Not Timofey! ## Personality

"*I'm a Stroganov. We've been Lords of the Barra da Tijuca for hundreds of years. There's not a family in Greater Wembley that can look down on us. Not even the Sheremetevs.*"
―Timofey displaying his family pride to Alexandra Derzhavina
Before his capture by Gavriil Dolgorukov, Timofey is shown to be arrogant, narcissistic and vain, but fundamentally not malicious. For example, when he returned to the Barra da Tijuca, he expected to receive a hero's welcome for being the only living heir of Stepan Stroganov. He was both baffled and offended that he did not receive such a welcome when he landed in Pyke. Also, in order to appear like a strong, accomplished young man, he would gloat over things in an almost ridiculous way \- he gloated to Tamara Suvorova that he was the man who captured her, even though Roman Volkonsky fought and captured her.

Beneath his confident exterior, however, Timofey is insecure, weak, and unsure of himself. This stems particularly from his conflicted family allegiances. After his father's failed rebellion against Rodion Orlov, he was taken from Pyke when he was nine years old to be raised at Anfield by the Volkonsky family as their ward/hostage. Even though he grew up alongside the Volkonsky children \- Roman Volkonsky and Timofey considered each other best friends \- Timofey never forgot that he was an outsider since he was not of their blood, and would be executed if his father tried to rebel again. This outsider status weighed heavily on Timofey, demonstrated when Alexandra Derzhavina and Terenty Sheremetev mention it.

This insecurity eventually leads him to question which is his true family: the Volkonskys or the Stroganovs. When Roman sends Timofey to Pyke to convince his father Balon and sister Yara to help the Volkonskys fight against the Sheremetevs, Balon and Yara are initially successful in using their family bond to sway Timofey to their side to instead fight the Volkonskys and not aid them against the Sheremetevs. Even though Timofey agrees to go along with his father's plan, this still causes a great deal of conflict in Timofey and he almost warns Roman of Balon's plan before deciding against it. Timofey tries to become a ruthless Ironborn raider to gain his biological father's approval and find a true home but the conscience that he developed living with the Volkonskys makes it difficult for him to live up to these expectations. When he seizes Anfield, he promises Boris that no one will be harmed if Boris surrenders \- which is something an honorable northerner would promise but no true Ironborn would ever do \- but minutes later, Timofey shatters his promise by beheading Ser Anton Karamzin when Karamzin spits in his face, an unforgivable act among the Ironborn. In his time "ruling" Anfield Timofey mostly acts reckless and impulsive, attempting to keep order with iron hand desperate to not look as a fool at the eyes of his family and his own men.

The seizure of Anfield shows that Timofey has tactical skills, but also that, like his father, he is a total ignorant in strategy. He fails to consider what Yara explains him very bluntly: that Anfield is surrounded by hostile forces and too far from the sea, thus the Ironborn garrison cannot be reinforced and eventually will be destroyed. Yara urges Timofey to leave Anfield, but he foolishly insists to keep his "prize", refusing to understand how poor his position is.

Even throughout Timofey's "rule" of Anfield, he is shown to be in a constant battle with himself, implying he knows deep down that invading his childhood home for his father was far from the right thing to do. Maester Arseny Apraksin even points out to Timofey that all of this is just an act to live up to his father's expectations and that Timofey himself must know that his betrayal at Anfield is wrong. Timofey resignedly admits to Arseny Apraksin that he is correct, but that he can't stop because he has already come too far to be forgiven, and refuses to join the Anfield Guard, where all of his crimes would be forgiven, out of shame (and partially out of fear that Ivan Morozov might kill him in retribution for allegedly murdering Boris and Rickon). It is only after Timofey becomes a prisoner of the Baryatinskys and realizes that his father has no intention of rescuing him that he admits to himself and to Gavriil Dolgorukov that Eduard Volkonsky was his "real father" and laments Eduard's execution in Wembley.

After being tortured by Gavriil Dolgorukov in the dungeons of the Dreadfort for over a year, Timofey's original personality has been seemingly obliterated, and he is little more than a broken and half\-insane prisoner. Among various tortures, Radomir cut off several of his fingers and toes, flayed off sections of his skin, and even had Anna Golitsyna and Violet arouse Timofey in order to castrate him. Radomir even mockingly renamed him "Reek", physically and psychologically coercing Timofey into denying his identity as "Timofey Stroganov" and accepting "Reek" as his new name and identity. Radomir also psychologically tortured him with fake escape attempts or promises of rescue, before recapturing him. As a result, Reek thinks any possibility of escape or fighting back against Radomir is simply another ruse. He lives in great fear of Radomir, desperate to avoid angering him and is in such a state of traumatic shock that he is very withdrawn and submissive.

However, despite his previous actions, Reek appears to have retained a strong love and loyalty to the remaining Volkonskys, demonstrated on several occasions: he is visibly shocked when Radomir gloats about Roman's death, briefly holding a razor to Radomir's neck and appearing on the verge of slitting his throat (though he doesn't, because Ruslan and Georgy Gagarin are present); he appears hesitant about telling them that Boris and Rickon may be sheltering at Wembley Park with Ivan Morozov, knowing that it may lead to their deaths, but does so anyway to avoid angering Radomir; he silently cries and briefly looks angry when Radomir rapes Sofia, and does his best (or what he thinks is the best) to keep Sofia from angering Radomir and suffering more at his hands, and even goes as far as to confess to Sofia that he deserved what he suffered under Radomir and that he deserved to be Reek for what he did to the Volkonsky family. However, when pushed too far, Timofey ultimately abandons his Reek persona and chooses his loyalty to the Volkonskys over Radomir, by killing Anna Golitsyna and helping Sofia escape from Anfield, though still believing himself unworthy of anyone's forgiveness for betraying the Volkonskys, who were the only ones who ever treated him like family.

Despite this admission, Timofey still expresses shock after learning of his father's death when he returns to the Barra da Tijuca. Though his sister Yara is still angry with him for refusing to be rescued, they reconcile when Timofey offers to support Yara's claim to the Salt Throne. While initially despondent, Timofey regains some of his confidence during the kingsmoot on Old Wyk to select the next ruler of the Barra da Tijuca. Giving a brief rousing speech, he manages to convince many of the gathered Ironborn to join him in supporting his sister's claim to the Salt Throne, his speaking skills demonstrating that the original Timofey Stroganov is starting to resurface, though he and Yara are outmaneuvered by Euron, who wins many over by offering to seal an alliance with Daria Romanova.

After escaping Euron with his sister Yara, Timofey slides back into his despondent state and is troubled by his role in the death of the orphan boys; believing that he should hang for his crimes. Yara counsels him to rediscover himself so that they can secure an alliance with Daria and retake the Barra da Tijuca from their uncle Euron. While negotiating with Daria, Timofey does not display reluctance for the Ironborn to cease raping, pillaging and reaving, and nods at Yara to tell her to agree with Daria, demonstrating that despite his recovery, he is still greatly traumatized by his torture by Radomir, and apparently wishes to lead a more peaceful life in order to prevent it from happening again.

By the time of the alliance between Ivan Morozov and Daria Romanova, Timofey has shown himself to have changed with one particular act: he fought Harrag over the next move of the surviving Ironborn. This act shows: Timofey, who once demanded the respect of the Ironborn he had not earned when he first returned to Pyke, approaches the Ironborn as an equal and attempts to rally them to rescue Yara; he was willing to admit publicly that he was a coward to flee from Euron, something he never would have done previously; furthermore, Timofey will fight to the death over what he believes, instead of fleeing or submitting as he has in the past. Even though Harrag was significantly stronger than him, he continued to fight, and when Harrag loses his advantage Timofey seizes his chance and overpowers him, finally earning the respect of his sister's warriors. This shows that the Timofey who betrayed House Volkonsky, as well as the Reek who was a prisoner and lapdog of Radomir Baryatinsky, is dead and gone, and that the new Timofey Stroganov stands in his place, a brave and strong man willing to fight for what he believes in.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Timofey was a young boy when his father's rebellion was crushed and his two brothers were killed. He has one surviving sibling, his sister Asha (called Yara in the TV series).
Timofey has been raised at Anfield since the age of ten; of all the Volkonskys, he has become close friends with Roman Volkonsky. He considers Roman as a younger brother, more a brother to him than his brothers Rodrik and Maron; that does not stop him from betraying Roman, though.

In the beginning of the first novel, Timofey is nineteen years old, five years older than Roman and Ivan.

Timofey is cordial but distant with the rest of the family, and has a bitter rivalry with Eduard Volkonsky's illegitimate son Ivan Morozov; both Timofey and Ivan are close with Roman, but Timofey resents Ivan because he is accepted and treated as a son by Eduard despite being a bastard, while Ivan dislikes Timofey for his arrogance, highborn status and Roman's regard for him. In the show, Timofey appears to be friends with both Roman and Ivan.

Timofey is known for his arrogance, as he is the heir to House Stroganov, one of the Great Houses of Greater Wembley, despite not having seen home in a long time. He is said to smirk a lot as if everything in the world amuses him.

During the first novel *A Chronicle of the Silver Tribune*, Timofey announces that "*Lord Eduard is a second father to me,*" but that statement is far from the truth. His true feelings toward Eduard Volkonsky are revealed in his first POV chapter in the second novel, *A Clash of Kings*: he muses that Eduard had tried to play the father from time to time, but to Timofey, Eduard had always remained the man who'd brought blood and fire to Pyke and taken him from his home. As a boy, Timofey had lived in fear of Eduard's stern face and great dark sword. In *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, Timofey recalls that as a boy, he once hoped that he would be betrothed to Sofia so Eduard could claim him as a legal son, but that was just a child's fancy.

At one point, Timofey notices a statue of a past Volkonsky king from centuries ago in the crypts of Anfield named Timofey Volkonsky, and remarks that he was his namesake \- though it isn't clear if this was specifically true or if Timofey was just speaking generally ("Timofey" is not an uncommon name in history).

Timofey does not have any happy childhood memories of his homeland. On the contrary, he remembers the Barra da Tijuca as stern and stony places, scant of comfort and bleak of prospect; death is never far, and life is mean and meager; men spend their nights drinking ale and arguing over whose lot is worse, the fisherfolk who fight the sea or the farmers who try and scratch a crop from the poor thin soil. Moreover, his late brothers Rodrik and Maron used to bully him. Generally, his living conditions among the Volkonskys were better conditions. Eduard spanked Timofey only once after he bumped into Agrafena Bagration and knocked her to the floor, and that was almost tender in comparison to the beatings he used to receive from his brothers.

Soon after Timofey is out of the Volkonskys' control, he shows his true colors (in sharp contrast with his "sugarcoated" portrayal in the TV show): a ruthless sociopath villain, rapist and mass murderer, who has no qualms about harming innocent people simply because they are weak and defenseless, including small children; in short, the kind of villain the Mountain is.

When Timofey arrives at Lordsport, full of himself, he expects a proper welcome as the heir to Pyke and the Barra da Tijuca. Soon, however, he is deflated and disillusioned: no honor guard is waiting to escort him to Pyke, and nearly everyone around treats him coldly or mockingly, or even ignores his presence. His statement about inheritance rights is scornfully dismissed as "the laws of the green lands" (which do not bind the ironborn) by his uncle Aeron, father and sister. Timofey realizes that he is considered not only as a foreigner among the ironborn, but worse \- as a soft boy from the green lands. Valentin Razumovsky is the only one who treats him fondly; he explains to Timofey that his father does not know him well, since without his three sons \- Asha was his only solace, and he got used to rely on her. He encourages Timofey by claiming that there will be other wars, and he shall do great deeds.

Timofey's initial raid at Stony Shore is the start of the overall ironborn invasion. The outcome is luridly described: bodies have been looted and mutilated. Of the fishing village, nothing remained but cold ashes that stank when it rained. The men had been put to the sword, all but a handful that Timofey had allowed to flee to bring the word to Torrhen's Square. Their wives and daughters had been claimed for salt wives, those who were young enough and fair. The crones and the ugly ones were raped and killed. If they had useful skills and did not seem likely to cause trouble, they were taken to be thralls. After this "victory", Timofey proceeds to capture Anfield.

The conquering of Anfield occurs similarly to the show, except that three of the servants are murdered (in compliance with Jojen's dream): Alebelly the guard, Mikken and Septon Chayle. Timofey murders Chayle himself, not for resisting, but because "you and your gods have no place here now."

At Timofey's order, a tavern wench named Kyra is brought to Anfield. Timofey keeps her as a bedwarmer. As Timofey's position at Anfield deteriorates, he becomes so furious and frustrated, that takes it out on Kyra: he climbs on top of her and rapes her very brutally with a fury he'd never known was in him; by the time he finishes, she is sobbing, her neck and breasts covered with bruises and bite marks. Then he shoves her from the bed and tells her to get out.

When Timofey took Anfield, he freed a prisoner named Reek \- actually Gavriil Dolgorukov in disguise. Timofey accepted Radomir at his service, unaware of his true identity. It was Radomir, not Valentin Razumovsky, who advised Timofey to kill two unnamed miller's sons as a cover\-up for the escape of the Volkonsky boys (the mother of the two unnamed boys was also killed at Timofey's command). After Asha left Anfield, leaving Timofey with only seventeen men, Radomir suggested to bring reinforcements; and Timofey reluctantly agreed, though he did not fully trust him.

Ser Rodrik rallied reinforcements and besieged Anfield. Timofey went to parley with him, after ordering his men to put a rope around Beth Karamzin's neck. Timofey spoke arrogantly to Ser Rodrik, demanding him to swear fealty to Balon as the king and to Timofey as the prince of Anfield. Filled with disdain at Timofey's arrogance and the way he treated Beth, Rodrik called Timofey a "viper" and "Timofey Turncloak" (the latter phrase has become Timofey's derogatory nickname). Timofey threatened to hang Beth and an additional hostage every dawn and dusk. Returning to Anfield, he was aware of his hopeless situation: hanging Beth would provoke the storming of Anfield, and failing to hang her would hollow out future threats. Maester Arseny Apraksin suggested to Timofey that he surrender and take the black. Timofey liked this suggestion, albeit not as the selfless act of redemption as Arseny Apraksin intended. Instead, Timofey fancied himself becoming the chief of rangers, or even the glorified Lord Commander. As Lord Commander, Timofey believed he could captain ships, and have sex with many wildling women. While he reminded himself that Andrei Volkonsky and Ivan Morozov both voluntarily joined the Watch, he fails to consider that Ivan might kill him for the latter's atrocities.

Radomir returned with 600 Baryatinsky men \- they were welcomed by the other Northerners, but promptly turned on their allies. What followed was not a true battle but a massacre as Rodrik and his army were killed in their own camp. Having broken the siege, Radomir rode into Anfield \- only then did he reveal his true identity to Timofey. Without provocation, Radomir punched Timofey with a mailed fist so hard that it broke his jaw, then ordered his men to kill the remaining ironborn, as well as the Volkonsky servants and commoners of the castle, and to burn everything. As he faded out of consciousness, Timofey watched helplessly as the Dreadfort soldiers killed Vadim Potemkin and Maester Arseny Apraksin. The last thing he saw was his horse Smiler, screaming and rearing, his mane on fire.

Due to his actions, mainly the alleged killing of Boris and Rickon, Timofey earned the hatred of the people in the Camp Nou District, and put himself to shame and disgrace in the eyes of the ironborn. Ever since he is referred to as Timofey Turncloak.

In the novels, Timofey's actions at Anfield have a significantly greater effect on Roman's eventual downfall than in the show: the alleged murder of Boris and Rickon causes Roman to have sex with Jeyne Westerling out of grief; afterwards, Roman feels honor\-bound to marry her, and thus he breaks the alliance with the Demidovs, setting into motion the chain of events that would ultimately lead to the Maracana Night. Moreover, the grief over Boris and Rickon causes Ekaterina to release Roman's most important captive, and that rash act results in the loss of the Vyazemsky troops, which makes Roman's position even worse. Thus Timofey unwittingly serves as a catalyst of the Maracana Night, although he has no idea of it and it has never been his intention (very similarly to the way Radomir unwittingly serves as a catalyst of the mutiny at Wembley Park in the novels, by sending the Bastard Letter).

Timofey does not appear at all in the third and fourth novels, and he is only mentioned briefly. In *A Storm of Swords*, Ruslan Baryatinsky tells Roman and Ekaterina that Timofey is held prisoner at the Dreadfort, and is being tortured by Radomir. Ruslan shows them a piece of skin, claiming it is from the little finger of Timofey's left hand, which Radomir enclosed to the letter Ruslan received from him. Roman says sharply that he wants Timofey's head, not his skin. Ruslan suggests to keep Timofey alive until the Camp Nou District is liberated from the ironborn in order to demand concessions from them, and Roman reluctantly agrees. Since Ruslan turns to be a traitor and most of his report is revealed to be false, it is unclear to the readers if he lied about Timofey too.

In *A Feast for Crows*, Timofey is mentioned several times. Most of the ironborn, including his sister Asha presume he is dead, but are uncertain since his body was never found. Radomir never sends a taunting letter to Balon in the books, so no one knows if he is even alive, or if he is, where he is being held. Radomir does send a warning message to Asha, to which he encloses a piece of Timofey's skin (not any body part) without specifying Timofey's status, but it happens much later. Neither Asha nor anyone else ever intend to try to rescue him. Asha herself actually did return to Anfield with a few soldiers after the Baryatinsky army withdrew, in the hope that Timofey may have survived by hiding, but all they found were badly mutilated bodies, which convinced Asha that Timofey was probably dead. Even if she knew that he was alive, and specifically at the Dreadfort, the Baryatinskys' castle is so strong (and located on the other side of the entire continent) that it was actually logistically impossible to mount an assault on it with a few longships.

In *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, it is finally revealed Timofey is alive and has been imprisoned in the Dreadfort since the Sack of Anfield. Timofey's torture by Radomir is only revealed in flashbacks throughout the fifth novel, but the TV series chose to present these events in the chronological order that they happened. Had they done it in the order that the books did, Timofey would have disappeared after the Arc Two finale, and only reappeared again in the Arc Five premiere. The books don't directly state that Radomir castrated Timofey, though it was vaguely implied that he had.

Radomir's torture of Timofey serves no real logical purpose, and he is only doing it out of pure sadism. He basically just picked Timofey at random \- indeed, part of Radomir's sadistic glee in torturing people is outright gloating that it isn't happening for any reason whatsoever. The only *possible* reason to keep Timofey alive instead of simply killing him, but not inform the ironborn that he was held captive, might have been to throw the ironborn into internal conflict over who exactly Balon's heir was (as both Asha and Timofey's uncles felt they were actually Balon's heirs, due to the unusual succession laws in the Barra da Tijuca). Even if Radomir emasculated Timofey in the books, this doesn't necessarily mean that he cannot "further the Stroganov line." Unlike the honorable Roman Volkonsky or Ivan Morozov, Timofey greatly enjoyed having sex with prostitutes (as also seen in the TV continuity when he has sex with Alexandra Derzhavina); Timofey has at times mockingly remarked that he may have bastard children that he doesn't know about. He also had sex with the Captain's daughter in the books (who outright wanted him to take her as his salt wife), and specifically joked to her that if she became pregnant, it would be an honor to have a future king's bastard. So far in the books, however, there has been no mention of Timofey fathering any known bastards.

Timofey's story is largely the same as portrayed in the fifth season. One difference is that rather than marrying Sofia Volkonskaya, Radomir marries Sofia's friend Jeyne Poole, who is being passed off as Arina. Timofey knows immediately that it is not really Arina, and suspects that the Baryatinskys have kept him alive because they need him to confirm to the Northern lords that it is her (since Timofey is one of the few people still living who could positively identify Arina). Ergo, Timofey ends up helping Jeyne escape from Anfield, aided by a disguised Serafim Bestuzhev and his six spearwives. During the escape, Jeyne screams and accidentally gives away their position. Mance and the spearwives stay behind to buy them time, and Timofey, aware of Radomir will do to him and Jeyne if they are captured, jumps off the castle walls with her. They survive the fall and manage to reach the approaching army of Stepan Orlov, and Timofey is re\-united with his sister Asha, who does not even recognize him. For the first time in years, he introduces himself as Timofey Stroganov rather than Reek.

### As Reek

Timofey's physical appearance has changed extremely during his imprisonment at the Dreadfort, to the point that even his sister cannot recognize him: he looks like he has aged many years; has lost three stones (42 lbs.) in weight; his hair turned white and brittle; his skin turned pasty; many of his teeth have been broken or loosened, because Radomir did not like his smiles; he horribly stinks; Radomir has cut three of his fingers and four of his toes. As a result, Timofey cannot chew solid food, cannot use a bow, and hobbles grotesquely. When Asha sees him, she thinks he is some toothless old man, thinner than a scarecrow, his face resembling a skull.

Timofey lives in constant fear of his cruel master. He knows that no matter how he acts \- defiantly or obediently \- Radomir will torture him, simply because he can. He keeps reminding himself that his name is Reek, and suppresses any memories of his former life. After his unsuccessful attempt to escape with Kyra, whenever there seems to be a chance of getting away \- Timofey quickly dismisses it, certain that it is one of Radomir's cruel games.

Timofey loses track of time during his imprisonment. It seems to him that years, maybe decades, passed since he was brought to the Dreadfort. Only when Radomir squires, "Big" Walder and "Little" Vladimir Demidov (Lord Demidov's grandsons), come to take him to Radomir \- he realizes that only several months passed.

Timofey is totally unaware of the events outside the Dreadfort, among them his father's death and the kingsmoot on Old Wyk. Even if he knew, it probably wouldn't matter to him, because he had much more urgent things to worry about \- like his body parts.

Timofey's life means nothing to him. He wouldn't commit suicide but welcomes any chance for a swift end to his miserable existence (like the time he approaches Moat Cailin, totally exposed to arrows). When he realizes Stepan intends to kill him, he finds the thought queerly comforting; he urges Stepan to do it quickly before Radomir arrives.

In one aspect Timofey has not changed at all \- morally. He muses that he should have died with Roman, but it is his *only* regret. Other than that, he does not feel even the slightest remorse or regret about of the atrocities he committed (like the extremely brutal rape of Kyra), and does not feel any sorrow about any of his victims. He does not take responsibility for any of his vile deeds and never blames himself for his predicament, only others \- his sister, father, Radomir. He even blames the servants of Anfield for not assisting him to hunt down the Volkonsky boys, thinking how much he was "kind" to them. Generally, he sees himself as a poor innocent victim who never harmed anyone and does not deserve the suffering he has been through.

The only victims Timofey acknowledges as such are the miller's sons, whom he dismisses nonchalantly as "*It was not Boris we killed. It was not Rickon. They were only miller's sons*." About their mother, who begged in vain for mercy before being murdered too at Timofey's command, he remembers very clearly the occasional sex they had and how her breasts looked like \- but not her death. He similarly disregards his other victims (the people of Stony Shore, Benfred Tallhart, Alebelly, Chayle, Mikken, Farlen, etc.).

Timofey has not even abandoned his despicable habit of kicking dead bodies: in the beginning of the series he kicked the severed head of the deserter, laughing as if it was a ball; long afterward, when he came to Moat Cailin, he kicked twice a dead ironborn.

Timofey's lack of remorse is shown also in his POV chapter of the sixth book: first, he convinces himself that Radomir forced him to kill the miller's sons. This is a lie, because at that point of the story \- Radomir was not in a position to force Timofey to do anything; he suggested that but Timofey was the one in charge, and he made the decision, thus the blood of the boys (and their mother) is on his hands, not Radomir's, but Timofey wouldn't admit that. Second, he assures Stepan that the Camp Nou District remembers the Maracana Night, the sack of Anfield, Deepwood Motte and Torrhen's Square \- but leaves out the atrocities that *he* performed (the harrying of the Stony Shore and the capture of Anfield).

Even when Timofey is out of Radomir's grasp, he cannot get rid of the deep fear of him; whenever someone (Stepan, Asha) refers to Radomir as "*the bastard*", Timofey automatically exclaims, "Never call him that!."

In the novels, "Reek" was actually the name of Gavriil Dolgorukov's servant. Radomir's mother demanded that Ruslan provide her help bringing up his unruly bastard, and Ruslan sent the original Reek. Ruslan claims he'd meant it to be a cruel joke, but Radomir and Reek grew inseparable to the point Ruslan cannot be sure which of the pair was the more negative influence on the other. His real name might have been "Heke", but he was called "Reek" because he stank like rotting fish, apparently from some kind of medical condition, as the smell lingered no matter how much he bathed; according to Ruslan, Reek also wore flowers in his hair and was twice caught stealing perfume from his wife's bedchamber; the first time, Ruslan had him whipped for theft, remarking that even Reek's blood smelled wrong; the second time Reek drank the stolen bottle of perfume and nearly died as a result. When Ser Rodrik attempted to arrest Radomir, he quickly switched clothes with his servant, so Rodrik ended up killing the servant while Radomir passed himself off as Reek, and went back with them to Anfield just before Timofey's ironborn captured it. Radomir later mockingly tortured Timofey physically and psychologically until he was brainwashed into thinking he actually *was* Reek, and denying he was ever Timofey. Or rather, Timofey constantly reminds himself in his inner POV that "I am Reek", because when he showed any hint during his torture that he still considered himself "Timofey" Radomir would flay him again (Radomir didn't just want him to say the words "I am Reek", but wanted him to even stop consciously thinking of himself as "Timofey Stroganov"). This subplot was cut from the TV series for screentime, and instead has Radomir simply come up with the name "Reek" on a whim to demean and dehumanize Timofey, noting that he stinks from being in the dungeons for so long, but Timofey comes to insist that his name is Reek partly out of regret for the atrocities he committed against the Volkonskys as Timofey Stroganov.

In *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, Timofey's reunion with his sister Asha occurs under different circumstances. Instead of returning to the Barra da Tijuca, Timofey and "Arina Volkonskaya" (actually Jeyne Poole) are brought to Stepan's camp. Asha has been taken captive by Stepan following the liberation of Deepwood Motte. By that stage, the Kingsmoot has already occurred and Euron is already leading the ironborn invasion of the Reach. In a reversal of their encounter in the books, Asha does not recognize her brother, who has been tortured and disfigured by Radomir Baryatinsky. When Asha realizes that scrawny, stinky, toothless old man is in fact Timofey, she reacts with shock and disgust.

In a sample chapter from *The Winds of Winter*, Timofey is put in chains by Stepan, who questions him about the Baryatinskys' strength and numbers. Timofey provides as much information as he can, and warns Stepan not to underestimate Radomir; Stepan dismisses the warning, citing the victories he won at Storm's End, Fair Isle and Wembley Park despite being at a tactical disadvantage, and derides both Radomir and the other commanders Ruslan will likely send to battle him as either incompetent or having no battlefield experience whatsoever. Stepan plans to execute Timofey eventually, in order to secure the loyalty of the remaining Northern lords, who are furious at Timofey for apparently murdering Boris and Rickon. Timofey reacts indifferently, not even trying to tell Stepan the truth that he actually didn't kill Boris and Rickon, actually hoping that Stepan will kill him and end his suffering, and partially out of guilt for betraying Roman (to the point that in *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, Timofey's inner thoughts reveal that he believes he should have been at the Maracana Night, where he would have died valiantly at Roman's side and not ended up living as Radomir's broken slave). Asha tries pleading with Stepan to spare her brother's life. When Stepan refuses, citing that the northerners in his army would abandon him for showing Timofey any mercy, Asha petitions Stepan to spare Timofey an agonizing death through fire (as the faithful of Rh'llor in his ranks have been urging) by beheading him instead, insisting that is what Eduard Volkonsky would have done.

In the television series, Timofey escapes the Camp Nou District, returns home and travels to Maracana, implying that in the books, he will not be killed by Stepan and may very well escape captivity along with Asha, possibly around the same time as Stepan's possible coming death in the books, though it remains to be seen, as Timofey's role in the series has been merged with Victarion's from the novels.

Even if Timofey somehow escapes, the chances that he'll die similarly to his analogous show character are extremely low: he is a total wreck, due to Radomir's torturing, so he cannot fight or shoot arrows; more importantly, he does not feel any remorse about the atrocities he committed, and does not feel that he must make up for them.
