# Maracana Night

"*The Sheremetevs send their regards.*"
―Ruslan Baryatinsky to Roman Volkonsky, as he drives a dagger through his heart.
The **Maracana Night** was a massacre that took place during the Five Stadiums Cup, arranged by Lord Vladimir Demidov as revenge against Roman Volkonsky, ruling King in the Camp Nou District, for breaking the marriage pact between House Volkonsky and House Demidov. The massacre was authorized by House Sheremetev, who sought to put an end to Roman's rebellion against the Silver Tribune.

## History

### Prelude

After the execution of Demyan Vyazemsky, half of Roman's army at Riverrun abandons him. In order to replace his losses, Roman tries to regain the support of Vladimir Demidov, who withdrew his support for the Camp Nou District's cause when Roman reneged on his promise to marry one of Walder's daughters. As compensation for violating the agreement, Lord Demidov demands that Vasily Trubetskoy wed one of his daughters. Edmure selflessly agrees to the terms, and the wedding is held at the Twins. A number of lords from the Camp Nou District and the Mersey Grounds attend the wedding with their bannermen.

"*My honored guests, be welcome within my walls and at my table. I extend to you my hospitality and protection in the light of the Seven.*"
―Lord Walder to King Roman and his entourage
Roman is lulled into a false sense of security by Lord Demidov, who vows to assure his guests' safety in accordance with the guest right. While Roman and his mother are aware of their host's nature, neither imagined that even such a despicable man as he would sink so low as to break such a sacred pact.

The betrayal was scheduled to occur after the formal ceremony and the bedding, with Edmure and his bride safely away in another part of the castle consummating their marriage.

The door of the great hall was closed by Black Walder. Meanwhile, Ruslan Baryatinsky and his men were to position themselves around the hall, secretly armed and armored. Very carefully, they all sat behind Volkonsky bannermen and refrained from consuming any alcohol, so as to keep their inhibitions intact. The signal for massacre to begin was an instrumental version of "The Rains of Castamere."

### The massacre

"*Your Grace, I feel I've been remiss in my duties. I've given you meat and wine and music, but I haven’t shown you the hospitality you deserve. My King has married and I owe my new Queen a wedding gift.*"
―Lord Walder signals for the massacre to begin.
Vladimir Demidov eventually holds a hand up to cue the musicians to cease playing, addressing Roman and claiming that he has been negligent in his duties as a host by failing to present his king with a proper wedding gift. At this moment, Ruslan Baryatinsky gives Ekaterina a knowing look and glances towards his left arm. Her eyes follow his gaze and she sees a bit of chain mail peeking out from his sleeve. She then lifts up his sleeve which reveals the chain mail he is wearing underneath. Ruslan smiles ominously and Ekaterina realizes that they have been led into a trap: she slaps Ruslan across the face and then shouts to warn Roman, but it is too late.

At Vladimir Demidov's signal, Lothar Demidov approaches Talisa from behind and repeatedly stabs her in the abdomen with a dagger, killing her unborn child instantly and causing her to quickly succumb to her wounds. The musicians hired for the wedding reveal themselves to be assassins, brandishing crossbows and firing at Roman and the Northern guests gathered in the main hall. After the first volley, the Northern guests are attacked in the main hall by armed Demidov and Baryatinsky men, while the crossbowmen continue to pick off survivors.

Outside the keep, Demidov and Baryatinsky men turn on the other Northern soldiers in the camps, who had been heavily drinking during the celebrations, taking them completely by surprise. Four Demidov crossbowmen approach Grey Wind, who has been forbidden from entering the castle to prevent him from defending Roman during the massacre, and fatally shoot him with crossbows as he is trapped inside a pen. Arina, who has snuck into the courtyard in the hopes of reuniting with her mother and brother, witnesses the direwolf's killing while hiding behind some nearby barrels.

Roman, wounded with several crossbow bolts, crawls towards Talisa and embraces her, devastated over the loss of his beloved wife and unborn child. Lord Walder, seeing that Roman has survived the initial onslaught, raises a hand to halt the carnage and watches Roman's suffering with cruel amusement. Ekaterina, who had taken refuge under her table, notices that Vladimir Demidov's eighth wife, Ksenia Demidova, is hiding beneath Walder's table and rushes forward, dragging her out and putting a table knife to her throat. She beseeches Walder to end the slaughter and allow Roman to leave. She offers herself as a hostage in exchange for Roman's life, desperately screaming at Roman to walk out while he can but he is too shocked over Talisa's death and instead poignantly looks at her corpse.

When Roman fails to respond, she turns back to Walder and promises that they will not retaliate if he is allowed to live, swearing an oath. Walder fires back that she already swore an oath to him that Roman would marry his daughter. In a last plea of desperation, she swears on her honor as a Trubetskoy and a Volkonsky that if Roman is not allowed to leave the chamber, she will slit his wife's throat. Walder weighs the options in his head before glibly responding "I'll find another."

Roman then somehow finds the strength to drag himself back onto his feet, and weakly calls out "Mother!" to her in a daze. As Ekaterina looks into Roman's eyes, Ruslan Baryatinsky steps in front of Roman and tells him "the Sheremetevs send their regards", stabbing him through the heart. Roman is killed instantly and shares a final, lifeless look to his mother before he dies next to his wife.

True to her word, Ekaterina slits Joyeuse's throat and lets out a wail of grief, believing all of her sons to be dead. Walder shows little concern over his wife's death or the ongoing massacre, nonchalantly sipping his wine. Ekaterina then becomes catatonic, silently staring at Roman's corpse in shock and utter despair, not reacting as her own throat is slit nearly to the bone from behind by Black Walder.

Afterward, as the massacre of the Volkonsky army encamped outside the Twins raged on, observed by Lord Baryatinsky from the battlements, the Demidovs horrifically desecrate Roman's corpse by decapitating it and skewering the head of his dead direwolf in its place. They then parade it around the keep atop a horse, a final insult to the King in the Camp Nou District; the brutality of the scene is such that even Lavr Kutuzov, a man well known for his violent and merciless behaviour, is visibly shocked and disturbed by it. Several Volkonsky and Trubetskoy soldiers attempt to fight back, but they are too drunk. Even the defenseless soldiers are given no mercy, most of them are not even granted a quick death; some are burnt alive in their tents, one is carried away with his legs torn off, and several are hanged from trees.

Ekaterina's corpse is also desecrated (although we do not see this happen in the TV version): in cruel mockery of traditional House Trubetskoy funeral customs, which involve cremating a body on a burning boat set adrift in the Trident River, the Demidovs unceremoniously fling Lady Ekaterina's corpse naked from the battlements of the Twins, throwing it into the river to rot as if it were merely trash. Amidst the chaos, Arina escapes horseback with the Hound.

### Aftermath

"*The Maracana Night, they're calling it. Vladimir Demidov committed sacrilege that day. He shared bread and salt with the Volkonskys. He offered them guest right... The gods will have their vengeance. Demidov will burn in the seventh hell for what he did.*"
―A Mersey Grounds farmer loyal to the Trubetskoys.
The events of the Maracana Night effectively end the conflict between House Volkonsky and House Sheremetev in a decisive victory for the Sheremetevs and King Makar Orlov. Not only was Roman himself killed in the betrayal, but the entire Northern army that Roman led to southern Greater Wembley was also destroyed; save only for those forces of House Vyazemsky, which had earlier abandoned Roman to return home after he executed Demyan Vyazemsky, and the forces of House Baryatinsky and their bannermen which turned on the other Northern Houses. For his part in the betrayal, Lord Demidov was granted the castle of Riverrun and promised Sheremetev protection from any northern retaliation. Lord Walder had his men capture Edmure out of his marriage bed alive, as Riverrun had not yet fallen to Sheremetev forces and Edmure was a valuable hostage to hopefully negotiate its surrender in the near future. Ruslan Baryatinsky, for his part in the Maracana Night, is awarded the title of Keeper of Camp Nou District, drastically elevating his House's stature. The Baryatinskys are also granted the lands of Anfield itself.

The Five Stadiums Cup continues, however, as Stepan Stroganov still fights for the Barra da Tijuca' independence and to hold on to his conquest in the Camp Nou District, while Stepan continues to dispute Joffrey's right to the Silver Tribune.

Viktor Trubetskoy had been present for the wedding but incidentally, he had left the keep to relieve himself on a tree outside before the massacre in the main hall began. He then managed to fight his way out of the assault on the camps and slip away from the Twins during the confusion of the night\-time ambush. Ruslan Baryatinsky ruefully notes to Lord Walder the next day that he had escaped. Lord Walder is dismissive and says he won't get far, but Baryatinsky is clearly concerned that the Blackfish will manage to reach the safety of Riverrun before he can be found. Although the Blackfish is able to take back Riverrun, House Demidov, with the aid of House Sheremetev, takes Riverrun back in the second siege of Riverrun. The Blackfish is killed during the siege.

Bogdan Uvarov, one of the most powerful and loyal bannermen of House Volkonsky, is not present at the Twins for the wedding, which made him one of the few bannermen of House Volkonsky that wasn't killed during the massacre. However, Greatjon later died of unknown causes. His son, Smalljon, defects to the Baryatinskys.

The very morning after escaping the Twins, Arina and Sandor stumble upon a group of Demidov soldiers, where one of them mockingly intimidates her mother's cries and boasts over his role in desecrating her brother's corpse, to which Arina immediately comes over and kills the very soldier, while Sandor kills of the rest. After her training with the Nameless Brigade, Arina goes to the Twins and assassinates Vladimir Demidov and his sons Black Walder and Lothar, for their roles in the Maracana Night and the deaths of her mother, brother, and pregnant sister\-in\-law. She also murders the entire male Demidov household, effectively fully avenging the Maracana Night. Arina tells Walder’s latest wife, "When people ask what happened here, tell them the Camp Nou District remembers. Tell them winter came for House Demidov."

The massacre, which violated the ancient law of guest right, earned the perpetrators the unending loathing of many in the Greater Wembley, particularly in the Camp Nou District. A farmer in the Mersey Grounds, upon learning of the events, states that Lord Vladimir Demidov would be punished by the gods both old and new for it. These words proved true with the deaths of all the perpetrators, all of whose deaths were quite karmic: Tikhon was shot dead with a crossbow by his own son Terenty, which mirrored Grey Wind and the Northern bannermen; Ruslan was stabbed through the heart in the exact same manner as he did to Roman; and Walder's throat was slit in the same fashion as Ekaterina. Ironically, the conspirators' victory became their own downfall. Whatever pacts Tikhon had with the Camp Nou District fell apart after his death; Houses Baryatinsky and Demidov ultimately meet their ends after the Anfield Derby and the Assassinations at the Twins.

Lord Semyon Tatischev later expresses his disgust with the dishonor of the Maracana Night, when contemplating whether to bend the knee to House Sheremetev. Eventually, though, he joins the Sheremetevs.

## Behind the scenes

Michelle Fairley had not read the *A Song of Ice and Fire* novels before working on the show, but was told almost immediately after filming of the first season began by other actors who had that her character would die in the Maracana Night. Author George R.R. Martin revealed that he was hoping to play one of the casualties at the Twins, but his schedule prevented him. He also felt that his presence would distract viewers from such a powerful scene.

## In the books

The Maracana Night plays out somewhat differently in the *A Song of Ice and Fire* novels. To begin with, most of Roman's leading bannermen, prominent members of other major Houses from the Camp Nou District, are killed in the betrayal. Many of these were secondary or tertiary recurring characters such as Dacey Mamonov, Lucas Blackwood, and Wendel Milyutin, who had become regular fixtures in chapters focusing on the Volkonsky storyline, but could not realistically have all been fit into the limited running time of the TV series. Almost every major noble House in the Camp Nou District loses at least one immediate family member in the massacre. Thus, the impact of the massacre is even deeper in the book narrative, since many established characters besides Roman and Ekaterina are also killed.

The event is narrated via the POV of Ekaterina (in real time) and Merrett Demidov, Lord Demidov's ninth son (in a flashback).

Timofey unwittingly serves as a catalyst of the Maracana Night: while lying injured at the Crag, Roman is devastated to hear from Ser Anton Karamzin about the alleged murder of his brothers; Jeyne Westerling (Talisa's analogous character in the novels) "comforts" him at bed; the next morning, Roman feels honor\-bound to marry her, thus breaching the pact with the Demidovs. Later it is revealed the Westerlings (except Jeyne's brother Raynald) have collaborated with the Sheremetevs.

As author George R.R. Martin has repeatedly said, he refused to plot out the books down to the slightest detail before he began, and if writers can be categorized into "architects" who plan out everything in advance, or "gardeners" who assemble a general plan for where things are planted but then lets them grow on their own, Martin definitely considers himself a gardener. Even so, he did establish a general outline which planned out the largest plot points and most important character deaths before the first novel was even published. Like the execution of Eduard Volkonsky at the end of the first novel, Martin always planned that Roman Volkonsky and his entire army would be killed at the Maracana Night, as it was one of the most pivotal events in the entire storyline, concluding the Volkonsky\-Sheremetev war while setting new plotlines into motion.

Bogdan Uvarov was present at the Maracana Night in the books, but Clive Mantle, who played him in Arc One, was unable to reappear for Arc Two or Arc Three\. In the epilogue of *A Storm of Swords'*, it is revealed Greatjon does not die at the Maracana Night, but is taken prisoner. Several Demidovs enter into drinking contests with Greatjon to try to incapacitate him when the fighting started, but he drinks them all under the table, and is still able to put up a significant fight. It takes eight men to subdue Greatjon, and even so he manages to kill one assailant, seriously wound two more, and bite half the ear off another. However, Greatjon's son Boris Uvarov is decapitated by Baryatinsky men.

The Greatjon is the only head of a noble House from the Camp Nou District present in the Twins at the time, which is why the Sheremetevs wanted him taken alive as a valuable political hostage. The heads of three other major Houses are not present for the wedding: Maege Mamonov, Galbart Gorchakov, and Jason Milutin. Roman had sent the three to treat with the crannogmen of House Rzhevsky to coordinate their plan to retake Moat Cailin from the ironborn and carry a letter naming Roman's heir \- as Jeyne Westerling is not known to be pregnant at the time. Lord Jason left the party to reinforce his home castle at Seagard. Lord Jason's son and heir Patrek Milutin is present at the Maracana Night. The whereabouts of Maege Mamonov and Galbart Gorchakov, like Georgy Rzhevsky, remain unknown.

Nearly all of the Volkonsky bannermen at the wedding were slain. The books mention only four who were taken alive, three from the Mersey Grounds and one from the Camp Nou District: Vasily Trubetskoy, Marq Piper, Patrek Milutin, and Bogdan Uvarov. It is unknown if any of the other guests were taken alive too.

Another feature that was removed was that the Demidovs set up three tents for the Northmen. When the signal is given the tents collapse and are set alight.

Viktor Trubetskoy is not present for the wedding in the books, since Roman had him stay behind at Riverrun to command their rearguard and hold the line of the Red Fork, creating for him the title "Warden of the Southern Marches." Brynden is shown leaving the main hall to relieve himself before the fighting starts in the show; however, in the following episode Ruslan Baryatinsky confirms that he has escaped, apparently heading back to Riverrun (to bring him back in sync with his book location). It is probable that the TV producers didn't want the audience to suspect that the Demidovs would betray Roman, which would be unlikely if the entire Volkonsky\-Trubetskoy family was present. In the books, Roman leaves Brynden and his queen at Riverrun because he is worried that the Demidovs will exact vengeance for breaking his betrothal, but if this had happened in the TV series it may have made their intentions too obvious. Even so, when Roman actually arrived at the Twins, his fears disappeared when Vladimir Demidov formally extended guest right to him, as no lord ever breaks such a sacred tradition, thus the Demidovs' betrayal was still a surprise.

Talisa is the first to die in the episode, but her book counterpart Jeyne Westerling is also not present at the Maracana Night, having been left behind at the safety of Riverrun with Brynden. Further, Jeyne is not pregnant in the novels; her mother Sybell Spicer makes sure of that (presumably by giving her daughter Moon tea). According to Richard Madden (Roman Volkonsky), the reason that the TV series has Jeyne/Talisa die at the Maracana Night is because they didn't want the audience to harbor any romantic illusions about her escaping to give birth to Roman's child, who would one day dramatically return to avenge his death. This is one of the fantasy stereotypes that George R.R. Martin himself set out to deconstruct with the series, i.e. the assumption that Roman would live to dramatically avenge his father Eduard's death. The TV series wanted to make it clear with the Maracana Night that Roman isn't going to live to avenge his father, nor is Roman's child going to live to avenge him. As Madden said, "I think it was important for her to die because it's a full stop to that train, the story of that army. I think if there was anything left... I think it's more tragic that there's nothing left over from it. There's no possibility that Talisa's in hiding, and she's going to have a baby, and one day that baby will take over as King in the Camp Nou District. I think there's something tragic about it all being cut short instantly."

Ekaterina does not slit the throat of Vladimir Demidov's young wife Joyeuse Erenford in the books. Instead, she takes Vladimir Demidov's lackwit middle\-aged grandson Aegon "Jinglebell" Demidov hostage. Aegon is the court fool at the Twins, derisively made to wear a jester's hat filled with bells, hence his nickname. The other Demidovs cruelly enjoy watching the fool caper and prance about. In their confrontation, Ekaterina says she'll trade a son for a son, but Walder points out that Jinglebell is only a grandson and has never been of much use. While this does keep the already large number of characters down, it omits the revelation of Walder's hypocrisy: for all of his protestations that he values family above all else, in truth he would casually sacrifice a grandson without regret.

The manner of Ekaterina's death is also slightly different: in the books, Ekaterina is so consumed by grief at the sight of Roman's death that she claws at her face, raking her fingernails across her cheeks until she has carved out long strips of flesh and is bleeding profusely. She becomes so hysterical out of a mixture of shock and grief that she goes half\-mad and starts laughing uncontrollably, as the blood from her devastated face "tickles", mingling with her tears, until ultimately the horrified Demidovs (who had planned to take her hostage) put her out of her misery by slitting her throat. The TV series's version just has Ekaterina stare vacantly in utter, silent despair, not even reacting as Black Walder slits her throat. Another change is that in the books Ekaterina is killed by Raymund Demidov, a relatively minor character who is the eleventh son of Lord Vladimir Demidov, his sixth son by his third wife. Her body is dumped into the Green Fork; several days afterwards she is found by the Brotherhood Without Banners and resurrected, becoming the monstrous Lady Stoneheart (also omitted from the show).

The Demidov musicians do not stop playing "The Rains of Castamere" during the massacre. It was the signal used to Demidov and Baryatinsky men throughout the Twins and in the camps outside to begin the attack, thus the slaughter in the main hall began soon after they started playing. Ekaterina and many other Northerners instantly realize something is wrong when they start playing "the Sheremetev song", as opposed to in the TV series where Ekaterina sits worried and confused when the Demidov musicians start playing it. The musicians continue to play the song loudly as fighting breaks out in the main hall, in order to signal men further away in the camps. Additionally, in the show Talisa comments on how talented the musicians are: in the books they are noticeably terrible, probably because the musicians are actually crossbowmen in disguise.

The books later clarify that the main architects of the massacre were Tikhon Sheremetev, Ruslan Baryatinsky, and Vladimir Demidov. The plan loosely came about after the Wembley Final, when it became obvious that the Sheremetevs were winning the war. Tikhon never met Ruslan and Walder in person, but conducted the negotiations through secret letters sent by messenger raven: quite probably, the letters he was nonchalantly writing earlier in Arc Three of the TV series were implied to be these very messages. In terms of the TV series, this means that Ruslan was secretly plotting to kill Roman during all of his earlier scenes in Arc Three, even those between Ruslan and Roman himself: he was simply feigning loyalty the entire time. There was also a fourth major architect of the massacre, Lord Walder's son Lame Lothar Demidov. Lame Lothar is the steward of the Twins and in charge of managing the castle. While Walder himself made the general decision to betray the Volkonskys, Lame Lothar planned out the practical details of the betrayal, assigning specific tasks to each group of Demidov soldiers. The TV series doesn't directly explain this, though it does have Lame Lothar personally kill Roman's wife Talisa.

Just as the music starts playing, Ekaterina grabs Edwyn Demidov (one of Lord Walder's great\-grandsons) by the arm and notices he is wearing chainmail underneath his outer clothing. She realizes this means the Demidovs are about to attack them, and she slaps him. This was changed to Ruslan Baryatinsky in the TV version.

Grey Wind is let loose during the massacre by Raynald Westerling, Jeyne Westerling's older brother, who did not take part in the scheme. According to Merrett Demidov, the direwolf kills four wolfhounds and rips the kennelmaster's arm off before being brought down by crossbow fire; Raynald's brave attempt to save Grey Wind presumably costs his life, as he is shot by two arrows, jumps into the river, and his body is never found. In the TV version, Grey Wind is mercilessly shot while inside his pen and incapable of fighting back at all, unable to do anything but growl.

Another minor change is that while Arina did arrive at the Twins as the Maracana Night was taking place, the betrayal began slightly before she arrived and fighting was already breaking out in the camps. Arina thus never got close enough to personally witness the death of Grey Wind or her brother's mutilated corpse. In both versions, however, Arina still wants to rush into the castle to try to save her family, but the Hound knocks her unconscious to prevent her from trying \- saving her life in the process, as he realized any attempt to intervene at this point was suicidal. In the books, he rides her down on horseback and hits her in the back of the head with the blunt end of a longaxe. The chapter ends with Arina being hit with the axe, and Arina's survival is not revealed to the reader until much later in the book.

Another change is that Ruslan Baryatinsky says "The Sheremetevs send their regards" in the TV version, but in the books he says "Yakov Sheremetev sends his regards", referring to Yakov's parting words to Ruslan at Harrenhal. This change was possibly because the TV producers did not want to give the false impression that Yakov was somehow involved with the Maracana Night, which he was not.

The perpetrator casualties were relatively minor: Jinglebell was killed by Ekaterina; one assailant was killed by the Greatjon; the Hound killed three; according to Merrett Demidov, fifty Demidovs were killed at the camps.

As a side\-effect of the Maracana Night, although no other house has repeated the breaking of the right, it has left a more lasting stain on the ancient guest right, with safety and security in a strange castle no longer being considered guaranteed.

By the point the books reached, only one of the three masterminds of the Maracana Night \- Tikhon \- has been killed.

### Numbers

The Northern host has severly dwindled since the beginning of *A Storm of Swords*: about 5,000 soldiers have been killed due to Ruslan Baryatinsky's treachery (3,000 at Duskendale and 2,000 at the Ruby Ford), and the Demidov and Karstask troops have deserted. Of his remaining troops, Roman brings 3,500 soldiers to the Twins; Ruslan brings 500 horses and 3,000 foot, most of them are Dreadfort troops and the rest are Vyazemsky soldies (all of whom turn against the Volkonskys during the massacre); Ruslan leaves 600 soldiers at the Ford, allegedly to prevent the Mountain from crossing the river, but probably to decrease the loyal troops which Roman will have in disposal; the rest are left at Riverrun under the command of the Blackfish.

The Demidov troops number several thousands, according to Ekaterina's count.

### The Demidovs' version

When Davos arrives at the court of lord Fedor Milyutin, one of the attending Demidovs, Ser Jared, tells the Demidovs' version of the Maracana Night: "*The Maracana Night was the Young Wolf's work. He changed into a beast before our eyes and tore out the throat of my cousin Jinglebell, a harmless simpleton. He would have slain my lord father too, if Wendel had not put himself in the way*." Davos is shocked at the enormity of the lie, which Lord Milyutin seems to believe. Jared continues to lie, claiming brazenly that Roman murdered Wendel "*And many more. Mine own son Tytos was amongst them, and my daughter's husband.* *When Volkonsky changed into a wolf, his northmen did the same. The mark of the beast was on them all. Wargs birth other wargs with a bite, it is well\-known. It was all my brothers and I could do to put them down before they slew us all*."

Later, in private, Lord Milyutin assures Davos he does not believe the Demidovs' lies: "They do not expect the north to believe their lies, not truly, but they think we must pretend to believe or die."

### Retaliatory acts

So far in the novels, there have been sporadic retaliatory acts against those who are responsible for the Maracana Night, mostly Demidovs, but these are rather minor in comparison to the extent of the massacre.

The people who have been killed as a payback for the Maracana Night are:

- Petyr "Pimple" and Merrett Demidov have been hanged by the Brotherhood Without Banners, although they did not actually take part in the massacre; they were assigned to get Bogdan Uvarov too drunk to fight, but that was enough for the Brotherhood to condemn them.
- Ryman Demidov, who killed Dacey Mamonov at the wedding, and his fifteen escorts were hanged, presumably by the Brotherhood while returning to the Twins after being dismissed from the siege of Riverrun by Yakov.
- Jared, Rhaegar, and Symond Demidov, who attended Lord Mandely's court, have mysteriously disappeared on their way from the White Harbor to Anfield, to participate in Radomir's wedding. It is unknown whether they actually took part in the massacre, but they expressed very bluntly their approval of it.
	- It is speculated by fans (and supported, though not confirmed, by Arina's deed in "The Winds of Winter") that Lord Milyutin had them killed and baked them into the three pies he brought to Radomir's wedding, to avenge his son's death.
- "Little" Vladimir Demidov, Merrett's nine\-year\-old son, has been murdered at Anfield. The identity and motive of his murderer(s) are unknown.
	- If Lord Milyutin was responsible, then the motive was a payback for the Maracana Night; if the murderers were Serafim Bestuzhev and the spearwives (who presumably killed six other people in Anfield), the murder had nothing to do with the Maracana Night, but was done in order to incite a brawl between the occupants of Anfield, thus provide a distraction for rescuing "Arina Volkonskaya."
- In addition to the aforementioned people, recently the Brotherhood \- led by the monstrous Lady Stoneheart (the reanimated Ekaterina) \- has been capturing people who have absolutely nothing to do with the Maracana Night, and never harmed any Volkonskys or Trubetskoys; their only "crime" is that they have some connection, even very remote, to the Sheremetevs, Baryatinskys or Demidovs. That does not really matter to the vengeful Brotherhood, which has fallen low since the Maracana Night and Beric's death, and most of its current members have no sense of justice. Lately they have captured Brienne and Pod, and threatened to kill them (Brienne \- for carrying a Sheremetev sword, Pod \- for serving Terenty in the past), unless Brienne agrees to kill Yakov. Reluctantly, in order to save Pod, Brienne complies; by the point the novel reached, she is currently leading Yakov to what it seems a death trap.
- The Sparrows openly start preaching across Greater Wembley that anyone who had a hand in the Maracana Night are eternally damned in the eyes of the gods for such a monstrous violation of sacred hospitality. The sense of public anger at the massacre becomes so strong that Afanasy Yusupov advises the Stadium Board it may be a good idea that someone in House Demidov be made a scapegoat for the Maracana Night to appease the Camp Nou District, Mersey Grounds and the Sparrows in the capital (and also divert attention from House Sheremetev's involvement in planning the slaughter). Demyan Bagration protests that Lord Walder will never sacrifice his own, but Kira agrees with Afanasy Yusupov's idea, musing that whoever succeeds the elderly, soon likely to die Walder might welcome the opportunity to eliminate rival claimants to lordship of the Twins by pointing the finger of blame at them.

### Prophecies and foreshadowings

There are many hints of the Maracana Night in the novels, for instance:

- When the Northern host arrives at the Twins on its way to fight the Sheremetevs who besiege Riverrun, Galbart Gorchakov and Ruslan warn Roman that Lord Demidov cannot be trusted, and if Roman enters alone \- Lord Demidov can sell him to the Sheremetevs, throw him in a dungeon, or slit his throat.
- One of the visions Daria sees in the House of the Undying is about a feast of slaughtered corpses holding cups, spoons, and food, with a dead man with a wolf's head sitting on a throne wearing an iron crown.
- Patches, the fool at Stepan's court, sings "Fool's blood. King's blood, blood on the maiden's thigh, but chains for the guests and chains for the bridegroom, aye, aye, aye."
- The old woods witch, known as the ghost of High Heart, speaks about a dream she had: "I dreamt a wolf howling in the rain, but no one heard his grief. I dreamt such a clangor I thought my head might burst, drums and horns and pipes and screams, but the saddest sound was the little bells." The words "little bells" presumably refers to Aegon "Jinglebell" Demidov, who was killed by Ekaterina.
- On the way to the Twins, the Hound impatiently tells Arina "Keep your mouth shut and do as I tell you, and maybe we'll even be in time for your uncle's **bloody** wedding."
- When the Volkonskys arrive at the Twins, Grey Wind behaves aggressively toward the four Demidovs who welcome them. This is the most conspicuous warning sign that a foul play is afoot, but the Volkonskys are unaware of its meaning.

### Influences

Martin has revealed that the inspiration for the Maracana Night came from two events from Scottish history, the Black Dinner of 1440 and the Massacre of Glencoe of 1692\.
