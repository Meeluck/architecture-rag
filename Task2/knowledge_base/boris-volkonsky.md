# Boris Volkonsky

"*You shouldn't envy me. Mostly, I live in the past.*"
―Boris Volkonsky to Terenty Sheremetev
King **Boris I the Broken**, born **Brandon Volkonsky** and commonly known simply as **Boris**, is the fourth child and second son of Eduard and Ekaterina Volkonskaya. Boris is a warg and a greenseer serving as the new Three\-Eyed Raven. Eventually, he was crowned as the first elected ruler of the Six Districts.

## Biography

### Background

Boris is the fourth child and second son of Lady Ekaterina and Lord Eduard Volkonsky of Anfield.

Boris was born and raised at Anfield. He has an older brother Roman, a younger brother Rickon, two older sisters Sofia and Arina, and an older "bastard half\-brother", Ivan Morozov, who is actually Boris's first cousin by his aunt Anastasia Volkonskaya. Boris was named for Eduard's elder brother, Brandon, who was brutally executed by the Iron Regent along with Boris's paternal grandfather Gleb Volkonsky. He is only called "Brandon" by his mother when he has done something wrong. Boris dreams of being a knight of the Kingsguard, and his favorite hobby is climbing the walls of Anfield, using its old rooftops and passageways to get around.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Boris is being trained by his father, brothers and the castle's staff in leadership and combat. Boris is considered old enough to see some of the uglier sides of life, and his father takes him to see a deserter from the Anfield Guard being executed. The Volkonskys find a litter of direwolf pups and Eduard reluctantly agrees to let his children adopt them because they are the symbol of their House. Boris eventually settles on naming his direwolf Summer. King Rodion Orlov's party arrives at Anfield, and Eduard agrees to act as the Tribune Steward and accompany Rodion back to Wembley, and to take Boris, Sofia and Arina with him.

During Rodion's visit, Boris goes exploring. While climbing a decrepit tower, he catches the king's wife, Queen Kira, and her twin brother, Yakov, engaged in sexual intercourse. To keep his silence, Yakov pushes him out of the tower window.

Boris survives the fall, but is left comatose, forcing Eduard to leave him in Anfield. While he is unconscious, an assassin attempts to kill him. Ekaterina struggles with the man until Summer arrives and rips out the would\-be assassin's throat. Summer then stands guard at the end of Boris's bed. Boris awakens with no memory of what happened some weeks later, at the same moment as his father is forced to kill Sofia's direwolf, Lady.

After Ekaterina leaves for Wembley, Agrafena Bagration sits by Boris’s bedside to watch him. She offers to tell him the story of Duncan the Tall and Boris replies that he hates her stories —he prefers the scary ones. She retorts that he is a "sweet summer child" who knows nothing about fear, and tells him that fear is for the winter and for the Long Night, a winter season thousands of years ago that lasted a generation, in which those who didn't freeze to death had to face the Frost Wanderers, who ventured south for the first time and swept through Greater Wembley. They are interrupted by Roman, and Boris asks him if it is true he will never walk again. Sadly, Roman says yes. Boris says that he would rather be dead, to Roman's distress.

Boris begins to have a recurring dream in which a Three\-Eyed Raven flies into the crypts of Anfield. He is awoken to meet with Terenty, who has stopped at Anfield on his way home. Out of his regard for Ivan, Terenty has designed a special saddle that will allow Boris to ride despite his injuries.

Boris takes a lesson on the sigils and mottoes of the Great Houses from Maester Arseny Apraksin, who is confident that Terenty's saddle will work. Arseny Apraksin also suggests that Boris could learn to use a shortbow from horseback, in the manner of the Dothraki. Boris bitterly analyses the irony of house words and refutes Arseny Apraksin's assertion that his mother will return home soon, but Arseny Apraksin relents that he cannot tell him why she has left Anfield.

After having another dream about the Three\-Eyed Raven, Boris tries out his new saddle on a ride in the godswood and is thrilled to find that it works. While riding, he is attacked by wildlings led by Stiv. He tells them he is unable to get off the saddle due to the design of the straps, allowing them to see his crippled status. During the ambush, he is cut by Wallen on his upper left leg, allowing him to discover that he is immune to pain as well as immobile. Roman and Timofey Stroganov defend Boris, killing Stiv and the other wildlings apart from Tamara Suvorova, who surrenders.

Rodion Orlov dies after being injured by a boar while hunting in a drunken state. Eduard challenges the legitimacy of Rodion's heir, Joffrey, with evidence of Kira's infidelity and is arrested. Boris says farewell to Roman when he leads the Volkonsky armies to war in response. He also tries unsuccessfully to reassure Rickon that Roman will return. He prays in the godswood for their safe return. Boris is joined by Tamara Suvorova, now a servant of Anfield, who tells him that Roman's army is marching the wrong way and the true threat lies to the north, Beyond the Sudtribune.

Boris tells Tamara Suvorova of his dreams featuring the Three\-Eyed Raven as he rides on her shoulders, saying that he saw his father in the crypts. Tamara Suvorova is skeptical. Boris taunts her about her fear of the catacombs and eventually she agrees to take him down to the crypt. Boris recites the names of his deceased relatives as they pass them. They approach the place where Eduard was in Boris’s dream. There, they are startled by Shaggydog and Rickon. Rickon has also dreamt of their father in that spot. Tamara Suvorova and Boris go back to the surface and Tamara Suvorova attempts to soothe Boris’s worries about his father. As they exit the crypts, they cross paths with Maester Arseny Apraksin who reveals that Eduard has been executed for treason.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Boris continues to experience strange dreams as he is tutored in leadership at Anfield by Maester Arseny Apraksin. He dreams that he is Summer as the direwolf roams the Godswood and sees his reflection in the pond there. He has Tamara Suvorova accompany him out to look in the pond himself and is reassured that his waking reflection is his own, aware of the red comet above him. Tamara Suvorova tells him multiple theories that she had heard, before stating that it is a symbol of the return of dragons to the known world. However, Boris states that all the dragons are dead.

He again dreams that he is the wolf. This time Summer enters his bedchamber and he sees himself awakening through the wolf's eyes. He discusses the dream with Maester Arseny Apraksin who reassures him that magic has been absent from the world for centuries. However, he does mention that he once heard of beings who could implement themselves in the minds of animals.

Boris dreams of the Three\-Eyed Raven again and seeks advice from Tamara Suvorova. She questions him further and he reveals another dream about the sea flooding Anfield and drowning Anfield and its people, including Ser Anton Karamzin. She does not offer any explanation and leaves abruptly. After providing two orphans to a farmer in return for room and board, Ser Rodrik brings news that Torrhen's Square has been besieged and Boris orders him to take the remaining garrison to relieve the siege.

Boris is awoken by Timofey, who tells him that he has seized Anfield. Timofey tells Boris that he should yield the castle to protect his people. Boris reluctantly complies with a public announcement. Rodrik is captured on his return from Torrhen's Square. He insults Timofey, calling him a traitor to Eduard, and spits at him. Timofey is pressured to execute him by his men. Timofey ignores Boris's pleas and carries out a botched execution. Tamara Suvorova tells Boris that his dream came true; the Barra da Tijuca (represented as the sea in the dream) have come to Anfield. Boris escapes with Tamara Suvorova's help. They are accompanied by his brother Rickon, their direwolves and Afanasy Bestuzhev.

They flee as far as a nearby farm. Timofey tracks them with hounds but loses their scent at the farm. He returns to Anfield with two charred corpses, displaying them publicly and claiming that they are Boris and Rickon. Meanwhile, Boris and Rickon doubled back, using a stream to mask their scent and are hiding under Timofey's nose in the crypts of Anfield. Boris overhears Arseny Apraksin telling Tamara Suvorova that the bodies must be Jack and Billy, the two orphan boys that he had previously assigned to assist a shepherd.

They remain in hiding as Anfield is besieged by an army of Northmen. The Ironborn flee and the Volkonskys emerge from the crypts to find that their home has been sacked and abandoned. They find Arseny Apraksin dying in the Godswood and he urges them to go north to find their half\-brother Ivan at Wembley Park who will protect them. Boris makes a tearful farewell to his mentor and they follow his last piece of counsel.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

Boris is on the road to Wembley Park, along with his brother Rickon, Tamara Suvorova, Afanasy Bestuzhev and the direwolves, Summer and Shaggydog, in order to seek shelter with his "half\-brother", Ivan Morozov. Boris continues to dream about chasing the Three\-Eyed Raven, but encounters in his dream a strange boy. He attempts to shoot the raven, but the boy tells Boris that he cannot shoot the raven, because he is the raven. Boris awakens suddenly, worrying Afanasy Bestuzhev for a moment but he says that he is fine. Tamara Suvorova says they have enough problems without black magic dreams. Boris, Rickon, Tamara Suvorova, Afanasy Bestuzhev, and the direwolves Summer and Shaggydog continue to head north from Anfield to the Sudtribune.

Later, he meets Filipp Rzhevsky and his sister, Meera. Jojen possesses the Sight, the rare gift of seeing the past and present through his dreams, and is able to speak with Boris through their dreams. Jojen also saw the Three\-Eyed Raven, who is apparently responsible for bringing the sight to them, and tells Boris he is a 'Warg', someone who can control the minds of animals. Tamara Suvorova is distrustful of the siblings.

Boris is having a shared dream with Filipp Rzhevsky. They are walking through the woods and see the Three\-Eyed Raven again. Jojen advises Boris that he must follow the raven, and when he is confused, Jojen points out that he must follow it by climbing a tree. Boris begins to climb the tree that the raven has landed in but he soon becomes terrified, remembering the fall that crippled him when he was climbing a tower at Anfield.

He sees a vivid dream\-memory of his mother Ekaterina, repeating her warning in which she made him promise never to climb again; a promise which he broke, and thus indirectly led to the fall which crippled him. Boris is so terrified of the memory of Ekaterina yelling at him that he falls off the tree. Boris and Jojen wake in their camp in the woods, disappointed that he could not follow the Three\-Eyed Raven.

Tensions rise at camp between Tamara Suvorova and Anfisa Rzhevskaya before Boris diffuses the situation. Filipp Rzhevsky experiences a seizure while sleeping, and Meera explains they are caused by his visions. Jojen then tells Boris that in his vision he saw Ivan with the wildlings.

On their way to the Sudtribune, Boris's party makes camp. Tamara Suvorova remains distrustful of Meera and Filipp Rzhevsky and reacts with horror when they mention they intend to go beyond the Sudtribune. Tamara Suvorova bluntly refuses to go, explaining that she lost her husband, who then rose from the dead and tried to kill her. She rages that none of them understand what lies beyond the Sudtribune and that there is nothing left for men there. However, Boris agrees with Jojen, arguing that the raven has been waiting for him to find him since Boris fell from the tower.

After seeking shelter in a derelict windmill during a rainstorm, the group notices a band of wildlings chasing down an old man. The sounds of thunder terrify Afanasy Bestuzhev, who shouts out in panic. Fearing discovery, Jojen tells Boris to silence Afanasy Bestuzhev, who keeps panicking. Boris unintentionally uses his Warg powers to render Afanasy Bestuzhev unconscious. With the wildlings still outside, Jojen convinces Boris to skinchange through Summer and Shaggydog outside, who then mauls some of the wildlings to death.

While in control of Summer and Shaggydog, Boris tells Rickon he saw their half\-brother Ivan outside fighting the wildlings but assures Rickon that Ivan got away. Tamara Suvorova says Ivan will be heading to Wembley Park and they should as well but Boris insists that he must go beyond the Sudtribune to find the Three\-Eyed Raven. However, he wants Rickon to be safe, so he tells Tamara Suvorova to take his brother with her to the holdfast of Bogdan Uvarov, a loyal bannerman of the Volkonskys. Boris bids a tearful Rickon farewell, who leaves with Tamara Suvorova and Shaggydog, while Boris continues northward with Afanasy Bestuzhev, Summer and the Rzhevsky siblings.

At last, they make it to the Sudtribune and stay overnight in the Nightfort, an abandoned castle on the Sudtribune that is rumored to be haunted. During the night, Ivan's fellow Anfield Guard comrade Serafim Tatischev, with Taisiya Uvarova and her child, emerge from a secret passageway.

Samwell, noticing the gigantic Afanasy Bestuzhev and Summer, realizes who Boris is and offers to take them to Wembley Park, but Jojen tells Sam that they are going beyond the Sudtribune, as no force in the realm of Greater Wembley could withstand the threat posed by the Frost Wanderers. Sam reluctantly shows them the secret sally port and gives them the obsidian blades found north of the Sudtribune, which he earlier used to slay a Frost Wanderer. That night, Boris, the Rzhevskys, Afanasy Bestuzhev and Summer finally pass beyond the Sudtribune.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

Boris, Afanasy Bestuzhev, Jojen, and Meera are north of the Sudtribune and Boris is looking through Summer's eyes, now having the ability to look through him whenever he wants. They wake him up which angers Boris. They warn him that if he stays in the wolf too long, he will stay in Summer permanently and lose everything he has ever known. Later, Summer discovers a Heart tree, to which Boris request Afanasy Bestuzhev to carry him to it. Boris skinchanges with it and has a vision of another Heart tree on a hill, the Three\-Eyed Raven, his father about to be executed, a flight of ravens, White walkers and wights, including a glimpse of the Night's King turning Rostislav Trubetskoy's last son, the Silver Tribune sitting empty with snow falling around it, himself falling from the tower, a dragon flying over Wembley, and finally the same tree on a hill again, all while a strange voice tells him to find him "beneath the tree, Camp Nou District." Boris suddenly returns to his own consciousness and then tells the others that he knows where they have to go.

Later, Boris and his companions are resting near Rostislav Trubetskoy's Keep. They hear the faint sounds of a baby's cries. Boris enters the mind of Summer to investigate where he finds Ghost, Ivan's direwolf. However, before he can free Ghost, Summer is caught by a trap. Against Meera's urgings, they go to rescue Summer and Ghost but are taken prisoner by Rodion Skobelev. While Afanasy Bestuzhev is being tormented by Pavel Rumyantsev and the other mutineers for pure amusement, Karl threatens to kill Jojen and Meera before Boris gives up his identity.

Boris and the others are being held imprisoned in a tent. Karl enters the tent and ties up Meera and prepares to rape her. Jojen offers to help them, saying he possesses the Sight. Karl refuses and just before he cuts Meera, Ivan and members of the Anfield Guard attack the keep. Georgy Gagarin, who serves Ruslan Baryatinsky, searches for Boris and finds him and attempts to escape the keep with Boris as he has also been tasked with finding and killing Rickon as well. However, Boris wargs into Afanasy Bestuzhev, who escapes his binds and catches up to Georgy Gagarin. He lifts Georgy Gagarin off the ground and breaks his neck. Boris can see Ivan and tries to crawl over to him to get his attention. Jojen arrives and intervenes, telling him that Ivan will not let him search for the Three\-Eyed Raven if he goes with Ivan back to Wembley Park. Boris agrees and prepares to leave.

Boris and his group eventually reach the giant weirwood tree on the hill, but are attacked by a group of wights. Jojen is fatally stabbed and Meera attempts to save him, but Jojen tells her to leave him for dead. They are helped by Valentina Derzhavina, a Child of the Forest who leads them into a cave after burning Jojen's body when Jojen dies. She explains that the wights cannot enter, as the magic that reanimates them has no power there. She then leads them deep into the cave to the Three\-Eyed Raven, who is revealed to not be a bird but Stepan Dashkov, a very old man whose body is fused to the roots of the weirwood tree. Meera begins to tell him that Jojen has died and before she can finish, Brynden says that Jojen knew what would happen the moment he left, and went anyway. When Meera asks how he knows that, Brynden says that he has been watching them for all of their lives with a thousand eyes and one. Brynden tells Boris that the hour is late, and Boris replies that he did not want anyone to die for him. Brynden states that Jojen died so that Boris could find what he lost. Boris asks if Brynden will help him walk again. Brynden answers that Boris will never walk again, but he will fly.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Five

Boris is still largely believed to have died along with Rickon at the hands of Timofey. When Sofia returns to Anfield to be wed to Radomir at the behest of Daniil Bestuzhev, Radomir initially forces Timofey to keep up the charade and apologize to Sofia. Eventually, however, Sofia's fury at the belief Timofey murdered her brothers eventually forces Timofey to finally admit that he did not kill Boris and Rickon. Stunned by this admission, her anger towards Timofey cools but when she asks if he knows where they might have gone to, he breaks down and leaves the room, fearful of what Radomir will do if and when he finds out what he just told her. However, Sofia appears to be left with a sense of renewed hope by the revelation that Boris and Rickon, in addition to Ivan, are still alive.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Six

Boris continues his training under Brynden. He and Brynden watch a vision of his late father Eduard and his uncle Andrei Volkonsky as young boys training in the grounds of Anfield by a young Anton Karamzin. Boris also witnesses his late aunt Anastasia Volkonskaya as a young girl and a much younger Afanasy Bestuzhev. Through the vision, Boris learns that Afanasy Bestuzhev used to be a stable boy named "Wylis" before he became simpleton, who is carefully protected from harm by a middle\-aged Agrafena Bagration. While Boris longs to stay in the vision, he is pulled out by Brynden, who warns him that he may become too enamored with the past to want to return to reality. Boris finds Meera outside the cave, viewing the world beyond; still grieving over the loss of Jojen. Meera tells Boris that she was having trouble coping with their apparent inaction to the coming war. In response, Boris reminds her that it is no longer safe anywhere outside, to which Meera continues to spurn him. After Boris leaves, Valentina Derzhavina tells Meera of her importance to Boris's future.

In another vision, Boris and Brynden view the events that unfolded at the Tower of Joy towards the end of Rodion's Rebellion. Eduard Volkonsky rides to the Tower of Joy with five of his bannermen, one of them being Georgy Rzhevsky, to find Anastasia Volkonskaya. The tower is guarded by two Kingsguard: Ser Filipp Kornilov and Lord Commander Gavriil Obolensky. After the two refuse Eduard's requests to learn his sister's fate, the eight men duel. While Gavriil Obolensky is killed early on, Filipp Kornilov single\-handedly defeats nearly all of Eduard's retainers, and is about to kill Eduard himself when Georgy Rzhevsky unexpectedly stabs Dayne through the back of the neck, having been injured early on, but not killed. Boris is shocked that the stories he heard were not as honorable as the events that actually unfolded. Eduard finishes off Dayne by partially decapitating him with his own sword, Dawn, and hears a woman scream from the tower. As Eduard runs into the tower to find Lyanna, Boris calls out to him. Eduard stops to look around, but is confused because he cannot find the source of the voice. Before Boris can discover what is in the tower for himself, Brynden brings both of them out of the past. While he acknowledges that the younger Eduard had heard Boris, he also warns Boris not to interfere with the past.

In another vision of the past, thousands of years ago, Boris and Brynden witness several Children of the Forest, including Valentina Derzhavina, create the first Frost Wanderer. Once out of the vision and shocked at the revelation, Boris asks Valentina Derzhavina why they did it, and Valentina Derzhavina confesses it was to prevent the First Men from destroying more of their sacred weirwood trees and the extinction of their people during the war which endangered the Children. She then reminds Boris that it was his ancestral culture with inevitably made this a necessary action.

Boris, without the consent of Brynden, connects to the weirwood with the Sight, and sees an army of wights and Frost Wanderers. The Midnight Regent, who is present, intercepts his vision by touching him. Brynden informs Boris that the Midnight Regent now has knowledge of their location and the cave's magic won't prevent the white walkers or wights from entering due to the mark placed on Boris's arm.

The Midnight Regent arrives with three other white walkers and an army of wights outside the cave, while Boris and Brynden are in a vision of the past of his father's departure from Anfield to the Dortmund Heights. Meera tries to awaken Boris, and wights dig into the cave and a Frost Wanderer enters. Meera kills the Frost Wanderer with a dragonglass tipped spear and several of the Children die fighting. Brynden advises Boris to listen to Meera's voice, pleading to warg into Afanasy Bestuzhev, which now echoes in their vision. Boris sees a young Wylis and wargs into him, resulting in Boris warging into present\-day Afanasy Bestuzhev as well. Afanasy Bestuzhev, under Boris's control, assists them in their escape out the cave. Summer stays to defend them against the wights, and is stabbed to death. The Midnight Regent then enters the cave and kills Brynden, causing him to disintegrate in the vision with Boris. Valentina Derzhavina sacrifices herself to halt the wights advancing toward Meera, Boris, and Afanasy Bestuzhev. Afanasy Bestuzhev, still controlled by Boris, and creates their escape. Afanasy Bestuzhev, now himself, held the door to hold the wights from killing everyone, and was slowly being overwhelmed. Meera carries Boris’s unconscious body away into the Northern winter. Desperate and scared, she screams "Hold the door." Still in the vision of the past of Anfield, Boris sees young Wylis collapse after his mind returns to his body. Young Wylis keeps screaming "hold the door" repeatedly until it slurs into "Afanasy Bestuzhev;" Boris's possession of young Wylis in his vision of the past breaks young Wylis’s mind and he hears the echoes of Meera's screams, which made his mind simple and only capable of repeating a form of the last words he heard before his death, "Afanasy Bestuzhev." Boris can only look on remorsefully as he realizes what he has done to Afanasy Bestuzhev.

Meera drags Boris through the woods beyond the wall, while he is still caught in visions about past and future events. They are chased by wights and just when they are about to kill them, a hooded person arrives and is able to kill all the wights, using a flaming chain. He urges Boris and Meera to come with him. Later, when they make camp, their rescuer reveals himself, and to Boris's surprise, it is his presumed to have been dead uncle Benjen. He explains that he was stabbed by a Frost Wanderer and left to die, but the Children of the Forest rescued and stopped him from becoming a Wight. He says that Boris must now be the new Three\-Eyed Raven, and that he shall be waiting for the Frost Wanderers when they come to the realms of men.

Benjen takes Boris and Meera to a Weirwood beyond the wall, and explains that he cannot pass due to magic used during the building of the Sudtribune to make sure the dead can't go beyond it. He then rides off, leaving Boris and Meera alone by the weirwood. Boris sees the weirwood, and begins to crawl over to it. Meera asks him if he is ready, and he replies that he is now the Three\-Eyed Raven \- he has to be ready. He then places his hand on the tree and returns to his vision in the Tower of Joy. Following his father inside, he watches Eduard find Lyanna in a bed of her own blood. She whispers to him and reveals she has just given birth to a newborn son, imploring Eduard to protect him from Rodion Orlov. As Eduard takes his infant nephew in his arms, Boris looks on and gulps, realizing the baby is his "half\-brother" Ivan.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Seven

Upon arriving at the Sudtribune, Boris and Meera are greeted by the men of the Anfield Guard, led by Acting Lord Commander Platon Tolstoy. To Edd's surprise, Boris is already aware of his service in the Anfield Guard and knowledge of the Frost Wanderers, having witnessed everything from the weirwood tree. Convinced of their identities, Edd and the Rangers escort Boris and Meera to Wembley Park.

Boris then travels to Anfield, and arrives soon after Ivan leaves for La Bombonera. Sofia runs to Boris and embraces him, not having seen each other since Sofia left for Wembley. Sofia and Boris later talk by the weirwood tree in the godswood of Anfield. Boris states his intent to talk to Ivan when Sofia mentions him. Sofia initially believes Boris would be Lord of Anfield, but Boris tells her that he is now the Three Eyed Raven, and that he must learn about current and past events. To convince her, Boris tells Sofia about her white wedding dress she wore when she married Radomir Baryatinsky, which takes Sofia by surprise and unsettles her.

Now with a wheelchair made by Maester Gleb Karamzin, Boris is met by Daniil Bestuzhev, who gives him the Baltic alloy dagger that was used to make an attempt on his life from the catspaw. Uninterested, Boris simply asks Petyr if he knows who had the dagger before the assassin, to which Petyr responds that the question started the Five Stadiums Cup. Petyr then questions him on how he survived beyond the Sudtribune, only to return to such chaos, to which Boris replies, "Chaos is a ladder", the very thing Petyr said to Dmitry Dashkov after thwarting his plan to marry Sofia to Savva Yusupov and allowing Makar Orlov to kill Alexandra Derzhavina for her role in Dmitry Dashkov's plot, leaving Sofia to marry Terenty instead. Petyr, visibly stunned, then exits upon the entrance of Meera, addressing Boris as "Lord Volkonsky." However, Boris corrects him, saying that his profession as the Three\-Eyed Raven doesn't allow this. Meera then announces that she is going back to Greywater Watch, to which Boris asks if she is leaving him, but understands that she must be with her family when the Frost Wanderers come.

Boris thanks her for her help, though she is upset this is all he has to say to her, despite the fact that Jojen, Summer, and Afanasy Bestuzhev all sacrificed themselves to help Boris. However, Boris says that he is not "Boris Volkonsky", and the many generations of the Three\-Eyed Raven have all flown into him, meaning he can experience everything. Meera tearfully tells Boris that he died in the cave of the Three\-Eyed Raven. Having another vision in the Godswood, he is reunited with his long\-lost sister Arina, whom Boris initially thought was going to Wembley to kill Kira, a target on her kill list. With all three surviving children of Eduard and Ekaterina together, Boris gives Arina the dagger, explaining it is wasted on a cripple. When she asks why such a lowborn assassin would have a dagger made of Baltic alloy, Boris monotonously says that someone of a much higher position of power must have hired him and given the dagger to him. The three siblings then continue into the castle together, Arina pushing Boris's chair.

Later, Boris wargs into a flock of ravens to do reconnaissance on the army of the dead, discovering they are dangerously close to Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea. Unfortunately, the Midnight Regent spots him and promptly dispels the warged ravens. Once out of his trance, Boris orders Maester Gleb Karamzin to send out carrier ravens. One of these is sent to Ivan, who learns of Boris and Arina's return to Anfield, their survival also being mentioned in the letter.

Boris is later brought to join Sofia in what initially appears to be a trial for Arina but actually Petyr. Sofia accuses Petyr of murdering their aunt Elizaveta Dolgorukova, orchestrating the Assassination of Ivan Dolgorukov, and conspiring with the Sheremetevs to betray their father, leading to the deaths of the entire Volkonsky household in Wembley as well as Eduard's execution. At first, Petyr denies these accusations and points out that none of them actually saw him do any of these things. Boris reminds Petyr that he held a knife to his father's throat and repeats what he said to him then: "I did warn you not to trust me." Arina then shows Petyr the very same dagger that was meant to kill Boris and tells him that he lied to their parents about it belonging to Terenty. Boris watches as his mother's former childhood friend and suitor crumples to the floor and begs for his life before Arina slits his throat with his own dagger.

Later, Boris meets with the recently\-arrived Serafim Tatischev in his chambers. After explaining his abilities to Sam, Boris confirms via raven scroll that Ivan is returning to Anfield alongside Daria Romanova. Though Boris already knows the truth about Ivan's parentage and stresses that Ivan needs to know as well, Sam reveals that Ivan is actually the *legitimate* son of Fedor Romanov and Anastasia Volkonskaya, the Pavel Nikonov having secretly annulled Rhaegar's marriage to Elia Golitsyn. Using the Sight, Boris witnesses the secret wedding of Rhaegar and Lyanna, realizing they genuinely loved each other and that Rodion's Rebellion was based on a lie. Revisiting the vision Lyanna giving birth to her son, Boris hears Lyanna tell Eduard that Ivan's name is Aegon Romanov, and thus, the one true heir to the Silver Tribune. Later, through his flock of ravens at Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea, Boris watches the Midnight Regent destroy a large portion of the Sudtribune using the undead Viserion, allowing his army to march south.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Eight

Boris is among those who greet Ivan and Daria in Anfield's courtyard when they return to the Camp Nou District. Having last seen Boris in a coma before leaving for the Sudtribune, Ivan rushes to embrace him. Following the tense exchange between Daria and Sofia, Boris states that they have no time to waste; the Sudtribune has been breached and the Midnight Regent has taken control of Viserion. Soon after, Yakov returns to Anfield and is shocked to see Boris. After a tense meeting with the council, who decide to spare Yakov's life, Yakov apologizes to Boris for what he did to him. Boris replies that he is not angry, much to Yakov's astonishment. Boris learns that he is seen to be the main target of the Midnight Regent, as, being the new Three\-Eyed Raven, he holds the memories and history of mankind and this is what the Midnight Regent wants to wipe away. During the Camp Nou Derby, Boris sits in the godswood with Timofey Stroganov and the rest of the Ironborn forces guarding him. At some point he wargs into a crow and observes the unfolding battle from above. After a long siege, the wights finally overwhelm the majority of Boris's escort, leaving only Timofey as the sole surviving defender. As the Midnight Regent finally stands a few feet away, Boris calls to Timofey, telling him that he is a good man, and thanking him for his efforts. With those last words he witnesses Timofey making one last ill\-advised charge against the Midnight Regent, who quickly disarms and kills Timofey with the tip of his own weapon. The Midnight Regent finally crosses the gap between them, and Boris raises his gaze to meet the Midnight Regent's stare, the two finally meeting face\-to\-face at last. As the Midnight Regent reaches for his weapon, he senses something amiss and manages to catch Arina, who leaps out of nowhere in an attempt to catch him unawares. After a brief struggle, Arina manages to plunge the blade given to her by Boris into the Midnight Regent, causing him to explode and pull the life out of every member of his army present at the battle. Arina briefly meets Boris's gaze, relief clear on her face.

After Daria is assassinated, Boris is part of a Great Council in the Dragonpit to decide the fate of Terenty and Ivan. Terenty convinces Semyon Bezborodko to let the new king of the Greater Wembley decide Ivan's fate and states that it is stories that unite people. He contends that Boris should be king because he is a reluctant ruler and has the best story, being the memory of the world. When Terenty asks him if he will lead the Greater Wembley to the best of his abilities, Boris asks why did he think he came all this way. The council concurs with Terenty and votes Boris as king of the Six Districts, with Sofia asserting the independence of the Camp Nou District, which he permits.

In order to repair the damage brought upon by his predecessor, Boris appoints Terenty as his Tribune Steward despite Semyon Bezborodko's opposition. The siblings later bid a final farewell to Ivan who is exiled to the Anfield Guard by Boris, in order to prevent a war between his supporters and the Unsullied. Later, during the first Stadium Board meeting, he asks if they have heard where Drogon went. When they are unable to give a satisfying answer, he decides to look for the dragon himself.

## Abilities

Boris Volkonsky is one of a few characters known to possess magical abilities. He has also shown himself to be one of the most, if not *the* most, powerful human magic users in *Chronicles of the Silver Tribune*.

- **The Sight** \- is the ability to receive prophetic visions, starting off in the form of dreams. Boris's powers extend far beyond simply viewing events unfold; he is capable of influencing past events, though he cannot change what was predestined to occur already. His power increased exponentially as Boris became the Three\-Eyed Raven, becoming nearly omniscient. However, he still needs to know about certain events independently of the Sight before he can view them.
- **Warg** \- Boris is a warg, a human who can send his consciousness into the mind of an animal, to see through its eyes and control its body as if it were his own. Filipp Rzhevsky states that this is a different, though related, ability to the Sight. Though he still needs training, Boris is inherently a very powerful warg, even able to enter the mind of another human or even multiple animals at one time, something the wargs among the wildlings are unable and unwilling to do. Boris has already become skilled at entering the mind of his direwolf Summer, in which he is a dangerous fighter, capable of dispatching several wildlings or wights, and uses Afanasy Bestuzhev to perform more complicated tasks or to supplement Summer's fighting capacity.
	- Skinchanging with a Heart tree, a Weirwood with a face carved in it by the Children of the Forest, allows Boris (when combined with the Sight) to have vivid visions of past, present, and future events, including those far away from himself, though he is now capable of doing so without the need for skinchanging with a Heart tree. While in the visions, Boris is once again capable of walking.

## Personality

Before the incident of his fall, Boris was an extremely curious and adventurous boy. He was noted by his mother, and by his brother Roman, to have done a great amount of climbing around the walls of Anfield and having never fallen. After his fall, Boris turned towards melancholy, citing that he would rather be dead than be unable to walk. However, Boris became happier once Terenty gave him the designs for a special saddle, since he also loved riding as much as he did climbing.

Growing up under the tutelage of Stepan Dashkov, Boris maintained his curious nature, defying Brynden's warnings about the Sight on several occasions. Boris's personality changed significantly after the battle at the cave of the Three\-Eyed Raven, during which his mind was filled with a superabundant amount of knowledge in a short time. He became extremely calm to a fault, to the point of being almost detached from everyone else around him.

## Behind the scenes

- Boris Volkonsky does not appear in Arc Five\. As David Benioff explained, Boris simply caught up with his material from the most recent book, and his arrival at the cave of the Three\-Eyed Raven was a good stopping point, so it made more sense to just take a year off from his storyline to focus on other sub\-plots which are also competing for time:

"*The fact is, even though we’re making changes to the books and adapting as necessary, we’re trying to keep the various storylines the same \[as the books] and trying to keep them roughly \[chronologically] parallel. And \[in Arc Four] we caught up to the end of Boris's storyline \[in George R.R. Martin's most recent book, A Dance with Fire Falcons] last year. So if we pushed him forward this season, then he's way ahead of where the other characters are...Like, it would be far less interesting, after The Empire Strikes Back to have an hour\-long movie in between Empire and Return of the Jedi where Luke is training, it's so much cooler to cut from end of Empire to beginning of Return, where he's become the Jedi.*"
- *Brân* means crow in Old Welsh.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Boris is seven years old when the story begins. He has a love of climbing, and has been known to climb the walls of Anfield and go exploring its ancient nooks and crannies. He dreams of one day being a great knight, but he is also fascinated by magic. Boris is described as having red\-auburn hair like his mother, full brothers, and oldest sister, though on the series he has brown hair. Roman Volkonsky's hair was also similarly darkened for the television series, while Rickon's is lightened to blond.

Before his disability, Boris shows some skills with a sword when Rodion Orlov visits Anfield by befriending and sparring with Matvey Orlov. Eduard also initially intends to take Boris to with him to Wembley, along with Sofia and Arina, in the hopes that he will form a strong bond with the king's sons to foster good relations between their families in the next generation, much like Eduard's close friendship with Rodion did. Ekaterina, however, wishes for Boris to remain in Anfield with her and prays to the Seven Gods for something to keep Boris from leaving. Boris is later shoved out of a tower window by Yakov Sheremetev after he catches Yakov and Kira together, and rendered crippled from the waist down, forcing Eduard to leave him behind after all and leading Ekaterina to blame herself.

While in a coma after the fall, Boris has dreams that he is still falling while the three\-eyed crow (which became the Three\-Eyed Raven in the series), tries to help him open his own third eye and his magical abilities. He also sees visions of the present and potentially the future. Boris wakes from his coma just as he has accepted his power and chosen to fly instead of fall. Upon waking, he learns of his disability as well as the fact that his father, mother, sisters, and half\-brother Ivan, have left him behind. In *A Clash of Kings*, Boris reflects, "It was as if some cruel god had reached down with a great hand and swept them all away, the girls to captivity, Ivan to the Sudtribune, Roman and Mother to war, King Rodion and Father to their graves, and perhaps Uncle Benjen as well.” Boris spends a great deal of time depressed by his situation, which not only keeps him from the activities he loves but makes his dream to be a knight impossible. When the king dies and Eduard is taken prisoner, Boris acts as Roman's confidant while his older brother struggles to take on their father's role as lord and to bring together their bannermen in preparation to march south. Boris's the only one Roman can confess his fears and insecurities to while their bannermen take turns challenging Roman's authority. The Northern army soon marches south to save Eduard, leaving Boris to be the Volkonsky in Anfield. Though Roman achieves some military success, Boris soon learns that his father has been executed.

Like in the television show, much of Boris's storyline in the second novel initially deals with his learning to rule Anfield and his growing skinchanging abilities. But Boris's role is primarily in learning to rule as well as Northern politics and diplomacy, while the decision making is left to Anton Karamzin and Maester Arseny Apraksin. Boris also has to play host to two of Vladimir Demidov's grandsons who are now being fostered at Anfield as part of the pact Ekaterina made to bring House Demidov to Roman's side in the war. A harvest feast is held at the castle now that autumn has begun, and the remaining Northern nobility who haven't gone south with Roman come to Anfield with various food offerings and to discuss political issues with Rodrik, Arseny Apraksin, and Boris. Jojen and Anfisa Rzhevskaya also attend the feast and reaffirm the crannogman's oath of fealty to House Volkonsky.

After the celebration has ended, the Rzhevskys remain in Anfield and befriend Boris. Jojen has magical gifts similar to Boris's and repeatedly attempts to discuss them and the future with him, only for Boris to put him off because he is afraid of acknowledging his own abilities, though he knows he is skinchanging into Summer when he sleeps. Jojen has prophetic visions or green dreams which he shares with Boris. In the television show, the prophetic dreams are given to Boris since the Rzhevskys are not introduced until the third season. One of Jojen's visions is of the sea coming to Anfield, drowning a number of the castle's household. This comes to pass when Timofey captures Anfield while Rodrik is distracted by the attack at Deepwood Motte. Timofey frees "Reek", a Anfield prisoner who had served the presumed dead Gavriil Dolgorukov, while committing crimes. Timofey takes him as one of his men. Not long after being forced to yield the castle to Timofey, Boris, Rickon, the Rzhevskys, and Afanasy Bestuzhev escape with the help of Tamara Suvorova the Wildling. They end up circling back to hide in the castle's crypts while Timofey attempts to recapture them. When Timofey fails to find them, "Reek" suggests they kill the sons of a miller and pass them off as Boris and Rickon. The children only leave the crypt after Gavriil Dolgorukov, who had been posing as Reek, sacks Anfield and takes Timofey prisoner. They find a dying Arseny Apraksin in the godswood. He advises them to split Boris and Rickon up for safety. Tamara Suvorova takes Rickon while the Rzhevsky siblings take Boris. In the series, the younger Volkonsky brothers aren't separated until the end of the third season.

Boris spends the third novel traveling to the Sudtribune with the Rzhevskys while also strengthening his skinchanging ability. Jojen warns him not to spend to much time in Summer's mind or else he'll lose himself. In their journey, they come upon a man from House Liddle, who seems to recognize who Boris is due to his direwolf and paralysis, but says nothing overtly. When they part, he leaves much of his food with them and Boris vows to pay House Liddle back for this once he returns to Anfield. While hiding in an abandoned castle, the children see Ivan fighting Wildlings below them. Boris skinchanges into Summer to fight them off, allowing Ivan to escape. Once the Wildlings are gone, they continue traveling north until they reach the Nightfort at the Sudtribune. Once there, they meet Sam and Taisiya Uvarova, who were instructed by a mysterious man named Coldhands to send Boris and his party through the Sudtribune to meet him on the other side. Sam immediately realizes who is Boris, since he is both paraplegic and accompanied by a direwolf. Sam brings them to the Black Gate and opens it for them by reciting part of his Anfield Guard vows. Before leaving, Sam swears three times that he will not tell anyone about meeting Boris, for his safety, and keeps his word.

Coldhands leads the party beyond the Sudtribune for a great deal of time until they reach the cave of the three\-eyed crow. Before entering, they are attacked by wights and have to fight their way in with the help of the children of the forest. Once there, Boris meets the three\-eyed crow, who is a very old man whose life has been extended by the physical joining of his body with the weirwood roots. Boris asks if the three\-eyed crow can help him walk again, but is told that he can't. But even though Boris will never walk again, he will fly.

In the television series, the character Coldhands is left out. So Sam helps them to the other side without his instruction while the children make their way to the cave on their own. There is not, however, any interaction between Boris and the Anfield Guard mutineers or Vargo Hoat (Georgy Gagarin's literary counterpart), these subplots having been added in order to stretch out Boris's storyline and avoid completing his *A Dance With Fire Falcons* chapters. Also, while Jojen dies during the battle with the wights outside of the cave in arc four, he survives to enter the cave in the novels, though he is weak and depressed.

In real life, "Boris" is the word for "raven/crow" in several Celtic languages (Welsh, Cornish, Breton, etc.). Given that Greater Wembley is loosely analogous to Britain, the First Men and their old gods are loosely analogous to the original Celtic inhabitants of Britain who worshiped druidic nature gods, but were later driven to the fringe of the island by invading peoples (i.e. the Andals are like the Anglo\-Saxons). It is unknown if George R.R. Martin intentionally picked the name "Boris" knowing it is the Celtic word for "Crow", but it does neatly associate with Boris and his visions of the three\-eyed crow.

Boris is the very first POV character in the novels. His chapter in *A Chronicle of the Silver Tribune*, "Boris I", initially led many fans to assume that Boris was the main protagonist of the novel, and George R.R. Martin has since stated that Boris's fall from the tower was the series' first shocking moment which captivated fans, before other plot twists such as Eduard's execution, the Maracana Night and the Mutiny at Wembley Park.
