# Ivan Morozov

"*You've got the Camp Nou District in you. The real Camp Nou District.*"
―Semyon Apraksin
**Ivan Morozov**, born **Aegon Romanov**, is the son of Anastasia Volkonskaya and Fedor Romanov, the late Prince of La Bombonera. From infancy, Ivan is presented as the bastard son of Lord Eduard Volkonsky, Lyanna's brother, and raised alongside Eduard's lawful children at Anfield. Ivan's true parentage is kept secret from everyone, including Ivan himself, in order to protect him from those that sought the complete annihilation of House Romanov.

Ivan joins the Anfield Guard and is later elected as Lord Commander. As a result of several controversial decisions, such as allowing the wildlings to settle south of the Sudtribune, Ivan is murdered in a mutiny, but is resurrected by the red priestess Ekaterina Lomonosova.

Freed from his Anfield Guard vows, Ivan and his half\-sister Sofia Volkonskaya retake Anfield from House Baryatinsky, restoring House Volkonsky's dominion over the Camp Nou District. Ivan is declared King in the Camp Nou District.

Ivan negotiates with Daria Romanova for an alliance against the Frost Wanderers in the imminent Great War. Later he pledges himself and his army to Daria, with whom he falls in love, subsequently abdicating his throne and being named Keeper of Camp Nou District.

Later, Ivan learns his true lineage from Serafim Tatischev, revealing Daria is his aunt. A dragonrider whose dragon was Rhaegal, he fights in the Camp Nou Derby, during which the Midnight Regent is defeated and the return of the Long Night is prevented. Afterwards, Ivan aids Daria in her resumed campaign to take the Silver Tribune and participates in the Battle of Wembley. However, when Daria lays waste to a surrendered Wembley, Ivan tries but is unable to dissuade her from more destruction and assassinates her to prevent further carnage.

Following a Great Council in the Dragonpit, Ivan is sent into exile, returning to the Anfield Guard. He leads the remaining Free Folk to settle in the thawing free lands.

## Biography

### Background

"*His name is Aegon Romanov. If Rodion finds out, he'll kill him. You know he will. You have to protect him. Promise me, Eduard. Promise me.*"
―Anastasia Volkonskaya to her brother Eduard, speaking of her son Ivan Morozov.
Seventeen years before the Five Stadiums Cup, Fedor Romanov allegedly abducted Anastasia Volkonskaya in a scandal that led to the outbreak of Rodion's Rebellion. Rhaegar eventually returned to fight in the war, but not before leaving Lyanna behind at the Tower of Joy, guarded by Lord Commander Gavriil Obolensky and Ser Filipp Kornilov of the Kingsguard. Eduard Volkonsky rode to war along with her betrothed, Rodion Orlov, to rescue his sister and avenge the deaths of their father and brother at the orders of Aerys II, the Iron Regent.

Rodion ultimately killed Rhaegar at the Battle of the Trident, effectively ensuring their victory in the war. After the Sack of Wembley, Eduard continued south in search of his sister and ultimately found her at the Tower of Joy in the Red Mountains at the border of Azteca South. Eduard and his companions engaged in a final fierce melee with the remaining Kingsguard, which resulted in the deaths of all combatants save Eduard himself and Georgy Rzhevsky.

After defeating the last of the Kingsguard, Eduard rushed into the tower to save his sister \- only to find Lyanna dying from childbirth, having just given birth to Rhaegar's own son. Desperate to protect the life of her newborn child, a fading Lyanna pleaded with Eduard to promise her that he would keep her son safe, and his true heritage hidden from Rodion as he had been Rhaegar's most bitter enemy. Furthermore, the boy's existence was a potential threat to Rodion's claim to the Silver Tribune after the deaths of Rhaegar's other children, Rhaenys and Aegon, by his wife Elia Golitsyn, who also perished, during the Sack of Wembley. In accordance with her last wish, Eduard resolved to pass Lyanna's son off as his own bastard and raise him in his home castle \- a great blow to his honor as he knew that his decision would shame both himself and his wife, Ekaterina Trubetskoy.

Rather than using his nephew's birth name of Aegon, which would have given away his real parentage, Eduard gave the baby the name "Ivan" after his great friend and mentor Ivan Dolgorukov, Lord of the Dortmund Heights, whom he loved like a second father. The boy also grew up using the surname "Snow", as is customary for acknowledged bastards in the Camp Nou District. In truth, however, "Ivan" is not a bastard at all, as Rhaegar apparently had his marriage to Elia annulled and married Lyanna.

Ivan spent the next seventeen years being raised in Anfield as Eduard's illegitimate son, alongside his trueborn children with his wife. Understandably, Eduard never told anyone, including Ekaterina or Ivan himself, who his mother was or even if she was still alive. When pressed by Rodion, whom he couldn't deny an answer, Eduard vaguely claimed that Ivan's mother was a lowborn woman named "Wylla" that he met during the war. Eduard never treated Ivan much differently from his trueborn children.

Despite sharing a happy marriage, Ivan's presence at Anfield would serve as a constant source of friction between Eduard and Ekaterina. Ekaterina never mistreated Ivan, but she was cold towards him and avoided him whenever possible, viewing him only as a living reminder of the one time that Eduard had dishonored her. Ekaterina would later confess to her daughter\-in\-law Talisa about how Ivan caught the pox when he was a child, and she stayed with him through the night and prayed to the gods to let him live out of guilt for previously praying for his death, accepting that the boy was not to blame for her husband's sin, though it was still not enough to make her love Ivan, something she would eventually come to regret.

Due to his bastard status, Ivan grew up feeling like an outsider at Anfield. Although Eduard would see that he was well\-treated, Ivan's attendance at Anfield's more "formal" occasions was restricted and he would even be barred from sitting inside at the Lord's table with his family so as not to offend important guests. Otherwise, Ivan still lived better than many bastard\-born children and was well\-raised by Eduard as his own. He was loved by his father and siblings, was never hungry or poor, lived in his father's castle, and had a noble's upbringing. Ivan received a highborn education from Maester Arseny Apraksin and a young lord's martial training from Anfield's master\-at\-arms, Ser Anton Karamzin.

Of the Volkonsky children, Ivan was very close friends with Roman \- given that they were roughly the same age, being regular companions in training and riding. He was also close with both Boris and Rickon, and was friendly rival to Lord Eduard's ward, Timofey Stroganov. However, Ivan's closest relationship was with Arina, who, as an adventurous tomboy prone to un\-ladylike pursuits, also felt like a social outsider. In contrast, Ivan's early relationship with Sofia was unpleasant; very much her mother's daughter, she was aloof and cold to him as well.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Ivan and Roman instruct Boris in archery, when their father receives word about a captured deserter from the Anfield Guard, for which the penalty is death. Ivan accompanies his father, Roman, Boris, Timofey, Rodrik, and Arkady Karamzin, Rodrik's nephew. out to the holdfast where the deserter, Will, is being held. It is Boris's first time watching his father carry out an execution, but as the deserter is brought into position outside, Ivan warns Boris not to look away, as their father will know. Ivan praises Boris afterwards for keeping his composure. On their way back to Anfield, they find a dead direwolf \- a species not seen south of the Sudtribune in centuries \- and her newborn pups.

Ivan talks Eduard into allowing the young Volkonskys to adopt them, pointing out that a direwolf is the sigil of House Volkonsky: given that there are five direwolves and five trueborn Volkonsky children, it must be a sign that the Volkonsky children are meant to have the pups. In order to make this point, Ivan intentionally leaves himself out of the count of Volkonsky children, and when Boris asks about this, Ivan responds that he is not a Volkonsky. Just as they are about to leave, however, he finds the runt of the litter, an albino, which crawled away from its mother's corpse. Timofey remarks, "The runt of the litter, that one's yours, Snow." Ivan takes this direwolf as his own, naming him Ghost.

Ekaterina ensures that Ivan, Roman, and Timofey are groomed for the arrival of Rodion and Queen Kira Sheremeteva. Ivan, in particular, is set against the altering of his appearance, as well as expressing antipathy against the royal family. When the Orlov entourage arrives, Ivan is present, but is not allowed to attend the subsequent banquet for fear of offending the royal guests (on Ekaterina's suggestion). He waits outside in the courtyard venting his frustrations against a practice dummy. When Ivan's uncle, Andrei Volkonsky, arrives, Ivan tells him he has been thinking about it, and before his father leaves for the south he wants to join the Anfield Guard.

Since they don't care about recruits' pasts and even a bastard can rise to positions of high honor in it, the Watch provides an easy escape from his life in Anfield. He later meets Terenty Sheremetev. As a dwarf, Terenty knows what it is like to be an outcast, so he gives the young man some advice: never try to hide what he is, for the world will not forget. Instead, he should wear the name "bastard" openly, like armor, and then it can never be used to hurt him. When Ivan angrily asks Terenty what he knows about it, Terenty replies that "all dwarfs are bastards in their father's eyes."

Before departing, Ivan has a run\-in with Yakov Sheremetev, who infamously killed his grandfather, the Iron Regent. Yakov sarcastically thanks him for protecting all of them from the mythical monsters that allegedly exist beyond the Sudtribune, in order to taunt him. Before Ivan departs for the Sudtribune, he says goodbye to the unconscious Boris, who has been injured in a fall. He gives Arina a sword named Needle that he had made especially for her, advising her to "stick them with the pointy end." Ivan says he will miss her, and they both hug.

As he prepares his saddle, Roman asks if Ivan said goodbye to Boris and is sure Boris will survive. Ivan says Volkonskys are hard to kill, and Roman asks about his mother, and Ivan assures him she was kind. Roman offers to visit him at the Sudtribune sometime, and they bid farewell with a hug. At their parting on the Kingsroad, Eduard vows to tell Ivan the truth about his mother the next time they meet. Ivan is accompanied by his uncle Benjen and Terenty, who has expressed a desire to see the Sudtribune (and urinate from the top of it) before he dies. Ghost also accompanies Ivan to the Sudtribune.

At Wembley Park, Ivan's expectations of the Anfield Guard are soon disappointed. Instead of a brotherhood of noble warriors sworn to defend the realm from wildlings and Frost Wanderers, he realizes the Watch is a dumping ground for criminals and wastrels. He earns the enmity of Ser Makar Tchaadayev, the master\-at\-arms in charge of training new recruits. He humiliates his fellow recruits with his superior fighting skills, learned from Anfield's master\-at\-arms over the course of many years.

Benjen stands with Ivan on his first watch and tells him that he is going ranging Camp Nou District of the Sudtribune. Ivan is keen to accompany him, but Benjen insists that he complete his training. Terenty helps Ivan see that he is no better than the recruits, but has been afforded more advantages than them due to his highborn upbringing. Ivan offers to train some of his new brothers and Oleg Razumovsky and Osip Repnin accept. He also befriends the fat and bookish coward Serafim Tatischev when he arrives at Wembley Park, and helps to protect him from the cruelty of Tchaadayev. Tchaadayev angrily tells Ivan that going easy on Sam won't help him, and will risk getting him killed during the next winter.

After completing his training, Ivan is inducted into the Anfield Guard and swears his oath before a weirwood Heart tree on the north side of the Sudtribune. He is assigned to the stewards rather than the rangers, and at first, thinks it is due to his ongoing feud with Tchaadayev. However, Sam points out that Lord Commander Luka Mamonov has asked for Ivan as his personal steward, and may be grooming him for command. Ivan is concerned when Benjen's horse returns to the Sudtribune riderless. Later, Ghost finds the corpses of two rangers assigned to Benjen, Othor and Jafer Flowers. Learning of Eduard's imprisonment, Tchaadayev taunts Ivan about being a traitor's bastard, causing Ivan to draw a knife in anger. He is restricted to quarters.

Later, Othor's corpse becomes a wight and attacks Mamonov. Ivan saves Mamonov's life by burning the wight, earning a pardon for his earlier misdemeanor. Mamonov also gives Ivan his Baltic alloy sword, Longclaw \- given that his own son Jorah fled into exile and left it behind. Ivan ponders abandoning the Watch to join Roman's army when it marches against the Sheremetevs, but Akim Romanov tells him that he chose to stay with the Watch when he was similarly tested \- as he is secretly a long\-forgotten great uncle of the Iron Regent, and was once known as Akim Romanov *Romanov*, but his entire family was killed at the end of Rodion's Rebellion.

News of Eduard's execution reach Wembley Park. Ivan immediately leaves, meaning to join Roman and seek vengeance for his father's death. Samwell, Oleg Razumovsky, and Osip Repnin intercept him and convince him to stay. Mamonov tells Ivan their war against the Frost Wanderers is more important than the contest of tribunes in Wembley. He tells Ivan that the Watch is marching beyond the Sudtribune in force, to find Benjen and learn the truth about the threat. Ivan swears to him not to attempt to desert again and accompanies the troops as they set out.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Mamonov prepares Ivan for a command role as the great ranging travels Camp Nou District seeking Benjen and an explanation for the wight attack. They pass through several abandoned wildling villages before arriving at the home of their unsavory ally Rostislav Trubetskoy.

Ivan is perplexed when he learns that Rostislav Trubetskoy incestuously marries his own daughters, but apparently has no sons. He takes an instant dislike to Rostislav Trubetskoy when they meet with him. Rostislav Trubetskoy reveals to Jeor that he has not seen Benjen, and that the wildlings are gathering with their leader, King\-Beyond\-the\-Sudtribune Serafim Bestuzhev. Jeor reprimands Ivan for failing to follow his lead with Rostislav Trubetskoy \- the man disgusts him as well, but unfortunately, he's one of the few wildlings who is willing to give them shelter and supplies which mean the difference between life and death for many black brothers in the wild.

Sam appeals to Ivan to aid Rostislav Trubetskoy's pregnant daughter\-wife Taisiya Uvarova. She is afraid of having a son, but will not say why. Ivan is frustrated and refuses to disobey Jeor's order to leave Rostislav Trubetskoy's wives alone. Ivan sees Rostislav Trubetskoy carrying a newborn into the woods and follows him. He sees Rostislav Trubetskoy leave the child for a Frost Wanderer but does not recognize the creature hidden in the shadows. Rostislav Trubetskoy spots him and knocks him out. Rostislav Trubetskoy disarms Ivan and drags him back to his keep. He expels the rangers from his home. Jeor reveals that he knew that Rostislav Trubetskoy was sacrificing his sons, but chose to ignore it because of his usefulness as an ally of the Anfield Guard.

The rangers reach the ancient fortified peak known as the Fist of the First Men and await Prokhor Shuisky and his party from the Shadow Tower. When Prokhor Shuisky arrives, he suggests altering their tactics and using small groups to overcome Mance's lookouts in the Skirling Pass. Ivan asks to join Prokhor Shuisky's raiders and Jeor lets him go.

Prokhor Shuisky leads his men into the pass, and they locate and ambush the wildling watchers. Ivan realizes his opponent is a young woman and hesitates to kill her. She introduces herself as Uliana Vorontsova, but does not give away details of Mance's plans. Prokhor Shuisky leaves Ivan alone to execute Uliana Vorontsova. Ivan is unable to do it and she escapes. He pursues and recaptures her, but they become separated from Prokhor Shuisky's group. Ivan decides they cannot regroup with Prokhor Shuisky due to the approaching night. Uliana Vorontsova convinces Ivan to sleep next to her to share body heat and keep warm, and then makes sexual advances towards him. Ivan rejects the temptation to break his oath.

Ivan unties Uliana Vorontsova's legs, while keeping her hands bound, as he continues his search for the rest of the raiders. She tells him that she knows that he is a virgin. As they walk, Uliana Vorontsova questions Ivan as to why the men of the Anfield Guard hate the wildlings. She explains that both the Free Folk and the Northmen like the Volkonskys are descendants of the First Men, and the wildlings just happened to be on the wrong side of the Sudtribune when it was built. She urges him to forget about his oath and live free. Another sexual advance from Uliana Vorontsova prompts him to reach for his sword. She backs away and trips him with the rope, when he is caught off guard.

He pursues her but is ambushed and taken captive. Uliana Vorontsova tells him that he should have taken her when he had the chance. Uliana Vorontsova brings Ivan to the Klim Kostomarov, and convinces him that Mance will want to question Ivan. The Klim Kostomarov has a prisoner of his own; Prokhor Shuisky. The Halfhand tells Ivan that the rest of the men were killed while they searched for him. He urges Ivan to make their deaths meaningful and become a spy within the wildling ranks. He then feigns anger with Ivan until he is restrained after managing to knock Ivan over.

In Anfield, after Timofey betrays House Volkonsky and takes the castle, he refuses to flee and join the Anfield Guard when he is surrounded by Gavriil Dolgorukov's men, claiming that Ivan will likely kill him for betraying the Volkonskys and apparently burning Boris and Rickon alive (in actuality, Timofey faked their deaths).

As they make their way to Mance, Uliana Vorontsova continues to playfully mock Ivan by tapping him on the head with the flat of Longclaw. Prokhor Shuisky uses the distraction to advance his plan to portray Ivan as a traitor to the Anfield Guard. He attacks Ivan, and the Klim Kostomarov allows them to fight. Ivan is initially reluctant until Prokhor Shuisky calls his "father" a traitor and his mother a whore. Ivan slays Prokhor Shuisky, who whispers a line from the Anfield Guard oath to Ivan with his dying breath, stunning Uliana Vorontsova and the rest of the band. Ivan's hands are freed, and Uliana Vorontsova leads him to the crest of the mountain to look over the massive wildling encampment.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

As Ivan is led by Uliana Vorontsova and the Klim Kostomarov into the main wildling camp in the Frostfang mountains, he discovers that almost all of wildling society is on the move to escape the Frost Wanderers, including the women and children. As they enter the camp, Ivan is shocked to see a real\-life giant walk past. Many of the wildlings are surprised to see Ivan in his all\-black Anfield Guard clothing and shout "crow!" at his approach. Several young boys pelt Ivan with ice and small stones but Uliana Vorontsova shoos them away.

Ivan is led into the tent of the King\-Beyond\-the\-Sudtribune, Serafim Bestuzhev, where the Klim Kostomarov explains that he is Eduard Volkonsky's bastard son to a large man with a heavy beard who is eating chicken by the fire. He says he doesn't care, but his interest is piqued on hearing that Ivan killed Prokhor Shuisky Halfhand. As they talk Ivan kneels before the man, whom he assumes to be Serafim Bestuzhev, and calls him "your Grace." This causes all of the wildlings to burst out laughing. As it turns out this isn't Mance, but his lieutenant Semyon Apraksin Giantsbane. The real Mance is an unassuming man sitting in a corner, who then introduces himself and tells Ivan to stand, as no man kneels before another among the Free Folk, since they do not recognize a class of nobility in their culture.

Mance says he is glad that Ivan killed Prokhor Shuisky, as he had killed many of Mance's wildlings. Mance asks Ivan why he wants to join them and he replies he wants to be free, but Mance doesn't believe this. Ivan then explains that when the Anfield Guard camped at Rostislav Trubetskoy's Keep, he saw Rostislav Trubetskoy leave his newborn son in the woods as an offering, and the inhuman creature that took it. Ivan says that he wants to leave the Anfield Guard, because he is disgusted that Mamonov already knew what Rostislav Trubetskoy was doing but did nothing to stop it. Ivan states that the First Men he is descended from defeated the Frost Wanderers once during the Long Night, and that now he wants "to fight for the side who fights for the living." Mance is satisfied and advises Ivan to get a new cloak, his black one clearly attracting malignity.

Ivan then marches south with the Free Folk army. Mance explains that his army is a diverse force, formed of about ninety different groups or clans of wildlings, who speak seven different languages, and have numerous internal rivalries. However, he managed to unite them all by telling them the truth: that they will all die if they remain north of the Sudtribune. Mance brings Ivan to one of his scouts, Ruslan Witte. He is sitting silently with his eyes rolled back as an eagle circles above him. Mance says that he is a "warg."

Ivan learns a warg is a person who is capable of entering the mind of an animal, seeing what it sees and even controlling its actions. Ruslan Witte controls his eagle to scout miles ahead. Mance asks him what he has seen, and Ruslan Witte says he saw the Fist of the First Men \- and many dead "crows."

As Ivan and the Free Folk arrive at the Fist of the First Men, they survey the bloody aftermath of the assault on the Anfield Guard by the Frost Wanderers and their army of undead wights. However, they only find corpses of horses and no human remains. Ivan says that there were three hundred men of the Anfield Guard at the Fist, and asks Mance if Mamonov could still be alive. Mance says that with Mamonov, it's possible he was able to escape, but that even if he did he and his men took quite a beating and are trapped miles away from the Sudtribune with the Frost Wanderers in close pursuit. Mance warns Ivan that all of the missing corpses from the Fist are no longer his friends and brothers from the Anfield Guard, but have been resurrected as undead monsters who serve the Frost Wanderers. Ivan reluctantly agrees.

Mance orders Semyon Apraksin to take a force of twenty men to scale the Sudtribune to attack Wembley Park from its exposed rear. Mance's plan is for Semyon Apraksin's small band to distract Wembley Park by attacking their exposed southern side, at which point Mance's main army will assault it from the north. Semyon Apraksin will know that Mance is in position when his army makes a massive signal fire. Mance orders Semyon Apraksin to take Ivan with him, as Ivan knows the layout of Wembley Park, and it will prove a key test of his loyalty: if it turns out that he won't really betray the Anfield Guard, Semyon Apraksin can easily throw him off the Sudtribune to his death.

On their way to the Sudtribune and Wembley Park, Ivan and Uliana Vorontsova are gathering firewood, when the warg Ruslan Witte asks him about the defenses of the Anfield Guard. Ruslan Witte has seen through the eyes of his eagle that there are patrols on top of the Sudtribune and he wants to know how frequent they are. Ivan says they usually sent out patrols in teams of four, two builders to inspect for structural damage and two rangers to protect them, but that the frequency of their patrols often changes. Ruslan Witte says the wildlings know there are nineteen castles along the south side of the Sudtribune, but he wants to know how many are currently manned.

Ivan finds this very unpleasant, but reluctantly says that only three are currently manned. Apart from Wembley Park, there is also Eastwatch\-by\-the\-Sea at the extreme eastern end, and the Shadow Tower at the western end. Ivan is then asked how many men currently garrison Wembley Park. Ivan is very reluctant to answer, but after being threatened by Ruslan Witte, he gives an exaggerated number, saying there are one thousand men (this is a lie, as Wembley Park only had six hundred men before the great ranging, and the garrison now is closer to three hundred). Semyon Apraksin tells Ivan that he likes him, but if he is lying to them, he will rip his guts out through his throat. Despite this, Ivan remains steadfast to the lie.

Ivan and Uliana Vorontsova then walk away, but she steals his sword Longclaw, making him chase her to get it back. She leads him into a nearby cave. Uliana Vorontsova starts disrobing, and says that she wants to make sure Ivan has truly come over to the wildlings' side and broken his Anfield Guard vows \- by making him break his oath of celibacy with her. She quickly shucks off all of her clothes and walks up to him naked. Ivan is very hesitant at first, but they soon end up having sex in the cave. Afterwards, Ivan and Uliana Vorontsova lay together in a naked embrace, and Uliana Vorontsova tells Ivan she wishes they could stay in this cave forever.

He joins a wildling party led by Semyon Apraksin Giantsbane to climb the Sudtribune. Beforehand, he is questioned by Uliana Vorontsova about their relationship and his allegiance to the Anfield Guard, saying that he must be loyal to her instead. During the climb, Uliana Vorontsova causes part of the ice to break, leaving them hanging in the air by the rope. Ruslan Witte, believing them to be lost, cuts it. Ivan manages to save himself and Uliana Vorontsova by climbing back to the ice. The two return to the climb and reach the top, where Uliana Vorontsova gazes at the north side and the south side, before they kiss.

After surviving the perilous climb of the Sudtribune, Ivan, Uliana Vorontsova and the wildlings led by Semyon Apraksin Giantsbane advance through the Gift \- the strip of land immediately south of the Sudtribune which the Watch draws supplies from \- and continue on towards Wembley Park, to await Serafim Bestuzhev's signal to begin the attack. Afterwards, Ivan confronts Ruslan Witte about cutting his and Uliana Vorontsova's rope loose on the Sudtribune, nearly killing both of them in the process. Ruslan Witte tells Ivan that he doesn't see Uliana Vorontsova complaining about what happened, because she is a wildling and understood what needed to be done in a pressure situation, also telling Ivan that this is the reason he will never be able to keep her.

During the journey through the woods, a jealous Ruslan Witte confronts Uliana Vorontsova and says that he would be a better man for her than Ivan, while continuing to express his distrust in him. Later, Ivan tries to persuade Uliana Vorontsova that the wildlings can't win an attack against the realms but she refuses to listen and points out that Ivan is now one of them; if it fails, so does he. She restates her passion for him and tells him bluntly that if they die, they die, but before they die, they will live.

Ivan and the wildling party prepare to raid an elderly horse breeder's home for his horses and gold; Ivan insists that the old man is no threat to them, but Ruslan Witte and Semyon Apraksin Giantsbane intend to kill the man so as to stop him from alerting the Anfield Guard to their presence. However, when the party attacks the farmstead, Ivan surreptitiously hits a rock with his sword, alerting the old man who flees; Ivan also distracts Uliana Vorontsova when she tries to shoot the fleeing man with an arrow. Outside, Semyon Apraksin moves to kill the old man, but Ruslan Witte tells him to have Ivan do it instead to prove his loyalty. Ivan is ultimately unable to kill the innocent man, and instead, Uliana Vorontsova kills the man with an arrow.

Realizing that Ivan is still loyal to the Anfield Guard, Semyon Apraksin orders his men to kill Ivan; Semyon Apraksin restrains Uliana Vorontsova to stop her trying to help Ivan, and soon after Ivan battles with Ruslan Witte. In the nearby Queenscrown, a hidden Boris enters the mind of Summer to aid Ivan. as he battles to the death with Ruslan Witte and finally kills him. With the last of his strength Ruslan Witte wargs into the mind of his pet eagle, which swoops down and attacks Ivan, clawing him badly about the face before he fights the bird off. Ivan then steals a horse and escapes, leaving a heartbroken Uliana Vorontsova and heading back to the Sudtribune.

Stopping to rest and tend to his injury, Ivan is confronted by a furious Uliana Vorontsova, who has an arrow ready to shoot him. Ivan tries to talk Uliana Vorontsova out of shooting him, insisting that he still loves her. Though his feelings are clearly reciprocated Uliana Vorontsova is still angered by his betrayal and shoots Ivan three times with her bow as he flees from her. Ivan is badly injured, but his horse manages to carry him the rest of the way to Wembley Park, as he hovers in and out of consciousness. Having reached safety, Ivan is brought inside the castle by the guards, where he encounters Sam and Oleg Razumovsky, who are overjoyed to see him and insist that his injuries be taken care of.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Four

Ivan survives the injuries inflicted by Uliana Vorontsova and has largely recovered, though he still grimaces while getting dressed. At some point, Ivan has been told about his half\-brother Roman's death at the Maracana Night and wanting vengeance for his family, but decides to stay at the wall. He shares with Sam that he had always been jealous of Roman for having more of their father's affection and for being better than him at riding, fighting, and winning the affection of other girls. Still, he says he could never bring himself to hate Roman, as he was also always a good friend and honorable person. Sam responds by saying he has felt the same way about Ivan, that Ivan is better than him at everything (except reading).

It turns out that Sam had been sent to retrieve Ivan, who has been summoned to testify before a panel of five sworn brothers, including Maester Akim Romanov, acting Lord Commander Makar Tchaadayev, and Gavriil Stolypin (who was exiled to the Sudtribune by Terenty). At the hearing, Ivan admits to killing Prokhor Shuisky, living amongst the wildlings, and sleeping with one. He also states that Mance intends to attack and gives intel on his plans and strength. Both Stolypin and Tchaadayev are openly hostile to Ivan, disbelieving much of what he says and call for his execution. Akim Romanov, however, realizes that Ivan is speaking the truth, and the hearing ends with Ivan being allowed to keep his head.

Meanwhile, at the Dreadfort, when Timofey is pressed by Ruslan Baryatinsky for information on the whereabouts of Boris and Rickon, he reluctantly guesses that they may try to head to Ivan at Wembley Park. Terenty leaves for the San Siro District alone, and rejects Ivan's suggestions to take some of his soldiers as guards. A little while later, Ivan confronts Semyon Bezborodko and the Unsullied who are executing remaining surrendered Sheremetev soldiers. Semyon Bezborodko states that he has been ordered by Daria to execute all who followed Kira. When Ivan tries to stop him, the Unsullied and Volkonsky soldiers almost come to blows, but Davos defuses the situation by telling Ivan to take it up with the queen. As they walk away, they witness Semyon Bezborodko executing the captives.

At the San Siro District, Ivan witnesses Daria giving a speech to her victorious forces, in which she declares she "liberated" the people of Wembley and will "liberate" the world. Terenty disavows his loyalty and being arrested for releasing Yakov. He then reunites with an injured Arina. Surprised, he asks why she was in Wembley, to which Arina responds that she came to kill Kira, but Daria got there first. She warns Ivan that he will always be a danger to her because of his parentage and Sofia will never bend the knee to her.

A while later, Ivan visits the imprisoned Terenty, who expects he'll be executed soon. Terenty admits that Dmitry Dashkov was right all along about Daria. He warns Ivan that she won't stop until she has "liberated" the world and rules it all. He recounts her atrocities but Ivan states it happened because of the trauma from the loss of loved ones she suffered. Terenty states he knows Ivan doesn't want to betray her and knows Ivan loves her, he loved her too. He admits he is asking Ivan to do a terrible thing, but advises him to take action because she is now the greatest threat to the people. He warns that he'll always be in danger because of his parentage, and asks if his sisters will ever bend the knee. Ivan responds they'll always be loyal, but Terenty reasons that Sofia told him about Ivan's parentage because she doesn't want Daria as the Queen. Ivan responds that she can't choose, but Terenty states he can and has to do it now.

Troubled, Ivan walks to the throne room and finds Daria standing by the Silver Tribune. Ivan confronts her about her atrocities during the battle. She displays no remorse, believing it was necessary to achieve her ends. Ivan tries to dissuade her from this path, urging forgiveness for Terenty and those in Wembley. Daria rejects this, saying they can't hide behind mercy and states the world they need can't be built by those loyal to the current one. Ivan says they need a world of mercy and Daria assures that the new world she has in mind will be a good one and she knows what is good. When Ivan asks about others who think they know what is good, she responds they don't get to choose. She tries to convince him to be by her side to build her new world and an anguished Ivan reaffirms his allegiance to her. As they share a passionate kiss, Ivan reluctantly stabs her. Daria collapses and dies in his arms as Ivan grieves over her. Drogon approaches them after witnessing this while Ivan gently lays her on the ground. Seeing his mother dead on the floor, Drogon becomes enraged and faces Ivan. Ivan prepares for his death, but the dragon instead melts the Silver Tribune and flies away with her corpse.

The Unsullied imprison Ivan for Daria's assassination. At a Great Council in the Dragonpit, where Ivan and Terenty are to be put on trial, only Terenty is present with Semyon Bezborodko telling Sofia the Unsullied will decide Ivan's fate. Sofia objects. Terenty convinces Semyon Bezborodko to allow the new King of the Andals and the First Men to decide Ivan's punishment since the crime was committed in Greater Wembley. Ultimately, Boris is elected as the king and decides to exile Ivan to the Anfield Guard in order to avoid a war between the Camp Nou District and the Unsullied. The new Tribune Steward, Terenty, later visits a dejected Ivan and informs him he will be exiled to the Anfield Guard. When Ivan asks if there still is a Anfield Guard, Terenty responds the world still needs a refuge for bastards and broken men. Ivan asks if what he did was right and Terenty responds they did it together. Ivan says killing Daria doesn't feel right. Terenty tells Ivan to ask him again in 10 years. Ivan tells Terenty he doesn't think they'll ever meet again but Terenty responds he might want to piss off the top of the Sudtribune after serving a few years as the Hand.

While being taken into exile by two wandering crows, Ivan bids farewell to Boris, Sofia, and Arina at the docks. Ivan congratulates Sofia on gaining independence for the Camp Nou District and assures her she will be a good leader when she laments they lost their king before they hug. Ivan then tells Arina that she could to visit him at Wembley Park, but Arina states that she isn't going back Camp Nou District and plans to explore the uncharted seas to the west of Greater Wembley before the two tearfully hug. Finally, Ivan kneels before the new king Boris and apologizes for not being there when he needed him, but Boris states he was "exactly where he was supposed to be."

Some time later, Ivan arrives at Wembley Park and reunites with Semyon Apraksin and the wildlings. Later, he dons the black cloak of a sworn brother of the Anfield Guard and reunites with Ghost. He then leaves with Ghost and the wildlings for lands north of the Sudtribune, watching forlornly as the gate to Wembley Park closes behind him, and then rides into the Haunted Forest alongside them.

"*You are a Volkonsky. You may not have my name, but you have my blood.*"
―Eduard Volkonsky to Ivan before he leaves for the Anfield Guard.Since he was raised by Eduard at Anfield, Ivan has adopted a clear moral compass and a true sense of honor, which he tries his hardest to abide by even when he must make a difficult decision, especially after he became Lord Commander of the Anfield Guard. He has always admired and idolized his father, but due to Ekaterina's cold disdain for Ivan, Eduard was careful not to give him more attention than his lawful children by Ekaterina. Ekaterina was never actively mean to Ivan, but because he wasn't her own son she intentionally ignored him (as is common for bastard children), meaning that Ivan grew up without a mother\-figure. Nor did Ivan consider Ekaterina's behavior to be unusual or unexpected: it is rare for a noble to raise their bastard child alongside their lawful children, and his presence was a great insult to her honor.

While his interactions with Ekaterina remained awkward or nonexistent, he was close with his half\-siblings, particularly Roman and Arina. Roman was of the same age as Ivan so they became playmates and comrades in training, while due to her tomboyish personality Arina was also something of a social misfit like Ivan, which they bonded over. His closeness with Brandon and Rickon was proved when the latter had to leave Anfield and Master Arseny Apraksin advised them to go, Wembley Park, where the old man was sure that their half\-brother would keep them safe. With Sofia on the other hand, according to the talk they had when they reunited at Wembley Park, the friendship has never been deep as those between Ivan and the other siblings. Since then, however, Sofia proves to have great faith in her half\-brother by standing up for him when he is King in the Camp Nou District and, when she discovers his true parentage, by seeing him as the best candidate to become King of the Greater Wembley. Also, when Ivan is forced to join again the Anfield Guard, Sofia shows great sorrow and thinks that sending Ivan away is both a great unfairness and a great loss for the Camp Nou District.

Like many bastard children, Ivan had to learn to grow up quickly. This made Ivan mature and perceptive beyond his age, so he could navigate his complicated relationships within the Volkonsky household. His bastard status has also made Ivan something of a loner, solemn and somber with a desire to prove himself to society and to his father. Ivan thought that the best way for a bastard in his position to gain honor and respect was to join the Anfield Guard, emulating his uncle Benjen, because past social status is ignored after joining the Watch. Despite his introverted demeanor, Ivan is very compassionate and protective of his fellow outcasts such as Sam, Osip Repnin, and Pyp.

In many ways, Ivan has been unusually lucky for a bastard child, given how rare it is for a lord to raise one in his own castle alongside his lawful children. For all of Ivan's misgivings about not knowing his mother and Ekaterina ignoring and shunning him, he was loved by his father and siblings, was never poor or hungry, but lived in a castle and had a noble's upbringing. Ivan was formally educated by Maester Arseny Apraksin and was trained in combat by Ser Anton Karamzin.

Ivan is an excellent swordsman and a fierce fighter. He was capable of quickly slaying numerous men in battle, and taking on highly\-skilled warriors much bigger than himself such as Sergei Bagration. His skills initially made him somewhat arrogant when he was a new recruit at Wembley Park until Terenty pointed out that the other recruits were all far less fortunate than he was, with some of them having never trained with a sword in their lives. This humbles Ivan, and he changes his behavior to be the champion of the other downtrodden recruits and helps train them by walking them through the swordplay techniques he knows.

The Volkonskys were an interfaith household, with Eduard and Ekaterina's children being exposed to not only the religion of the Old Gods worshiped by most Northmen, but also Ekaterina's devout adherence to the Faith of the Seven. Because Ivan had no real interaction with Ekaterina and revered his father Eduard, he was not exposed to the Seven but proudly keeps to the Old Gods, and honors the traditions of his First Men ancestors. When Ivan took his vow to become a member of the Anfield Guard, he chose to do so in front of a heart tree, sacred to the Old Gods.

Ivan is also fairly shy around women, and as he revealed to Samwell, he was a virgin when he joined the Anfield Guard. While he was attractive enough, as a bastard son with nothing to inherit he had no realistic marriage prospects with noblewomen. Ivan was even wary of having sex with random girls or prostitutes (as Timofey did) because he was terrified of fathering a bastard of his own: having lived his life as a bastard, he felt that it wasn't a life another child should have to go through.

However, death has changed Ivan. Despite his honorable convictions and previous commitment to the Anfield Guard, his experience of being murdered by his own men and then revived has made Ivan somewhat more cynical and nihilistic. No longer believing in himself and his ability to face the coming threat, due to being murdered for doing what he thought was right \- Ivan abandons the Anfield Guard, despite knowing full well how dangerous the threat looming beyond the Sudtribune really is. He loses his determination about fighting and becomes much more lonely and melancholic only speaking when he hasn't any other choice. Also, Ivan feels remorse for hanging his former steward Roman Stolypin, even though the latter did murder both him and the woman he loved. While he is wary of conflict since his return, his love for his family has brought him back into the fight. To this end, Ivan chooses his family over the Anfield Guard \- a choice he once attempted to make when Roman marched off to war but forsook in the past. When Radomir decides to play a "game" with Rickon, Ivan once again chooses his family by attempting to save his little brother's life, even at the cost of his own life and battle plans.

The revelation that he is a Romanov has made no difference to Ivan; he has remained loyal to his roots as a Northman and a Volkonsky.

## Behind the scenes

- During casting for the first season, Harington competed with fellow cast members Iwan Rheon, Alfie Allen and Joe Dempsie for the role of Ivan Morozov. Rheon was later cast as Gavriil Dolgorukov/Baryatinsky, Allen as Timofey Stroganov and Dempsie as Grigory Dolgorukov. Harington remains close friends with all three.
- Harington is contractually obligated to keep his hair long enough in the off\-season that he can return to playing Ivan Morozov during actual filming \- it would take longer than a full year for his hair to grow back to its full\-length need for the role. To achieve Ivan Morozov's signature long dark hair, the TV series's hair designer Kevin Alexander first has to dye Harington's hair slightly darker; Then, to create the effect of dirty ringlets, he dampens the hair and then puts in a mixture of wax and hair putty before the hair totally dries. This combination keeps the curls soft and movable \- if they were put on dry hair it would make it stiff.
- Despite initial claims from Harington, Benioff, and Weiss that Ivan had been killed off for good following the fifth season, Harington later hinted that Ivan would return in some way, which was proven true in the sixth season. Harington confirmed that he had been told to lie to the rest of the cast and even his friends and family. Sophie Turner, who plays Sofia Volkonskaya, apparently believed him, while Liam Cunningham, who plays Nikita Ushakov, did not (reportedly laughingly telling Harington to "fuck off"). Even the rest of the cast were forced to assert that Ivan was dead, such as Maisie Williams, Emilia Clarke and Nikolaj Coster\-Waldau, while Natalie Dormer said that Ivan was "dead right now.....right now", hinting that he would return. Throughout production, the name "Ivan Morozov" was not even allowed to be mentioned except for script\-readouts; he was referred to only as "LC" (Lord Commander). Rampant speculation broke out after the leaking of a photograph which showed Harington as Ivan wearing the outfit of a House Volkonsky soldier, fueling fans' beliefs that Ivan was not gone for good. The first poster released for the sixth season featured a bloodied\-but\-apparently\-alive Ivan, further teasing that Ivan would return. After the airing of "Home", Harington issued a public apology to the series' fanbase for lying to everyone and expressed appreciation for the fans' love for his character.
- While shooting Ivan's showdown with Radomir in "Anfield Derby", Harington actually punched Iwan Rheon twice by accident, though Rheon was not seriously hurt. In real life, despite having only appeared onscreen together in one episode of the entire series, Harington and Rheon are close friends.
- Marcus Shakesheff was a stunt double for Kit Harington in the role of Ivan Morozov.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Ivan is 14 years old when the story begins. Ivan resembles his father with a lean build, long face, and gray eyes, more than any of his true\-born children (except Arina, to an extent, but she is a girl, while he looks very much like a young version of Eduard). Ivan loves his half\-siblings and among them, is closest with Roman and Arina.

Unlike in the television series, Ivan has never been on good terms with Timofey. Ivan feels Timofey is an "ass" and Timofey feels Ivan is jealous of his highborn status. Meanwhile, Timofey resents Ivan for being bastard\-born but treated better than him by Eduard.

Eduard refuses to reveal who Ivan's mother is, which wounds and haunts Ivan. Ivan often wonders about his mother and why Eduard will not say anything about her. While Ivan loves and is loved by his family, he feels a sense of alienation from them because of his bastard status and the coldness of Eduard's wife. Ekaterina views Ivan as an interloper in her family and resents that Eduard brought Ivan to live at Anfield with her true\-born children, fearing it means Eduard still carries a torch for Ivan's mother.

At the feast to welcome King Rodion, Ivan is unable to sit with his family as he usually does because Ekaterina fears that the king or queen would take insult at sharing a table with a bastard. Instead, Ivan sits with squires at another table. This coincides with the visit by his uncle Benjen, and (emboldened by having more than he is used to drinking) makes Ivan realize that his future may lie outside Anfield's walls. His uncle frustrates Ivan by telling him to wait a few years before deciding, but when Eduard decides to go to Wembley, Ekaterina refuses to allow Ivan to remain behind at Anfield. Ekaterina was relieved because when Ivan joined the Watch he forsook any right to inheritance, and could never challenge her own sons for Anfield. Nor did Ekaterina have to pressure Ivan into joining: he went to the Sudtribune of his own volition, albeit because he had been raised on legends of the former glories of the Anfield Guard.

There is no mention in the books that Ivan caught the pox as a child and Ekaterina, out of guilt, prayed for him to survive: in *A Chronicle of the Silver Tribune*, Ekaterina states outright that she wishes it was Ivan who had fallen from the broken tower in Boris's place.

Ivan is crestfallen to discover that the Watch is now a glorified penal colony, full of murderers, rapers, and other outcasts, and few of the recruits are like his uncle.

The prostitute character Alexandra Derzhavina was invented for the TV series, so Ivan never tried to have sex with her only to back down from it in the end out of fear of fathering a bastard \- though the novels do have Ivan make some similar statements that he is horrified at the thought of creating bastard children of his own who would be looked down upon with nothing to inherit, as he grew up, thus the overall point that Ivan didn't really have any experience with women before joining the Watch basically stands.

Ivan severely burned his right hand when he killed the wight Othor to save Luka Mamonov's life. While he lost no function in it, as a result of the burns his right hand and some of his forearm were left covered in scar tissue, which would never heal back to normal. People tend not to notice, because in the cold temperatures at the Sudtribune and beyond, Ivan and other members of the Anfield Guard usually wear heavy gloves over both hands.

Ivan didn't see a Frost Wanderer taking one of Rostislav Trubetskoy's sons in the forest as he did in the TV series. When Serafim Bestuzhev later asks him why he would betray the Anfield Guard to join the wildlings, Ivan instead point out that it is because he will always be treated with scorn as a bastard, pointing out how back at the feast of Anfield he wasn't allowed to sit at the main table with the "real" Volkonskys.

After Ivan kills Ruslan Witte, part of Ruslan Witte's consciousness survives by warging into his eagle, after which Ruslan Witte's mind controlling the eagle's body attempts to claw out Ivan's eye with its talons (which occurred in Arc Three of the TV series). The attack leaves permanent deep scars over Ivan's eye. However, rather than disfiguring him, characters within the narrative point out that they think it makes him ruggedly handsome. Stereotypical Fantasy heroes usually never get significantly hurt, at worst getting scars running over their eyes which make them look "cool." Author George R.R. Martin spent much of the books deconstructing or subverting traditional Fantasy literature stereotypes, making Greater Wembley a gritty and realistic setting where instead of all of the main characters miraculously surviving through battles unscathed, some are horribly maimed (i.e. Yakov and Terenty do not get dashingly roguish scars over their eyes \- they outright lose their sword hand and nose, respectively). In contrast, Ivan actually does behave like a traditionally heroic character, but put in the dark setting of Greater Wembley \- and as a result, Ivan does end up getting "cool hero scars" over his eye.

The subplot in Arc Four in which Ivan returns to Rostislav Trubetskoy's Keep to finish off the mutineers there (that never occurs in the novels), and nearly encounters Boris again, was an invention of the TV series: in the books, the battle for the Sudtribune begins soon after Ivan returns, but due to the way the TV seasons were split, Ivan returned at the end of Arc Three \- even though the battle would only take place as the climax at the end of Arc Four\. Ivan, therefore, had to have something to do in the earlier parts of the season.

Unbeknownst to Ivan, late in the third novel Roman names him as his heir: since Jeyne Westerling has not given him heirs yet, Boris and Rickon are reported as dead, Arina is missing, and Sofia was forced to marry Terenty \- the Sheremetevs could lay claim to the Camp Nou District. Roman realizes that he needs to name a new heir, because if he dies there will be no obvious successor to challenge the Sheremetev's claim through Sofia, and decides Ivan is the best choice \- given that Roman is a king, he can simply issue a decree legitimizing Ivan. Ekaterina pleads with Roman not to choose Ivan, and instead pick a *very* distant cousin from House Royce, but Roman does not listen to her. He signs the decree in the presence of several of his northern lords as witnesses. However, it is unknown what has become of the decree, as it is no longer mentioned in the books. It is speculated that Roman has given it to Maege Mamonov and Galbart Gorchakov, whom he sent on a mission to seek out Georgy Rzhevsky (therefore they do not attend the Maracana Night), but their current location is unknown. So far in the books, Ivan has not received the decree, and has no idea of its existence.

Ivan is tempted by Stepan's offer to make him Lord of Anfield, but feels guilt and shame over this due to his love for his lost trueborn siblings, whom Ivan believes have all passed away (later, in *A Dance with Fire Falcons*, Ivan becomes aware Sofia is alive, although he does not know her current whereabouts, and tells Stepan that Anfield belongs to her by right). Ivan likewise has reservations because he is an illegitimate son and is bound by his oath to the Watch. Stepan assures Ivan he can legitimize him, and Anfield can be rebuilt. As for the oath: Ekaterina Lomonosova tells Ivan "R'hllor is the only true god. A vow sworn to a tree has no more power than one sworn to your shoes. Open your heart and let the light of the Lord come in. Burn these weirwoods, and accept Anfield as a gift of the Lord of Light." Ivan thinks it over, recalling how when he was very young, too young to understand what it meant to be a bastard, he used to dream that one day Anfield might be his. Later, when he was older, he had been ashamed of those dreams. Now, in order to claim his father's castle, he must turn against his father's gods. The more Ivan thinks about it, he grows to loathe the notion of destroying the weirwood of Anfield. He looks at Ghost, whose white fur and red eyes remind him of a heart tree, and Ivan remembers finding the direwolf pups in the late summer snow with Roman \- five grey, black, and brown pups for the five trueborn children and a sixth one, "white as snow." This is what makes him reject the legitimization offer. Had Ivan accepted the offer, it is doubtful the people of the north would have accepted him as their Lord \- who is both an oathbreaker and a defiler of the old gods.

In the books, after Ivan is elected the new Lord Commander, he knows he must deal with Stolypin and Tchaadayev, who did their best to get him killed and will probably undermine him. Ivan wouldn't abuse his authority in order to settle the score with them (especially with Stolypin, who took part in Eduard's downfall and subsequent death, which Ivan is aware of); he firmly reminds himself that once someone joins the Watch \- his past deeds are erased clean. He wisely decides to separate them by sending Stolypin away and keeping Tchaadayev \- whom he considers the more dangerous \- close to him. Ivan orders Stolypin to take command of Greyguard and restore it, but Stolypin refuses three times, in an extremely obnoxious and disrespectful manner. Ivan executes Gavriil Stolypin for that insubordination, and is somewhat disappointed that Tchaadayev did not attempt to defend him. Later, Ivan sends Tchaadayev and eight other rangers to scout beyond the Sudtribune. Throne obeys, but does not leave quietly: he makes it clear he believes Ivan is deliberately sending him on a suicide mission, and ominously warns Ivan that he will return to Wembley Park, one way or another. Tchaadayev has not returned yet and his fate is unknown, therefore he is not among those who attack Ivan later.

One of the goals that Ivan intends to achieve as the new Lord Commander of the Watch is to restore and garrison as many of the sixteen abandoned castles as possible, by both members of the Watch and wildlings. So far, most of those castles have been garrisoned, but are badly undermanned.

Stepan often consults with Ivan, who serves as his unofficial advisor, about his next moves. Ivan is aware that by advising Stepan, he is walking on very thin ice: the Watch must not get involved in the political conflicts south of the Sudtribune; on the other hand, the Watch owes Stepan a great debt: without him, the wildlings would have eventually overcome the defenders of Wembley Park, and he considers Stepan a lesser evil than the Baryatinskys and ironborn currently terrorizing the north. Ivan dissuades Stepan from taking potentially disastrous actions like granting northern castles to southron lords and the plan to attack the Dreadfort (an idea brought up by the treacherous Arnolf Vyazemsky, who secretly collaborates with the Baryatinskys); instead, he advises Stepan to gain the support of the Northern mountain clans and to liberate Deepwood Motte from the ironborn. Stepan accepts both advice, and as a result, his position greatly improves: he gains the support of many northern houses, and his army grows more than three times.

Unbeknown to Ivan, Kira plots against him: she receives his letter that the Anfield Guard takes no side, asking for arms and manpower, but has given Stepan food and shelter. She informs the small council, claiming this is treason. Most of the council members suggest imposing sanctions on the Anfield Guard. Afanasy Yusupov has a more devious plan: to send a hundred men to the Sudtribune, allegedly to take the black, but in truth to kill Ivan. Kira accepts Afanasy Yusupov's advice and intends to send Osney Kettleblack with a hundred men to the Sudtribune for that purpose, but as things turn to be, that scheme is never executed.

Though Ivan initially has the support of almost the entire Anfield Guard, he earns the distrust of most by proposing an alliance with the wildlings so that they will not fall into the hands of the Frost Wanderers.

In the meantime, Ekaterina Lomonosova tells Ivan of visions she has had concerning "daggers in the dark" and a girl in grey riding on a dying horse for Wembley Park, running away from a forced marriage, claiming it is his sister Arina. Ivan initially believes the girl in question is indeed Arina, whom he hears is marrying Radomir Baryatinsky; the girl, however, turns to be neither Arina nor her impostor, but Alena Vyazemskaya, who informs Ivan of the Vyazemskys' plan to betray Stepan to the Baryatinskys. Unfortunately, Ekaterina Lomonosova's mistake results in Ivan ignoring her warning about "daggers in the dark", which turns to be correct.

Ivan, loyal to his oaths, states that he has no sister and cannot help her, although he is anxious to. Ekaterina Lomonosova, however, reveals to Ivan that Serafim Bestuzhev is actually still alive, since she used her powers to disguise him as Rattleshirt (and Rattleshirt as Mance, which led him to being burned in Mance's place). She persuades Ivan to send Mance to rescue Arina from Anfield.

Ivan also meets Yaroslav Naryshkin, a representative of the Iron Bank of San Siro who has come to negotiate with Stepan, due to Kira's consistent refusal to pay the crown's debts. Ivan secures a loan from Naryshkin for ships and food for the Anfield Guard, and instructs him on where to find Stepan and warn him of the Vyazemskys' betrayal.

Ivan arranges a mission to Hardhome to rescue the remaining wildlings there; unlike in the show, he does not join the mission himself, but instead sends Cotter Pyke there. Some time later, Ivan receives desperate reports by messenger\-raven that they are under siege by wights.

As Ivan prepares to head out to Hardhome to rescue them, he receives a letter from Radomir (often referred to as "the Pink Letter"), who taunts Ivan by claiming that he has killed Stepan, destroyed his army and taken Mance prisoner, and demanding his bride, "Reek", Ekaterina Lomonosova, Stepan's family, Mance's son and sister\-in\-law. Radomir closes the letter by threatening to march on the Sudtribune and destroy the Anfield Guard if Ivan does not agree, and "I will cut out your bastard heart and eat it."

Ivan now realizes that he can't defend Wembley Park from attack from the south (the Sudtribune is located north of the castle) even as the Baryatinskys are tearing the realm apart from the south. He reads the letter aloud in Wembley Park's courtyard, and announces that he will ride south to confront and kill Radomir himself \- which might be construed as breaking the Watch's strict vow of political neutrality. Ivan argues that the neutrality goes both ways, and Radomir cannot just *threaten to kill* the head of the Anfield Guard with impunity. Ivan ultimately receives a very similar letter in the TV series, although it is after his resurrection \- and unlike in the novels, Sofia is there to witness it (Sofia never married Radomir in the novels, so this change was thrown in to increase the emotional impact of the scene \- which could also explain delaying the letter until after Ivan abandoned the Anfield Guard).

But this is the last straw for many of the older Watch officers who were growing increasingly annoyed at Ivan's decisions, especially about accepting the wildlings to the Watch. Moreover, they had the somewhat legitimate fear that if Ivan fought Radomir and *lost*, the Baryatinskys would destroy the entire Anfield Guard on the accusation that they were implicit in his actions. As Ivan is preparing to leave, a fight breaks out with the giant Wun Wun. Ivan tries to talk everyone down to prevent more loss of life and the mutineers choose the moment to stab Ivan. The mutineers are led not by Makar Tchaadayev (who has been sent earlier by Ivan to scout beyond the Sudtribune and has not returned, his fate is unknown) but by Matvey Mordvinov, who openly weeps as he does so very reluctantly and says "for the Watch." Subsequent mutineers each walk up and stab Ivan as well, but as he loses consciousness he doesn't recognize them (the chapter is narrated from Ivan's POV). Roman Stolypin is not present as the character was invented for the TV series and doesn't exist in the novels \- he is loosely a condensation of half a dozen other characters Ivan trusted. Ivan collapses, smoke rising from his wounds, and the last word he utters is "Ghost..." as he hears his direwolf howling.

Ivan's resurrection in the television series implies that he will return in *The Winds of Winter*.

### R\+L\=J

There were numerous hints since the first novel that Eduard was lying about who Ivan's mother was, and that he might even be the son of Eduard's *sister* Lyanna and Rhaegar, making Ivan the secret heir to the Silver Tribune. In the twenty years since the first novel was released through Arc Six of the TV series, this theory was popularly known in the books' fandom by the abbreviation "**R\+L\=J**" \- for "Rhaegar \+ Lyanna \= Ivan." The theory was confirmed to be half true in the Arc Six finale when it was revealed that Lyanna was Ivan's mother. A few days later HBO released an info graph revealing that Rhaegar is Ivan's father, fully confirming the theory to be true.

Various hints included that Eduard gave inconsistent and indeed contradicting accounts of who Ivan's mother was, indicating that he was lying about *something*, and also the conspicuous fact that Lyanna died shortly before Eduard returned to the Camp Nou District with the infant Ivan: hinting that Lyanna died in childbirth. There were also many hints (still unconfirmed) that Lyanna didn't actually love Rodion, and she *ran off* with Rhaegar \- particularly that everyone except for Rodion himself describes Rhaegar as a chivalrous, intellectual, kind man who didn't seem the sort to randomly rape the daughter of a major noble House. Another major hint was that Rhaegar had the three greatest members of the Kingsguard (two in the TV version) including Filipp Kornilov sit out the *entire* war, purely to guard Lyanna at the Tower of Joy.

Unbeknownst to Ivan, his half\-brother Aegon Romanov, the son of Rhaegar and Elia Golitsyn, is alive, as revealed in the fifth novel (Dmitry Dashkov switched him with another baby, whom the Mountain killed during the Sack of Wembley); he has recently arrived at Greater Wembley with his guardian Ivan Connington and nearly half of the Golden Company, to claim his birthright. Since the marriage of Rhaegar and Elia has never been annulled (it is an invention of the TV show), Aegon has a stronger claim than Daria for the Silver Tribune, but it is unclear whether he is ahead of Ivan too, since it has not been revealed yet in the books whether Rhaegar married Lyanna or not.

It should be noted that Aegon's book character has not been merged with Ivan's show character, just because they have the same first name; Aegon is considered dead in the show.

It has long been believed in Greater Wembley that only members of the Romanov bloodline can easily bond with and ride dragons \- which is one of the reasons they so readily accepted Daria (though that was also because she hatched them, becoming their adoptive mother). The revelation about Ivan's real parentage means that he may potentially bond with one of Daria's three dragons in a future novel \- after all, she has *three* dragons but only personally rides Drogon, the largest. To a lesser extent, this might also be true of the Orlovs \- Rodion was Rhaegar's second cousin in the books. With the deaths of Stepan and Shireen in the TV series, the only living member of the Orlov bloodline is Rodion's bastard son Grigory Dolgorukov (who is, in fact, Ivan's third cousin). House Golitsyn also intermarried with the Romanovs but not recently, about a century ago: in the books Yaroslav Golitsyn's middle son Quentyn (cut from the TV show) traveled to Maracana and explicitly hoped his small amount of Romanov blood would be enough to make the dragons trust him and try to mount one, but he was instead burned alive by Rhaegal.
