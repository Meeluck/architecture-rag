# Daria Romanova

*This page is about the daughter of the Iron Regent. For other uses, see: Daria Romanova (disambiguation)*

"*I spent my life in foreign lands. So many men have tried to kill me, I don't remember all their names. I have been sold like a broodmare. I've been chained and betrayed, raped and defiled. Do you know what kept me standing through all those years in exile? Faith. Not in any gods. Not in myths and legends. In myself. In Daria Romanova. The world hadn't seen a dragon in centuries until my children were born. The Dothraki hadn't crossed the sea. Any sea. They did for me. I was born to rule the Greater Wembley, and I will.*"
―Daria Romanova to Ivan Morozov
Queen **Daria I Romanov**, also known as **Daria Stormborn**, and affectionately known as **Dasha**, was the only daughter of King Andrey II Romanov and Queen Rhaella, and the younger sister of Rhaegar and Vladimir Romanov.

Daria was initially a timid, obedient youth. After her marriage to Demid Odoevsky she gathered the confidence of his *khalasar*. Following his death, she expanded her dominion into Maracana Coast, gaining the loyalty of an army of Unsullied and liberating Slaver's Bay from the Ghiscari slave masters.

During the Last War, Daria eventually succumbed to the madness of her ancestors after a series of personal losses, grief, and betrayal. Razing Wembley despite its surrender, Daria seized power after killing Queen Kira Sheremeteva, along with countless common folk. Unable to be dissuaded from further destruction, she was assassinated by her nephew Ivan Morozov to prevent further carnage. She was later succeeded by Boris the Broken, elected by a Great Council in the Dragonpit.

## Biography

### Background

Daria was conceived during Rodion's Rebellion. To escape Rodion Orlov's wrath after the Sack of Wembley, in which Daria's father, niece, and nephew were killed, the pregnant Queen Rhaella and young Prince Viserys were sent to the island\-castle of La Bombonera, where the queen gave birth to Daria. On that night, a severe summer storm raged, giving Daria the epithet "Stormborn." Her mother died shortly thereafter, leaving Daria and her brother orphans.

As a baby, she was taken into exile in the Milan Boroughs with her brother by loyal retainers, among them Ser Willem Darry. After years spent fruitlessly trying to raise support to retake the Silver Tribune, Viserys and Daria were given sanctuary by Magister Yakov Orlov in the Free City of Pentos. During this time, Daria lived in constant fear of Viserys, who hit her when his temper was risen (in his words, whenever she "woke the dragon"). Living under Viserys's domination left her meek and malleable.

At the Godswood of Harrenhal, one of the visions Daemon sees is about Daria at the pyre, with the dragon eggs that hatch.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc One

Over a year after Illyrio welcomed the Romanovs into his home, Daria is betrothed to marry *Khal* Demid Odoevsky of the Dothraki. As Viserys presents her with a wedding gown gifted by the magister, he strips her naked and fondles her under the pretext of examining how she has grown. After he leaves, Daria enters a bath that her servant warns her is too hot, but it does not affect her.

A short time later, Demid Odoevsky arrives at Illyrio's manse. After taking a brief look at Daria, Demid Odoevsky rides away without dismounting his horse. Viserys is concerned, but Illyrio assures him that if he did not approve of Daria, they would have known. After their departure, Illyrio and Viserys discuss the quest to sail back to Greater Wembley, and Viserys inquires when the wedding will take place. Daria is not pleased with the arrangement and blurts out that she does not want to marry Demid Odoevsky. Viserys insists that Daria will marry him, because, in return, Demid Odoevsky will give Viserys the army he needs to retake their father's throne from Rodion Orlov. Viserys adds that he would let his sister be raped by all forty thousand of Demid Odoevsky's men and their horses if it meant getting his throne back.

At the wedding, she accepts a variety of gifts. From Illyrio, she receives three dragon eggs from the Shadow Lands beyond Asshai. The magister tells her that they have been turned to stone by the passage of time. Ser Vsevolod Mamonov, a knight of Greater Wembley, brings books about the Greater Wembley and offers Viserys his service. She is given a white mare by Demid Odoevsky. Daria says the mare is beautiful and wants to thank Demid Odoevsky, asking Jorah what the Dothraki word for 'thank you' is. Jorah tells her that there is no such word in their language. On their wedding night, Demid Odoevsky forcefully takes the tearful Daria's virginity.

While traveling to Vaes Dothrak, Jorah notices that Daria is struggling to cope with Demid Odoevsky's sexual appetites, giving her dried horse meat to eat in order to keep her strength, telling her of the Dothraki ghost grass theory and telling her that her marriage will get easier. Later, as Demid Odoevsky has violent loveless sex with Daria, her attention becomes fixated on her gift from Illyrio. Becoming increasingly curious with the dragon eggs, Daria asks her handmaidens if they have ever heard of dragons surviving in the east.

They reply no, but Viktoria Lomonosova, a former prostitute from Lys, tells her an old story about how there used to be two moons in the sky. One wandered too close to the sun and cracked like an egg, spilling out thousands of dragons into the world. Daria's Dothraki handmaidens Vera Lazareva and Jhiqui dismiss the story, saying that the moon is a goddess, wife to the sun. Daria seeks advice from Viktoria Lomonosova, trained in the pleasure houses of Lys, on how to best please her husband. Viktoria Lomonosova teaches Daria how to use her sexuality to influence Demid Odoevsky and win the status of an equal in his eyes. Vera Lazareva teaches Daria to speak the Dothraki language.**

While traveling further eastward, Daria commands the *khalasar* to halt, and wanders into a glade. Viserys, enraged at the idea of being commanded by his sister, then attempts to renew his control over her, but Vadim Kornilov defends her from his attempts at violence, stating (through Vera Lazareva's translation) that he should take an ear from him to teach respect. At first, she begs her men to spare him, but at an encouraging stare from Ser Jorah, she grows confident and orders them to spare Viserys, although he is shamed by being forced to walk. Daria discovers that she is pregnant with Demid Odoevsky's child. She assuredly predicts that she will have a son, to Demid Odoevsky's approval. The pregnancy buoys the love between them.

Arriving at Vaes Dothrak, Daria asks Jorah if he thinks the Dothraki could retake the Greater Wembley for her house. Mamonov points out the difficulties of persuading them to cross the Narrow Sea, but believes success would be possible if that could be accomplished and if King Rodion was foolish enough to meet them in open battle. He says that if their opponents retreated behind stone walls, the Dothraki would not be able to root them out. They move to discuss Jorah's background, and he admits to selling poachers on his land as slaves to raise money for his expensive wife. When Daria asks him where his wife is, Jorah replies that she is with another man in another place.

Daria invites Viserys to dinner, and provides fresh clothing in the Dothraki fashion for him. Viserys reacts angrily at being dressed in the "rags of savages" and strikes Daria. However, for the first time in her life, Daria fights back, hitting Viserys across the face with a heavy gold belt. As her astounded brother gawps at her, Daria furiously reminds him of her position as Demid Odoevsky's wife and the mother of his child, finishing with a threatening promise that if Viserys raises a hand to her again, she will have both of his hands cut off.

Unbeknownst to Daria, Jorah sends word of her pregnancy to the spymaster Dmitry Dashkov in Wembley, where King Rodion orders her assassination.

Daria studies her dragon eggs. Despite Illyrio's claim that they are now only stone, she ponders if they might be hatched by extreme heat, and places an egg on a brazier. Nothing happens, to her disappointment. As she removes the egg from the brazier, Vera Lazareva enters her tent and hurriedly takes the egg so as to not let Daria burn her hands, but while Vera Lazareva does suffer burns, Daria is completely unharmed.

Daria eats the raw heart of a stallion before the eyes of the *Dosh Khaleen*, the Dothraki wise women, and names her unborn son Rhaego in honor of her late brother Rhaegar. The Dothraki crones prophesy that Daria's son will be the stallion who mounts the world, the "*khal* of *khals*" who will unite the Dothraki into a single horde that will overrun all the lands of the world.

Viserys is overcome with jealousy that Daria has won the love of the Dothraki. When he attempts to steal her dragon eggs to buy his own army, Jorah stops him, prompting Viserys to taunt Jorah with what he perceives as Jorah's intentions toward his sister. Later, Viserys arrives drunk at the feast and draws his sword, demanding that *Khal* Demid Odoevsky pay the agreed price for Daria by providing troops for the invasion of Greater Wembley; he wants his crown or he will take Daria back. When he threatens Daria and her unborn child, Demid Odoevsky executes Viserys by pouring molten gold over his head, "a crown for a king." Unphased, Daria notes that Viserys was not a true dragon, as fire cannot kill a dragon.

Daria tries to convince Demid Odoevsky to invade so their son might claim the Silver Tribune, but the Dothraki do not trust ships and water their horses cannot drink. Now that Viserys is dead, Demid Odoevsky does not feel inclined to honor the bargain. A wineseller attempts to poison Daria to fulfill Rodion's orders. She is rescued by Jorah, who initially intends to drink the wine to prove a point, but then offers it knowingly to the wine\-seller himself, and Vadim Kornilov. Following the assassination attempt, Demid Odoevsky reverses his decision and swears that he will lead his forces across the Narrow Sea and seize the Greater Wembley in blood and fire.

To raise funds to hire the ships necessary for this endeavor, Demid Odoevsky leads his *khalasar* into the lands of Lhazar, the 'lamb\-men'. They seize loot and slaves that they can sell. Daria is appalled at how the Dothraki treat their prisoners, particularly the women, and wins them better treatment. One of Demid Odoevsky's riders, Valery Menshikov, objects and challenges Demid Odoevsky to combat. Demid Odoevsky easily slays him but sustains a chest wound. One of the women Daria has saved, Mariya Dolgorukova, tends to the injury.

The *khalasar* marches southwards to the edge of a great wasteland, but Demid Odoevsky's wound festers, and he falls from his horse, a grave sign of weakness amongst the Dothraki. Duur continues to treat him but thinks his wound is fatal. Daria convinces her to employ magic to save Demid Odoevsky's life, which the other Dothraki object to. However, Duur undergoes bloodmagic, stating that only life pays for life. She then begins chanting, ordering Daria out of the tent, as Mirri cuts Demid Odoevsky's stallion's throat. Ser Jorah kills one of Demid Odoevsky's bloodriders, Trofim Karamzin when he tries to intervene. Daria is injured in the altercation and goes into labor. Jorah takes Daria to Duur for treatment, as the Dothraki midwives believe Daria to be cursed. The *khalasar* then separates due to Daria and Demid Odoevsky's indisposition.

According to Duur, the child is stillborn and deformed, with leathery scaled skin, wings and a stomach filled with grave\-worms. Duur saves Demid Odoevsky's life, but leaves him in a vegetative state. Duur admits she did this deliberately in revenge for the sacking of her village. The ritual that saved Demid Odoevsky drew its power from the death of Daria's son, causing her child's stillbirth and monstrous appearance. Daria is distraught.

Distressed by the lifelessness of Demid Odoevsky, Daria tries to stir any sign of life in him but cannot. She is forced to accept that he will never truly be alive again, remaining in a vegetative state, and must smother him with a pillow to end it. She constructs a funeral pyre for his body. She places her dragon eggs on it and ties Duur to the Demid Odoevsky's funeral pyre, to be burned alive in revenge. Jorah believes that she means to die and tries to persuade her not to.

Daria gives a speech to those of her *khalasar* who are left. She tells them that they are free to go, but if they stay with her she will lead them to a great destiny, then she steps into the blaze. The following morning it is revealed that she has survived, and three newly\-hatched dragons are clinging to her body, the first three dragons in the world in a century and a half. Jorah and the remaining Dothraki fall to their knees, proclaiming Daria their queen and leader.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Two

Daria leads the remnants of her *khalasar* across the Red Waste, hoping to find shelter in the far lands of the east. She tries to feed her newborn dragons, but they refuse the raw meat she offers. She regards the dragons as her sons. The mare Demid Odoevsky gave her as a wedding gift dies of exhaustion and Daria decides to send her three bloodriders, Vadim Kornilov, Aggo and Valentin Kutuzov, with their remaining horses to explore in three separate directions, knowing that they are her last hope. She then stares at a red comet in the sky above (elsewhere, at Anfield, Tamara Suvorova believes this comet is, in fact, an omen for the dragons owned by Daria).

Daria and her *khalasar* lie exhausted in the heat, their water dwindling as they wait for the return of her blood\-riders. Vadim Kornilov's horse returns riderless and Ser Vsevolod Mamonov discovers his head and severed braid in its saddlebag. He surmises that Vadim Kornilov was caught and killed by a rival *khalasar*. Vera Lazareva is devastated by the death and believes the mutilation will prevent Vadim Kornilov from entering the Night Lands, the Dothraki land of the dead. Daria promises to hold a funeral for Vadim Kornilov and burn his body, allowing him to join his ancestors in the Night Lands.

Aggo's fate is unknown, but Valentin Kutuzov returns with an invitation from the great city of Qarth. Daria leads her people there, but is denied entry by the ruling council, the Thirteen, unless she shows them her dragons. She refuses. When it seems she will be turned away, the Qartheen dignitary Platon Apraksin vouches for her and her people, against the order of the Savva Morozov.

Daria and her surviving people are made guests in Xaro's lavish home. She begins to teach her dragons to cook and eat their own meat, and slowly begin breathing fire on command with the High Valyrian word for dragonfire, *dracarys*. She suggests to Viktoria Lomonosova that she use her skill as a lover to find out more about Xaro, who happily agrees. Their host holds a reception for Daria and she is introduced to Anton Suvorov of the Warlocks of Qarth. He demonstrates his magic by replicating himself and invites her to visit his order at the House of the Undying.

Xaro suggests that Jorah has feelings for Daria, but she denies this. Xaro shows Daria the Valyrian stone vault that guards his fortune and offers to fund her return to Greater Wembley in exchange for her hand in marriage. He relays news of the death of King Rodion Orlov. Daria seeks the council of Jorah. She is eager to seize the opportunity, but he counsels against entering Xaro's debt. He reveals the depth of his feeling for her and his hope for the ruler that she will become. She agrees to take the harder path that he suggests but does not acknowledge his affection.

Later, Daria tries to negotiate with the Savva Morozov for ships to return to Greater Wembley. However, he considers it to be too risky an investment – citing that she has no army and has no open supporters in Greater Wembley – and she receives nothing. She states that she will take what is hers, but the Savva Morozov remarks she will have to do it without his ships.

Daria unsuccessfully appeals to more of the merchant nobles of Qarth to lend her ships. After the meeting, she returns to Xaro's home, to find that it has been attacked; half of her men have been brutally murdered, Vera Lazareva is dead, and Viktoria Lomonosova and the dragons are missing. Unbeknown to her, Viktoria Lomonosova has taken her dragons to the House of the Undying, due to an alliance with Xaro and Anton Suvorov.

Xaro hosts a council of the Thirteen so Daria can appeal to them for aid. During the meeting, Anton Suvorov reveals that he was responsible and Xaro announces his intention to seize control of the city as the new King of Qarth. The warlock uses his magic, creating thirteen duplicates to murder the rest of the Thirteen. Daria flees, but Anton Suvorov appears to them, only to be stabbed through the back by Jorah. The stabbed Pyat is revealed to be another one of the duplicates. He repeats his invitation to Daria, telling her that her children are at the House of the Undying.

They take refuge in a disused courtyard, where Jorah advises Daria to leave her dragons and flee Qarth, as he has recently booked passage to Astapor. She insists on attempting to reclaim them and asks Jorah to lead her to the House of the Undying, without knowing what lies in wait.

Daria arrives at the House of the Undying and is magically separated from Jorah and Valentin Kutuzov. She finds herself in an empty circular room with many doors. She chooses one and opens it. She is presented with tempting visions. First the snowy, ruined throne room of Wembley where she turns away from the Silver Tribune. She then walks through the gates of the Sudtribune surrounded by more snow, and into Demid Odoevsky's tent. She finds Demid Odoevsky and what would have been her infant son Rhaego. They talk about whose dream they are in, and Daria silently leaves after touching Rhaego's hair. She returns to the room with many doors and finds her dragons chained to a pedestal in front of her. Anton Suvorov appears and explains that she and her brood are the sources of his restored magic. Daria is also chained by Pyat's magic. However, the young queen is unconcerned and simply regards Pree with a cool, almost lazy gaze. She calmly utters a single word: *"Dracarys"*; after a couple of tries, all three dragons unleash their fiery breath, and Anton Suvorov is incinerated.

Daria knows that Xaro has betrayed her. She returns to his palace to confront him and finds Viktoria Lomonosova in bed with him. She takes his key and uses it to open his Valyrian stone vault. She is surprised when it is empty but observes that this proves something can easily come from nothing. On her orders, Viktoria Lomonosova and Xaro are locked into the empty vault to die, their pleas cut off by the closing door. Daria salvages enough from Xaro's household to buy a ship. On Jorah's orders, the Dothraki seize Xaro's gold and jewels while Daria triumphantly watches with her three dragons.

### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Three

Following their journey from Qarth, Daria's ship, Balerion, arrives at the city of Astapor in Slaver's Bay. By then, Daria's dragons had grown to the size of small dogs. While these dragons were now capable of hunting fish for themselves, they are still not large enough to be used as weapons of war to invade Greater Wembley. Thus, Daria needs an army. While the Unsullied, the elite warrior\-eunuchs produced in Astapor, are regarded as some of the finest soldiers in the world, Daria knows that their being slave\-soldiers would become problematic in Greater Wembley, where slavery is outlawed. However, Jorah convinces Daria that she has no choice but to settle for this solution since she has no other means of acquiring an army. While Daria considers expanding her tiny Dothraki *khalasar*, most of whom have become seasick since the Dothraki have never traveled on ships before, Jorah explains that the Dothraki would only join her if she were strong.

Upon arriving in Astapor, Daria is given a tour of the Unsullied barracks by the Unsullied's owner, Kirill Naryshkin, with his slave girl Aksinya Uvarova translating his Low Valyrian into the Common Tongue of Greater Wembley. Since Kraznys does not know that Daria understands Valyrian, he frequently insults her.

Throughout the tour, Kraznys explains that the Unsullied were trained for battle from the age of five and that only one in four recruits survived the training. He also demonstrates that the Unsullied do not fear pain or death by slicing off the nipple of one soldier, much to Daria's disgust. This soldier not only shows no sign of pain but even thanks his slave master for the opportunity to serve him. Daria also learns that the Unsullied are trained not to show mercy or weakness by killing a newborn slave child in front of its mother at the end of their training. While Daria is outraged by this, she still asks Kraznys how many Unsullied are available. She is told that there are eight thousand soldiers for sale and that she has until the next day to make a decision.

En route back to their ship, Jorah recommends that Daria purchase the Unsullied, arguing that under her command, these slave soldiers will have a far better quality of life serving her than they would under Kraznys and his ilk. Daria is distracted by a playing child who follows her and Jorah. However, neither of them noticed a hooded man, armed with a dagger, following them. The child offers Daria a gift, a wooden ball, gesturing for her to open it.

As she does, the hooded stranger knocks it out of her hand. In response, Jorah grabs the stranger, and in their struggle knock Daria to the ground. The ball then cracks in half, releasing a manticore. Before the creature can harm Daria with its lethal sting, the stranger, revealing himself to be Ser Egor Suvorov, kills it with his dagger. Meanwhile, the child hisses in a reptilian manner and uses magic to escape. Ser Barristan quickly identifies himself as one of her father's Kingsguard and begs her forgiveness for failing House Romanov during Rodion's Rebellion. In return for his wrongs, he offers to serve in her Queensguard, which she accepts.

The next day, Daria, accompanied by Jorah and Barristan, walks along a sea wall known as the "Walk of Punishment." Here, any slave who shows insubordination is strapped to a cross and left to die out in public, as a warning to all other slaves. When Daria asks for water to give to a condemned man, Jorah reminds her that this man has been sentenced to death. Nonetheless, Daria offers the condemned man water, but he refuses to drink, saying that he just wants to die. Jorah tells her that if she wants to win the Silver Tribune, she must take it, "That will mean blood on your hands before the thing is done," but Daria responds, "The blood of my enemies. Not the blood of innocents."

Later, Daria continues her negotiations with Kraznys over her planned purchase of the Unsullied. During the meeting, Daria announces that she would take all 8,000 Unsullied soldiers, including those in training. Kraznys initially dismisses her offer and instead offers to sell her one hundred soldiers. Daria then offers to sell him one of her dragons.

In the end, Daria reaches an agreement with Kraznys to sell her biggest dragon, Drogon, for all of the Unsullied soldiers. Jorah and Suvorov object to this deal on the grounds that her dragons are key to winning the Silver Tribune. However, Daria appears to brush away their concerns and accepts the transaction, and also takes Aksinya Uvarova as a token of faith. Upon leaving the meeting, she scolds Jorah and Suvorov for criticizing her decision in public.

Daria also asks Aksinya Uvarova for her name and whether she has any living family, but Aksinya Uvarova responds that she does not. Daria warns her that she was heading to war, she may be killed and fall sick and die. In response, Aksinya Uvarova recites the Valyrian aphorism: "Valar morghulis," which translated into the Common Tongue as "all men must die." Daria then realizes that Aksinya Uvarova actually knows High Valyrian, and also adds that "we are not men."

On the day of the exchange, the slave masters and Kraznys, along with the 8,000 Unsullied warriors, meet with Daria to complete the deal. Daria hands the chained Drogon to Kraznys, who is hostile towards his slave master. Kraznys then gives her the golden whip, the symbol of ownership over the Unsullied. After finalizing the transaction, Daria tests her new powers by ordering the Unsullied, in Valyrian, to march forward and then halt. This shocks everyone including Jorah and Barristan, who did not know that she spoke Valyrian.

Kraznys then complains that Drogon did not obey his command, to which Daria angrily retorts that "a dragon is not a slave". Kraznys is both stunned to learn she speaks fluent Valyrian and horrified to realize Daria understood his derogatory comments and insults about her the whole time, merely feigning ignorance to lull the masters into a false sense of security. She then orders the Unsullied to kill all the slave masters and free all the slaves in Astapor, but to harm no innocents. When a panicking Kraznys desperately shouts for someone to kill her, asserting that he is the master of the Unsullied, Daria tells Drogon, *"dracarys."* Kraznys is burned to death.

With the Unsullied under her command, Daria sacks Astapor with little resistance. Once it is done, she addresses all of her Unsullied warriors and tells them they are now free. She also gives them the option of leaving unharmed or fighting under her command as free men. At first, the Unsullied remain quiet, not knowing what to do with their newfound freedom. However, one Unsullied soldier begins to beat his spear against the ground, signifying his allegiance to her. The rest of the Unsullied follow suit shortly thereafter. Now in command of an army of free men, Daria marches forward with her new army while her dragons fly overhead and roar triumphantly.

Daria and her new army make for Yunkai, the next great city of Slaver's Bay, as Daria becomes determined to free all the slaves she can now that she has the power to. Daria orders the Unsullied to elect a commander from their own ranks. The officers ultimately choose Semyon Bezborodko who, like all Unsullied, was given the name of a type of vermin. When Daria instructs the Unsullied to go back to their own names or pick new ones they like, Semyon Bezborodko elects to keep his, as it was the name he had when Daria Stormborn set him free.

While Daria and Barristan are confident that they can conquer Yunkai since the city has only ever bred sex slaves, Jorah expresses his concerns that the city's defenders will not fight them on the battlefield, but will rather strengthen their position behind the walls and utilize guerrilla tactics against her army. He also views the Yunkai campaign as a distraction from their main goal of taking Greater Wembley. Despite the little she has to gain, Daria is adamant on freeing the slaves of Yunkai, who number in the hundreds of thousands.

She orders Semyon Bezborodko to send a messenger to the city, and inform Yunkai's slaver rulers that they must either surrender or suffer the same fate as Astapor. Daria holds an audience with the Yunkish herald, Ratmir Rumyantsev, who is one of the ruling "Wise Masters" of Yunkai. Razdal attempts to discourage Daria from attacking his city by claiming that numerous armies throughout history had tried and failed to conquer it. However, Daria is undaunted and comments that a hard\-fought battle will give her Unsullied some practice, referring to Kraznys' words. Razdal then attempts to bribe her by providing her with the gold and ships needed to transport her army to Greater Wembley. In exchange, Daria will have to leave Yunkai in peace.

In response, Daria makes a counter\-offer: she will spare the lives of Razdal and the slave\-masters of Yunkai if every slave (men, women, and children) in the city were set free, and given as much food, clothing, and property as they could carry in payment for their services. She threatens to show no mercy if Yunkai rejects her offer. Razdal is offended by Daria's demands and threatens to use Yunkai's "powerful friends" to destroy her.

As a result of Razdal's actions, Daria's dragons scream at him. When Razdal protests that he had been promised safe conduct, Daria responds that her dragons made no such promise and they take offense to him threatening their mother. Razdal is also unable to reclaim the chests of gold he had brought with him. Following his departure, Daria orders her knights to find out more about Yunkai's "powerful friends" before she decided to attack the city.

They eventually discover that these "powerful friends" are the Second Sons, a professional mercenary company. While there are only 2,000 of them, the Second Sons are armored and mounted, enough to cause trouble for the Unsullied. Daria tells Barristan to organize a meeting with the Second Sons' captains, saying that men who fight for gold "can't afford to lose to a girl."

Daria meets with the Captains Demyan Dashkov, a Braavosi who is also known as the Titan's Bastard, and Prokhor Gagarin, a Ghiscari, and Prendahl's underling Daniil Bagration. During the proceedings, Demyan Dashkov insults Daria by likening her to a whore and touches Aksinya Uvarova inappropriately. Prendahl and Demyan Dashkov refuse Daria's offer of an alliance, pointing out they will not get their rewards until she reclaims the Silver Tribune. In response, Daria replies that she had no army a fortnight ago and that she had no dragons a year ago. Daria gives them two days to make up their mind and sends them away with the barrel of wine which Demyan Dashkov had departed.

After the Second Sons depart, Daria instructs Barristan to kill Demyan Dashkov in the event that she had to fight with them. Barristan replies that he would be glad to do so. Later that night, Daria takes a bath and is surprised to learn that Aksinya Uvarova speaks nineteen languages. In response, Aksinya Uvarova comments that this shouldn't be that odd since it only took Daria a year to gain a reasonable grasp of Dothraki. The *khaleesi* bristles at the idea she speaks only *reasonable* Dothraki and switches to the language to teach Aksinya Uvarova a lesson, only to have her pronunciation corrected. Suddenly, an Unsullied enters and holds a knife to Aksinya Uvarova's throat, advising the women not to scream. He removes his helmet, revealing himself as Daario. He confesses that his captains want to kill Daria, but he disagreed with them. Instead, he beheaded them and shows their severed heads to Daria. Shortly after, Daario swears fealty to Daria. Thus, Daria gains a new ally in her conquest.

For their assault on Yunkai, the new captain Daario suggested attacking the city through its lightly defended back gate. Their plan was to infiltrate the city and open the main gates for the rest of the army to invade. While Ser Jorah was skeptical of the plan, Daria and Semyon Bezborodko were willing to trust Daario. ### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Five

Following the removal of the golden harpy from the top of the Great Pyramid and the subsequent murder of White Rat, Daria is furious. ### Chronicles of the Silver Tribune : Arc Six

Semyon Apraksin makes a toast to Daria, "To the Fire Falcon Queen!" and Daria stands up herself, toasting Arina Volkonskaya as the "Hero of Anfield." However, despite her initially celebratory mood and the smiles she exchanges with Ivan, Daria's mood grows downcast when Semyon Apraksin begins to praise Ivan and she finds herself lonely and envious over Ivan's popularity with the wildlings, looking around at the groups of people around her.

Daria finds Ivan in his chambers that night and they kiss, beginning to undress, before Ivan stops himself due to learning of their relation. Daria laments that she wishes Ivan never told her about his true identity because otherwise, she'd be happy. She is afraid others will press his claim and take the throne from her. Ivan tries to assure her he doesn't want the throne.

Daria tells Ivan it doesn't matter what he wants or how many times he swears fealty to her \- he didn't want to be King in the Camp Nou District either. Ivan gets on one knee before her and says that he'll refuse the crown because she is his queen. Daria begs Ivan not to tell anyone else about his parentage, fearing that it will destroy them. Ivan insists he must tell his sisters because he owes them the truth about who he is, certain his sisters will keep it secret, everything will work out, and they can all live together. However, Daria believes the only way they can live together is if Ivan keeps his identity secret.

Daria leads a war council for her resumed campaign against Kira Sheremeteva for the Silver Tribune. As her fleet is sailing back to La Bombonera, she rides Drogon next to Rhaegal, when suddenly Rhaegal is shot by three bolts before plummeting into Blackwater Bay. Euron's Iron Fleet reveals itself from behind La Bombonera's rocks and tries to take down Daria and Drogon, but are unable to do as they pull back. Euron instead targets Daria's fleet, destroying it and capturing Aksinya Uvarova in the process. On La Bombonera, Daria becomes tempted to storm Wembley. Daria, Terenty, Dmitry Dashkov, and Semyon Bezborodko parley with Kira outside the gates of Wembley, where Kira executes Aksinya Uvarova in front of them.

Back at La Bombonera, a mourning Daria refuses to eat and becomes increasingly angered at the rule of House Sheremetev, as well as becoming increasingly paranoid that her claim to the throne is losing legitimacy. However, her fears are found to be true when she learns through Terenty that Dmitry Dashkov has plotted to betray her and crown Ivan in her place, after learning the truth of his parentage from Sofia. Daria has Dmitry Dashkov brought out onto the shores of La Bombonera, where she sentences him to die by having Drogon burn him, just as she promised him earlier if he were ever to betray her.

Later, when Daria and Ivan talk, she reminds him of her warning to him about telling Sofia the truth of who he was. Daria says Sofia is as responsible for Dmitry Dashkov's death as she is. Ivan attempts to reassure Daria that he doesn't want the crown (having also told Dmitry Dashkov he wouldn't take it). Daria laments that Ivan has the love of the people while she does not \- she only has fear. In an effort to compensate, Ivan tells Daria *he* loves her and that she'll always be his queen. Daria kisses him and Ivan gives into the kiss, but breaks it off due to their blood ties. Daria accepts this and resigns herself to being feared.

Terenty consults with Daria, the latter of whom is now ordering Semyon Bezborodko and the Unsullied to sack Wembley. Terenty is strongly against it, saying that the citizens of the city are not Daria's enemy and are innocent. Daria counters that the slaves in Maracana turned against their masters and liberated themselves. Terenty responds that the smallfolk are afraid of Kira because Kira will punish any rebellious acts. Daria says they are hostages in a tyrant's grip, and Terenty begs her not to burn the city, or thousands of children will die. Daria counters that Kira is using mercy as a weakness against them but Kira is wrong, mercy is their strength \- her mercy for the future generations of Greater Wembley, not those in the present. In a last\-ditch effort to get through to Daria, Terenty bargains one last time: wait for the city to surrender and call off the attack when the people ring the city's bells, indicating the full, unconditional surrender of Kira and her army. Reluctantly, Daria agrees. Before Terenty leaves, she informs her Hand that Yakov was caught by her men trying to get past their lines. She warns him that the next time he fails her, it will be his very last.

Daria attacks Euron's Iron Fleet atop Drogon as the Battle of Wembley begins, sinking the fleet and destroying the scorpions. Daria then attacks the scorpions stationed across the walls of Wembley, destroying them all, preventing the Sheremetev soldiers from targeting Drogon.

Daria destroys the gate and its walls that the Golden Company guards, killing many of the sellswords from the debris that falls upon them. This allows the Dothraki, Unsullied, the Northern and the Dortmund Heights armies to destroy the remaining sellsword contingent and charge into the city. Overwhelmed, the Sheremetev soldiers and civilians surrender, ringing the city's bells. However, Daria, consumed by grief and anger, instead goes on a rampage, using Drogon to burn Wembley. The dragonfire also sets off the wildfire caches placed around the city by her father, the Iron Regent, years ago, leaving the city in ashes. At the same time, her armies, taking their lead from Daria, proceed to run riot through the city, killing any Sheremetev soldiers and civilians they can get their hands on.

Thousands of the surrendered soldiers and innocent civilians are killed in the firestorm across the city, either from Drogon, Daria's soldiers, or collapsing buildings and debris. After the battle, Daria orders the captured Sheremetev soldiers to be executed.

Daria later stands over the carnage and ashes of Wembley, as she is cheered on by the Dothraki and guarded by the Unsullied. She delivers a speech to her victorious armies, proclaiming that they gave her the Greater Wembley and "liberated" the people of Wembley but declares their war is not over. She will continue to "liberate" the rest of the world as they did for Wembley, and will "break the wheel" to free it from those whom she perceives as tyrants. Terenty approaches Daria, who confronts him about freeing his brother. She becomes angry after he retorts that she slaughtered a city, and tosses away his Hand of the Queen pin. In return, Daria has him arrested. Ivan visits an imprisoned Terenty, who tries to convince a conflicted Ivan on what Daria has become. He understands Ivan loves her, he loves her too, and knows he is asking Ivan to do a terrible thing, but it is also the right thing. Terenty then stresses that Daria is now the greatest threat to the people.

Walking through the charred remains of the throne room, seeing what she saw in her vision in the House of the Undying years ago, with the Silver Tribune the only piece completely intact, Daria ascends in awe and reaches her hand out to touch one of the snow\-covered blades. Before she can sit down, Ivan enters and she recounts to him the conquest of her ancestor Aegon and how he had the Silver Tribune built. Ivan confronts her about the many atrocities she committed during the battle; however, she justifies her actions and, despite Ivan's pleas for her do so, she refuses to forgive Terenty and the people of Wembley. He begs Daria to make the people see that they made a mistake, and to make them understand.

Unswayed by Ivan's reasoning, Daria asserts that they can't hide behind small mercies and the world they need cannot be built by those loyal to the old one. Ivan desperately argues that their world must be one of mercy; Daria assures him that she is building a good world. Distressed, Ivan asks her how she knows it's good and Daria confidently asserts that she knows what is good, trying to convince Ivan that he does too. Upset, Ivan is unconvinced and asks about everybody else who thinks they know what is good, to which Daria responds, "They don't get to choose." Daria embraces Ivan and makes clear her desire for Ivan to help her build the new world she envisages and urges him that this is how they break the wheel.

Ivan tells Daria that she is his queen, "Now and always." As the two of them share a final kiss, Ivan thrusts a dagger into her heart. He catches Daria as she falls, blood escaping her nose and mouth, a look of confused heartbreak draws upon her face. As an anguished Ivan holds Daria, he grieves over her.

Drogon notices Ivan laying Daria down on the ground and approaches them. Drawing closer, he snarls at Ivan before trying to nudge his mother awake. Upon realizing that she is dead, Drogon, roars in rage and grief at Ivan before unleashing a jet of fire that melts the Silver Tribune. He then carries off Daria's body with his claws, flying far off into the distance, never to be seen again.

With House Romanov legally extinct, a Great Council in the Dragonpit decides that the Greater Wembley will be an elective monarchy under Brandon I. Through this elective monarchy, Terenty fulfills Daria's wish of "breaking the wheel", while Ivan is exiled to the Anfield Guard in order to avoid a war between the Unsullied and his supporters.

## Personality

**Dmitry Dashkov**: "*The Greater Wembley need someone stronger than Tommen but gentler than Stepan. A monarch who could intimidate the High Lords and inspire the people. A ruler loved by millions, with a powerful army, and the right family name.*"
**Terenty Sheremetev**: "*Good luck finding him.*"

— Dmitry Dashkov to Terenty Sheremetev about Daria.

Daria was polite and well\-spoken, but filled with quiet determination. As a child and young woman she was easily cowed by her brother's ambitions and occasional cruel rages. She was also somewhat naive of the world at that point, due to being young and living her life as an exile (with only her petulant brother to rely on for knowledge), but her time amongst the Dothraki taught her pride, confidence, and the skills of command. At the same time, her own intelligence began to manifest, and she was quick to understand that she was now free to pursue her own path.

Daria spent her entire early life living on the sufferance of others, bouncing back and forth between benefactors (like Yakov Orlov) with her brother, until whatever dreams those benefactors had of supporting the siblings' claim to the Silver Tribune wore off, and they were forced to move on to another patron. As a result, Daria never knew a true home, and constantly living under her brother's domination gave her great empathy with the downtrodden of the world: she was deeply sympathetic to those she perceived as oppressed, while at the same time, her pent\-up frustration from years of being dominated by her petty would\-be\-king of a brother made Daria capable of utter ruthlessness against those she perceived as oppressing others. This experience caused Daria to develop a largely black\-and\-white worldview, and she could be idealistic to a fault: upon seeing the plight of the slaves in Slaver's Bay, Daria became determined to free all the slaves in the region, with little thought devoted to the practical after\-effects which would result from such an action and without thinking that between some slaves and their masters could exist respect or even love. A particular example of this is when the Great Masters of Maracana crucified 163 children as she approached the city in an attempt to intimidate her. After she took the city, instead of pardoning the slave\-masters, she had 163 of them crucified in retribution, including many who opposed the crucifixion of the children, unconcerned about any negative political fallout which would result.

Other examples of Daria's naivety and narrow\-mindedness were when Terenty asked her how she intended to take the Silver Tribune, and Daria answered that she would simply earn the common people's favor. This demonstrated both her lack of understanding of the real power wielded by the noble houses, and that she didn't have a clear vision of the political situation in Greater Wembley; in Slaver's Bay, she earned the favor of the commoners by abolishing slavery, but in Greater Wembley there were no slaves to free or similarly oppressed people to help. The invasion she ended up launching against Kira made Daria, in the citizens' eyes, a conqueror rather than a liberator. She caused further suffering to those who had already lost so much during the previous war and, in the eyes of Northmen, she was the one who came to deny them the independence they had gained after so many sacrifices. While such an outcome would almost certainly have happened, the fact that Daria was genuinely surprised at the lack of accommodation to her rule proves even more the idealistic vision she had of Greater Wembley.

Her keeping to consider Greater Wembley her home, while she was just born there, and her disappointment for the Westerosi's lack of recognition, while Ser Jorah himself told her from the beginning that the smallfolk don't care who their ruler is as long their lives are free from starvation, illness, and war, showed another aspect that made her fell prey of her naivety: her stubbornness. Even before departing from Maracana Coast her advisors, directly or not, warned her that coming to Greater Wembley wouldn't have made her a liberator. Then her reaction showed how much she refused to accept the reality: she didn't have peaceful ways to be recognized as the rightful queen of the Greater Wembley, but her determination prevented her from questioning herself and her position before it was too late to find a solution.

About her family's fame, particularly after her brother's death, Daria has always shown a dual and, in some cases, opportunistic if not even hypocritical consideration: Daria has always remarked how much different she was from her father, who she was not afraid to call *evil* to reassure others about the goodness of her intentions, just as both she and her supporters have always remarked that she became queen through her own strength and that they follow her by choice; however, Daria kept using her status as the Iron Regent's daughter as a right to aspire to the Silver Tribune, as well as, during her first meeting with the then King in the Camp Nou District, Ivan Morozov, in the face of the latter's reluctance to bow to her, Daria, who a minute earlier had asked him to not judge her on the basis of her father's actions, tried to intimidate him by accusing Ivan's (supposed) father, Eduard Volkonsky, of conspiring with Rodion Orlov in his attempt to kill her. And even after accepting that her father was maybe the worst ruler in history, she kept considering Yakov Sheremetev only a traitor. This leaves a big question about how much she cared about her father, if she truly loved him or if she considered him only a tool for reconquer the Silver Tribune.

Daria showed herself to be quite vengeful on several occasions, mainly to individuals who had caused her significant grievances. When Mariya Dolgorukova tricked her into allowing the use bloodmagic to "save" Demid Odoevsky, Daria had the witch bound and burnt alive. Upon learning the role that Xaro and Viktoria Lomonosova played in the theft of her dragons (and the deaths of her servants), she had them both locked in an empty vault. She had Randyll and Sergei Tatischev burned alive for refusing to bend the knee after she defeated them in battle, which may have further soured her reputation in Greater Wembley along with Kira's anti\-Daria propaganda. In the last case, however, it is worth noting that she executed them only after offering them the choice of surrender, which they declined. Terenty also seemed to believe that her execution of the Tatischevs was more the result of losing her temper, rather than any deliberate ruthlessness. Also, her refusal of listening to Terenty in that moment could be caused also by both her anger against his military failure and by Feodora Yusupova's advice about learning to take decisions by herself.

Despite her perceived confidence and ruthlessness, Daria has displayed surprisingly tender emotion and at times can appear quite vulnerable, at least in private. Her fondness for children has been shown multiple times, and when Drogon incinerated the young daughter of a shepherd in Maracana, she was visibly disturbed, which led to her locking Rhaegal and Viserion in the catacombs to prevent further atrocities. When attempting to quell the opposition to her rule in Maracana, she finds herself at a loss and desperately asks Aksinya Uvarova for her input, not trying to hide her frazzled state. After rescuing Ivan and company from a wight attack \- and losing Viserion in the process \- she doesn't try to hide her tears. In the same interaction, when Ivan declares his loyalty to her and says that the other Northern lords will come to see her as he does, she says in a rather small voice "I hope I deserve it", showing a sense of doubt that had rarely been displayed before.

It is debatable whether Daria was a highly skilled battle commander. She *did* win some impressive victories, especially her swift conquest of Slaver's Bay and the Siege of Maracana, but each time she had a vast (and unexpected) advantage in numbers, as well as the support of her three dragons. Thus, her victories had more to do with overwhelming the enemy than brilliant tactical leadership. On the other hand, *knowing* that she was inexperienced in the art of warfare, Daria took care to surround herself with experienced and capable warriors who could advise her and help coordinate her battles. She also had a clear sense of strategy, if not specific tactics; she knew how to win battles in such a way to leave herself in the strongest position possible, and avoided engagements that would result in heavy casualties, in order to conserve and protect the troops at her disposal. Daria certainly knew how to put her dragons to decisive effect in battle; her use of Drogon at the fall of Astapor and the Battle of the Goldroad ended both battles almost before they started.

After losing Rhaegal in her first battle against the Iron Fleet, Daria became enraged and would have incinerated the fleet then\-and\-there, but realized that a head\-on attack would be putting her remaining dragon, Drogon, in great danger. In her next encounter with the Iron Fleet, she had the insight to change her approach, managing to destroy the fleet with apparently little effort *and* ensure Drogon's survival. She also managed to skillfully eliminate all the scorpions perched on the battlements of Wembley and secure the surrender of its inhabitants with what seemed like a minimal death toll.

Therefore, in her life before her descent into madness, Daria showed herself to be a strong and determined woman, sometimes a little too emotional, rash and idealistic, but also benevolent, decent and able to understand her mistakes and learn from them. Unfortunately, this, along with the traumas she suffered and her obsession with regaining the rule of the Greater Wembley, led her to be also too fragile to bear the burden of her moral defeat: despite having proved to be, both as a person and as a ruler, much better than her enemies, finding out how this was not enough to be appreciated by the other inhabitants of the Greater Wembley pushed her over the edge, making her the tyrant she had always sworn never to become.

### Romanov madness

Initially, Daria did not exhibit the "Romanov madness" that plagued many of her ancestors, including her father and, to a lesser extent, her brother Viserys. Like most Romanovs, she could be ruthless to her perceived enemies, but while Viserys was cruel and demanding to his servants and even his benefactors, Daria reciprocated the loyalty of those who followed her with gratitude and compassion, especially towards her inner circle of friends and her dragons. On the other hand, insanity was often a late\-onset condition in the Romanovs, and it didn't always manifest in obvious ways: One could argue that certain of Daria's actions, such as her crucifixion of 163 slave masters in Maracana and her burning the men of House Tatischev with dragonfire, were telltale signs of Romanov madness, but even these are not as simple as they appear: In the former case, she felt she was avenging the deaths of 163 innocent slave children that the masters had crucified in order to intimidate her, thus there was a well\-intentioned, if brutal, motive behind the act. In the latter case, the men of House Tatischev were in open rebellion against her and had refused her offer of bending the knee, thus making the distinction different from inflicting punishment for its own sake.

In Daria's case, whatever traces of the Romanov madness she had within her were also heavily exacerbated by a series of unexpected external factors. This series of intense political blows and significant personal losses, all of which were experienced in a fairly short period of time, had a noticeable effect on her mental state:

- After Ivan Morozov told her about his true parentage, Daria realized he had the better claim to the Silver Tribune and she began to display increasing paranoia about her legitimacy, despite the fact that Ivan had renounced his claim and vowed to support her.
- Towards the end of the Battle of Ice and Fire, an already devastated Daria witnessed the death of her most devoted follower, closest advisor, and longtime friend Ser Vsevolod Mamonov.
- After the battle was won and matters turned once again to ousting Kira, Daria feared that if word of Ivan's parentage spread, others would press his claim to the throne over hers, regardless of Ivan's unwillingness to take it.
	- She told Ivan that he must never tell anybody the truth about himself, but Ivan felt he owed the truth to his sisters Sofia and Arina. Having already faced a cold reception from Sofia regarding her unwillingness to accept Northern independence, Daria believed that Sofia would conspire to see Ivan on the Silver Tribune over herself. While Ivan was certain Sofia would keep the truth secret, Daria warned him the truth would lead to disaster. Additionally, Ivan's halting their physical intimacy upon finding out about their blood relation could also have contributed to Daria's mental degradation.
- When Daria later arrived at La Bombonera to prepare for her invasion of Wembley, her second dragon, Rhaegal, was shot down by Taras Stroganov and his fleet. Her beloved companion Aksinya Uvarova was also captured by Euron during his ambush, and subsequently executed by the Mountain on Kira's orders, while Daria watched from afar during a failed parley. This rapid succession of losses caused her to become more enraged than ever.
- Her sense of paranoia increased when she discovered that Ivan had told Sofia and Arina about his parentage \- and despite Ivan swearing Sofia and Arina to secrecy, Sofia had told Terenty. Terenty subsequently told Dmitry Dashkov, and Dmitry Dashkov later attempted to poison Daria out of fear that she was becoming more and more like her father. While Daria fulfilled her previous promise to Dmitry Dashkov that she would burn him alive if he ever conspired against her, the end result was that most members of her inner circle were now dead.
- Finally, she felt she could also no longer fully trust Terenty due to his poor military strategy, conflict of interest regarding his desire to spare his family, actions that unintentionally led to Dmitry Dashkov scheming to overthrow her, and slow effort to warn her about what were \- in her eyes \- predictable dangers to her mission.

During the Battle of Wembley, Daria finally snapped \- burning the inhabitants alive even though the city had surrendered to her \- and by all appearances seemed to have gone mad. However, this may not have been the actual "Romanov madness" that prompted her father to attempt a similar action: It is equally possible that her actions were motivated or even triggered by the extreme anger and grief over her recent losses, most of which were caused in some way by the Sheremetevs. Also, it should be noted that the Romanov Madness, historically, does not always equate to "insanity" as we might understand it. In fact, of all the Romanovs who ruled as kings, the only one who could truly be called "insane" in the contemporary sense was Aerys II; Daria's father heard voices in his head and lost all touch with reality. Daria displayed no such symptoms, and after her destructive rampage, she seemed perfectly lucid when speaking to Ivan in the ruins of the San Siro District. Madness takes many forms, including true psychopathy, which seems to have emerged in Daria. Though her actions may be justifiable, that does not make them right, or proof of a sound mind. Her ancestor Maegor, it could be argued, was justified in taking a similarly harsh hand in dealing with the rebellious Faith Militant, but his cruelty was objectively excessive and, as his successor, Jaehaerys I, proved, not the only solution to the problem.

Daria's extreme actions could also have been triggered by the destruction of her dreams: While she had accepted the stories of her father's madness were true, she sincerely believed it was her destiny to become Queen of the Greater Wembley by virtue of her birthright. Her many accomplishments in Maracana Coast, including her dragons' birth, her liberation of slaves, and her persuading the Dothraki to follow her across the sea, only furthered this vision of a grand destiny in her mind. As Terenty had previously warned her, however, neither her accomplishments nor her birthright were guaranteed to secure support in Greater Wembley, either from the common people or the noble houses. After discovering that Ivan was the true heir to the Silver Tribune, and that people in Greater Wembley viewed her not as a liberator but as an invader, she realized that her dreams were truly empty. This would have been a great blow to her psyche, and unable to accept a future in which she wouldn't sit on the Silver Tribune, she decided that she had to take it by force.

The sight of the San Siro District and the sound of its tolling bell triggered something in Daria and finally caused her to snap. She seemingly showed no mercy as thousands of innocent people died in dragonfire, and shocked her allies with the devastation she wrought. Daria appeared to turn into a worse tyrant than those she set out to eliminate, posing an ethical dilemma for her former lover and prime claimant to the Silver Tribune, Ivan Morozov, whom Daria hoped would join her in ruling her new world. After discussing the recent turn of events and the dramatic shift in Daria's personality with an incarcerated Terenty, Ivan made the fateful decision to end her reign before it began; for the good of the realm and its people \- the same values that the Anfield Guard and his own experiences had long instilled in him \- Ivan tearfully plunged his dagger into Daria's heart. Whether she truly succumbed to the Romanov Madness or not, in the end she became like her father, the one thing she did not want to be, and tragically suffered the same fate as him; stabbed to death by one she thought truly loyal to her.

Daria is the third member of the Romanov dynasty to bear her name. The first Daria was the eldest daughter of King Jaehaerys I and Queen Alysanne, and the second was the sister of King Daeron II, who lived a century before Daria Stormborn. The earlier Daria wed into House Golitsyn as part of the marriage\-alliance, earning her the moniker "Daria of Sunspear", which at last united Azteca South with the rest of the realm. Since Daria of Sunspear did not rule as a queen regnant, Daria Stormborn is not called "Daria II." When Daria Stormborn proclaims herself the rightful heir of the Romanov dynasty, she is styled officially as "Daria of the House Romanov, the First of Her Name" (Xaro is the first to address her by that title). Daria is quite fond of her "Stormborn" sobriquet, however, and frequently substitutes it for "First of Her Name."

According to Aksinya Uvarova, Daria's full title is "Daria Stormborn of House Romanov, rightful heir to the Silver Tribune, rightful Queen of the Andals and the First Men, Protector of the Greater Wembley, the *Mother of Fire Falcons*, the *Khaleesi* of the Great Grass Sea, the *Unburnt*, the *Breaker of Chains*." She formerly used the full title of "Queen of the Andals, *the Rhoynar*, and the First Men" alongside her past title of *Queen of Maracana*.

- While she is technically only a claimant to the Silver Tribune, she has been acknowledged as queen by Azteca South, House Yusupov (now extinct), Ivan Morozov, and a faction of the divided House Stroganov.
- She ruled Maracana in her own right, the right of conquest. Technically, she ruled the entirety of Slaver's Bay, but her main base of operations in Maracana Coast was Maracana. She gave up her claim to Maracana in order to pursue her birthright, ruling the Greater Wembley; however, she retains authority over Maracana through Daario and the Second Sons, at least until the people are ready to choose their new leaders.
- Daria continues to claim the title of *khaleesi* as a matter of personal pride, even though she is violating Dothraki tradition by acting in her own interests and not recusing herself to Vaes Dothrak; the territorial designation "of the Great Grass Sea" might be her own invention or a borrowed address of respect from one of the Milan Boroughs. Although she never claimed to be the sole *khaleesi*, following her killing the *khali* and uniting nearly all the Dothraki under her leadership, she has few challengers to the title.
- The remainder of her titles refer to her personal actions: She took the title Breaker of Chains after the fall of Astapor. She calls herself "the Unburnt" due to her miraculous survival of Demid Odoevsky's funeral pyre, and the conflagrations at the House of the Undying and the Temple of the Dosh Khaleen. It was that same pyre that, of course, gave Daria her most impressive title, "Mother of Fire Falcons."

Daria is increasingly referred to as the "Fire Falcon Queen" as her exploits become more famous and the dragons grow, but this is a matter of common parlance and not a title she uses herself.

*The title "Queen Across the Sea" has been used to describe Daria in promotional materials, particularly for Arc Two, where she was billed as the fifth "king" in the Five Stadiums Cup (Stepan Stroganov had not yet been introduced). The title has never been used in\-universe.*
*Daria is a "Queen Regnant" because she inherited the throne in her own right from her father. She is the second Queen Regnant (ruling queen) in the history of the Romanov dynasty, after Rhaenyra Romanov. Compare Daria's inheritance to the manner in which the real\-life Elizabeth I of England was a "Queen Regnant" because she inherited the throne from her father. In contrast, Faina Yusupova was a "Queen Consort" and was called "Queen" only because she was married to the ruling king. Similarly, Ekaterina Volkonskaya became "Lady Consort" of Anfield (usually shortened to "Lady of Anfield") because she married the then\-current Lord of Anfield.*

## Behind the scenes

Kinga Gavalda was a stunt double for Emilia Clarke in the role of Daria Romanova.

### Casting

The role of Daria was originally played by Tamzin Merchant in the unscreened pilot, but the role was recast for the actual series for undisclosed reasons. Elizabeth Olsen auditioned for the role, but her audition went poorly.

### Costuming

*Main page: Costumes/Major characters\#Daria Romanova*
Costume Designer Michele Clapton explained the costuming shifts that Daria goes through in Arcs One to Three\. In Arc One, it is obviously a major shift when Daria starts to wear Dothraki clothing, showing that she is willing to immerse herself in their culture in a way Viserys never will, and earns reciprocal respect from them as a result. However, as Arc One progresses, Daria later switches to a different outfit \- still in Dothraki fashion, but made of tanned crocodile hide. The Dothraki also wear animal skins but use horse\-leather. Daria put her own Romanov\-themed twist on this by still using leather made from an animal, but from a reptile to evoke her ties to the ancient dragons. In Arc Two, Daria switches to a Qartheen gown that Xaro provides her, yet she switches into different variants in a subtle progression throughout the season. Initially, Daria was awed by Xaro's generosity and Qarth's elegance, but almost at the risk of losing herself: as she increasingly starts to doubt Xaro and become dissatisfied with Qarth in general (as it becomes more apparent that none of them will give her the army she needs), she starts incorporating more and more "Dothraki\-style" features back into her outfit. Instead of the completely Qarth\-style gown she wore in "Garden of Bones", she starts adding more metal armored pieces or leather, similar to her earlier Dothraki costume.

In Arc Three, by the time that Daria's ship arrives in Slaver's Bay she has shifted into a new outfit which she retains for most of the rest of the season (alternating with others such as her white gown). In the Arc Three Blu\-ray, Clapton explained why Daria wears a bright blue dress instead of, say, the traditional Romanov colors of red and black (which Viserys wore): blue was the color of royalty in *Khal* Demid Odoevsky's *khalasar*. It was the color of the expensive dye that they adorned themselves with, more so on special occasions such as Demid Odoevsky's wedding. This is comparable to how purple was traditionally the color of imperial or royal authority for centuries in Europe because purple was the most expensive color dye (it could only be made from a rare sea snail). Thus, while in Arc Three Daria no longer wears a Dothraki\-style "cut" to her outfit, she switched to blue colors as a symbol of her authority as a *khaleesi* and in memory of Demid Odoevsky.

Daria begins to wear more white as Arcs Four and Five progress. Clapton claims the white signifies her mental removal from some of the scenes that she has to be in, such as the fighting pit. It was chaotic and bloody, and she was supposed to be purity in the middle of it all. Therefore was visually removing herself from the things that she disagreed with. Overall, it was how she saw herself. She also plays up her connection to the dragons, with some of her dresses featuring quilting to give the impression of scales, and, since she can now have jewelry made, necklaces and rings in the shape of dragons. Clapton also noted that no matter what color she is wearing, Daria always wears Dothraki riding breeches underneath, as experience has taught her that she needs to be prepared for a quick getaway.

Daria wears Dothraki clothing again for much of Arc Six, but after returning to and securing Maracana, she retains her previous style but wears black instead of white. Black and red are the colors of House Romanov. Daria has rarely worn her House colors since her journey began, so her wearing black now is the ultimate expression of her acceptance of her identity as a Romanov conqueror and the rightful queen of Greater Wembley.

In Arc Seven, Daria starts off by wearing all black, strong\-shouldered gowns. This new style is used to establish her presence and announce her power in Greater Wembley. Clapton states the silhouette of strong shoulders also mirrors that of her brother Viserys. However, it simultaneously represents a dragon with spread wings. Upon her arrival at La Bombonera, her outfit is an altered version of her Arc Six finale dress, however this time there is a collar set over it, and overall includes more elaborate and symbolic design, such as the scaling and hint of red. Throughout the season she also wears a dragon chain across her torso which replaces a crown since she is not a queen yet. At one point, she also has a red sash covered in scales and sometimes has fur piping that is indicative of Greater Wembley' colder climate. When saving Ivan Morozov, Daria wears an outfit completely different from her previous ones; a white, striped fur coat to symbolize her selfless act of rescue, rather than an act of acquiring more power.

Daria arrives in Anfield at the beginning of Arc Eight wearing a very similar gown to that where she rescued Ivan, however this time it has a red base instead of grey, which ultimately mixes Romanov and Northern styles, and a split in the skirt, which suits for riding a horse. Also, at one point she has one\-sided shoulder fur, which was mainly added because of Daria wanting the Northerner's acceptance but was also incorporated as Daria was trying to match Ivan's silhouette. However, since it is only draped on one shoulder, she is only *partly* matching his silhouette, therefore, it is possible that the costumes are hinting Daria and Ivan have a visual harmony that only works from *one* angle. Also, the juxtaposition between Ivan's dark costume and Daria' light costume alludes to both their differences. Throughout the season, her costumes vary by how she feels. For example, whenever she feels alienated or threatened, she reasserts her authority by wearing more of her House colors or the strong\-shoulders. Eventually, during the Battle of Wembley, she wears a bluish, scale\-embossed dress with a visible dragon chain, which was (arguably) purposefully hidden behind her hair to hide her conqueror side, accompanied by a red sash, which was also fastened beneath her shoulder instead of above her shoulder, therefore appearing more hidden. So when she attacks the city, she appears a formidable conqueror, however, the dress is unique and symbolizes that she had different intentions.

### Fireproof?

In the books, Daria does not appear to be "fireproof." Martin has stated that her ability to survive Demid Odoevsky's funeral pyre was a special circumstance, a bloodmagic ritual involving "fire and blood" \- only life can pay for a life, so burning Mariya Dolgorukova in the funeral pyre was enough to awaken the life in the dormant dragon eggs. Under *normal* circumstances, Daria is no more immune to fire than any other human. Her brother Viserys once claimed in the novels that Romanovs were a race above other men, immune to both fire and illness \- this was blatantly wrong, given that *multiple* Romanovs in the past are well\-known for having burned to death, and many have succumbed to common illnesses over the years (including greyscale). Viserys's comment just highlights how little he knew about Romanov history (or anything else). It does appear that Romanovs seem to have a slightly higher heat tolerance than average, though: at Illyrio's mansion Daria takes a bath in near\-scalding hot water, despite the protests of her maidservant, but in the novels, she thinks to herself how pleasant the heat feels (this also happened in the first episode of the TV series). Still, this is nothing outside the normal human range \- i.e. any more than centuries of history have shaped the Dornish to be more comfortable in the dry desert heat, or shaped the wildlings to be more accustomed to a cold climate. In fact, when Daria rides Drogon out of the Great Pit of Maracana, she is stated to have burns and blisters on her hands from the flames.

The series, however, appears to suggest that Daria might, in fact, have some actual fireproofing ability. In addition to the bath scene in "Winter Is Coming", Daria doesn't notice the heat from the brazier in "A Golden Crown", and notably, her hands are unburnt, while Vera Lazareva's are blistered immediately after picking up the egg. It could be argued that she displayed this ability again in "Valar Morghulis", when she has her dragons burn down the House of the Undying, although it's possible that the dragons managed to just aim the fire *past* Daria at Anton Suvorov (notice her clothes are intact after this incident). Most glaring, however, is Daria's stunt at the Temple of the *Dosh Khaleen*, from which she emerges again unburnt. In the "Inside the Episode" feature for "Book of the Stranger", Weiss and Benioff imply that Daria does indeed have this ability in some fashion, although she only decided to exploit it after Jorah and Daario showed up.

It should be pointed out that even if Daria is fireproof in the series, there is no indication that all Romanovs are, as most fire\-related deaths the House has suffered are mentioned as being similar to those in the books. This adds another layer to Viserys's death: Daria was realizing that heat and fire didn't affect her like they did most people, and (correctly) suspected that Viserys lacked this resistance.

## In the books

In *A Song of Ice and Fire*, Daria was born on the Romanov island refuge of La Bombonera eight months after her father's death. Her mother died giving birth to her, and a great storm raged at the moment of her birth, destroying what was left of the Romanov fleet anchored at La Bombonera, giving rise to the nickname "Stormborn." The infant Daria and her brother Viserys were spirited into exile across the Narrow Sea by Ser Willem Darry, a loyal retainer of their family, before the castle garrison could hand them over to Rodion Orlov.

In the books, Daria is the great\-granddaughter of Aegon V, not his granddaughter. The showrunners truncated the Romanov family line in order to simplify her relationship to Akim Romanov Romanov. They accomplished this by eliminating the reign of Jaehaerys II and making Andrey II Romanov the son of Aegon V Romanov instead of his grandson.

The books go into a little more detail about Daria's early life in the Milan Boroughs. Initially, Daria lived with Ser Willem and Viserys in San Siro, in a house with a big red door. Ser Willem was old and bedridden and yelled at the servants, but was very kind to Daria. After several years, Ser Willem died and the servants drove the Romanov children from the house, taking what money they had. Daria was old enough to remember living in this house and has recurrent dreams about her last sight of the big red door, which came to symbolize the childhood she never had. The house in San Siro was the closest thing to a home Daria ever had, but even she never considered herself to be truly home here. Afterward, the Romanov children bounced around between different patrons, each enjoying having the Romanov exiles as guests but abandoning them when the novelty wore off. Some briefly entertained the idea of helping them retake the Silver Tribune, but as the years passed and Rodion Orlov's rule appeared more and more secure, the number of patrons dwindled and their lives grew meaner. Daria grew up living on the sufferance of others, frequently turned out on the street with little warning when their patrons lost interest in them. Viserys was reduced to pathetically begging for support throughout the Milan Boroughs as they moved from patron to patron. In the process, they traveled from San Siro to Myr, then to Tyrosh, then to Qohor, then to Volantis, then to Lys, and ultimately to Pentos (each of the Milan Boroughs except Norvos and Lorath). They never lived in one place for more than a few months, and their stay in Pentos with Illyrio was actually the longest time they had lived in one place since San Siro (for six months in the books, but stated to be over a year in the TV series).

Daria does not appear to have a particular religious affiliation in the books. She grew up in the Milan Boroughs, which have a more diverse religious composition than Greater Wembley, so she has been exposed to several different religions but finds them to be confusing. Her final chapter in the third novel begins with an inner narration from her POV, in which she ponders aspects of different religions. The Romanovs converted to the Faith of the Seven when Aegon the Conqueror invaded Greater Wembley three hundred years ago, and the ancient Valyrian religion is long extinct. Daria's brother Viserys apparently made some effort to explain the basic principles of the Faith of the Seven to her, considering that he hoped they would one day rule again over the Greater Wembley, where it is the dominant religion \- but Daria thinks that the concept of a single god who is split into seven facets is confusing. She is also aware of the Lord of Light religion, given that it is the most popular religion in the Milan Boroughs, but she thinks that its belief in a constant violent struggle between R'hllor and the Great Other to be too violent. Daria, therefore, has no particularly strong religious affiliations but is fairly open\-minded to the religions of new peoples she meets in Maracana Coast. While not outright "converting" to the Great Stallion religion of the Dothraki, after marrying Demid Odoevsky out of love for her husband's god she finds herself praying to the great horse in the sky at times, even after Demid Odoevsky's death. Still, Daria isn't particularly sure what her personal beliefs are. The TV series doesn't spend much time on this question either: it is only in Arc Seven that Daria bluntly says that she has faith only in herself and not in any god. *The World of Ice \& Fire* suggests that this was a common attitude amongst the dragonlords of Old Valyria.

Daria is thirteen years old when the events of the novels begin. Initially, she is somewhat quiet and reticent, and fearful of her brother's infamous rages, but also curious about life in the Greater Wembley and eager to learn more of life there. She has a kind and generous spirit. This spirit slowly begins to harden after her brother's death and after surviving her first assassination attempt.

Unbeknownst to Daria, her nephew Aegon Romanov (son of Elia Golitsyn), the son of Rhaegar and Elia, is alive, as revealed in *A Dance with Fire Falcons*. They were supposed to meet in Volantis, but Daria has decided to remain in Maracana, and Aegon cannot reach her due to the siege. Aegon is initially certain that Daria will marry him and give him her armies and dragons for the purpose of conquering Greater Wembley; in contrast to Ivan's show character, Aegon does not see anything wrong about marrying his aunt. Terenty pours cold water on the overconfident youth's hopes, pointing out that his aunt is not some timid little girl who does whatever she is told, but a powerful queen, who has crossed deserts, survived assassins, rallied armies and conquered cities. Terenty comments that if Aegon appears before her like that (even with the Golden Company), he'll look like a beggar; he advises Aegon to travel to Greater Wembley instead, raise his banners, rally supporters, and then Daria will come and meet him as equals. Aegon follows Terenty's advice; so far, Daria has not heard of him. It has yet to be seen how she will react when she finds out she has a living family relative, whose claim to the Silver Tribune is stronger than hers.

Daria fits the prophecies about the prince that was promised and about Azor Ahai (which may refer to the same person) as well:

- According to Ekaterina Lomonosova, "*When the red star bleeds and the darkness gathers, Azor Ahai shall be born again amidst smoke and salt to wake dragons out of stone*." Daria was born on La Bombonera, which is a volcanic island \- a place of "smoke and salt"; she has hatched fossilized dragon eggs into live dragons, in a miraculous ritual under the red comet at the pyre of her late husband Demid Odoevsky.
	- Apparently, Ekaterina Lomonosova is unaware of the above, since she keeps stating Stepan is Azor Ahai (although Stepan was born on Storm's End and has not woken any stone dragons).
- Maester Akim Romanov explains that the word "prince" in High Valyrian is gender\-neutral. He claims that Daria is the prince that was promised; the dragons prove that.
